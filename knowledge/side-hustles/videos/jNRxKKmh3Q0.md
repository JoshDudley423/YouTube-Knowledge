# How to Make $15k/Month from Facebook Marketplace
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNRxKKmh3Q0 | Published: 2024-08-21

Interview with Alex Hansen ("The Shelf Dude," theshelfdude.com), who built a six-figure custom-garage-shelving business selling locally via Facebook Marketplace.

## The business idea
- Builds custom wood shelving units sized specifically to hold stackable storage totes (e.g. 27-gallon totes) in garages — a narrow, specific niche ("empty space gets filled; everyone has storage, everyone has stuff").
- Started after seeing a similar shelf design on Facebook and building one to solve his own garage-organization problem ("see a need, fill a need").
- Sold almost entirely through personal Facebook Marketplace listings, not a business page or paid ads (at least initially).
- Also sells DIY build plans (digital product) via his own website as a lower-touch offshoot of the physical product.

## Numbers
- Material cost per shelf: ~$200 (buying 2x4s in bulk from Home Depot, ~300 at a time; wheels sourced direct from a China contact; screws/hardware in bulk from Amazon).
- Sale price: $770 per shelf (4-high by 5-across configuration on wheels) — strong margin before labor.
- Build time: 3-4 hours for a newcomer following a DIY plan with no distractions; ~2 hours per unit once batching multiple shelves at once (with an employee helping).
- Volume: ~20 shelves/month average once full-time.
- Revenue split: roughly 60% Facebook Marketplace (organic, in-person sales/installs) / 40% website (mostly digital plan sales + some physical). Physical shelves are still the large majority of revenue vs. digital plans.
- Instagram grew to 56,000 followers within about a year of starting the account (started ~Jan/Feb after the business began ~Nov 2023); credited as a major driver of demand alongside Marketplace.
- Quit his day job ("full-time job... 9 to 5") after ~6 months of doing this on weekends/evenings, once the numbers (revenue minus taxes/materials vs. current job pay) penciled out with his wife.

## Process / steps
- Started as a side hustle nights/weekends alongside a full-time job; borrowed a friend's truck for pickups/deliveries before buying his own truck.
- Decision to quit day job was made via literal spreadsheet/whiteboard math with his wife: shelves needed to sell to net more than the day job, factoring materials and taxes.
- Batches production like an assembly line: cuts verticals first, then rungs, then bases, moving down stations in the garage (later upgrading to a warehouse). One employee builds/cuts while he does installs and client-facing work.
- Sells via direct Facebook Marketplace listings + Instagram; has not yet used Facebook/Instagram/Google ads (was working with a marketing agency to start running ads on Google/Facebook at time of recording) or TikTok/YouTube in any serious way.
- Offers customization upcharges: any paint color, plywood tops/sides, add-on hooks for lawn chairs, cabinet-style doors to hide bins.
- Considering (not yet built): an "installer" network model — training people in other cities/states to build and install shelves under his brand, splitting revenue on leads he generates via ads/Instagram, rather than a costly formal franchise/licensing structure. Already trained one installer in Florida this way.
- Host (Chris Koerner) suggested, unsolicited: package the business model as a low-cost PDF/course, plus a higher-ticket in-person training (analogy: an epoxy-flooring installer who now earns more from $5k/head training sessions than from installing epoxy himself) with graduates becoming "authorized installers" who get sent leads and pay a referral cut back.

## Tools / platforms named
- Facebook Marketplace (primary sales channel), Facebook boosted listings (tried, saw little ROI — his niche is specific enough that boosting doesn't add much visibility beyond organic search).
- Instagram (organic growth channel, 56k followers).
- Home Depot (bulk lumber), Amazon (bulk hardware), a China-based supplier for wheels.
- His own website (theshelfd.com) for selling DIY plans and taking service inquiries.
- Considered/planned: Google Ads, Facebook Ads via a marketing agency.

## Caveats / risks
- Local/regional business by nature — shelves aren't practical to ship nationwide, which limits competition to local copycats but also caps a single operator's addressable market.
- Facing increasing copycat competition locally (people copying his photos/listings, undercutting price) as his Instagram following grew his visibility — no clear moat beyond brand/quality/reputation.
- One-and-done purchase dynamic: unlike a subscription/membership service, once a customer buys a shelf they typically don't need another (unless they move or need more storage), so no recurring revenue from existing customers — noted explicitly as "not necessarily the best business model" compared to a membership/service model.
- Considered but hasn't executed on: upselling into garage-organization/cleaning services, or franchise/licensing (rejected as "legally expensive").
- General business-starting advice from the guest: fear of rejection/failure is the main hurdle for people with a hobby-level skill; get a deposit up front before building (his own cost of materials was covered by the customer's deposit, de-risking the first sale) rather than needing a loan to start.
