# AI Builds the Websites That Pay You Monthly
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDE7InaRxDA | Published: 2026-07-09

Walkthrough of the "rank and rent" local lead-generation business model using AI (Hostinger Horizons) to build the sites, referencing an earlier episode's guest who reportedly makes $300-$3,000/month per website.

## The business model
- Build a website for a local home-service niche (e.g. "poolcleanersdallas.com") and optimize it to rank organically in local search for that service + city.
- Site captures leads via a contact form/phone number.
- Once the site generates real leads, contact a local business in that niche/area and offer to sell them the leads (they usually say yes).
- You own the website, domain, and phone number — you're not selling the business owner a website, you're bringing them customers (leads-as-a-service), then charging recurring monthly fees for exclusive access to the leads.
- Once one site works, the exact template can be copy-pasted to a different niche or a different city for the same niche, cheaply scaling the model.
- Why it works: local service businesses are typically bad at internet marketing (not their core competency) and often run on "if it's not broke, don't fix it" — an old, unoptimized website (e.g., from 2009) that still generates some leads but far below potential.
- Expect variance: not every site will generate leads — described as "fishing with a trotline" — the more sites (hooks) you put out, the more that will produce fish (leads), but individual sites are unpredictable.

## Niche/market selection process
- Use Google Trends (trends.google.com) to confirm the service category is stable or growing (example: "pool service near me" search interest trending upward long-term, with expected seasonality).
- Rationale example given: COVID drove a pool-building boom; those pools now need ongoing service, making pool service a growing category.
- Pick a fast-growing city/market as the geographic target — example used: Bentonville, Arkansas (cited as the fastest-growing city in Arkansas and 5th fastest-growing in the US at time of filming; also home to Walmart HQ, high household wealth).
- Domain strategy: buy an exact-match domain (EMD), e.g. "bentonvillepoolservice.com" — combining service + city name signals relevance to Google and matches what searchers will type.

## Build process (using Hostinger Horizons, an AI website builder)
- Search/register the domain directly within Hostinger (hostinger.com/domain-name-search).
- Use the AI chat interface to generate the site: give it full context/intent in the prompt (niche, city, exact URL, that the goal is to generate organic local leads to eventually resell to real service companies, and that the site design should be reusable/copy-pasteable for other niches/markets).
- Hostinger Horizons has two chat panes: a main build chat and a "Cody" assistant that can help refine/write your prompt if you're unsure how to phrase it.
- Generated site starts as a full lead-gen template: homepage, services, service area, about, contact, built with SEO best practices (meta tags, meta descriptions, H1s, local keyword mentions, a few blog articles, mentions of nearby outlying cities), mobile-first, scalable/reusable structure.
- Iterating is done conversationally: ask the AI in plain language to add/remove form fields, add new sections (e.g., a "Why Choose Us" trust-building section — important because local customers make decisions based on trust), or edit copy/labels directly via an "edit content" toggle.
- Publish the site (temporary domain first to verify it works), then connect the purchased custom domain and republish.

## Lead-form friction/conversion tradeoff ("intent vs. friction")
- Direct tradeoff between form friction and lead intent/quality: fewer required fields (e.g., just email) yields a higher raw conversion rate but lower-intent, lower-quality leads; more required fields (phone, service needed, address, etc.) lowers conversion rate but raises the quality/close rate of the leads that do convert.
- Illustrative example from the host's own past business (sport court construction, a high-ticket service): a lead form asking only name+email generated many leads but a 0% close rate; adding more friction (house size, yard size, etc.) dropped total lead volume but raised close rate to roughly 30%.
- Rule of thumb: higher-ticket services warrant more form friction/qualification; low-ticket, low-consideration services (example: $50 lawn mowing) should keep friction low.
- There's no universal "right" amount of friction — it must be tuned per niche/price point.

## Monetization/integration options
- Primary monetization: sell exclusive access to leads to a local business in that niche, charged as a recurring relationship (not a one-time website sale).
- Can integrate Stripe (via API key) directly into the site to build a simple invoicing/payment portal for billing the business customers who buy leads (a hidden/password-protected admin page), rather than billing the actual homeowner leads.
- Can alternatively/additionally monetize with Google AdSense by adding a publisher ID and choosing ad placement — noted as a legitimate option though not the strategy emphasized in this model (the video suggests it's viable since visitors are already used to/ok with ads).
- Even after the AI build, still recommends manual off-site SEO work: get backlinks from local sources like Chambers of Commerce, and set up a Google Business Profile.

## Getting the first customer
- Do not try to sell leads to a business before you actually have leads flowing — you'll come across as unproven.
- Instead, give the first batch of leads away for free to a target local business so they can verify both that the leads are real and that they're good quality.
- Once they've experienced free, working leads, businesses tend to want to buy all future leads and often want exclusivity — which lets you charge more and removes the need to keep shopping the leads to multiple buyers.

## Caveats
- Not every website will succeed — some will be "duds," and even successful ones can take weeks or months to start generating meaningful lead volume.
- Requires ongoing off-platform SEO effort (backlinks, business profile) beyond what the AI builder auto-generates to fully rank.
- The scale-economics example ($8K/month across 10 sites) is presented as an illustrative "let's say" projection by the host, not a documented, verified outcome from his own execution of this specific method.
