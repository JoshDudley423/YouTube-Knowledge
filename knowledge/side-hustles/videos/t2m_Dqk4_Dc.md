# Overlooked Businesses You Can Start for Almost Nothing
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t2m_Dqk4_Dc | Published: 2026-03-03

Interview with Alex Smereczniak (Franzy — a franchise-matching platform, prior founder of a laundry business that scaled to ~$18M/yr and franchised 118 locations in 14 months). Topic: low-cost franchise concepts with outsized per-unit revenue. Uses figures pulled from FDDs (Franchise Disclosure Documents, FTC-regulated, publicly filed) and Item 19 sections (audited franchisee financial performance).

## General franchising mechanics
- SBA loans can finance up to 90% of a franchise's startup cost; if a franchise costs $100K to start, you may only need ~$10K out of pocket (which can itself come from friends/family), since SBA loans are designed specifically to help fund new franchise purchases, not just buying an existing business.
- Franchise maturity as a risk signal: a brand with 80-100+ open units and 5-10+ years of operating history indicates proven systems/market fit and lower risk; earlier-stage/newer concepts carry more risk but allow you to claim exclusive territory early.
- Exit multiples: an independent single-location business (e.g., a standalone gym) might sell for ~3-4x EBITDA; a mature national franchise brand (e.g., Planet Fitness) can command ~5-7x; Orange Theory at its peak reportedly traded around ~19x EBITDA (a SaaS-like multiple) due to brand strength, diversification, and de-risked recurring cash flow. Owning multiple units/territories ("multi-unit, multi-brand operator," called a "MUMBO") pushes your exit multiple up toward what larger multi-unit owners get.
- Comparison point on franchise economics: a Subway location reportedly nets only ~$30K/year — you effectively need to own ~10 units to make real money; a Batteries Plus location nets roughly $300K/year; Planet Fitness locations reportedly net $500-600K/year each.
- Evaluation heuristic raised: the harder a business would be to replicate independently (a real "moat" — skilled labor requirement, brand recognition, supply chain, proprietary data/tech), the more a franchise fee is actually buying you; a business anyone could easily start solo (example: pooper-scooper franchises) offers little moat even though several franchise chains exist for it.
- Franchise buyers should read the FDD's Item 19 (audited financial performance) but check footnotes for "adjusted EBITDA"/"adjusted revenue" caveats; suggested workflow: upload the full FDD (often hundreds of pages) to an AI tool (Claude/ChatGPT) and ask it to flag red/yellow/green flags.

## Concept #3: Private insurance claim adjusting franchise
- What it does: represents homeowners/claimants in negotiating with their own insurance company after property damage (fire, water, hail, etc.) to increase the payout; takes a flat percentage of the total claim amount (with a guaranteed minimum improvement so the client isn't worse off).
- Market gap: only ~1% of insurance claims currently use a private adjuster — cited as a large "white space" opportunity.
- Numbers: claims are typically increased ~7x (e.g., a $2K claim becomes ~$17K) in the example given; franchise cost ~$43,000, but with an SBA loan you may only need ~$5-10K out of pocket; average per-franchisee revenue cited at **$2.6 million**; gross margin 70-90%; net margin 25-45% (roughly $650K-$1.17M net profit for average operators, more for top operators, described as ~$700-800K profit for "better operators"); takes a few years to ramp to the average revenue figure.
- Franchisor support: provides leads, AI/data-driven tools (e.g., weather-driven data systems), training, help obtaining required licensure — you don't need prior insurance experience.
- Viability solo (without a franchise), per guest's own 1-10 scale: **3-4 out of 10** — heavily regulated, credibility-dependent (clients are dealing with a stressful, high-stakes situation and want a recognized brand/track record), and you'd be building the carrier relationships, data systems, and lead engine from scratch.

## Concept #2: Home-sale prep / light renovation franchise
- What it does: preps a house for sale — staging, cleaning, and "smart" small renovations (bathroom refresh, exterior paint, new garage door) — priced/justified by data suggesting these specific improvements increase sale price by roughly 2-3x their cost. Operator acts as a general contractor/project manager coordinating subcontractors (electrician, drywall, plumber), not doing the physical work themselves.
- Numbers: franchise investment $110K-150K (roughly $10-15K out of pocket via SBA financing); average annual revenue **$2.7 million**; average ticket ~$15,000 (implying roughly 180 closings/year, "one closing every two days"); gross margin 40-50%; net margin 12-22%. Some businesses offer "pay at closing" financing since sellers may not have $15K liquid before their home sale proceeds land.
- Customer acquisition channel: relationships with real estate brokers/agents, who refer sellers because the service both increases the seller's payout and the agent's commission — aligned incentives ("the realtor acts like a salesperson for you"). Host draws a direct parallel to his own prior house-cleaning business that only took referrals from realtors for the same reason.
- Best-fit operator profile: people with real estate industry ties (current/former brokers/agents) or construction/general-contracting/project-management backgrounds (ex-military and corporate project managers specifically called out as strong fits).
- Viability solo, per guest's 1-10 scale: **6/10 for an average person, 7-8/10 for someone with project-management/military background** — a franchise mainly buys pre-built referral relationships with regional/national real estate brokerages (e.g., Keller Williams, Sotheby's) and a subcontractor bench, saving an estimated 1-2 years of relationship-building.

## Concept #1 (guest's favorite): Custom window box / seasonal planter subscription franchise
- What it does: installs custom decorative window boxes/planters on homes' exteriors, then runs a recurring subscription swapping in seasonal flowers/decor (Valentine's Day, Christmas, Easter themes) on a monthly/quarterly cadence.
- Two revenue streams: (1) one-time installation, priced per window, "in the thousands of dollars" per job; (2) recurring subscription for flower swaps/maintenance, averaging **$1,300-$2,200 per customer per year**.
- Numbers: franchise investment under $140K (roughly $14-15K out of pocket via financing); average annual revenue per territory **$4.5 million**; gross margin 60-65%; net margin 20-25% (implying roughly $900K-$1.1M+ net profit per territory for the average operator).
- Low overhead: no storefront needed, just a small operations/materials hub for supplies before installs.
- Recession resilience note: guest's prior laundry business taught him that discretionary home services skew toward more affluent households, which are comparatively more recession-resistant; he expects this business's customer base (upper-middle-class homeowners) to behave similarly, though it's not a true "need" business like laundry.

## Wild card #1: Artificial turf installation franchise
- Driver: homeowners tired of lawn maintenance, plus emerging municipal water-restriction laws — cited example: Las Vegas banned growing natural grass starting 2026; guest expects similar restrictions to spread to other water-scarce regions (California, Florida named as candidates).
- Numbers: investment under $150K; revenue often exceeds **$2 million/year**; profit roughly $300-400K/year.

## Wild card #2: Commercial/short-term-rental (Airbnb) cleaning franchise
- What it does: residential/short-term-rental and commercial office cleaning; franchise provides tech/tooling to source leads across Airbnb hosts, businesses, and single-family homes (including tools to identify/scrape lists of local Airbnb owners/operators).
- Numbers: investment $50-60K; average revenue **$1.26 million** (note: transcript garbles the exact out-of-pocket figure as "6k to get started," likely meaning a small SBA-financed down payment); mostly margin since it's typically owner-operated at first (buyer works as one of the cleaners initially, then hires/scales a team).
- Lifestyle note: guest describes franchisees who have scaled their local cleaning team enough to relocate abroad (Europe, Africa) and run the business remotely/nomadically.
- Framed as a realistic replacement for a $150-300K/year corporate salary rather than a path to becoming "a multi-millionaire."

## Broader franchising anecdote
- Guest cites a franchisee who started with zero locations 7 years ago and now operates 120 units across multiple unrelated brands (Dave's Hot Chicken, Pop Up Bagels, Marco's Pizza, European Wax) — a 120-unit system of this type is estimated to generate roughly $600 million/year in system-wide revenue; this operator's role is purely site selection, capital-raising, and team operations — brand/menu/supply-chain/marketing is handled entirely by the franchisor.
- Guest personally owns franchises himself, including a golf simulator brand called "Another Nine."

## Caveats
- Revenue/profit figures cited are franchise-wide averages from FDD Item 19 disclosures — actual results vary by operator, territory, and time (all these businesses take "a few years to ramp" to average figures); FDDs can present "adjusted" EBITDA/revenue figures, so read footnotes carefully.
- Insurance-adjusting franchise requires navigating licensure/regulatory requirements; the home-prep-for-sale business's core pitch (that specific renovations reliably return more than they cost) is acknowledged as based on estimates/studies, not provable on any individual sale.
- Resource named: Franzy.com (guest's company) — AI-based matching tool across ~4,000 franchise concepts, plus free one-on-one franchise coaching and a podcast ("How I Franchise This").
