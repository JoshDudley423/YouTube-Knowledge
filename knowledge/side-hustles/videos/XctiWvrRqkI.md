# We Turned Random Words Into $20K/Month Businesses Ideas
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XctiWvrRqkI | Published: 2025-11-19

Improv-style episode (Chris + guest Sam Thompson) using a random-word generator to riff on business ideas targeting $20K/month profit, interspersed with genuinely concrete business philosophy and a couple of specific playbooks.

## Framework: high-ticket/low-volume beats low-ticket/high-volume for hitting $20K/month solo
- Core argument: to reach $20K/month profit, the price point per customer matters enormously for how "demoralizing"/hard the required volume is.
- Example: at $40/month per customer (typical low-end SaaS/subscription pricing), you need ~500 customers to hit $20K/month — a brutal customer-acquisition slog, especially without an existing audience (an ads-driven CAC battle where you're "losing money for your first year").
- Contrast example: a garage cleanout service charging $700/job needs only ~1 job/day (about 6 hours of work including 1-2 hours of lead gen/admin) to hit ~$21,000/month — described as simple, replicable, and something "anyone can go and do."
- Recommended sweet spot: a $500-$1,000/month-per-customer price point — high enough that you only need a manageable number of customers (dozens, not thousands) to reach meaningful income, but low enough to still be an easy local/service-business sell.
- The garage-cleanout/local-service model is also recurring (e.g., a customer's garage won't stay clean forever — think annual "spring cleanout" repeat business).
- Scaling path suggested: start solo, then bring on help at a fixed sub-rate once you have overflow demand (example: pay a hired helper $400/job while you charge the customer more, pocketing the spread) — described as an easy "stair-step" way to grow without over-engineering a scalable SaaS from day one.

## Two conceptual "boxes" for a solo, no-employee ~$250K/year business
1. **Digital/broad-stroke box:** software, e-commerce, agency/freelance work — location-independent but requires strong customer-acquisition skills, especially at low price points.
2. **Physical/local box:** service businesses tied to a specific location (pressure washing, stump grinding, car washing, garage cleanouts, etc.) — much faster path to meaningful cash flow because it doesn't require solving customer acquisition at scale; especially suited to someone with strong local community ties.

## Recurring advice: "start with an audience" when you don't know what to sell
- Claim: almost any business can start as a media company (blog, newsletter, TikTok, etc.) — build an audience around something you're genuinely interested in, and monetization opportunities (selling ads, referring leads to local businesses, spotting a service gap) tend to reveal themselves organically once you have engaged followers.
- Concrete example (Chris's own project): a local newsletter (with a co-host, "Lauren") — ~3,200 subscribers, not actively monetized via ad sales but occasionally used to shout out local businesses for free; primary value framed as being an "asset" from which the owner can launch adjacent local service businesses later (leveraging owned local-business domain names as one signal of this strategy — e.g., owning SouthBayCarpetCleaning.com, SouthBayBookkeeping.com, etc., to plug in a service later).
- Companion low-effort audience tactic mentioned: posting simple TikToks (e.g., "10 best bars in [town]" video using stock/B-roll footage) tied to a local newsletter brand grew to ~100 followers within about a week, drawing engagement from local businesses (restaurants, realtors) — framed as a cheap, fast way to start building a local audience/asset.

## Business idea: programmatic acquisition of local-service domain names
- Concept: build/run an automated agent (or bot) that systematically checks every US city (by population) crossed with every common home-service business category (e.g., "[City] landscaping," "[City] carpet cleaning," "[City] plumber") and registers whichever domain combinations are still unregistered.
- Suggested filtering parameters to avoid wasting money on junk domains: skip domains over a certain character/syllable length, and skip cities under a population threshold (e.g., under 100,000 people).
- Monetization path described: you don't need to operate the underlying service business yourself — you can sell leads generated through the domain/site to an existing local operator (example given: approaching an existing carpet-cleaning business owner and offering to sell them leads at a markup versus what they currently pay elsewhere).
- Real example cited by one host: personally owns domains like HireLosAngeles.com and HireNewYork.com, intended as job-board sites for large metro areas.
- Related aside: high-value premium domain names as a speculative asset — one host mentioned negotiating (unsuccessfully, offers of $150K-$170K rejected) to buy a specific premium .com domain listed for $250K, and separately claims a different niche domain purchase (an "international lawyers"-style domain, ~$10K cost) "paid for itself in one lead" for a divorce-related project.

## Domain/name-driven business idea generated live via random-word game: "Full Lounge" — a marketing agency for bars/nightclubs
- Prompt words: "lounge," "full," "play" (random word generator).
- Idea: a marketing agency specializing in helping bars and nightclubs stay full/busy — differentiated primarily by a clever, on-brand domain/name ("FullLounge.com") rather than a technically novel service (host explicitly notes "we're not reinventing the wheel... but the name makes the idea," drawing a parallel to how he previously bought the domain "Unlimited Content" and built an SEO/content-focused agency around it).
- When graded head-to-head by Claude (prompted to compare it against a competing idea — an airport-lounge one-time-access app — purely on speed-to-profitability with minimal time/money/effort) FullLounge scored 8/10 vs. 3/10 for the airport lounge app. Claude's stated reasoning for FullLounge: can be started immediately with just a laptop and phone, bars have urgent/ongoing need for foot traffic so first-client conversion can be fast (1-2 weeks with hustle), a clear ROI pitch is easy to make, and niche specialization gives a competitive edge over generalist marketing agencies. Reasoning against the lounge-access app: requires significant upfront capital and airline/lounge partnership deals, highly competitive space, long sales cycles, low margins. (Aside: the "airport lounge access app" idea turned out to already exist commercially as "LoungeBuddy.")
- Illustrative revenue framing for FullLounge: ~20 nightclub/restaurant clients paying ~$1,000/month each would hit the $20K/month target.

## General philosophy/caveats raised
- Warning against "shiny object syndrome": hosts describe themselves as professionally prone to chasing new business ideas (part of their content format), but stress that any idea only becomes meaningful with sustained, unglamorous execution over time — abandoning an idea after the initial "fun" 2-week phase because the real work (not the idea itself) is what makes it profitable is called out as the actual failure mode to avoid.
- Caution on "passive income" expectations for digital products/micro-SaaS: the barrier to launching small apps/SaaS products (e.g., via AI/"vibe coding") has dropped a lot, making them easy to launch but hard to sustain — competitors can replicate quickly, so a "set and forget" portfolio of small apps is unrealistic; continual improvement/investment is required to defend any digital product's traction.
- General encouragement: making your first dollar from any small flip or side project (example: buying something for $100 and reselling for $140 on Facebook Marketplace) is framed as a meaningful psychological threshold worth pursuing early, rather than waiting for a "perfect" bigger idea.
