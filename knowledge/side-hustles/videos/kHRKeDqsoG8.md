# She Buys $1 Pallets And Sells Them For $5,000 (30 Mins/Day)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHRKeDqsoG8 | Published: 2025-12-23

Interview with Mindi Reinke (X: @hey_mindi), a homeschooling stay-at-home mom of four married to a firefighter, on her book/curriculum-pallet reselling business plus a follow-on high-end clothing liquidation venture.

## The business idea(s)
1. **Book/curriculum pallet arbitrage**: buy pallets of surplus/liquidated books from school districts via GovDeals (a government/public-sector surplus auction site) for as little as $1, sort them (picture books, chapter books, teacher curriculum sets), and resell primarily on eBay — with curriculum sets being by far the most profitable category.
2. **Liquidation apparel live-selling**: separately bought a large liquidated lot of Rent the Runway (high-end designer clothing rental company) returned/blemished inventory via B-Stock, and resells it via live-selling on Whatnot.
3. Both are framed as extremely low-barrier: "if you have a phone, you can do this business... today," starting for as little as $0 (picking up free books/curriculum people are giving away on Facebook Marketplace) up to a few dollars per pallet.

## Numbers — books/curriculum
- First purchase: two pallets of books for $1 each (~$2.26 total after tax) from GovDeals, originally intended just to keep for personal home-library use, not resale.
- Pallet contents: roughly 2,000 books total between two pallets — one pallet ~all middle-school novels/chapter books, the other ~50% picture books / 50% curriculum.
- Selling channels: eBay (primary — ~98% of what she actually sells is curriculum), Facebook Marketplace (recommended as likely better for picture/chapter books, especially in metro areas — she didn't use it because she lives too rurally for FB Marketplace demand), and donated most chapter books to her local library (used for library fundraising).
- Revenue: ~$5,000 gross from the first two pallets over about 2 months of selling (net lower after ~15% eBay platform fees + ~15% shipping fees, working out to roughly 60% net margin per the hosts' estimate).
- Time investment: ~3 hours/week. Host's back-of-envelope math: ~40 hours total work, 60% net margin on $5,000 gross ≈ $3,000 profit ÷ 40 hours ≈ $75/hour, which extrapolated to a full 40-hr/week job would be ~$150,000/year (explicitly caveated as a rough, non-scaled estimate).
- Since then: bought ~11 pallets total over ~3 months; has never lost money on a pallet/listing. Most she's paid for one pallet: $32 (when she could see valuable contents in photos). One curriculum listing bundle sells for ~$45 average; range is roughly $15 (her stated floor, below which shipping eats the margin) up to $200-350+ for premium curriculum sets. Recently bought 10 copies of a curriculum she'd sold before (worth ~$50 each) for $1 total on GovDeals that morning.
- A school purchasing manager proactively offered her 50 pallets of curriculum headed for the trash, illustrating scale of available inventory beyond what's auctioned.

## Numbers — Rent the Runway / Whatnot apparel
- Bought two pallets of Rent the Runway liquidated clothing (blemished, no-longer-rentable designer pieces + handbags/accessories) via B-Stock; manifest MSRP was ~$355,000 combined.
- Total cost: ~$5,400 (~$6/item across ~900 items), funded entirely from book-business profits ("playing with house money").
- After 6 one-hour Whatnot live shows with zero existing following: $1,200 in total sales (~$200/hour gross before item cost; ~$180/hour profit per the hosts' calc).
- Her own projection: if she sold every item at a flat $29 starting price, she'd clear roughly $30,000 after platform fees, estimated to take a couple of months of consistent shows.
- Mix of inventory: roughly 50% formal dresses, 50% casual/cocktail attire, plus sweaters, outerwear, jeans.

## Process / steps
- Sourcing: monitor GovDeals (and similar sites like PublicSurplus) daily/constantly for school-district surplus auctions; set a search radius (she uses up to 250 miles) since pickup is local-only (no shipping on heavy book pallets). Bids on this site rarely get competitive — most close at the $1 opening bid.
- Because manifest photos are often too low-quality to identify book titles by eye, she screenshots pallet listing photos and asks ChatGPT to identify titles/publishers/curricula visible in the image — this is explicitly described as the innovation that makes the business viable ("this wasn't nearly as intriguing 3 years ago before ChatGPT... it would have taken the better part of a year to identify all of those books by hand").
- After winning a pallet: photograph groups of books (e.g., lined up in ~20 labeled boxes on a table), upload all photos to ChatGPT (paid $20/month plan), and have it generate: a master manifest (box number, title, author, reading level), suggested bundles of ~10 books grouped by reading level/genre, a pick list telling her which box/title to pull for each bundle, and complete SEO-optimized eBay listing copy.
- Physical listing setup: photograph books on a white poster board on a folding table for clean, consistent product photos.
- Workflow fits around parenting: ~1 hour during nap time to prep a batch, ~30-40 minutes in the evening to actually post listings; posts consistently multiple times a week rather than all at once, because eBay's algorithm rewards consistent/frequent posting (she observed faster sell-through when posting regularly).
- Always offers free shipping on every listing (buyers favor free-shipping listings even at equal or higher total price) and ships books via USPS Media Mail (cheaper).
- For identifying likely high-value pallets before bidding: look for visible complete/boxed sets in the photos (signals a complete, sellable set); look for large single-title lots (can sell both as bulk lot for co-ops/homeschool groups and split into individual listings — hits two buyer types); recognize recurring high-value publishers/curricula by name (examples cited: Lucy Calkins "Units of Study," Heinemann, SRA — SRA sets reportedly sell for $60-100 each against ~$7-8 shipping cost).
- Cross-checks ChatGPT's suggested resale values against actual eBay current listings and sold listings before pricing, because ChatGPT's price estimates are "sometimes pretty off" — use it for identification/organization, not final pricing.
- For the Whatnot apparel side: learned the shipping/label workflow carefully via YouTube tutorials first, citing it as the single biggest way new live-sellers lose money if mishandled (per both host and guest — "you can literally lose your shirt[s]").

## Tools / platforms named
- GovDeals.com and PublicSurplus (government/school surplus liquidation auction sites).
- B-Stock (bstock.com) — used for the Rent the Runway apparel lot.
- eBay (including its "sold listings" research tool) — primary book/curriculum sales channel; has a built-in AI item-identification feature in-app.
- Facebook Marketplace — recommended alternative for picture/chapter books, especially in metro markets.
- Whatnot — live-selling video-auction platform for the apparel resale; noted as having just raised ~$250M in funding and still being an early/growing platform for resellers.
- ChatGPT (Plus, $20/month) — image-based book/curriculum identification, bundling logic, and listing copywriting.
- Poshmark — originally intended sales channel for the Rent the Runway clothing before she pivoted to Whatnot live-selling once she saw the volume (900 mostly-unique items).

## Caveats / risks
- You often don't know exactly what's in a pallet before buying (school liquidation manifests can be vague) — mitigated by the low cost ($1-$32 typically) making even a "dud" pallet low financial risk; she has never lost money on a pallet but frames this as inherently a low-risk, not zero-risk, bet.
- Geographic/demand variation: her rural location has little Facebook Marketplace activity for books, pushing her to eBay; she's noticed most of her curriculum buyers are in the Midwest/East Coast, rarely the West Coast — possibly tied to how different states fund and refresh school curricula (Washington State reportedly has unusually high pallet supply).
- Time-boxed/niche appeal: she explicitly calls this "a boring category for most people" and says the opportunity works because so few other resellers compete in it (e.g., she's one of only three eBay sellers listing one particular curriculum).
- Shipping cost/logistics is a real constraint — books are heavy, so she only buys local-pickup lots and won't ship raw books; sets a $15 minimum listing price to keep shipping-fee-to-price ratio reasonable.
- On Whatnot specifically: as a totally new seller with no following, results are modest in absolute terms ($1,200 from 6 shows) — building a following/higher average sale prices takes sustained, repeated live shows ("a grind to get established").
- General framing caveat from the host: this is a "very niche market" — success depended on genuine willingness to do something not intrinsically exciting, and on treating it as a real, if small-time-investment, business rather than a one-off treasure hunt.
