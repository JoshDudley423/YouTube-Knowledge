# AI Agencies Just Got Simple Enough for Anyone to Start
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rJ1ZpfuajkA | Published: 2025-09-19

Interview with Flo Crivello, founder of Lindy (AI automation/agent-building platform), on the Lindy 3.0 release and starting an AI automation agency.

- Core idea: start an "AI automation agency" -- insert yourself into small/medium businesses and use Lindy (or similar) to automate their processes. Pricing model named: charge $500-$5,000/month recurring, OR $300-$5,000 one-time setup + $200-$700/month ongoing.
- Why now: Lindy 3.0 (released ~1 month before this episode) added two major features that remove the technical barrier: (1) "agent builder" -- create a full automation just by typing/dictating a plain-language prompt (no drag-and-drop flow building, no API keys), and (2) "computer use" -- agents get a virtual mouse/keyboard so they can operate any web-based tool through the browser UI, even internal tools with no API/MCP integration. Removes the prior requirement of being technical/an engineer to build these.
- Concrete AI-agency service ideas discussed:
  - AI SDR (sales development rep) agency: agent finds leads online, qualifies them, researches them, sends personalized cold emails, handles objections, A/B tests subject lines/copy, and books calls -- fully autonomous. Framed as "should be a Lindy" (i.e., a task a dozen VC-funded startups already do as their entire business).
  - Lead-arbitrage/reselling model: as a non-employee, build an AI SDR agency in someone else's niche (e.g., Salesforce sales), generate warm/qualified leads via automated outreach, then sell/hand off completed conversations (not cold intros) to a rep for a fee -- pitch: "deliver the value before you ask for payment," which dramatically raises response/close rates vs. a cold pitch.
  - Recruiting agency automation: agent scrapes company job postings/requirements from career pages, searches LinkedIn for matching candidates, and sends the company an email with 2-3 example qualified leads plus their contact info, offering "1,000 more like these" if they sign a contract -- described as getting very high response rates because it leads with delivered value.
  - Facebook/Meta ads agency: charge ~$2,500/month; use agent to scan Meta Ads Library cross-referenced with LinkedIn to find decision-makers at companies running active ads with few creatives, then cold-email them a specific, researched pitch (e.g., "you only have 4 ad creatives; accounts with 40+ see X% better ROAS; I can 10x your creatives for 30% less using AI") -- can go further and have the agent also research the prospect's competitors' top-performing ads and show them in the pitch email, or pre-build sample ad creative before the prospect even responds.
  - Influencer/creator services: (a) build a targeted influencer outreach list (e.g., "20 YouTube influencers, 20-40K subscribers, in productivity/AI niche") -- agent scrapes YouTube/TikTok/LinkedIn/Instagram/Twitter per influencer, finds contact emails, and even surfaces existing brand-partnership history unprompted; (b) sell a comment-response/DM-management service to influencers/brands drowning in cold sponsorship pitches and public comments -- an agent can auto-negotiate sponsorship deals in the inbox, moderate/delete bad-faith or abusive comments via sentiment analysis, auto-respond to fans' DMs, and turn LinkedIn/Twitter "comment magnets" (e.g., "comment X and I'll DM you a template") into an automated reply-and-DM funnel at scale (real example cited: 3,300 comments on one LinkedIn post, agent handled outreach that would've taken an estimated 55+ human-hours).
  - Vertical-specific back-office automation: example given of an existing Lindy customer/agency that specializes in automating dental-office back-office software (an intentionally narrow niche: "dentists still run on extremely antiquated software"). General framing: the value of an AI agency = intersection of (a) AI/agent tooling expertise + (b) deep knowledge of a specific business vertical's pain points -- most people only have one side of that intersection, which is the differentiator.
  - Full end-to-end example described: cold-email agency where Lindy is used to (1) find/verify prospect emails, (2) craft and send the cold email campaign, (3) handle multi-turn replies/follow-ups, (4) close the deal, and (5) collect payment via Stripe API integration -- claimed to be fully buildable today with no custom engineering.
  - Contingency-recruiting fraud-catching agent: an agent that periodically checks candidates' LinkedIn profiles post-placement to detect if a client secretly hired someone the recruiter introduced without paying the referral fee, then auto-sends an invoice/follow-up demanding payment.
- Tool/platform comparisons named: Lindy positioned as easier/more accessible than n8n (n8n requires technical setup -- manually configuring memory/vector stores, etc.) and than Zapier/Make (analogized as Zapier=user-friendly/expensive vs. Make=cheap/technical). Lindy claims no known use case that absolutely requires n8n instead, aside from native MCP support being less mature in Lindy than in more technical tools. MCP explained as "a USB port for AI agents" -- a standard protocol/connector letting agents plug into external tools/APIs.
- Cost comparators cited: replacing a paid comment-automation tool like ManyChat (~$350/month) with a custom Lindy agent doing the same comment/DM automation job.
- Go-to-market advice: don't just pitch "here's what I can offer" -- lead with "here's what I already did for you" (deliver a completed, personalized asset/lead/analysis in the outreach itself) to massively increase response rates. Also: your own tech-forward social circle (Twitter/tech bubble) is not representative -- most working professionals outside tech have never heard of AI agents, which is exactly the opportunity (this window of "advantage" phase before AI agent adoption becomes table-stakes, then eventually a disadvantage not to use them, similar to business websites historically).

Caveats/risks:
- Even with prompt-only agent building, Flo Crivello states a human consultant/expert is still needed to correctly scope automations for a specific business and to iterate -- the tools remove the coding requirement but not the need for judgment/expertise.
- Agent "computer use" automation on platforms like Twitter/LinkedIn/Instagram sidesteps official APIs by literally clicking through the UI like a human -- this is explicitly framed as a way to avoid automation-detection bans that hit API-based tools, but is still automated behavior on platforms whose terms of service typically restrict bot activity.
- MCP support in Lindy is acknowledged as less mature/friendly than in more technical platforms (n8n) -- may require workarounds for advanced integrations.
- This episode features paid sponsor reads for Lindy (affiliate link) and Design.com (logo/branding tool) -- the enthusiastic framing throughout should be read with that sponsorship in mind.
- The described "fully autonomous AI organization run by one person" and most of the more ambitious agency ideas (lawyer AI agent for collections, fully autonomous recruiting-to-invoicing pipeline) are aspirational/illustrative rather than proven, operating businesses in this transcript.
