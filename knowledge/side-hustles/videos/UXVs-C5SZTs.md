# How to Make Money With a No-Code App: A Step-by-Step Guide (Part 1/2)

Idea/riff episode with recurring guest "Sam." Part 1 of 2 (part 2 not in this batch). Covers "software dropshipping" via no-code mobile apps, a wedding-proposal-consulting lead-gen idea, and detailed Facebook-ads/affiliate-marketing math.

## Core idea: "software dropshipping" — cheap no-code consumer apps monetized via Facebook ads
- Concept: pay a developer (~$800-1,000) to build a simple consumer mobile app using no-code iOS builders, then run Facebook ads to acquire paying subscribers; treat each app like a disposable, testable asset — if it works, scale it; if not, sell it for parts.
- **Tools named**: FlutterFlow and Thunkable — no-code, mobile-first app builders that publish directly to the Apple App Store and Google Play; steeper learning curve than simpler no-code web tools but more capable. Found his developer via a Twitter DM from someone who'd watched a prior podcast episode.
- **Case in point being built live**: a recipe-directory app (vegan/keto/healthy categories) with an AI "sous chef" feature (user inputs available ingredients, e.g., "eggs, thyme, bacon," and it suggests a recipe) — named informally "Instant Chef" with an AI persona (working name "Pierre").
- **Monetization**: no free tier — 30-day free trial, then $60/year ($5/month billed annually) as the only way to access the app; explicitly avoiding a freemium/premium-upsell model.
- **Unit economics modeled live**: 
  - Target paid app-install cost: ~$5/download (though he notes competitors get sub-$2); download itself is free, paywall hit after download.
  - Assumed funnel: $2 cost per download → 20% start a free trial (~$10 cost per trial) → 33% of trials convert to paid (~$30 cost per paying customer/CAC) against $60 ARR — i.e., roughly 2x return on CAC within a 30-day cash-conversion cycle.
  - Mobile apps with recurring revenue reportedly trade at **4-5x revenue multiples**; even a conservative 3x multiple on ARR was used to argue $30 in acquisition cost creates ~$180 of enterprise value per customer (cash return + multiple).
  - At 1,000 paying customers ($60/year each = $60K ARR), guest speculated the app could be sold for roughly $250K-$300K.
- **Risk framing (explicit asymmetric-bet logic)**: total downside capped around $3,000-$4,000 (developer fee ~$800, plus ~$100/day on Facebook ads for ~2 weeks to validate, i.e. roughly $1,400 in ad testing) against a potential $30K+ ARR asset worth $100K+; worst case if it totally flops is selling the finished, live App-Store app "for parts" on marketplaces like Flippa or Acquire.com for a few thousand dollars (e.g., "here, take it for $5 grand"). Guest estimated ~50 hours of his own time either produces a working, semi-passive income stream (hire someone to run ads, he sits as a "management layer") or teaches him it doesn't work — either way not wasted, since he'd be spending that time on something else anyway.
- **Distribution/testing tactics discussed**:
  - Same "algorithm training" tactic referenced elsewhere in the channel: launch multiple automated Instagram accounts posting scraped/curated content (here: programmatically scraping the highest-performing viral cooking videos tagged e.g. #veganrecipe) and scheduling them via **Hopper HQ** (a cheaper alternative to Buffer for scheduling social content).
  - Publishing to both Apple App Store and Google Play simultaneously costs no extra effort ("one button"), even though the guest's own audience skews ~84% iPhone.
  - Co-host, inspired live during the recording, announces he'll independently build a copy-cat/adjacent app (a "sourdough companion" app) with the same developer, targeting a narrower niche, to compare notes publicly in a future episode — illustrating how cheap and fast this model is to replicate/iterate.
- Framing/rationale: consumer demand signals (viral recipe/DIY videos with millions of views, existing large recipe-content accounts, "my mom has 18 cookbooks," "people already have 7 recipe apps") indicate large addressable markets that individual creators aren't capturing directly with a monetized product — same "arbitrage the demand signal" framework used elsewhere on the channel (see also the video on viral DIY/recipe content, ID U9-cVF9QHwo).
- Personal narrative context from guest: describes shifting his own role over the past year from "creator" (hands-on builder) to "allocator" (deciding where to deploy time/money/people) as his existing businesses have grown — this app experiment is framed as a small, high-leverage side allocation rather than his main focus.

## Idea: Free wedding-proposal consulting as a lead-gen wedge into wedding planning referrals
- Insight: most wedding planning marketing targets the bride directly; instead, target the groom during proposal planning (ring, staging, photographer, after-party/restaurant) as a **free proposal consulting service**.
- Monetization: consultant doesn't charge the groom directly — instead refers him to a vetted network of wedding planners/vendors (photographers, restaurants) who pay the consultant a **referral/kickback fee** on the resulting bookings. When the impressed bride asks who helped plan the proposal, the groom name-drops the consultant, creating a natural warm referral into full wedding-planning services.
- No numbers, execution steps, or validation given — presented as a raw brainstorm, not a case study.

## Facebook ads + affiliate-program monetization model (detailed, numbers-heavy)
- General thesis: guest has shifted away from a personal-brand/"build in public" growth strategy toward pure paid-acquisition math, arguing that any business aiming for real scale (e.g., going from $3M to $10M ARR) needs ad spend eventually — so "you might as well start running ads now" rather than relying solely on organic/personal-brand growth.
- **Own newsletter ad economics** (for his other business, referenced as a live example): converting cold Facebook traffic to newsletter subscribers at **~$1.27/subscriber** at time of recording (down from ~$2.50 five days earlier, trending toward sub-$1); subscribers are then monetized via **SparkLoop** (a newsletter-referral/affiliate network) yielding roughly a **4x return** on subscriber acquisition cost. Notes this specific run targets his existing (warm/trusting) audience, but a prior campaign to a completely cold, faceless entrepreneurship-content audience (no personal brand, just lead magnets like "no-code tools directories") also achieved sub-$1.50 subscriber costs.
- **B2B service ("Unlimited Content") ad funnel example** (his own SEO-for-small-business offer): ad → squeeze page (a landing page between the ad and the booking page, shown to lift a "subtle secondary opt-in") → short video sales letter (VSL, ~4 minutes summarizing the sales pitch) → call-booking page. Reported economics: cost per **seated/qualified call** typically $60-100; a 20% call-to-close rate (deliberately modeled conservatively) at a $500 price point per new client yields a **~$500 CAC**, meaning the business breaks even on ad spend in the client's first month; because their cost structure is largely fixed overhead (an existing account manager and existing software licenses) rather than per-client COGS, added customers are close to pure margin once CAC is covered, which he says supports scaling ad spend aggressively once the CAC/close-rate math is proven. Notes real-world sales-cycle lag (~7-day average) means ad spend is effectively "eaten" for about a week before it's recovered.
- **Affiliate-arbitrage model** ("run ads for someone else's SaaS, keep the recurring commission") — presented as a lower-risk alternative to building your own SaaS:
  - Example used: GoHighLevel affiliate program pays **40% lifetime recurring commission** on referred subscriptions; framed as effectively owning a SaaS revenue stream (~$40/month per customer on a $100/month product) without any of the fulfillment, development, or support overhead of actually running a SaaS company.
  - Tactic: repurpose a single video into 5 different "hooks" (same underlying content/retention, different opening lines) and test across short-form platforms to find the best "swipe-through rate" before spending ad dollars behind the winning cut; tool named for making ad creatives: **CreativeOS.com**.
  - Modeled funnel for this specific affiliate case: $0.23 cost per click → 10% convert to a lead ($2.30/lead) → 10% of leads convert to a paying customer ($23 CAC) → $40/month recurring commission (at $99/month product price, 40% commission), with an assumed ~18-month customer lifetime — i.e., paying $23 to generate a stream of ~$40/month for ~18 months without owning the underlying company.
  - Generalized playbook for "you don't have to build the software" content-arbitrage: pick a niche (e.g., "grow your Twitter audience," "LinkedIn growth for real estate agents"), generate a lead magnet (e.g., a ChatGPT-written 10-page PDF guide) behind an email opt-in landing page (mentioned tools: GoHighLevel or Kit/ConvertKit, both with landing-page templates), then monetize the resulting list two ways simultaneously: (1) affiliate links to relevant tools in the guide's niche (named examples: Hopper HQ, CapCut, Tweet Hunter/Hypefury, Memelord.tech — social/content tools, each with affiliate programs) via SparkLoop-style newsletter monetization, and (2) selling a complementary paid service to the same list (e.g., "we make LinkedIn content/memes for real estate agents") and/or renting out CPM-based newsletter ad placements to a high-value niche audience (mentioned: ~$100/send based on CPM for a well-targeted list).
  - Total infrastructure cost cited as minimal: roughly $100/month max for GoHighLevel/ConvertKit-equivalent tooling, plus ad spend — explicitly stated you do **not** need to put your own face/personal brand on this model (ran a separate ad campaign under generic "Grow Faster" branding using static creatives from CreativeOS, no personal branding, emails not signed by name).
  - Caveat flagged: understand your CAC and **cash-conversion-cycle timing** before scaling — many affiliate networks pay on a delay (SparkLoop ~net-15, others ~net-30), so early ad spend is effectively "float" you're covering until commissions arrive.
  - Extension idea (half-joking, not executed): use AI-generated faces/avatars (mentioned app: "Captions," ~$24/month, ~80 avatar options) to front faceless content if a human face improves conversion without needing a real personal brand.
