# 5 Ways to Make Money From an AI the Government Fears
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q_FIJmSB0Ro | Published: 2026-06-20

Solo Chris Koerner episode built around a hypothetical/narrative premise: Anthropic released a powerful "Fable 5" model (from a held-back "Mythos" tier) that was shut down by a US Commerce Department export-control order within 3 days over jailbreak/security concerns, before it could be widely used. Note: this frames the episode's premise as a story/hook — treat the "Fable 5 ban" narrative as the video's setup, not verified fact; the actionable content is the business ideas built on the *capability description* (an AI agent that can work autonomously for long stretches without losing context, self-correct, and spin up parallel copies of itself).

## Premise/context (as presented in the video)
- Positions the capability jump as: unlike typical chat AI that "loses the plot" on long tasks as context windows fill, this model can be given a multi-step job, plan it, execute it, check its own work, fix mistakes, and run unattended for hours or days, including spinning up parallel copies of itself.
- Cites examples used to illustrate the capability (as stated in the video, not independently verified here): a 50-million-line codebase migration allegedly done in a day (vs. a team taking 2-4 months); a physics "frontier problem" allegedly advanced further in 36 hours than a competing model did in 4 days; the model reportedly scored ~2x a normal top model on a security/hacking skill test when guardrails were removed (cited as the real reason for the alleged shutdown).
- Framing/thesis for viewers: a government export-control/ban signal on a technology should be read as a strong buy/opportunity signal, not a reason to avoid it (draws a historical parallel to 1990s PGP encryption software being classified as a "munition," which the government ultimately lost control of, arguing regulation is a sign of category-defining power, not a dead end).

## Five business ideas pitched (to prepare for/build around powerful autonomous-AI-agent capability)

1. **Legacy code migration / modernization service**
   - Market comp: existing "modernization" agencies charge $30,000-$70,000+ (sometimes hundreds of thousands for larger companies) to migrate old software systems to modern ones, jobs that can take a team of engineers ~6 months.
   - Pitch: use a highly capable AI agent to do the migration essentially autonomously, undercutting on price/time.
   - Customer sourcing: use BuiltWith, Outscraper, or Chamber of Commerce directories to find businesses running outdated systems; pitch the office manager (most likely to be personally frustrated by an old system) directly.
   - Suggested easy first target: small local businesses (e.g., ~13-employee company migrating QuickBooks Desktop to QuickBooks Online) rather than enterprise clients, to get initial experience/proof.
   - Contextual stat cited: ~95% of ATM transactions reportedly still run on COBOL, illustrating how much legacy infrastructure exists.

2. **Overnight bookkeeping cleanup service**
   - Market comp: messy/behind small-business books typically cost $3,500-$10,000 to clean up manually; bookkeepers commonly turn down this "cleanup work" because it's tedious (evidenced by dedicated Facebook groups where bookkeepers pass on these jobs).
   - Pitch: drop a client's full financial mess (bank statements, spreadsheets, etc.) into an AI agent at end of day, have it reconcile everything overnight, review and deliver by morning — no bookkeeping background required to run this.
   - Suggested numbers: doing even one cleanup job per week at ~$3,000 each nets six figures a year, with near-zero cost of goods (AI usage cost) once set up.
   - Customer acquisition channel: post directly in bookkeeper Facebook groups offering fast (48-hour) turnaround on cleanup/overflow work they don't want.

3. **AI-staffed productized creative/design agency**
   - Reference case (cited from a separate earlier podcast episode) : a one-person design studio ("Design Joy") earning $60,000-$80,000/month charging ~$5,000/month per client on a productized flat-rate design subscription model, but capped by being a single human doing all the design work.
   - Pitch: use an AI agent as the "team" behind a similar productized service subscription (one request in the queue at a time), removing the single-operator ceiling.

4. **Hyper-personalized outbound sales/email service**
   - Premise: personalization measurably increases email reply rates (first name -> better; first name + business name -> even better; multiple personalized data points -> further lift), but manually personalizing at scale is too labor-intensive for humans and current cheaper AI tools aren't accurate/thorough enough.
   - Pitch A: run a cold outbound service that personalizes with many (8-12) unique data points per prospect using a highly capable AI agent.
   - Pitch B (suggested as the less spammy option): instead of cold outreach, take an existing business's *underused email list* (gym, med spa, any small business with an email list they're not fully utilizing) and use the AI agent to build out a much stronger email marketing program/sequence for them.

5. **Self-improving "digital asset" portfolios (website landlording)**
   - Reference: people who run portfolios of small niche websites/directory sites each earning roughly $300-$3,000/month, which compounds when run in bulk but is a grind to build and maintain by hand.
   - Pitch: use an AI agent to build these sites and continuously improve/maintain them — including logging into Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, and Google Search Console to find and auto-fix underperformance — largely unattended, over days.
   - Caveat: explicitly expects the agent to "make a lot of mistakes" and require ongoing training/correction, even though the training loop is much faster than with earlier-generation AI tools.

## General caveats/advice given
- Don't wait for the specific banned/restricted model to return — start now with whatever current AI tools are available (the video names "Opus 4.8" as an example of an accessible strong model) to prototype one of these five ideas or a personal variant, so you're ready to layer in the more powerful model once available.
- Explicit risk warning: "if this model got switched off once, it can get switched off again, or it can get nerfed" — don't build a business entirely dependent on a single AI vendor/model; treat AI as "the engine, not the car" and test multiple tools so you have a comparison baseline.
- No concrete personal revenue numbers are given for any of the five ideas — they are pitched as directionally sound based on named real-world revenue comps (Design Joy, bookkeeping cleanup pricing, code-modernization agency pricing, website-portfolio income ranges) rather than as businesses Koerner has personally run.
