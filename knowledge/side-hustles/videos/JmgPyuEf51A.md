# My 4 Favorite Seasonal Business Ideas this Week
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JmgPyuEf51A | Published: 2025-01-10

Podcast-style brainstorm episode (Chris Kerner + co-host Nick) covering several distinct business ideas, mostly Christmas-tree-adjacent plus two unrelated ideas (customer-list monetization agency, business-owner accountability service).

**1. Christmas tree concierge / delivery service**
- Observed price gap: a BLM (Bureau of Land Management) permit to cut your own tree costs $10-15; a pre-cut tree at a grocery-store lot cost $120 in his example.
- Proposed model: buy ~20 permits/year (~$10-15 each), cut trees, deliver to customers for a flat fee (e.g. ~$60 delivered, or less for pickup) -- undercutting the $120 retail price while still profiting (example math: 10 trees for ~$100 in permits, delivered at $60 each = ~$600 revenue, labor included).
- Suggested add-on revenue: charge ~$50 extra to pick up and haul away the tree after Christmas.
- Suggested pricing structure: collect payment upfront via Venmo; e.g. $60 base (with $15 passed through to cover the permit) or $100 with delivery, vs. pickup at a scheduled time/location.
- Distribution/customer acquisition ideas: flyers at competing tree lots (e.g. Albertsons parking lot); posting in local Facebook groups (mentions joining ~20 local groups in a small town as free distribution).
- Caveat raised: unclear legal/regulatory status of reselling permit-cut trees commercially -- host admits "I haven't looked at any of the regulations."

**2. Buying an existing Christmas tree lot / ancillary "unsexy" local business**
- Anecdote: a Christmas tree lot that turned out to be owned by a landscaping company operating for 30 years (two generations), found to have no functioning website (broken links, "under construction" placeholder) despite active Google reviews -- read as a signal of high profitability with zero marketing investment.
- **General acquisition heuristic proposed**: look for long-established ("30 years," two generations) local service businesses with strong reputation/reviews but neglected/absent digital presence and generic-sounding personal email addresses (e.g. name@aol.com) -- signals an aging owner nearing retirement who may be open to selling, and who is making enough money not to bother modernizing. Cited stat: "10,000 Baby Boomers are retiring every day, and 1,000 of them are business owners."
- Two paths discussed: try to buy just the tree-lot segment (deemed unlikely -- owners probably see it as high-margin and won't carve it out) vs. buying the whole business.

**3. Robotic/autonomous adjacent ideas (spun off from the landscaping business discussion)**
- Idea (credited to Andrew Wilkinson via podcast appearance): buy a landscaping business, then deploy a fleet of autonomous robot lawnmowers (Roomba-style) to a subset of customers -- drop off in the morning, pick up later, cheaper than a full lawn crew; robots are described as cheaper than commercial riding mowers (which run $8,000-$15,000) since they're small/electric with no need for someone to sit on them. Downside noted: robots take longer per lawn but that's less of an issue since no labor is tied up sitting on them. Best suited to "cookie cutter" suburban lawns (~5,000 sq ft).
- Speculative far-future idea: a business that rents out humanoid robots (e.g. Tesla Optimus) for home cleaning -- drop off, robot cleans, pick up later -- analogized to an existing appliance-rental business the host covered (renting $300 appliances for $60/month for years).
- Christmas tree subscription/rental spin: use tree-transplanting technique (large hydraulic root-ball diggers) to deliver a live potted tree to a home for the season, then return it to the farm afterward to keep growing -- effectively a recurring rental model; if the tree dies, charge full replacement price.

**4. Land-clearing-to-mature-tree-brokering middleman idea**
- Premise: land clearing (chainsaws, skid steers, stump removal) is an established industry that destroys trees; meanwhile buyers of mature/transplantable trees pay thousands to tens of thousands of dollars for large (5-15+ year old) trees from tree farms.
- Proposed business: act as a broker/middleman between land-clearing operations (or newly sold raw land, sourced via city permits or real estate sale records) and tree farms/nurseries or buyers wanting mature trees, redirecting trees that would otherwise be destroyed. Suggested as a possible new customer-acquisition channel for existing tree farms/nurseries specifically.

**5. Customer-list monetization consultancy (unrelated idea, mid-episode)**
- Premise: many small e-commerce/Shopify businesses have large customer email lists (cited example: a supplement seller with a "leaky gut" subscription for dogs had 5,000+ total past customers but only 300-400 active, and did zero marketing/upselling to lapsed customers) that go completely unused.
- Proposed service: an agency/consultancy that audits and improves a small business's use of their EXISTING customer list (surveys, giveaways, product-launch announcements, newsletters) rather than doing full email-marketing/funnel management.
- Recommended engagement structure: start only with existing customers/list (avoid scope-creep into rebuilding funnels/landing pages up front); charge a one-time project fee (suggested ~$5,000) with a stated ROI guarantee (e.g. refund half if X sales aren't generated within Y days); can evolve into an ongoing monthly retainer for campaign management afterward.
- Alternative pricing model discussed (for home-service businesses specifically, e.g. tree-trimming companies using Jobber CRM): charge 15% of every job generated from re-engaging the dormant list, or a flat $50 per generated lead/quote -- aligns incentives instead of charging a flat retainer regardless of results.
- Target customer profile: businesses with revenue between roughly $500K-$5M, an established track record (some history/reputation), and an existing but underused customer list; sub-$1M businesses seen as too small to justify a $5,000 project fee.

**6. Business-owner accountability service (unrelated idea, listener question)**
- Premise, from a listener: a paid one-on-one "accountability partner" service for entrepreneurs (both those starting and those running businesses), modeled after existing $600-700/month fitness-accountability services that combine automation + a real human checking in daily via text.
- Hosts' view: plausible business, similar in function to existing masterminds (EO, YPO, Vistage, Tiger 21, Hampton) but one-to-one instead of group-based; biggest challenges flagged are (a) the intimacy/trust required for someone to open up about their business, and (b) needing measurable, provable actions (not "on your honor") to make accountability meaningful.
- Pricing view: needs to be priced high (~$2,000/month) with either a real experienced coach, or built as a low-cost app model where the user deposits money that's forfeited if they don't complete a provable action (verified via API integration, e.g. to a phone/dialer tool for cold-call tracking, or to Duolingo for language-learning completion) minus a small processing fee.
- Recommendation: pick a narrow niche (e.g., accountability specifically for cold outbound activity, or specifically for a measurable habit) rather than a generic "business accountability" service, since measurability is the core mechanic that makes the money-forfeit model work.
- General advice given to the listener: stop seeking external validation and just test the idea directly rather than asking repeatedly.

No specific revenue/cost figures were given as validated outcomes for any of these ideas -- this is a brainstorming/ideation episode, not a case study of an operating business.
