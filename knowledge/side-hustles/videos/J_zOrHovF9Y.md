# What I Learned Starting 80 Businesses (The Truth)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J_zOrHovF9Y | Published: 2025-12-30

Not a specific business-idea video -- a mindset/framework piece from Chris Kerner (host, started 80+ businesses, 17 years in, generated "hundreds of millions" revenue / "tens of millions" profit, also had failed partnerships and lawsuits).

- **GRIT framework** for the downsides of entrepreneurship, each with a matching solution:
  - **G**rind -> antidote is **G**ratitude. Progress always takes longer and costs more than expected; treat that as normal, not a sign of failure. No effort is wasted -- compounding doesn't care that an initiative "failed."
  - **R**isk -> antidote is **R**eminders. Starting most businesses isn't a big monetary risk (he starts things with little capital) -- the real risk is time/opportunity cost. His stated philosophy: spread risk across multiple ventures rather than "focus" on one, because betting everything on one idea is itself a risk. Remind yourself why you started (revisit the original numbers/spreadsheet that got you excited) and that most delays are normal (he estimates ~95% of the time things take longer than expected, ~5% of the time faster).
  - **I**solation -> antidote is **I**nteraction. Entrepreneurship is more isolating than people expect; friends/family who haven't done it can't relate. Communicate explicitly with your spouse about what kind of support you need (e.g., he had to tell his wife to just listen rather than get angry on his behalf, because that added stress).
  - **T**urbulence -> antidote is **T**raction. Financial and emotional volatility is constant (he reports more zero-income years than positive-income years as a full-time entrepreneur, though positive years outweighed them in total $). Cling to small wins ("traction") as proof of progress; notice traction that's already happening rather than assuming you have none.
- Anecdote: a partnership with John McAfee that he expected to bring in tens of millions fell through -- used as an example of the emotional swings of deals collapsing.
