# Start This Business With No Experience
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GiGCLwVRoCk | Published: 2026-07-02

Guest: Alex Kirby (Bridgeway Staffing), interviewed by Chris Koerner. Business: placing overseas ("global talent") virtual employees with blue-collar small business owners in the US, for a placement fee.

## The business idea
- Overseas staffing/recruiting agency focused on blue-collar small business owners (landscaping, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, fertilization, land clearing, insurance). ~90% blue collar clients, 50% green industry / 50% not.
- Two business models discussed:
  - **Markup/staffing model**: agency employs the overseas worker, marks up hourly rate (e.g. costs you $5/hr, charge client $9-10/hr), handles payroll/compliance/hiring/firing. More money long-term, more headache.
  - **Placement/recruiting model** (what Alex does): find, vet, and place a candidate; client hires them directly; agency takes a one-time placement fee. Less overhead, less headache.
- Sources overseas talent mainly from Latin America (Argentina, Colombia, etc.); general principle extends to Philippines, Eastern Europe.

## Numbers
- Launched March 1 (2026), did $100K in sales in first 100 days (~end of June), "most of that was profit."
- Started with about $200 in capital.
- Tech stack cost: ~$100/month for Lovable (AI app builder used to build the recruiting portal/database) + ~$100/month for QuickBooks (invoicing/payments) + WiFi + a computer. That's it.
- Fee structure: one-time fee of 28% of the placed employee's first-year salary, OR flat lock-in of ~$4,500-$5,200 average ticket (typical range $4,500-$5,000). Payment: half down, half at signing (some clients as little as $100 deposit if it's your very first customer, just to prove commitment).
- Membership add-on: $200/month for training, customer support, webinars, resource library. ~75% of clients pay for it. Membership perk: every hire after the first one drops fee to 14% (half off).
- Guarantee: 100-day replacement guarantee for non-members, 6 months for members.
- Overseas worker example pay: admin coordinator hired at $1,600/month full-time (vs. costing far more domestically); another example, a Venezuelan single mom hired remotely making $1,200-$1,400/month against a $320/month mortgage (~5x local buying power) — called this "currency hacking."
- Alex's own prior experience: replaced his marketing company's US staff with global talent within ~90 days, saved ~$150,000/year, productivity went up.
- 100 days of results = ~30 placements across 26-27 clients (a few clients hired twice); a couple of early placements were discounted (half price) as "proof of concept."
- Of ~30 placements, only 1 replacement needed (poor personality fit, ex-Microsoft employee who didn't work out in customer support after 4 days).
- 90% of clients were first-time hires to add a new function/handle growth, not to replace an existing (often American) employee.

## Steps/process to start this business
1. Get your first client via warm network: text/call friends, family, church community, business-owner acquaintances — a short 3-sentence text is enough. Estimate: ~10% of people you know are business owners (33M business owners / 330M Americans).
2. Get on a call with prospect: identify their pain point (e.g., "I'm missing 8 calls a day, that's $8,000/day potentially lost revenue") and define the role/hours needed.
3. Source candidates: post on job boards/freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr, OnlinePH/jobs.ph for Philippines), run LinkedIn ads, Facebook/Meta ads. No secret sauce — volume + vetting is the differentiator.
4. Screen candidates: require a 2-minute video/audio application (don't let people apply without it) so you can judge English proficiency and presence before a call.
5. Interview candidates yourself (don't outsource this) with key questions: "Why should I hire you over somebody else?" (tests confidence) and get them to articulate what they're great at (tests self-conviction).
6. Vetting signals for quality overseas talent: strong English (signals discipline/effort — "how you do one thing is how you do everything"), a college degree (more meaningful signal in Latin America than the US, since it usually reflects real commitment), curiosity/ongoing upskilling (e.g., taking AI or marketing certifications).
7. Present ~4 vetted candidates to the client (via a Lovable-built portal, or just email + recorded intro videos if you don't have that); client narrows to top 2, does final interview, picks winner.
8. Onboard: ~1.5 hour onboarding/implementation call; recommend a tech stack (Slack, CapCut, Canva, etc.).
9. Payment/payroll: use a Deel-like international payroll/compliance platform (or Gusto's international option); Alex's team recommends paying overseas employees once a month rather than weekly/bi-weekly, since payment-platform fees are per-transaction — one monthly fee vs. multiple.
10. Charge the placement fee via QuickBooks invoice (the entire billing stack is just QuickBooks + Lovable).
11. Ongoing (optional, monetized via $200/mo membership): 2 training calls/month for placed talent covering AI tool use, English/accent coaching, etc.; building a resource-library of how-to videos.

## Tools/platforms named
- Lovable (AI app builder — used to build entire internal recruiting system/database/candidate portal in ~3 days)
- QuickBooks (books + invoicing/payment collection)
- Upwork, Fiverr, OnlinePH/jobs.ph (sourcing overseas candidates)
- LinkedIn ads, Facebook/Meta ads (candidate sourcing)
- Deel-like payment/compliance platform ("like Venmo" for international payroll/compliance) and Gusto international as alternative
- Slack, CapCut, Canva (recommended client tech stack for the placed employee)

## Caveats / risks
- Vetting quality overseas talent is harder than finding talent generally — the "elite" candidates don't cost much more but are harder to find; requires deliberate screening (video app, English bar, degree, curiosity signals).
- Building the software/tooling is now easy (AI tools), but customer acquisition/distribution is not — "AI hasn't made business easier, it's made it simpler"; getting your first clients still requires manual outbound (texting, calling, in-person visits).
- Niche specialization (e.g., staying blue-collar-focused rather than taking every industry, like insurance) is described as a deliberate choice to build authority/confidence, even at the cost of turning down some unrelated leads.
- Guest floats a general criticism: won't AI/chatbots replace these workers? His rebuttal: job losses observed so far are more about companies needing fewer people overall (efficiency), not AI directly replacing specific human roles; he views "global talent + AI" combined as durable, not a fad.
- This model relies on you personally vetting people early on (don't outsource initial interviewing) to learn what a good hire looks like before scaling.
