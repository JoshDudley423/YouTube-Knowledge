# How Much YouTube Paid Me in 2 Months
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KRRs_lfiDvA | Published: 2024-10-15

Transparency breakdown of Chris Kerner's early YouTube monetization numbers -- useful as a data point for "content creation as a side hustle" rather than a specific business idea.

- **YouTube monetization requirements** (as of filming): 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in the trailing 12 months for long-form; YouTube Shorts monetization requires 3 million Shorts views instead (Shorts views don't count toward the 4,000 watch-hour long-form threshold). Any views/virality before you hit monetization earn $0 -- so get monetized ASAP.
- **His timeline**: first video mid-February; took ~5 months (to mid-July) to hit monetization thresholds while posting 1-3 long-form episodes/week, then added ~1 Short/day starting about a month in.
- Had a pre-existing audience elsewhere (~60,000 Twitter followers, ~7,000-subscriber newsletter) but says cross-platform audience pull is weak -- people who follow him on Twitter for business content mostly don't go to YouTube for the same content; a "head start" but "nothing you can rely on."
- Views by week: ~92 in week 1 (Feb), climbing to ~177,000 total views in March (mostly Shorts-driven; long-form-only was more like 92/243/309/251/week early on, then 1,000+/week by late March/April). May-July (excluding a June vacation slowdown): 8,000-12,000 long-form views/week on average. Traffic exploded starting the week he got monetized (July 16) -- he flags two possible explanations: (a) YouTube's algorithm may favor monetized channels since it shares in ad revenue, or (b) coincidental timing with his first viral hits (which actually went viral on Instagram Reels/TikTok first, not YouTube Shorts, and cross-promoted viewers into YouTube via CTAs).
- **Revenue since monetization** (~July 16 to Oct 7, ~2.5-3 months): **$4,359 total** -- **$1,575 from Shorts, $2,775 from long-form**. Long-form earns far more per view (higher RPM) but Shorts drove far more raw views.
- **RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) benchmarks he cites**: a "good" long-form RPM is generally $6-$10; his own average RPM was ~$16, rising from $8.66/1,000 views at the start of monetization to $22.60/1,000 in the most recent week measured (~2.5x growth in ~2.5 months). Reasons cited: rising ad CPMs generally, and YouTube taking time (weeks/months/years) to find the right audience persona. His Shorts RPM: started below $0.20, averaging ~$0.25/1,000 views by the time of filming (so 1M Short views ≈ $250).
- Notes channels with younger/international audiences (cites Mr. Beast, Dude Perfect as examples) may only earn $3-5 RPM despite huge views, because advertisers value his audience (business owners/aspiring entrepreneurs) more highly.
- **Interesting content-performance insight**: videos featuring already-wealthy, "aspirational" guests (e.g., a video on starting a pet cremation business had RPM $10.84; a video interviewing someone who built a high-end restaurant chain had RPM ~$10) underperformed on RPM compared to his best-ever video (RPM $24.7/1,000, i.e. "$247" per thousand-ish as stated) about a simple, actionable side hustle: building and selling shelves out of a garage on Facebook Marketplace. His theory: viewers of "big win" aspirational content are passive/inspiration-seeking and less valuable to advertisers than viewers actively looking to make an extra $1,000/month, who are "action-oriented" and about to spend money (e.g., on lumber at Home Depot).
- Last-90-days stats at filming: 7.3M total views (mostly Shorts), 98,000 watch-time hours (vs. 5 months to first reach 4,000 hours), 48,000 new subscribers in 90 days (total ~50,983) vs. 3 months to get the first 3,000.
- Shorts optimization targets he names: swipe-through rate >70%, average view percentage >70%.

Caveat: he explicitly frames this as one data point, not a guaranteed path -- stresses that consistency alone doesn't guarantee virality ("if you post every single day and you never put out good content, it will not go viral"); every video needs a strong hook, strong content, and shareability (aim for 1-3% share rate on non-YouTube platforms).
