# AI is STEALING These Jobs! Here's How to Profit
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmbO1tGlz5s | Published: 2026-02-12

Business-idea rundown targeted at workers in healthcare, retail, and professional services facing AI-driven job disruption. Stats cited: 25% of jobs today could theoretically be fully performed by AI now; 70% of US jobs are "exposed" to AI and could be partially replaced within 5 years; healthcare, retail, and professional services together make up roughly half of all US jobs.

## Sector context (stats cited, not idea-specific)
- Healthcare: ~1/5 of the US economy, ~$5 trillion/year spend, ~22 million workers. AI cited as already outperforming humans at reading medical images (dermatology, radiology, pathology, imaging generally). AI projected to replace 25-40% of clerical/administrative healthcare roles (billing, coding, insurance pre-authorization).
- Retail: $5.3 trillion industry, ~55 million employees; described as shrinking annually, accelerated by AI/automation, ghost kitchens, and fast delivery logistics (drone/same-day).
- Professional/business services: $3.3 trillion industry, ~27 million employees (software developers/engineers, lawyers, accountants, consultants, architects, judges, managers, ad/PR specialists) -- argued to be the sector most exposed to AI disruption (e.g., ChatGPT-class tools).

## Healthcare-adjacent business ideas
- **IV hydration / vitamin therapy**: charge $120-$350/session; startup cost $5,000-$15,000 (often credit-card financeable); target customers: gym-goers, hangover crowd, executives, health-focused consumers.
- **Health coaching / hormone consulting**: can be launched HIPAA-compliant for under $5,000; charge $150-$300/session (or "$300 and up" for coaching, transcript unclear on ceiling); profit margins cited as ~90%; target customers: women 35-55, men 40+.
- **Mobile Botox** (including "Botox parties" for both women and men): requires licensing, injector training, upfront inventory purchase, and malpractice insurance. Earnings: $200-$600/hour or $400-$900 average ticket size; profit margins cited as 65%+ (upper bound unclear in transcript); can charge per-treatment or discounted recurring packages (since Botox is a repeat-use product); target customers: women 25-55.
- **Semaglutide/GLP-1-adjacent businesses**: e.g., mobile apps for dosage management, coaching services. Startup cost $5,000-$10,000 depending on approach. Caveat: direct medical/prescribing services require an NP, PA, or MD license -- not accessible to an unlicensed person -- but "tangential" adjacent services (non-clinical, e.g., apps/coaching/logistics) don't require that licensing.

## Retail-adjacent business ideas
- **Product sourcing agency**: uses public import records (cited tool: Import Yeti) that track container shipment contents/manufacturers required by law to be disclosed -- lets you identify exactly which factory/supplier makes a given product (example given: tracing the maker of On running shoes or Stance socks). Business model: charge retail brands a fee, or a percentage of their sourcing spend, to find and vet suppliers. Chris's own parallel: at 23 he ran an iPhone-screen import business and paid a sourcing agent ("Brian") on Alibaba a 3% cut of every dollar sent to Chinese suppliers in exchange for quality assurance and price negotiation -- same model, replicable in any sourcing corridor (international or domestic/interstate).
- **Facebook Marketplace reselling from liquidation sources**: source inventory from liquidation platforms (named: B-Stock, GovDeals.com) and resell on Facebook Marketplace with strong photos/video and optimized titles/descriptions. Risk-reduction tactic: list an item for sale (using stock photos or comparable listings) BEFORE actually purchasing the liquidation pallet, to gauge real demand before committing capital.

## Professional-services-adjacent business ideas
- **Business dashboard / analytics visualization services**: build simple executive dashboards (revenue, headcount, web traffic, etc.). Pricing: $2,000-$10,000 setup fee plus $500-$1,000/month in ongoing maintenance/feature-add fees. Framed as not technically complex to execute.
- **Buy and modernize small accounting/bookkeeping practices with AI**: acquire small mom-and-pop bookkeeping firms and use AI tools to increase each employee's client capacity/productivity (framed explicitly as "empowering" staff to handle more clients rather than outright replacing them, though acknowledged as a controversial angle given AI's disruption of accounting work).
- **Fractional executive (CFO/COO/CMO) agency**: pitch is that businesses doing $5-50 million/year in revenue can't justify a $250,000/year full-time CFO but will pay $2,500-$5,500/month for fractional coverage. Agency model: hire finance-background professionals, assign each to multiple (e.g., 5) client engagements simultaneously, collect roughly $30,000/month in combined recurring revenue per fractional hire, and pass through about half of that to the fractional worker (i.e., ~50% agency margin). Said to work best specifically in the finance/CFO niche versus other fractional-exec categories. Cited as a growing trend per Google Trends.
- **AI implementation / automation consulting (the video's top pick, "not even close")**: target small-to-medium businesses ($1M-$50M revenue) who know they need AI but don't know how to implement it (contrasted with crypto, where adoption hesitancy was about legitimacy/trust -- AI adoption hesitancy is purely a know-how gap). Services to sell: AI call-answering/receptionist services, AI chatbots trained on a business's historical customer service data, general automation using accessible no-code/low-code AI tools (named: Replit, Lindy, ChatGPT, Claude). Pricing: businesses reportedly willing to pay $500-$5,000/month recurring. Framed as not requiring deep technical skill, comparable in difficulty to learning Wix/Squarespace.
- Related resource plugged: the host's own paid community for people starting AI automation/implementation businesses, "Playmakers AI" (playmakersai.com).
