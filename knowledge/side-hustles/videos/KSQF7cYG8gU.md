# You're Overthinking It. Here's the Simple Way to Start (ft. Nick Huber)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KSQF7cYG8gU | Published: 2025-04-30

Interview with Nick Huber ("Sweaty Startup," owns ~$100M of self-storage real estate with investors, plus Titan Risk insurance, Bolt Builders construction consulting, AdRhino, Bolt SEO, WebRun, and Somewhere.com staffing -- 50+ employees, ~$800K/month revenue there). Mostly mindset/management content, but contains concrete "sweaty startup" business-idea list and a proven no-cost customer-acquisition tactic.

- **"Sweaty startup" business idea list** (from Huber's free PDF at sweatystartup.com -- "500+ business ideas," 7 years in the making): lawn care, pest control, firewood delivery, residential painting, pressure washing, thermal imaging (home inspection), vacation rental management, custom closet buildout, mobile car detailing, tree trimming, on-demand irrigation, green home consultation, on-demand holiday decorations, pool/hot tub services, deck staining, gutter cleaning, maid services, laundry services, and more low-skill service businesses. His framing: "if you got no money, no job, no experience, no skills... go clean something -- you can make 50 bucks an hour doing it."
- **Zero-cost customer acquisition tactic (Huber's own case study)**: got his first 7,000 customers for his college dorm-room pickup/storage business (Storage Squad, at Cornell) without paying for any marketing -- wrote 5 chalk advertisements ("free boxes, free pickup, free delivery, free packing supplies, summerstorage.com" -- just plain text, no graphics) on a bridge every student walked across to class each morning during finals week. Got 40+ signups per day this way.
- **Bandit signs** (cheap yard signs, ~$2 each, ordered in batches of 500 for ~$1,000 total) are cited as the #1 customer source for his brother's lawn care company and for his own tree-trimming business -- they never leave a job site without leaving a bandit sign.
- General philosophy: "we bend over backwards trying to think of really creative, innovative ideas when the obvious ones are usually the right ones" -- simple, unsexy, proven acquisition/business models beat novel ones.
- On focus vs. diversification: Huber advocates trying many things because of the power law -- most bets fail, a small number of businesses/hires/marketing channels are massive winners, and you can't know in advance which one it'll be, so "you got to be constantly trying a lot of things."
- On AI (skeptical take, relevant to anyone building an AI-based side hustle): Huber says he demoed 20-30 AI tools over 6 months, tried implementing ~8 into his companies, and 7 of them were "a total giant pain" not worth it. Sees AI as useful mainly for quality control/notifications (e.g., an AI Slack alert whenever a client leaves negative feedback about a recruiter) rather than as a transformative agent-based tool; explicitly skeptical of AI "agents" replacing real work today, comparing hype to past overpromises on self-driving cars and Web3/crypto/NFTs.
- On starting a business generally: "it really is as simple as getting out and getting that first customer to pay you money, then figuring out how to fulfill it" -- but he now believes only a minority of people (his estimate: many people, maybe half, lack the discipline) actually have what it takes to run a business under stress; recommends people who are drawn to it should just test it rather than fully committing upfront.
- His new book "The Sweaty Startup" (Harper Collins, out April 29, 2025) is structured in three sections: (1) opportunity / how to pick and market-test a business idea (explicitly anti-"change the world" VC-style ideas), (2) skills needed (delegation, sales, urgency), (3) people/hiring/recruiting/scaling through others.

No specific revenue/cost breakdowns are given for any of the listed sweaty-startup ideas beyond the "$50/hour" cleaning-work estimate and the bandit-sign cost (~$2/sign, ~$1,000 for 500). This episode is primarily a mindset/management interview rather than a step-by-step how-to for a single idea.
