# 4 Ways to Make Money by Spying on People
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U--WJujA2hY | Published: 2024-10-09

Guest: Stephen Olman (owns Alarm Masters, a marketing agency called "Quicker," and does M&A/advisory work). Freewheeling idea-brainstorm episode, not a single-business case study.

## Idea 1: HR-focused call/meeting monitoring tool
- Take existing AI meeting-notetaker tech (Firefly-style) and build an HR-oriented version that flags inappropriate/harassing comments on calls (Zoom, phone) automatically, rather than just transcribing for sales notes.
- Pitch: works even if it never "catches" anyone — the value is the deterrent/compliance effect (compared to dog vitamins: efficacy is unfalsifiable but perceived value is high).
- Target: enterprise / compliance budgets rather than small business (small biz would resist it).
- Suggested distribution: bottoms-up virality — target women/employees who'd want it, so they request it from management, rather than cold enterprise SaaS sales (called "brutal").
- Build approach: don't build NLP/transcription from scratch — white-label an existing transcription API and layer HR-specific flagging/database on top to prove concept cheaply before heavy R&D.
- Value framing: even a ~$20/user/month tool is cheap insurance against harassment-lawsuit liability, which justifies premium-ish pricing.

## Idea 2: Sales call "talk-time" monitor (Zoom app / Chrome extension)
- Real-time popup meter showing what % of a sales call the rep is talking vs. the prospect, since less talking / more listening correlates with closing more.
- Reports rollups to the sales manager (e.g., "you talked 78% of the time, target is ~24%") to see if there's a correlation between low talk-time and top performers.
- Rationale for market gap: Zoom's app store has ~2,000 apps for ~100M users vs. iPhone's ~3M apps for ~1B users — a supply/demand imbalance = opportunity for niche Zoom tools.
- Go-to-market: sell top-down to sales managers (not bottoms-up like Loom), likely via outbound/cold ads; suggested tactic — install free for a month, show the manager the data, convert or churn.
- Enhancement idea: aggregate talk-time across an entire sales cycle (multiple touches/calls) and attach it as a "deal note" for post-mortem analysis.
- A comparable/adjacent existing tool mentioned (unnamed by guest) tracks employee email response times for business owners — started as a Chrome extension, grew into a full company; underlying logic is the same (faster response = more sales/productivity).

## Idea 3: "Tattletail" / whistleblower businesses (regulatory & compliance driven)
- IRS whistleblower program: report tax fraud and collect **30% of whatever the government recovers** from the reported party — requires solid, substantiated evidence, not just accusations.
- New home-services lead-selling regulation (mentioned as taking effect Jan 27, [year following recording]) will require lead-gen platforms (Home Advisor/Angie's List-style) to present homeowners with all competing companies and let them choose ONE, rather than blasting the lead to 3-4+ companies simultaneously. This shifts competitive advantage toward reviews/ratings/tenure (Google Business Profile) and away from "pay to play speed-to-lead."
- Opportunity flagged: build a service that proactively audits/reports competitors (or non-compliant lead sellers) to platforms like Home Advisor for a fee, as this regulation creates new "gotcha" opportunities.
- General framework given: best opportunities are things "people refuse to talk about" — e.g., guest mentioned (anonymized) a contact who makes ~$8K/sale as a middleman between a medical device manufacturer and healthcare providers on a niche device, capturing margin the big company doesn't know how to capture themselves.
- Caveat: whistleblower/tattletail plays require meeting a high evidentiary bar to actually get paid, and carry real legal/reputational risk ("careful out there").

## Idea 4: Call-listening / customer service quality agency for home service businesses
- Existing practice at guest's marketing agency: they listen to client sales calls for lead quality/scoring; incidentally this surfaces poor customer service (e.g., a friendly office employee who's actually bad on the phone).
- Pivot idea: turn call-listening into a standalone agency product focused on customer-service quality/optimization for home service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, etc.) who spend heavily on lead gen (Google/SEO) but never audit how leads are handled on the phone.
- Concrete example given: an AI tool plugged into a $5M HVAC business's phone system could report things like "no one answers phones 11am-1pm (lunch), you got 38 calls during that window, at a 20% close rate on $800 jobs that's ~$36,000/month lost."
- **Tooling**: CallRail (~$79/month) named as the backend software to record/analyze calls; agency owner just "pushes the button," sets it up, and delivers a human-curated report — framed as low-effort, high-margin.
- **Economics discussed**: pricing by-the-minute creates customer billing confusion/friction and lowers close rates — better to price by tiers based on call volume and back into pricing off a target gross margin (mentioned: aim for ~70% gross margin; can be capital-efficient, "nearly all margin," 90-98%+ gross margins when self-run). Different pricing for a $1M plumbing company vs. a massive multi-location company.
- Related "framework" the guest likes: find an under-the-radar tool/service (e.g., Construction Journal — $200/month for building-permit leads) that target customers don't know about, arbitrage/resell it as a packaged, "humanized" service (buy leads for $100 wholesale via a $200/mo unlimited plan, resell each lead at markup).

## Bonus idea: Niche mentor marketplace (two-sided)
- Observation: broad mentor-matching exists informally (Facebook groups) but niche communities (e.g., atheists seeking an older mentor who shares their worldview) struggle to find matches.
- Proposed build: a "BetterHelp for mentoring" — scrape/aggregate existing coaches/therapists' bios, use AI to tag them by niche/identity/interest categories, and initially list them for free to build supply before charging for placement/leads once demand exists.
- Framing for adoption: gives people who are "too proud" to seek a therapist (but want life improvement) a lower-friction entry point — similar to how early dating platforms helped shy people signal interest without an awkward real-life ask.
- Suggested monetization: short paid "intro" calls (e.g., billed in 15-minute increments, maybe first 5 minutes free) so users can gauge fit before committing to a mentor relationship.
- Caveat: two-sided marketplaces are inherently hard to bootstrap (need supply and demand simultaneously) — recommended sequencing is to build the supply side first (often free), prove traction with the demand side, then start charging supply to remain listed.
