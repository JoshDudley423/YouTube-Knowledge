# How We'll Double the Value of this $3M RV Park Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dRLBWIjidlI | Published: 2024-02-27

Chris Koerner walks through his due-diligence/acquisition analysis for a specific 60-acre RV park he had under contract in Louisiana (45 min from Baton Rouge, ~1 hr from New Orleans). Framed as a real-time deal breakdown, not a general tutorial. Note: per a later year-in-review video, this specific deal ran into a problem post-purchase (the city shut down park operations), resulting in a loss for Koerner -- but that outcome isn't mentioned in this video itself.

## The specific deal (numbers)
- Purchase price under contract: $3,000,000.
- 2023 net operating income (NOI): $330,000.
- Implied cap rate at purchase: 11% (NOI / price) -- described as a great entry cap rate.
- Property: 60 acres total -- ~30 acres developed with 135 RV/mobile-home pad sites (~90% occupied), 8.7-acre lake with gazebo, ~20 acres of vacant land in back (with a creek/river) previously used for camping but abandoned by the prior owner.
- Site mix: FEMA trailers (small single-wide mobile homes) in good condition, mostly RVs, and about a dozen wood cabins used for long-term living.
- Amenities: pool, arcade, mini/putt golf course, volleyball court, cheap on-site laundromat, cement/asphalt/crushed-granite/dirt road mix (cement roads had some cracking).
- Current rent: $595/month per site vs. $1,000-1,200/month market rate in the surrounding area -- meaning the park is fully occupied even while significantly under-market on rent, a key value-add lever.
- Estimated post-optimization valuation trajectory: ~$3M purchase → ~$4M after raising rents to market → ~$5-6M after full optimization (pad additions/prettying up) → potentially $8-10M with glampsite additions and/or expansion to ~200 pad sites within a few years.

## Evaluation framework used (the general, reusable part)
Koerner frames every deal in four buckets: (1) facts, (2) what he likes, (3) what he dislikes, (4) "levers left to pull" (i.e., value-add opportunities post-purchase). Concretely for this deal:
- **Likes**: well-maintained overall (visible "pride of ownership" from the original builder/owner), room to grow (raising occupancy then adding new pad sites), a lake (a "highly sought after amenity" in RV parks), varied amenities beyond the norm (pool, arcade, mini-golf), decent road infrastructure.
- **Dislikes**: minor cement cracking, needed tree trimming, general "tired" look -- attributed to the previous owner having passed away a couple years prior and the park falling into mild disrepair. Notably, Koerner says he was "reaching" to find real dislikes -- this was a genuinely strong deal.
- **Levers to pull** (concrete value-add plan):
  1. Raise rents from $595/month toward the $1,000-1,200/month market rate -- the single biggest lever, since the park is already full at below-market rent.
  2. Add Wi-Fi/Starlink connectivity (cited as the #1 tenant complaint) -- described as a cheap fix (mentions 150 Mbps download speed via Starlink on both sides of the park) that can be used as a marketing hook to drive occupancy/demand.
  3. Use the ~20 vacant back acres (on a creek/river) for glamping sites listed on Airbnb, commanding an estimated $150-300/night.
  4. Alternatively/additionally, add 30-50 more standard pad sites to bring the park to ~200 total pad sites, rather than building out glamping.
  5. Minor cosmetic/deferred-maintenance fixes (tree trimming, cement repair).

## Sourcing/talking to tenants as diligence
- Talked directly to an on-site tenant (in the laundromat) during the visit, who confirmed people love living there and corroborated the below-market rent and the Wi-Fi complaint -- used as informal ground-truth validation of the numbers and pain points before closing.

## Caveats
- This is a single anecdotal deal walkthrough, not general advice on RV park investing mechanics (financing, park management, regulatory risk) -- no cap-rate benchmarks, financing terms, or risk factors beyond this one property are discussed.
- Location risk flagged only lightly: guests must drive through a casino parking lot to reach the property (described as "a little odd" but also potentially beneficial since guests get free access to casino-area amenities).
- Deal was still under contract at time of filming (~8 weeks from closing, pending investor documentation) -- reflects a plan/thesis, not a completed, proven outcome as of this recording.
