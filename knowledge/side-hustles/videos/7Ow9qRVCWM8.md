# How to Start a Trash Bin Cleaning Business from Scratch
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Ow9qRVCWM8 | Published: 2024-10-24

Interview with Jason Feller (Crystal Clean Cans, crstl.com, Southern Chester County, PA), who left a 20-year private country-club management career to run a residential trash bin cleaning business full-time, started February 2023.

## The business / how it works
- Purpose-built trailer with a boom/lift that raises a residential trash can over a wastewater receptacle, then rotating spray heads pressure/power-wash the inside; captured wastewater is contained (not dumped on the ground/street, unlike some pressure-washer competitors who just hose cans out in the driveway).
- ~90% residential, ~10% commercial in his case; industry broadly skews similar. Started idea from noticing his club's dumpster stank and cost ~$1,000/year to have hauled and cleaned once a summer by the trash hauler.
- Origin overseas (Europe), driven partly by water-conservation and rodent/pest control concerns; commercial clients have reported rat populations dropping to near zero after cleanings removed food debris/residue that had been sustaining them.
- Sourced a used trailer instead of new to reduce upfront capital risk.

## Numbers
- Used trailer cost: ~$48,000 (a new one from the same builder ran ~$70,000 with a 6-12 month wait). Bought branding/website/marketing materials from the previous owner for an extra ~$5,000.
- Started with only ~$4,000 cash in the operating account (in hindsight, he says he should have kept $20-30k in reserve).
- Pricing (all for 2 cans): $25/month, $35/quarter, $50 twice-a-year, $65 one-time. ~80-85% of subscribers are on the quarterly plan.
- Revenue: ~300 recurring subscription customers after ~18 months, plus lifetime total of 500+ customers (200 churned or were one-time). Only 12 of the true subscribers churned; 2 churned due to dissatisfaction (price complaint, "never clean enough"), 10 churned due to moving.
- Annual revenue per customer ≈ $140/year (quarterly plan math).
- Fixed cost: ~$3 per stop (per 2-can cleaning). Marketing spend: $6,000 (year 1) → $8,000 (year 2).
- Customer acquisition cost: ~$50/customer currently; goal to get to ~$20.
- Net margin: ~20% currently; expected to improve as route density/scale increases (fixed costs like equipment loan payments are already sunk).
- Time investment: ~12-15 hours/month of actual cleaning time; ~3 hours/day of "windshield time" driving a 180-square-mile rural route, working ~3 days/week. Route pace once in a neighborhood: 6-12 homes/hour.
- Total addressable market estimate in his route area: ~120,000 homes / ~400,000-500,000 people; he estimates capturing just 1-2% of that would be a great business.
- Growth target: +75% customers next year (not expecting to double).
- Distributed 6,000 direct-mail-style trash-can "tags" (postcard + discount code, stuck to the can or door hanger) — conversion is slow and hard to attribute directly (customers often sign up 2-12 months after being tagged), unlike Facebook or Google search leads which convert almost immediately.

## Customer acquisition channels (ranked by effectiveness)
1. **Organic Facebook groups** — best performer. Posts religiously on the 1st and 15th; best results come from customers posting their own testimonials into local Facebook groups (word of mouth), which drives spikes in website traffic and signups.
2. **Facebook paid ads** (proper ads, not just boosted posts) — targets 35-54 year-olds with kids and pets at home, dual-income households, who are "busy" and don't want to deal with cleaning cans themselves (not the intuitively-assumed 65+ demographic).
3. **Local chamber of commerce networking** — face time, being visible with the trailer.
4. **Trash can tagging** (postcards/door hangers) — labor intensive, low/slow conversion, best used as a long-tail brand-awareness play, not a direct-response channel.
5. Google search (organic/branded) converts immediately once someone finds the site.
6. Not using Facebook Marketplace listings for lead gen.
- Uses "My Service Area" software to define and defend a tight route/service boundary — explicitly turned away leads outside the core area to protect route density (referred one 50-minutes-away lead to a local competitor).
- Neighborhood-level group discounts: offers a price break if 10+ homes in one neighborhood sign up; one neighborhood has 23/many homes signed up already.

## Target customer profile observed
- Households in the ~$500-600k home value range (range seen: $300k-$3M homes, plus some mobile home park customers); younger, upwardly mobile dual-income families in their late 30s-early 50s with kids and pets, often in HOA communities requiring cans stored in the garage (walking past a smelly can daily is the trigger).
- Seasonality: demand for monthly service is lower in cold winters (Mid-Atlantic) because cans live in temperature-controlled garages and odor/flies are reduced; would push hard for monthly (or even biweekly) service instead in hot/humid climates like Florida/Texas.

## Competition
- Local pressure-washing companies encroaching on trash-can cleaning are viewed as an "annoyance" — they underprice (e.g., $5/can) and dump dirty wastewater directly onto driveways/streets, undercutting quality and creating a race to the bottom.
- One direct trash-bin-cleaning competitor 45 minutes away is treated as complementary, not a threat — both benefited from increased local marketing/awareness (the "Pepsi and Coke" effect: co-location and shared advertising can grow the whole category, similar to Tesla's Elon Musk being glad competitors also built EVs).

## Caveats / lessons learned
- Explicitly recommends NOT doing this part-time — "your back's against the wall" full commitment mentality is core to his growth; a prior operator of the same franchise/model working part-time in Pittsburgh had far fewer customers after 4-5 years than Feller got in 18 months full-time.
- In hindsight, he'd have kept far more starting cash reserve ($20-30k rather than $4k) and considered keeping a day job / hiring someone to run the trailer initially, treating the first period as a loss leader rather than needing immediate profitability.
- Considered but rejected adjacent verticals (window washing, solar panel cleaning) as not profitable enough without also scaling to hundreds of customers; did add power washing/house washing since it uses the same equipment.
- Considered but hasn't pursued: Christmas tree rental (live potted trees delivered/replanted seasonally), vertical urban horticulture/food-desert planting, car undercoating.
- Watch out for classified/business-marketplace listings claiming other operators failed — those sites exist specifically to sell businesses, so failure narratives there are unreliable; better to talk directly to current operators/podcast guests about what worked/didn't.
- Business only reached profitability with intense focus on a single vertical/route density; scattering attention across many "shiny object" side ideas was flagged as a real risk to avoid.

## Tools/platforms named
Facebook (organic posts, groups, Marketplace, paid ads/Meta Ads), My Service Area (route/territory management software), Zillow (suggested for correlating home values with customer likelihood, not yet done), direct-mail-style trash-can tags/door hangers.
