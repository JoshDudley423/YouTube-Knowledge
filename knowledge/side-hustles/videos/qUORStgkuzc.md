# The Most Overlooked $1K/Hour Business Anyone Can Start
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qUORStgkuzc | Published: 20260925

Interview with Mike Herod, owner of VoSKILL trade school in West Palm Beach, FL, who turned a single unplanned Facebook post into a kids' woodworking/shop-class business.

## The business idea
- Teach woodworking/shop-skills classes to kids (originally framed as a woodworking-themed birthday-party alternative to bounce houses), split into two age groups (7-9 and 10+); expanded into homeschool co-op contracts, corporate/private-school bookings, and adult DIY classes.
- Fully mobile-capable — can be run with no commercial space at all, out of a garage, at a park, or via a mobile setup; a whole 40-student class's equipment fits in a pickup truck (F-250 Super Duty).
- Started with a single Facebook post with no photos, no price, and no plan — answered questions from commenters in real time to improvise pricing and age ranges.

## Numbers
- Origin post cost $0; first classes were run free intentionally to generate photos/video/social proof (no prior pictures or reviews existed).
- November (launch month): ~$800 revenue from one father-son event (38 kids, $150/kid built a wooden robot) plus two small free-turned-word-of-mouth sessions.
- December: ~$6,000.
- March-July (spring/summer season, run out of the trade school's own space): $50,600 total, scaling month over month (roughly $8K → $12K → $16K).
- Single week of spring break camps: $12,000 in that week alone, mornings only (9am-noon).
- Signed a $30,000 contract for fall 2026: 14 weeks, 40 homeschool co-op students, 2 hours every Friday (~$55/head/week, over $1,000/hour gross); after paying extra tools, labor, and materials for this specific contract, expects to net ~$10,000 (33%) on it — future repeat contracts (no new-tool cost) will net far more.
- Pricing: $75/hour/kid for the younger group (7-9, needs more oversight), $50/hour/kid for the older group (10+); a 1-hour class of 10 young kids runs ~$750 revenue against ~$150 in materials cost (~$600/80% margin).
- Startup cost: as low as $300-450 for the youngest-age-group tier (folding table, miter boxes, tape measures, squares, screwdrivers, sanders, clamps) — can even borrow a table to cut this to ~$300; power tools for the older group cost roughly $4,100 for a set serving 6 kids (about $1,500 for 3 drills+drivers, plus jigsaws and nailers), though most of that gets subsidized via sponsorship (see below).
- Insurance: standard business liability policy, ~$95/month.
- Purely mobile, nights-and-weekends version (no commercial space, keeping a day job): a full Saturday can net ~$1,500 and a Sunday ~$600 — roughly $2,000/weekend, or ~$4,000/month running 2 weekends/month.
- Specialty one-off events: a skateboard-building class (parents supply trucks/wheels, business supplies maple + forms) grossed $500/student at ~$60-70 material cost/student.
- Estimated 500+ students served since Thanksgiving (low estimate), out of a single local homeschool Facebook group of 9,000 members not yet fully tapped.

## Process / steps
- "Post, collect, host" framework: post a plain, no-price static image to local homeschool/mom Facebook groups ("Woodworking or shop class, kids build confidence, message me for details" — deliberately no price, since parents rarely read the linked website/Facebook page anyway and prefer to ask directly), collect payment/reservations via a simple payment link (Square), then host the class.
- Do the first 1-2 events free specifically to generate photos, video, and Google/Facebook reviews before ever charging — social proof is what converts subsequent posts into paid bookings.
- Post daily; consistency matters more in a niche as tight-knit as homeschooling, where moms in the same groups all talk to each other and referrals compound quickly once you're established.
- Tool sponsorship: cold-called (via LinkedIn) a contact at Milwaukee Tool asking for a sponsorship, pitching the customer lifetime value of getting a teenager hooked on the brand at 18 — received roughly $5,000 of free tools; framed as "the worst they can say is no."
- Funding growth from revenue, not savings: collect deposits/payment up front for a class before it happens, and use that cash to buy the next batch of tools rather than financing equipment out of pocket.
- Government/institutional payment channel: became a registered vendor for Florida's "Step Up" school-choice scholarship program, so homeschool/private-school families pay $0 out of pocket — the state pays the business directly within 24 hours of invoicing.
- Simplify projects deliberately (a repeat lesson from early over-scoping mistakes): stick to a rotating library of ~10 proven project templates (a small wooden robot, an American flag from a $9 Etsy plan, cornhole boards, a squirrel feeder), buy plans off Etsy ($9-15) rather than designing from scratch, use butt joints/screws/glue only (no routers, no nails — screws are less injury-prone than nails), and keep a scrap-wood bin from prior classes for free bonus activity.
- Safety-first operating ritual: a fixed "respect first, safety always" mantra recited at the start of every class, mandatory safety glasses, posters emphasizing that mistakes are fine but quitting isn't; zero accidents reported to date.
- Privacy handling: waivers ask parents up front whether photos can be posted; workstation name tags (not on-body name tags) make it easy to blur/scratch out a specific child's identifying info if requested.

## Tools/platforms named
- Facebook (personal profile, business page, and niche mom/homeschool groups) as the sole marketing channel to date.
- Square (payment collection/links).
- Etsy (buying pre-made woodworking project plans, e.g. a $9 American flag plan).
- Milwaukee Tool (sponsorship relationship, cold-outreach acquired).
- Harbor Freight (budget hand-tool sourcing; explicitly avoided for kids' power tools, which are too heavy/full-size).
- Claude Code (used elsewhere in the founder's broader business, e.g. a self-built CRM/app — no coding background).

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly warns this is not effortlessly scalable without limit: at full weekend-warrior pace (2 weekends/month, no employees) the realistic ceiling is described as ~$4,000/month; larger recurring revenue (the $30K contract tier) requires B2B relationships, not more Facebook posts.
- Physical/liability risk is real and named directly — a single injury "could wreck the whole business," hence the hard safety-first operating rules.
- Space matters for perceived legitimacy: parents reportedly feel safer sending kids to a class in a real commercial space vs. someone's garage/backyard, even though the garage-based version has identical instruction quality.
- The founder still describes the business as physically and mentally exhausting despite it looking simple from the outside ("new respect for teachers").
