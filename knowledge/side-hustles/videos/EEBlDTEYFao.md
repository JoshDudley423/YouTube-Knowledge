# I Found the Perfect First Business for Introverts
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EEBlDTEYFao | Published: 2026-08-17

Interview with Ryan Schuetter (26, Cincinnati), a self-described shy introvert with no sales background or coding experience, who left a $52,000/year clinical research job and now makes $10,000-$15,000/month running an AI-implementation consulting practice for small businesses, started for $20 total.

## The business
- Service: helping small/mid-size business founders and teams (typically sub-$10M revenue, though has worked with pre-revenue up to ~$20M companies) actually adopt and implement AI tools and automation into their operations — explicitly not selling one-off software, but adoption, education, and operational systems built around AI.
- Started with zero coding background ("an ops guy," not an engineer); total startup cost was $20/month for an early ChatGPT subscription.
- First proof-of-concept project (recommended as a good confidence-building starting exercise for anyone new to AI/vibe-coding): built a personal hobby project (a Wordle-style ski-resort trivia game called "Lift Line") just to learn the tools without business pressure.

## Cold-start customer acquisition (the core "introvert-friendly" playbook)
- Explicitly avoided cold outreach to strangers — a mass cold-email campaign he tried generated **zero** results. All traction came from his existing personal network plus local/online community groups, expanded deliberately over time.
- Process: made a target list of ~100 people (contacts list on phone: friends, family, former coworkers, church, plus ~4 group memberships — 2 local, in-person groups of ~100 people each, e.g., "Cincy AI" meetup found via Google search, and 2 larger online masterminds of ~500 people each) — roughly 60% of his 100 real conversations came from those 4 groups (split evenly local vs. online), the other 40% from direct personal contacts/LinkedIn.
- Offered 100 completely free, no-pitch, hour-long conversations (all done over Google Meet, not in person, out of personal introversion/comfort, though he explicitly states in-person would have converted meaningfully better — estimates roughly double the follow-up rate). Outreach messages were personalized ("hey, haven't talked in a while, how's [specific thing]" based on light research on their LinkedIn/Facebook) — never opened with a pitch or a copy-paste template (explicitly contrasted with spammy MLM-style outreach that reads as insincere and gets ignored). Needed to contact roughly 300-450 people to actually land ~100-150 real hour-long conversations.
- Funnel/conversion data reported (over the first ~150 conversations, across roughly 3 months while still working his day job):
  - ~150 first conversations (free, no pitch) → ~30 second conversations (~20%) → closed 1-2 low-ticket deals ($500-$1,000 each) directly off second calls, just to "get a foot in the door."
  - ~30 second conversations → ~15 third conversations (~50%) → closed the majority of these (~10 of 15) at that stage, arriving at a total of ~12 paying customers from the original ~150-conversation cohort — roughly an 8% overall close rate from total conversations.
  - Total time invested across the first cohort: roughly 300-350 hours (mostly the calls themselves) to generate an initial ~$25,000 lifetime revenue from those first 12 clients (~$2,000 average per client — effectively $50-100/hour amortized, explicitly framed as "getting paid to build a business" rather than a strong hourly rate).
  - Framing note (interviewer's calculation): a cold email campaign to the same volume of strangers would likely yield close to 0% close rate, versus 8% here — attributed to working exclusively a "warm" personal-network funnel rather than cold outreach.
- Ongoing/current outreach cadence: still reaches out to ~10 people/day from his network (texts/calls) pitching new cohort offers; roughly half of his current $15K/month in revenue comes from brand-new (first-engagement) clients sourced this way, the other half from repeat/expanding work with earlier clients.
- Content as a trust-builder, not a growth channel: publishes low-production, unedited, one-take screen-recorded YouTube videos and guides (e.g., prompt frameworks, SEO guides, "how to use Claude Code") — has only ~100 YouTube subscribers even after ~1 year, and states view count doesn't matter; the point is having demonstrable public proof of expertise/credibility ("immediate credibility... why not upload it? What's the downside?") and pre-built assets ("lead magnets") to hand out during and after sales conversations.
- Philosophy on giving away methodology: shares his entire process/frameworks openly with cohort members, on the belief that clients still want an ongoing thought-partner/operator relationship even if they technically could DIY it afterward — cites Bob Burg's "The Go-Giver" as a direct influence, and reports that giving everything away consistently converts into more (not less) repeat/expanded paid work.

## Offer structure and pricing (as it evolved)
- **Private 1:1 coaching / "fractional AI officer"** — his highest-ticket offer: a 3-month deep-dive engagement embedded with a founder/leadership team on AI/tool adoption and employee "adoption" specifically (not just building tools). Priced at **$5,000-$10,000/month**; deliberately caps himself at 1-2 concurrent clients at this tier due to time/capacity, and admits most of his own network can't afford it anyway.
- **Cohort groups** — his preferred/favorite offer: month-long group programs (e.g., 10 people building websites together using Claude, optimized for SEO/lead funnels) run at $1,000 per person per cohort (so a 10-person cohort = $10,000/month in revenue for roughly ~40 hours of his time that month — a Monday full-group lesson, a Thursday/Friday full-group review call, plus scattered 30-minute 1:1s through the week, effectively ~$250/hour). Has run vertical-specific cohorts before (e.g., a real-estate AI cohort, a "business acquisition agent" cohort covering LOIs/broker relations/underwriting) as well as general ones. States he's comfortable scaling a single cohort up to ~20 people. First cohort attempt had only 1 participant; second had 3 (still worthwhile at $3,000/month).
- **Custom implementation builds** — one-off projects (AI agents/tools scheduled into a business's workflow, marketing content-repurposing systems, sales outreach funnels, newsletter systems) priced using a self-set hourly rate of roughly **$200/hour**, typically landing in the **$1,000-$20,000** range per project depending on scope (average cited around $2,000).
- Tools used with clients: adapts to whatever CRM/stack a client already has (GoHighLevel, Salesforce, HubSpot commonly cited), uses n8n for automation workflows, and tells anyone starting from zero today (as of August 2026) to focus specifically on learning Claude rather than chasing every new AI tool.
- Explicitly avoids/distrusts "Open Claw" (an AI agent tool) for client implementations specifically because of the access/security risk of handing broad autonomous tool access to a business's systems, even though he considers it fine for personal use by someone who understands the tooling.

## The "DASA" framework (his methodology for scoping any AI project)
1. **D**iagnose — identify a real, existing problem worth solving; avoids AI-for-AI's-sake projects with no genuine business outcome attached.
2. **A**rticulate — describe what an ideal "dream solution" would look like for that business's situation (time saved, money made, workload lifted), producing an "aha moment" for the client when the problem is reflected back to them clearly.
3. **S**ystemize — before adding AI, gather the team's existing informal knowledge into an actual system/SOP, studying how the best operators in a space are already doing the process manually. Explicit principle: "if you're automating chaos, you're scaling chaos" — AI amplifies whatever process (good or bad) it's layered onto.
4. **A**I-implement — only at this final stage does the actual AI/automation get built and deployed on top of the now-systemized process.
- In practice, his 3-call sales sequence roughly maps to this: call 1 covers Diagnose + start of Articulate; calls 2-3 continue building out Articulate and lead into a paid Systemize/Implement engagement post-close.
- Technique for surfacing a prospect's real (not superficial) problem on a discovery call: avoids generic questions like "what problems are you having?" (people can't map vague problems to a vague question); instead asks pointed, higher-signal questions like "what's keeping you up at night?" or drills into specific parts of their business ("how's this particular offering going?") to elicit a genuine, non-rehearsed answer.

## Caveats
- Results are self-reported and specific to one operator's network, effort level, and local market (Cincinnati) — he explicitly attributes part of his positioning advantage to being in a market (Midwest/Ohio) with comparatively few AI-focused consultants, versus saturated markets like Austin or San Francisco where potential clients may already have in-house AI expertise or well-known local competitors.
- His stated $15K "best month ever" was preceded by inconsistent, sometimes near-zero months, including a self-described slump when travel and introversion caused him to pause outreach — earnings were not steady/linear.
- All numbers (call counts, conversion rates, hours, revenue) are approximate/recalled by the guest during the interview, not from precise tracking (he explicitly says he wasn't using a CRM or tracking rigorously in the early months).
