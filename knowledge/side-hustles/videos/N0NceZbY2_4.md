# The Easiest $3K/Day Business Nobody Is Talking About
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0NceZbY2_4 | Published: 2026-03-08

Interview with Sariah Howell, founder of Memory Magnets Co (memorymagnets.co.com) — custom photo magnets business.

## The idea
- Print custom photo magnets using a dedicated magnet-making machine; sell online, at markets/farmers markets, and live at weddings/events (guests take a photo, it's printed onto a magnet on the spot).
- Started with under $1,000 (put the machine on a credit card) while broke, pregnant, husband earning $14/hr.

## Numbers
- Revenue: $1.63M in first full year; $600K profit (~37% net margin) that year.
- Monthly revenue examples: $104K (February, off-season/shortest month), $233K (August, best month), first $10K month ~2-3 months in (July 2024), first $100K+ month at ~6 months in (Nov 2024, $100,500).
- First month profit: "a little over a thousand dollars."
- Margins: smallest magnets ~91% gross margin. Magnet material cost (paper, ink, metal, mylar) ≈ $0.35/magnet.
- Machines/kits sold to other aspiring operators: ~30% margin, higher price point.
- Markets: booth fees range $20-$300 (pricier/established markets = more traffic and better ROI). Revenue per market: as low as $150, as high as $3,000+ (one $3K result was an 8-10hr, 2-day market). One 20-hour 2-day market: ~$1,500 revenue after ~$1,000 labor (helpers at $12-$20/hr) and ~$300 costs, netting ~$1,300-1,400 (~50% margin, ~$75/hr net to owner).
- Weddings: charge $1,200-$3,000 per 2-4 hour event (couple pays, magnets free to guests). Real example: $2,000 quote, 3 hours, 40 min drive, staffed by 4 family members (own labor), made 300 magnets. Hard costs ~$100 (magnets) + ~$5 gas ≈ $105 total cash cost → ~80% net margin on that job even using paid labor estimate ($25/hr x4 x3hr=$300 labor + $100 materials = $400 cost against $2,000 = 80% margin).
- Retail sale format at markets: 9-packs of magnets for $25.
- Baby shower/event example: charged only $300 for a 2-hour Air Force base event (discounted for military), still profitable; suggests $500 is reasonable market rate for a 2-hour event with one helper.

## Process / how it works
- Sold at markets first to build local awareness before doing weddings (organic: wedding customers approached her at a market after buying repeatedly).
- Wedding logistics: guest scans a QR code → uploads/crops their own photo via custom software → auto-populates a print template → operator hits print → magnet stamped on the spot, handed out free (cost absorbed in the couple's fee).
- Originally (first ~8 months) ran the whole process manually via phone — texting photos, downloading, placing into template, printing — no computer needed, just a printer and a phone.
- Printer used: Epson ET-8500 (~$550 on sale) but says any decent inkjet + photo paper works.
- Two magnet sizes offered: square and rectangle; photos centered/cropped by customer via the QR software.
- Pricing structure for events: bulk per-magnet rate + hourly fee (~$50/hr per person) or flat rate; Koerner suggests simplifying to a flat per-head price (e.g., $20-30/head) since it's a price-insensitive category (wedding vendor spend) and even outlier demand (avg 2 magnets/person) still leaves 70-80%+ margins.
- Lead generation: business cards handed out at markets convert into online orders in the following ~5 days (customers didn't have time to wait in line/choose photos on the spot).
- Growth channels: organic social media (behind-the-scenes/process videos), markets, word of mouth/repeat buyers, wedding referrals — deliberately did almost no paid outreach.
- Add-on revenue: sells the printing machines/kits plus refill supplies (paper, ink, metal, mylar) to other people wanting to start the same business; also developing a training course covering business setup (EIN, Shopify store build, live-events operations) since demand for 1:1 help outpaced her capacity.
- Expanding beyond weddings to baby showers, graduation parties, corporate/military events.
- New product lines in development: re-stickable (non-magnetic) backing reusable ~20 times and washable; a round machine for pins/bottle openers.

## Caveats / risks
- Business is seasonal — warmer months (more markets/weddings) outperform winter.
- Local market saturation is a real perception risk but the guest argues true saturation is unlikely because reach is inherently hyper-local (need "multiple people in every city of 30,000+" to even scratch total wedding demand — ~2-2.5M weddings/year in the US).
- Cheap/price-sensitive local markets (example given: Utah) can suppress per-unit pricing even with high demand.
- Offers a lifetime warranty on kits sold to others, which can eat into margin on that product line but is used as a trust/differentiation lever.
- No current way to print directly on the magnetic backing itself (some competitors use stickers instead); considered a QR-code tracking sticker for own marketing but decided it's not worth the labor at high volume (thousands of magnets/month).
