# Your Shortcut to Building a $1M Business in 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3PqSi22RbBg | Published: 2025-01-01

Chris Koerner solo episode pitching HighLevel (aka GoHighLevel) as a white-label platform for launching niche SaaS/agency businesses without needing to be a developer or a subject-matter expert. Note: Koerner discloses he receives referral payments from HighLevel and is not their employee/agent.

## The idea
- Core thesis: the two "holy grail" business models are agencies (easy to launch, high-ticket recurring cash flow, e.g. $1,000-5,000/month clients) and SaaS (great exit multiples, 5-50x ARR) — but both normally require deep expertise (agencies: you must actually know the skill, e.g. PPC, to justify the fee; SaaS: you need dev skills/capital and a long product-market-fit runway).
- HighLevel (an all-in-one CRM/marketing-automation platform positioned as a competitor to ClickFunnels, Zapier, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Twilio, Google Voice, Leadpages, Pipedrive, Calendly, Trustpilot, and Podio) can be "white-labeled" — an operator rebrands its existing tools/automations as their own niche-specific software product or agency service, without building any of the underlying technology themselves.
- Model: pay a flat ~$500/month HighLevel agency account fee, create unlimited white-labeled "sub-accounts" for individual clients at near-zero additional marginal cost, and charge each client $200-$5,000+/month for what appears to the client to be custom, industry-specific software.

## Concrete examples cited (real, named businesses)
- A solo operator charges $1,500 one-time + $200/month recurring for "custom" dentist website design — in reality, he just clicks a template-generation button inside HighLevel and swaps in the client's name/logo/copy; his own underlying cost for that $200/month is near-zero since it rolls up into his master agency account.
- Automated Google Business Profile "reputation management": HighLevel (using OpenAI's API) auto-generates and posts responses to incoming Google reviews (1-5 star) — a built-in HighLevel feature, resold by operators for ~$100/month per client.
- Automated Google review solicitation: after a job/quote/invoice is marked complete, an automated text+email first asks "Are you happy with your service today?" — a "no" answer routes to a private feedback form (never asking for a public review); a "yes" answer routes directly to the Google Business Profile review page. Entirely built with HighLevel's existing automation/workflow tools.
- **DigiWoof** — marketing software specifically for "R+" dog trainers; a HighLevel "Gold tier" partner (100+ sub-accounts/customers). Koerner's estimated math: 200 customers x an estimated (conservative) $500/month ≈ $100,000/month (~$1.2M/year revenue), with estimated 50%+ net margins (~$500K+/year net profit) given HighLevel's own underlying software cost is a low single-digit percentage of that revenue and the business likely runs on just a founder + a salesperson driving traffic via Facebook/PPC ads to a landing page, closing dog trainers by phone.
- **REI Reply** — a marketing platform for real estate investors; a HighLevel "Diamond tier" partner (1,000+ sub-accounts/customers) priced at $99/month; estimated at $99 x 1,000+ customers ≈ $100,000+/month (~$1.2M+/year), possibly higher given customer count could exceed the 1,000 minimum threshold.
- A niche product helping chiropractors specifically find Spanish-speaking patients — also a Gold tier partner (100+ customers); pricing not publicly listed (interpreted by Koerner as a signal of higher, custom-quoted pricing).

## Process / how to find and execute ideas
1. Sign up for a HighLevel agency account (a free 30-day trial is referenced) to explore the ~800 built-in features/automations available to white-label.
2. Use HighLevel's own public "SaaSpreneur Awards" page as an idea-generation tool — it lists agency partners by tier (Gold = 100+ sub-accounts, Diamond = 1,000+ sub-accounts) along with their niche/website, letting you reverse-engineer what niches and offers are already working at scale, then replicate the model in the same or a different niche/industry.
3. Estimate the target business's revenue/profitability yourself: multiply their approximate sub-account count (inferred from their award tier) by their publicly listed monthly price; because HighLevel's own underlying cost structure is known (flat agency fee, minimal per-account marginal cost), assume high net margins (Koerner's estimate: 50%+ net margin is plausible for many of these businesses).
4. Pick or invent a specific, narrow niche + service combination (framed as roughly "1,000 industries x 100 service variations = 100,000 potential specific business models") rather than a generic, broad offering.
5. **Customer acquisition tactic specifically emphasized**: instead of cold-scraping a broad list of business owners in your target niche (many of whom have no marketing budget), find business owners who are *already* paying for leads/marketing — search directories/platforms like Thumbtack, Angie's List, and Google Local Service Ads (LSA) for your target trade (e.g., roofers) to identify businesses already spending real money (often thousands/month) acquiring leads through those channels; pitch them a cheaper or better lead-generation alternative built on your white-labeled HighLevel service, since they've already demonstrated willingness and budget to pay for this exact category of service.
6. Deliver the product/service to the client using HighLevel's existing tools/automations under your own brand; charge a markup (potentially very high margin, since the underlying software cost is fixed and low).

## Tools/platforms named
- HighLevel / GoHighLevel (core platform)
- OpenAI API (used within HighLevel's automated review-response feature)
- Thumbtack, Angie's List, Google Local Service Ads (LSA), Facebook local groups, Facebook Marketplace, Google Pay-Per-Click — all named as customer-acquisition channels, with Thumbtack/Angie's List/LSA specifically reframed as places to find *already-paying* prospective clients rather than as competitor lead sources.

## Caveats / risks
- All specific revenue/profit figures for DigiWoof, REI Reply, and the chiropractor-marketing example are Koerner's own estimates/extrapolations based on publicly available tier thresholds and listed pricing — not confirmed, audited figures from the businesses themselves.
- The pitch assumes a "1,000 industries x 100 approaches" level of white-space opportunity; no discussion of how saturated any specific niche currently is, or of client churn/retention challenges inherent to reselling a third-party platform you don't own or control.
- Koerner explicitly discloses a financial/referral relationship with HighLevel, which is a conflict of interest relevant to weighing his enthusiasm for the platform.
