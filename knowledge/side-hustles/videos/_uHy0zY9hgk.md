# Sell These $3.5K AI Pitch Decks Built in 12 Min (+4 More Ideas)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uHy0zY9hgk | Published: 2025-06-11

Guest: Nik (Nikonomics Podcast). Multiple distinct business ideas discussed in one episode.

**1. AI-generated pitch deck agency (using Manus AI)**
- Existing real-world example cited: a Twitter "pitch deck guy" scrapes SEC filings for newly launched investment funds, cold-outreaches the fund managers ("congrats on launching, I'd love to make you a pitch deck, here's my portfolio"), charges **$3,500 per deck**.
- Key differentiator vs. ChatGPT/GPT-4o: Manus AI can generate an entire multi-slide, editable PowerPoint (.pptx) deck from a single simple prompt (demo: "Make a PowerPoint deck for me showing the growth of the RV park industry" — produced a 12-slide, professional-looking deck in 12 minutes 31 seconds, fully editable, not a flat PDF).
- Automation opportunity described: use an automation tool (Gumloop, Lindy, or Zapier) to scrape SEC filings for newly launched funds, feed data to Manus via its **API** to auto-generate a pitch deck in a target style, then use a contact-finder tool (Apollo) to get the fund manager's email and cold-email a watermarked preview (first 5 slides) offering to sell the full deck — described as "could be fully automated as of today."
- Other names/examples: Matthew Gore ("the Pitch Deck Cowboy") — builds pitch decks for clients as his core business (previous podcast guest).
- Adjacent opportunities: (a) monitor SlideShare.net (5-10M visits/month; sold to LinkedIn for $112M in 2012) for newly uploaded decks, auto-redesign them with AI, and resell the improved version back to the original uploader for $20-200; (b) target TeachersPayTeachers.com sellers (who sell lesson plans) and pitch converting their lesson plan into an accompanying slide-deck version for a $20 add-on fee — generalizable to anyone creating a lesson plan/outline (e.g., church teachers) who could pay to convert an outline into a polished slide deck.
- Market size cited: pitch deck / corporate presentation creation described as a "multi-billion dollar" market.

**2. "HowDoYouUseAI.com" — crowdsourced AI use-case idea board**
- Built live during the episode using Replit (vibe-coded), domain bought minutes before recording after confirming it was unregistered on Namecheap.
- Core mechanic: gated content — a visitor must submit their own "how I use AI" idea before they can view/upvote anyone else's submitted ideas; users can then subscribe to a weekly email of the top-upvoted use cases (newsletter/email-capture play).
- Validated via Google Trends: search interest in "how do you use AI" showed a local peak in February (attributed to the "Operator" AI product launch) and was still climbing at time of recording.
- Framed as solving a real pain point: people don't know how to find applications for new AI capabilities/features even when they're impressive.

**3. Personality-quiz funnel as a lead magnet / recruiting tool**
- Demonstrated example: "The Entrepreneur's Guide to LLMs" quiz (built via ChatGPT to generate quiz logic + Lovable to build the actual interactive site) that matches a business owner to the best LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Grok) for their needs based on quiz answers; requires email entry to see results (lead-gen gate).
- Build time: ~2 hours the first time doing it; estimated ~1 hour if repeated with an established process (synthesize source data → prompt ChatGPT to build the quiz logic → build the front end in Lovable).
- Recruiting/HR application proposed: build a "culture fit" personality quiz (not skills-based) that both job-seeker and hiring company fill out, then match candidates whose "culture fit score" (e.g., 1-100 scale) aligns closely with the company's ideal profile — pitched as a way to undercut traditional recruiter fees (typically 10-30% of first-year salary, e.g. $10,000+ on a $150,000 role) via automated LinkedIn outreach to HR/hiring decision-makers.
- Personal-brand/audience-based variant: an influencer with a large following (example: 800k-1M views/video, 1% conversion = 8,000-10,000 respondents) could build their own hiring/culture quiz as a lead magnet, building an email list of pre-scored potential hires to query whenever a role opens (e.g., "need a social media manager — who scored highest on my quiz?").
- Suggested quiz questions to reveal real personality traits (vs. generic self-report questions people answer strategically): "which stresses you out more — being dropped in a foreign country with zero plan, or having every minute scheduled with no flexibility?" and "what's the last project you lost sleep over / stayed up all night working on?"

**4. Niche personality-based job board (à la Dribbble/Wellfound/Contra, but matching on cultural fit instead of skill)**
- Concept: apply the specific-vertical job board model (Dribbble = designers, Wellfound = startup/VC roles) to a personality/culture-fit matching layer instead of a skill/background layer, on the theory that cultural fit predicts success as much as or more than technical skill (skills can be trained, fit often can't).

**5. Perplexity Labs for market research and lead-magnet reports**
- Distinguished from standard "deep research" (ChatGPT/Perplexity): Perplexity Labs ($20/month Perplexity subscription required) produces multimedia outputs — proprietary interactive charts (built from raw scraped data, not just embedded existing images), downloadable PDFs/CSVs, and full written analysis with cited sources (demo pulled 37 sources analyzing the keto-app market: iOS ratings, pricing, weekly downloads/revenue, active users — completed in 11 minutes).
- Business model proposed: digital agencies/consultants/B2B sellers generate a free, highly-specific industry report (e.g., a growth/trends report for a prospect's exact market — cited example: HVAC supply company sending market reports to HVAC business owners) and send it unsolicited as a high-value lead magnet/foot-in-the-door before a sales conversation — compared favorably to IBISWorld's $2,000 industry reports, which this method can approximate for a fraction of the cost/effort.

**Prompting technique shared (cross-cutting tip, from a Reddit post)**
- Advanced prompt-engineering method: (1) ask the LLM to generate a detailed "prompt engineering guide" for your target role/audience; (2) paste in 5 example outputs you like as reference; (3) explicitly instruct the LLM to "generate a prompt that could have generated these example outputs, and include a better set of examples" — i.e., have the LLM reverse-engineer the ideal prompt from desired outputs, rather than trying to write the perfect prompt yourself. Can be extended further: generate a "prompt evaluation guide," apply it to critique your prompt, then generate 3 improved alternative versions and pick the best.
