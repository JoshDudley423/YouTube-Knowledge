# $1K a Day From This Simple Side Hustle (Here's How to Copy It)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OWhPtx1SAWg | Published: 2025-09-29

Interview with Clayton and Haley Bowman, "Backyard Pizza," a family-run wood-fired pizza tent/stand in Cameron, Missouri (population ~8,500), plus a follow-up demo segment by Chris Koerner making pizza at home.

## The idea
- Sell authentic Italian-style, wood-fired pizzas ($15-$20 each) from a tent set up in the parking lot of a local hardware store, with a bigger custom-built 24-foot food truck in progress.
- Self-taught over ~2 years; first pizza was "horrible," improved through repetition.
- Started because both spouses were working separate jobs and barely seeing each other; wanted a business they could run together with their three kids (homeschooled/alt-schooled, work the tent with them).
- Long-term roadmap: tent → food truck → eventually a brick-and-mortar bakery (not a pizza restaurant) whose purpose is to supply fresh dough to multiple future food trucks, avoiding reliance on a commissary kitchen. Described as a 10-15 year plan.
- Location arrangement: parking-lot spot at the hardware store is free — they trade the store owner pizza/lunch in exchange for the space; framed as symbiotic (pizza customers may become hardware customers and vice versa).

## Numbers
- Regular (non-event) day at the parking-lot spot: ~30-40 customers served, roughly $500-700/day in revenue (e.g., ~20 orders/day ≈ $600-700).
- Cost structure: food cost ≈ 20% of revenue; other overhead (gas, supplies) ≈ 15%; net profit margin ≈ 60% on regular days.
- Some individual nights at the same stationary spot have generated $1,000+ in a single sitting.
- Upcoming/first larger public event: goal of $1,000 revenue (no track record yet at events beyond a smaller July 4th one).
- Comparable businesses cited by host: "Doughboy" in the UK runs two pizza ovens out of a small trailer and makes "thousands of dollars a day"; a full-time professor in California runs a wood-fired pizza side hustle at two farmers markets on weekends and nets $175,000/year in profit from that alone.
- Host's own home demo: claims a wood-fired pizza made this way can be sold at 80-90% profit margin (per-pizza cost of ingredients vs. $15-20 sale price).

## Process (Koerner's home wood-fired pizza demo, meant as a replicable base recipe/technique)
- Dough recipe (65% hydration): 1 kg flour (00 flour), 650g water, 25g salt, 2g yeast, splash of olive oil. Mix, rest on counter 1 hour, refrigerate 2 days, then split into 8 sections (~200g dough balls), proof ~2 hours before use.
- Shaping: use fists/fingers to stretch the dough by letting its own weight do the work — never use a rolling pin, and avoid pinching/touching the outer edge so the crust puffs properly.
- Dust with a 50/50 mix of double-zero flour and cornmeal to prevent sticking.
- Use fresh mozzarella; do a "shake test" on the peel before loading to confirm the pizza will release cleanly into the oven.
- Wood-fired oven target temp: 800-925°F (836°F cited as ideal; above ~925°F, reduce heat). Cook time: 60-90 seconds, rotating/jerking the pizza to get even browning (bottom cooked by the stone/peel, top by the overhead flame).

## Advice given for someone wanting to copy this
- Start local before expanding to a wider area/state — don't scale distribution too fast.
- Don't buy all-new equipment up front; buy used/minimal gear to get started faster and start earning and promoting sooner, then reinvest to replace equipment over time. The Bowmans regretted buying everything new initially, saying it slowed their launch.
- Most popular pizza items: pepperoni + green pepper (red sauce); sausage + mushroom (and optionally banana peppers for spice) on Alfredo-base pizzas.

## Caveats
- Very early-stage/small business (first "official" larger event hadn't happened yet at time of filming) — the $1K/day headline is aspirational/best-case, not an established average.
- Reliant on free/bartered location access (hosted rent-free at a hardware store); a paid location would change the margin math.
- Family labor (unpaid, including children helping) is baked into the current cost structure — margins would look different with paid staff.
