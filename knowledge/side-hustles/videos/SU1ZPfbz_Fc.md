# How To Profit from Washing Teslas at Supercharging Stations
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SU1ZPfbz_Fc | Published: 2024-02-24

Short clip (podcast segment) covering two related mobile-service business ideas targeted at Tesla/car owners: mobile wheel repair and mobile car washing at Superchargers.

## Idea 1: Mobile wheel repair / curb rash repair
- Business model: travel to the customer's home driveway or workplace parking spot and repair curb-rashed/scuffed wheels/rims on-site, restoring them to near-showroom condition.
- Real example cited: a guy in the St. Louis area does this exclusively for Teslas (though he'll do any vehicle) and stays "nonstop" busy -- can't find or retain help ("you train somebody then they don't show up for work"), so he's resigned to doing it solo.
- Numbers: average ticket ~$300 per job; each job takes about 1-1.5 hours; work is done directly at the customer's home or workplace.
- Customer acquisition: almost entirely organic through a local Tesla owners' Facebook fan/club page; supplements slow periods with dealer work (dealerships pay him a couple hundred dollars to make curb-rashed rims on trade-in vehicles look new before resale).
- Why Tesla specifically: all Teslas ship from the factory with black rims, so curb rash is highly visible/an eyesore immediately; Tesla owner community is described as unusually passionate/vocal ("rabid" fan base, prone to going viral), which drives organic word-of-mouth demand.
- Suggested go-to-market: brand/name the business around Tesla/EVs initially (e.g., "Bolt Wheel Repair") to capture that highly engaged niche audience, then quietly expand to all vehicles later by just updating website copy -- the name doesn't need to change.
- Adjacent expansion path noted: mobile tire replacement/repair is a growing franchise category (with dedicated service vans) that an established wheel-repair customer base could expand into over time.
- Caveat/gap noted: surprising to the hosts that this isn't already a bigger "known" niche/industry (compared to how mobile pressure washing has become a recognized business category) -- possibly limited by the availability of skilled labor (as illustrated by the operator's own inability to hire/retain help).

## Idea 2: Mobile car washing at EV Supercharging stations
- Premise: EV drivers are a captive audience for ~30-45 minutes while charging and could pay for a car wash during that dead time.
- Proposed setup: two buckets, a water tank in a truck bed, approach charging customers directly ("how long are you charging? I'll wash your car for $25").
- Estimated volume: could plausibly wash up to ~10 cars/day at a single Supercharger location before facing pushback from the site/municipality.
- Product/technique named for the "no rinse" pitch: "Optimum No Rinse" (aka ONR) -- a two-bucket wash system (one clean water, one dirty water) that doesn't require a rinse step, hose, or power washer, addressing a specific Tesla-owner concern about damaging paint/sensors via automatic car washes (several owners avoid automatic washes entirely, per the hosts).
- Suggested upsell: offer a full wash-and-detail service post-charge as a premium add-on.
- Caveat: risk of being told to leave by property/municipal management since Supercharger lots aren't necessarily zoned/permitted for commercial activity; unaddressed in the segment beyond acknowledging it as a likely eventual pushback.

## Broader context
- Both ideas framed around the thesis that EV/Tesla ownership creates new "car care" pain points (paint/wheel sensitivity, no-touch car wash aversion, longer-held vehicles due to high interest rates and pricier new cars) that traditional car-care businesses haven't fully capitalized on yet.
- No specific startup capital figures given beyond basic equipment (buckets, water tank, truck) for the car wash idea; wheel repair startup costs/equipment not detailed in this clip.
