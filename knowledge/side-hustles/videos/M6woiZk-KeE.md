# How I Turned A Gas Station Visit Into A Multi-Million Dollar Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M6woiZk-KeE | Published: 2025-12-26

- Business idea: **reselling Buc-ee's (gas station chain) merchandise and snacks online** at a markup, via texassnax.com (Shopify store).
- Origin story: 5 years before filming, founder walked into a Buc-ee's, noticed Buc-ee's doesn't sell online, and spent about **$3,000 buying one of everything** to test demand. It went viral; the resulting business did **$160,000 in its first month**.
- Model: buy full retail in bulk directly from a Buc-ee's store (coordinate with the store beforehand so they palletize a large order), pick it up with a box truck/sprinter van, haul to their own warehouse, restock shelves, list on Shopify, email their list, and sell.
- Team size: about a dozen employees; runs out of a dedicated warehouse.
- Concrete product margins/pricing named:
  - Halloween shirts: buy ~$18, resell ~$33-$35 (2nd/3rd best seller every year).
  - Beaver Nuggets (corn puff snack, top seller): buy $5, resell $9.
  - Jerky (Bohemian Garlic best seller, Steakhouse #2): buy $9 (small bag)/$16 (large bag), marked up only ~40% ("hard to justify paying $20 for a bag of jerky") — worst margin category mentioned.
  - Candy: store sells at ~$4/bag, resold at ~$8/bag ("does okay").
  - Fudge was their #2 best seller early on, but Buc-ee's corporate asked them to stop selling it because freshness/refrigeration couldn't be guaranteed — they complied to stay compliant/on good terms with Buc-ee's.
  - Non-Buc-ee's-branded housewares (~10% of store floor space) don't sell well online at all — even generic "Texas-branded" (not Buc-ee's-branded) items sell poorly. Customers specifically want the Buc-ee's brand/logo, not just Texas themes.
  - A giant novelty beaver statue bought for ~$2,000 has been listed on the site for years and never sold — acknowledged as a pure gimmick/marketing prop.
- Broader "hidden opportunity" thesis: an entire local economy grows around a Buc-ee's location once it opens — HEB grocery stores, restaurants, and Tesla Superchargers (locations often have 10-40 charging stalls) cluster nearby. Buc-ee's runs 50-150 gas pumps per site and treats gas as a loss leader, not a profit center, to draw in the retail/food traffic. Tesla drivers reportedly pay ~$0.37/kWh to charge, then spend $200-$300 inside on snacks/merch while waiting.
- Caveats raised: margins in the resale business "are not great" overall; there's heavy competition; it took the business "a few years" to reach profitability despite the viral first month. Any online reseller of a retailer's goods risks the retailer objecting (as happened with fudge) — must stay compliant with the source retailer's wishes to keep the relationship.
- Secondary idea floated (real estate angle): savvy real estate investors can buy land near where a new Buc-ee's is announced/opening and profit as the surrounding commercial ecosystem develops.
