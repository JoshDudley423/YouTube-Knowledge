# How I Make $35k/Month With Other People's Content (Legally)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPnafDDgj6o | Published: 2026-01-27

## The business idea

Repost/repurpose other creators' viral short-form video clips (with added original commentary/insight) natively to Facebook, monetized through Facebook's Content Monetization program, then funnel that traffic to an email newsletter for a second, larger revenue stream. Costs ~3 hours/week of the creator's own time once the process is dialed in.

## Why Facebook specifically (the market gap)

- Facebook has 2.1 billion daily active users but most people who used to post personal updates there have shifted to Instagram — supply of good content is low relative to demand/attention, so Facebook pays creators to keep people engaged.
- Instagram, by contrast, has plenty of creators and doesn't need to pay for content supply — so this strategy is Facebook-specific, not cross-platform.

## Revenue and cost numbers

- Reported profit: $10,000-$35,000/month on Facebook from largely repurposed content, ~3 hours/week of work.
- Facebook Reels payout: ~$0.25 per 1,000 views (creator's own rate; varies by niche — business/finance content pays more than unboxing/gaming content aimed at lower-spending audiences).
- Comparison: YouTube long-form pays roughly 60x more per 1,000 views for this creator (~$17 net per 1,000 views) than Facebook Reels — but Reels/short-form is framed as the best current arbitrage on Facebook specifically.
- Example single-post earnings shown from Meta Business Suite: an original bonfire video made $2,000; repurposed content examples made $150, $110 (Costco-related), $102 (sober living housing), $80 (sport courts), $70 (donuts).
- Production cost per video: 5-10 minutes of the creator's time plus ~$10 paid to an editor; those videos generated $500-$2,000 each.
- A viral business-niche Reel/video typically gets 500,000-5,000,000 views; ceiling for the niche is roughly 12-15 million views (rare).
- Newsletter growth over the prior 12 months: from 96,000 to 250,000 subscribers, almost entirely organic, driven by a single comment link on every Facebook post.
- Email capture tool "SparkLoop" pays $0.50-$5 per verified email referred, as a separate income stream from the content itself ("Facebook is paying me to take their users off of Facebook").

## Process / steps

1. **Source inspiration, don't steal outright**: browse a dedicated, fresh Instagram "research" sub-account (Instagram allows up to 4 sub-accounts per main account) built around your content niche, swiping Reels until something resonates. Also source screenshottable viral text/image posts from Twitter/X (must fit in a single screenshot to perform well on Facebook — multi-image carousels reportedly underperform there).
2. **Add original commentary/insight to qualify as fair use** — do not just re-upload someone else's video unedited (that's the kind of copying that gets accounts banned). The creator's method: react on-camera (green-screen overlay) giving his own critique/alternate plan for the business or idea shown (e.g., reacting to a "how I grew a junk removal business to $150k" video by pointing out the $150k figure was revenue not profit, that buying a dump truck up front is bad advice, and outlining a leaner startup path instead — rent a U-Haul 26-footer for ~$40/day + $0.75/mile rather than buying a truck, charge ~$50/hour to start, don't buy your first truck until ~$20k/month revenue, junk removal profit margins run 10-35%).
3. Reaction/"just watching and going 'oh, mhm'" style videos are explicitly NOT protected fair use and should be avoided — must add real value/analysis.
4. **Never use the cross-post toggle** between Instagram and Facebook (or any platform) — always upload natively to each platform separately; cross-posted content gets significantly reduced reach/virality on both ends. Same content should be uploaded separately to Instagram, Facebook, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat Spotlight, X/Twitter, Threads, and LinkedIn.
5. **Editing tools**: Instagram's own "Edits" app is easy to use but has a weaker green-screen effect than CapCut; CapCut is used for green-screen overlay work despite privacy/data concerns about the Chinese-owned app (author says he tries to avoid it otherwise). Workflow: trim dead space/pauses out of the foreground selfie clip, then in a new project add the sourced background video, add the trimmed selfie as an overlay, apply "remove background" auto-removal, mute the background audio, shrink/position the selfie in a corner, add captions, optionally overlay supporting images (e.g., pulled from Google Images).
6. **AI-assisted scripting for people who struggle with commentary ideas**: download the target video, upload it to an AI tool — the creator specifically recommends **Gemini** as best for watching/analyzing video content (vs. Claude, ChatGPT, Grok) — ask it for value-add ideas, then ask it to write a ~60-second script with a strong hook and a "retention hack" to keep viewers to the end. Paste the resulting script into a teleprompter app and read it via selfie camera.
7. Take a **clear, hard stance/hot take** rather than a nuanced/hedged one when commenting on sourced content — algorithmically and virally, decisive opinions ("hard disagree," etc.) outperform hedged ones, though the creator only takes positions he genuinely believes to preserve integrity.
8. **Attribution**: search for and tag the original creator on Facebook when reposting their content/idea — not legally required, but done as an ethical practice (and sometimes impossible if the original creator has no discoverable/followable Facebook profile).
9. **Getting monetization access**: Facebook's creator monetization program has no published, fixed eligibility metrics (unlike YouTube) — you wait for an invite/qualification email once content is picked up consistently, isn't flagged/struck for policy violations, is properly attributed, and clearly qualifies as fair-use commentary rather than stolen content.
10. **Convert platform reach into an owned email list**: put a link (via Card or Beehiiv) to a simple landing page in the top comment of every single post; use SparkLoop to additionally monetize each new subscriber. Rationale given: social platforms can algorithmically bury you or shut you down without notice, but an email list is owned and durable.

## Equipment/tools named

- DJI Mic 2 (wireless lav mic + iPhone receiver) for on-camera commentary audio.
- A teleprompter app for reading AI-generated scripts.
- CapCut (green-screen/overlay editing) and Instagram's "Edits" app.
- Gemini (recommended specifically for video analysis/script generation), also mentions Claude, ChatGPT, Grok as alternatives.
- Card (carrd.co) or Beehiiv for a simple email-capture landing page.
- SparkLoop for per-email referral payouts.
- Meta Business Suite for tracking per-post earnings.

## Caveats / risks

- **Post-publication update (per video description, dated 2/23/26, roughly one month after upload): Facebook banned the creator's account specifically because of this video, and all the revenue referenced in it is gone.** This is a direct, real-world illustration that this exact monetization stream is fragile and platform-dependent — the video's own author lost the business it describes shortly after publicizing the tactic. (He subsequently sold a private guide with additional undisclosed tactics as a result — a monetization pivot, not evidence the original method still works reliably.)
- Explicitly distinguishes legitimate fair-use commentary from outright content theft; warns that some creators who just re-download and repost others' videos unedited do get caught and banned by Facebook.
- Cross-posting via the native toggle measurably reduces reach — a common mistake to avoid.
- Building an audience/getting monetized from zero followers takes real time; new accounts are shown to only a couple hundred people at first — this is active income requiring sustained consistency, not a passive/instant payout.
- Facebook's monetization program is described as new/still evolving with no fixed, published qualification bar, adding uncertainty to when/whether a given account gets approved.
- Niche matters for payout rate — content posted outside your established topical lane (e.g., a business-content creator posting an off-niche viral clip) is unlikely to perform as well because the algorithm has typed your audience.
