# 9 Beginner-Friendly Ways to Make $10K
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GER_aZkQQ_0 | Published: 2025-10-21

Chris Koerner and Brandon Doyle brainstorm specific business ideas sized to hit $10,000 across four timeframes (a year, a month, a week, a day), including live updates on ideas they'd actually launched since a prior recording.

## $10K-in-a-year ideas
- **Niche-hobby "personal CRM" apps built via vibe-coding** (using no-code/AI tools like Replit, Claude, ChatGPT, Bolt to build an app via natural language, no coding knowledge required): target a large, obsessive, "anti-AI"/analog hobby community and build them a simple tracking app.
  - Example: a board-game tracker/CRM (log plays, scores, opponents, ratings, schedule game nights) — cited a single board-game subreddit with 533,000 members plus a separate 54,000-weekly-visitor board-gaming subreddit; monetize at ~$10/month; doesn't need many subscribers to clear $10K/year.
  - Same pattern suggested for other die-hard analog hobbies: a quilting pattern/wish-list tracker or a "quilting companion" AI chatbot (quilters skew older, are active in large Facebook groups, have high discretionary income, and are easy to reach via Facebook ads) — floated as plausibly a $10K/month opportunity, not just $10K/year; also a gardening journal app (track plant varieties, yields, soil/fertilizer mix per season).
  - Marketing angle suggested: target ads directly at the pain point of the community (e.g., "I hate it when my friends don't remember the score of last week's game") and buy ads on niche-relevant subreddits — Reddit ads were noted as having recently improved.
  - General principle: build small features people find "cute," because sometimes an unplanned side feature becomes the actual core product (cited Slack's origin as an internal chat tool built for a video game that failed).
- **A themed stock-screening/backtesting web app** ("Neffle" — network effects + founder-led): a vibe-coded tool (pulling from Yahoo Finance data) that scores S&P 500 companies on a "network effects" and "founder-led" spectrum, lets a user filter/backtest a portfolio of qualifying stocks against the S&P 500 historically, and displays the comparative return.
  - Live demo result cited: a portfolio of ~25 companies scoring 7+/10 on both criteria returned ~29%/year vs. ~8.4%/year for the S&P 500 over the tested period.
  - Monetization ideas discussed: charge ~$5–10/month, offer notifications when a new stock meeting the criteria IPOs, offer backtesting access, or segment by exchange/market cap; a $1 trial converting to a recurring monthly charge, or a lifetime-access one-time price.
  - Explicitly framed as a repeatable template: pick any investment thesis, vibe-code a simple app/screener around it, and sell access — "there are thousands of theses" like this one.
  - Site built from this idea: neflstocks.com (offered free as a landing page/demo). Explicit disclaimer given in the video: not investment advice.

## $10K-in-a-month idea
- **Local in-person "AI for small business" seminar with a backend high-ticket upsell**: run geo-targeted Facebook ads to local business owners promoting a $75 (or similar) in-person AI bootcamp/seminar at a rented venue (e.g., a Holiday Inn conference room, ~$400 for an afternoon). Structure: ~1 hour of general instruction (e.g., "how to build an AI voice agent"), plus a Q&A, with the real goal being a backend high-ticket pitch/consultation offer at the end (framed as a classic "meet my friend in the back of the room" upsell).
  - Revenue model: ticket sales mostly offset Facebook ad spend (estimated to consume up to half of gross revenue) and venue cost; real profit comes from closing a handful of attendees on $1,000–$5,000 packages (either one-time or monthly retainers, scaled to the prospect's business size — e.g., a one-time low four-figure fee for a $400K/year landscaping business vs. a $2,500/month, 12-month contract for a $5M/year HVAC business).
  - Example target economics: closing 5–10 attendees at ~$3,000 each from an afternoon session.
  - Branding advice: position as a local expert/brand (e.g., "[City] AI Experts") rather than a traveling consultant, to build local trust; ~10% of the population are business owners (cited: ~33 million business owners among ~330 million Americans), so even a mid-size city has tens of thousands of prospects, a meaningful fraction of whom can afford a premium offer.
  - Real-world validation update from the co-host: posted a Google-Form interest-check in his own newsletter for a $100 Saturday AI seminar and got 27 signups before recording this follow-up, validating real demand cheaply before committing to logistics; planned to use ChatGPT to help plan the seminar content and the tiered upsell offers.
- **Higher-value same-day version of the seminar model** ("shadow and systemize" offer): a $10,000 one-time fee to spend a full day directly observing a business owner's actual operations (email, sales calls, bidding, staff communication) and then build out automation/systems for them afterward — sold as a single flat project fee rather than a recurring package.

## $10K-in-a-week ideas
- **Shipping container storage rental**: buy used 20 ft or 40 ft shipping containers off Facebook Marketplace for $2,000–$5,000 each; rent out as oversized alternative to traditional self-storage units. Cited comparison: a standard 10×10 storage unit (100 sq ft) rents for $85–$185/month in most markets, while a 40×8×8 ft container (~320 sq ft, roughly 3x the space) bought for ~$4,000 could be rented whole for $150/month (payback in ~2–2.5 years) or split into quarters at $75/month each (effectively $300/month total, payback in ~1 year).
  - Scaling to hit $10K in a week specifically: buy multiple containers at once and sell/rent them out in bulk, or offer an annual-prepay discount.
  - Related land-acquisition tactic mentioned (from a contact on Twitter): approach a landowner with high-traffic road frontage and propose a lease-option deal — pay a modest monthly lease (e.g., $2,000/month for 12 months) with an agreed purchase price (e.g., $400,000) exercisable if the storage business proves out; if it doesn't work, both parties walk away after the lease term. Used as a way to de-risk land acquisition while proving the model to investors/lenders.
- **"Garage sale as a service" for affluent homeowners**: pitch a full-service garage-sale management offering to high-income neighborhoods where residents have valuable unused belongings but no time/interest in running a sale themselves. Operator collects items from the homeowner's driveway, transports, prices, stages, and sells them (either on-site or off-site for image-conscious clients, at an added fee), taking a straight service fee plus a commission on sale proceeds.
  - Framed as a strong pitch using a "BATNA" (best alternative to negotiated agreement) argument: without this service, the homeowner's realistic alternative is paying ~$500 to rent a dumpster and simply throw everything away — so even a modest cut of sale proceeds beats the alternative of a net loss.
  - Cited real example (a similar existing but rare service in Southern California) where their single best sale grossed $40,000.
  - Suggested compounding tactic: build an email/SMS list of repeat luxury garage-sale buyers in the target area, so each subsequent sale can be pre-marketed to thousands of known buyers — used as the differentiator when pitching new homeowner clients ("I already have a list of 3,000 people").
- **Estate-sale leftover liquidation ("sell your sawdust")**: a real acquaintance's business model — show up at the tail end of estate sales (once the official sale weekend ends) and offer the heirs a lump-sum cash buyout (e.g., $500) for all unsold remaining inventory (which may carry $5,000+ in individually discounted price tags), framed against the heirs' BATNA of paying to rent a dumpster (and the emotional discomfort of throwing away a deceased relative's belongings). Buyer then resells the liquidated goods for a profit margin on top.

## $10K-in-a-day ideas
- **Backyard structure "matchmaking"/referral business (real, launched example: "Backyard Fun House")**: after building his own backyard sport court (getting quotes of $50,000–$60,000 from full-service contractors but self-managing 5 individual subcontractors — concrete, surfacing, fencing, hoop, electrical — for ~$29,000 total), Chris and a partner (James, of Fast Tree Care) turned the vetted subcontractor list into a lead-gen business for sport courts, pools, and in-ground trampolines.
  - Execution: built a simple (but polished, non-"vibe-coded"-looking) website via Vercel; created 6 unscripted selfie videos (self-shot, edited in CapCut with auto-captions, ~5 minutes of editing each) with hooks like "Pools are old news, sport courts are the new thing" and "Do you want your kids on screens all day?" (headline/ad copy generated via ChatGPT).
  - Ran as Meta/Facebook ads, $100/day budget per video, each in its own ad set, using Meta's native "instant form" lead capture (name + phone number only), auto-piped via Zapier into a Google Sheet for lead scoring (1–10) by the partner.
  - Results after ~7 days and ~$2,500 total ad spend: cost per lead ranged $23–$36 (one underperforming ad variant, initially at $150/lead, was cut); roughly 80% of leads went unresponsive ("ghosted"), consistent with normal cold-outreach conversion; despite that, the campaign was projected to generate an estimated $25,000–$45,000 in net profit off the initial ad spend, with all installation work subcontracted out (not performed by Chris/James themselves).
  - Alternate lower-overhead variant proposed (without a fulfillment partner like James): charge homeowners a flat $1,000 fee just for a curated list of 5 pre-vetted contractors per project type (sport court, pool, putting green, etc.), and separately negotiate a referral cut from the contractors for any jobs they win.
  - Related result mentioned in passing: Chris is separately selling his tree-trimming business (Fast Tree Care) because James is now focused on the backyard-structures business instead — offered for sale directly via email in the episode.
- **Pop-up headshot/photo studio booth**: rent a portable professional camera setup and offer 10-15 minute paid headshot/product-photo/quick-UGC-video sessions at business conferences, office buildings, or co-working spaces; charge a premium for the short, high-value session and deliver fully edited photos. Cited real-world comparable: at a business conference ("Main Street" in Missouri), a company (OpenPhone) sponsored free attendee headshots via a local photographer as a marketing/goodwill tactic — suggested this same setup could be run as a directly paid service (e.g., a $25 Venmo-QR-code walk-up offer) in high-foot-traffic office building lobbies.
- Cited alternative "$10K in a day" framing from earlier in the episode: an in-person seminar charging $100/head to 100 attendees, run as a single high-volume event day, as a lower-touch version of the "AI seminar" idea above (breakeven on the event itself, real profit still driven by the backend high-ticket upsell).

## Caveats
- The board-game/quilting/gardening app ideas and the stock-screener app are explicitly framed as easy-to-build side projects using AI/no-code tools, but none of the revenue figures given for them are real, achieved numbers — they're back-of-envelope projections based on subreddit/community size and hypothetical conversion, except where explicitly marked as live/validated (the AI-seminar interest-check and the Backyard Fun House ad results, which are real in-progress numbers, not full P&L outcomes).
- The Backyard Fun House economics ($25K–$45K projected net profit) are a projection based on early lead cost and lead-to-quote conversion, not a completed, closed-deal total at time of recording.
- The lease-option land strategy for shipping-container storage assumes a landowner willing to accept a multi-year deferred purchase structure and carries execution risk (permitting, whether "portable" storage avoids permanent-structure rules varies by locality — not addressed in detail).
