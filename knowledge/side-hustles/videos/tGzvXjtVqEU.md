# 17 Years, 75 Businesses, 1 Marriage: How We Make It Work
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tGzvXjtVqEU | Published: 2025-05-09

Personal interview with Chris Koerner's wife, Jessie, about being married to a serial entrepreneur. This is a relationship/mindset episode, not a business-idea or how-to episode — there is no actionable business model, pricing, or process content. The only substantive business-relevant material is a handful of biographical references to specific ventures Chris ran over the years, noted below for context/continuity with other episodes.

## Businesses referenced (biographical, not explained in detail)
- **Phone/iPad repair store**: his first business; ran it out of a ~2,000 sq ft retail space split by a drywall wall — customer-facing repair counter up front, family living space (including an infant) in the back. Took months to become profitable even in a good location because it required being a destination, not relying on walk-in foot traffic.
- **"LCD Cycle"**: a business involving sourcing/inspecting LCD screens (phone/tablet replacement parts) at the kitchen table before shipping them back to and from China; had a stretch of steady/predictable monthly income around 2017 (notably called out as one of the only periods with two consecutive months of similar income in their entire entrepreneurial history).
- **"Presto Cars"**: a concierge car-buying service — customers specify the car they want, the operator attends auto auctions and buys it for them for a ~$700 fee. Chris tried it years after first wanting to, and disliked it — attributed partly to not being "a car guy" and partly to running it alongside an already-successful business, making the friction/hassle feel not worth it by comparison. Explicitly not scalable per his own assessment.
- **Wealth-management-adjacent ambition and an ~18-month period with no personal income**: a stretch (during COVID) where a venture he didn't have full decision-making control over consumed significant time without generating pay, eventually requiring the family to sell rental properties to cover their mortgage/bills.
- **Home bakery**: Jessie ran a from-home bakery business (sparked by a local farmers market) for almost 5 years as a family-business experiment.
- Overall business count referenced in the title/context: 75 businesses over 17 years, income described as "feast or famine" rather than steady — the couple has gone stretches of $0 income before a large distribution or asset sale covers the gap.

## Non-business takeaways (mindset/relationship, included briefly since they recur across the channel's framing)
- Chris cites Jeff Bezos's "regret minimization framework" (asking which choice you'd regret more) as his personal decision heuristic for taking entrepreneurial risk.
- Family/relationship practices mentioned as supporting sustained entrepreneurship: weekly date nights, keeping a work laptop confined to a home office rather than carrying it everywhere, no phones at the dinner table, and proactively communicating major business decisions with a spouse rather than acting unilaterally.

## Caveats
- This episode contains essentially no transferable business idea, financial figures, or operating playbook — it is a personal/relationship reflection. Treat the business names/anecdotes above as background context only; none are explained in enough operational detail here to replicate.
