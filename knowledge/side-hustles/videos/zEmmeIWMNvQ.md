# The Most Profitable AI Business You've Never Heard Of
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zEmmeIWMNvQ | Published: 20260927

Chris Koerner solo episode, live-demoing how to use AI (Claude, via an MCP connector) to turn a dormant email newsletter into an active paid community, and pitching this as a service you can sell to other business owners with an underused email list.

## The business idea
- Service: connect a business owner's existing email newsletter platform (Beehiiv) to an AI assistant via MCP, then use the AI to build and activate a "community" (like a private subreddit) attached to that list — for a client with a large but disengaged list, charge them a flat monthly fee for the ongoing management/growth work.
- Explicitly framed as accessible to non-list-owners too: most people personally know several business owners with an email list they don't actively use, so you don't need your own audience to do this as a service for others.
- Two-sided distribution options for a local business's list specifically: build a topical community around the business itself, or — more effective for a genuinely local audience like a plumbing company's customers — spin the same address/geography data into a **local newsletter**, since customers don't want to discuss "their plumbing issues" together but do live in the same area.

## Numbers
- Host's own newsletter: ~273,000 subscribers, but before this episode only 8 people had joined the auto-created community attached to it — illustrating how common a large-but-dormant list is.
- Worked example economics for a hypothetical landscaping-business client: a 20,000-subscriber list, conservatively assume half are disengaged (10,000 active), a 40% open rate on that group (4,000 opens), and just a 0.1% conversion rate on a compelling offer to that engaged segment yields 4 new landscaping jobs/month; at $3,000/job, that's $12,000/month in new revenue for the business, growing over time as fresh subscribers (who engage better than aged ones) are added.
- Pitch framing: that business owner would reasonably pay $1,000/month for a service generating $12,000/month in new revenue for them (a ~12x return to the client) — and finding 10 such business-owner clients at $1,000/month each is presented as very achievable, since the average person knows roughly 500 people, of whom roughly 50 are typically business owners.

## Process / steps
- Setup: search "[platform] MCP URL" (e.g. Beehiiv MCP), copy that URL into the chosen AI tool's connector/settings menu (demoed with Claude, works the same with ChatGPT/Gemini/Grok), grant access, and set tool-call permissions (the host used "always allow" since he already trusts the specific actions involved, but recommends more cautious users require step-by-step approval for higher-risk actions like sending an email).
- Prompted the AI directly to: (1) attach/activate a community to his existing newsletter, (2) identify his most engaged subscribers ranked by real open/click data, and (3) do all of the top-priority community setup steps at once rather than one at a time, since he explicitly wanted a fast, low-effort build.
- AI-suggested "day one" community setup checklist, in priority order: seed 10-20 founding members by hand (must be genuine super-fans); make the room's purpose obvious in a single read; give every new member a lightweight first-60-seconds assignment (like a simple intro question) since brand-new joiners engage far more than long-tenured "OG" subscribers who've often already mentally checked out; keep total channels to 3-4 max even though the temptation is to create many (plan to prune down after a few months once usage data shows which ones are actually active); reply to every single comment for the first 14 days to visibly demonstrate presence; and establish one recurring weekly ritual (same post, same day, every week).
- Suggested a follow-up step beyond the AI-generated checklist: take the AI's list of your most engaged subscribers, build a targeted segment from it, and personally get those highest-value people on the phone — a phone call carries more weight than any automated email, even a personally-written one.
- For migrating someone else's list into this system: export the list as CSV from their existing platform (Mailchimp, Kit, Substack), create a free Beehiiv account, connect it to an AI assistant, and either ask the AI to import the CSV directly or ask it to explain the exact steps to do it yourself.
- Extendable "shiny object" prompt used live: ask the AI what other subscriber variables the MCP connection can rank/segment by (it named engagement, geography, subscriber tenure/loyalty, acquisition source/channel, paid-vs-free status, and general behavior-over-time signals) — useful raw material for designing additional paid offers to pitch a client.

## Tools/platforms named
- Beehiiv (newsletter platform) and its newly launched "Communities" feature (subreddit/Facebook-group-like feed, channels, DMs, events via Luma integration, gamification badges, customizable onboarding flow) — sponsor of this video; host discloses he is also an investor in the company.
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) — the connector mechanism used to let an AI tool directly read/write inside the Beehiiv account.
- Claude — the AI tool used in the live demo (noted as interchangeable with ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok).
- Substack, Mailchimp, Kit — named only as platforms a prospective client's list might already live on before migrating.

## Caveats / risks
- The host explicitly had never used the Beehiiv Communities feature before recording, presenting the entire demo as a real-time, unscripted first attempt — offered as proof that a viewer with equally little platform-specific expertise could replicate this for a client.
- The core "resurrect a dead community" tactic assumes the underlying list is a real permission-based list with actual relationship equity (people who signed up and once cared), not a purchased or scraped list — the video's math and tactics (segment-then-call, founding-member seeding) rely on some pre-existing trust to reactivate.
- No case study with a real, paying third-party client is presented — the $1,000/month pitch and the 4-jobs/month landscaping example are a worked hypothetical/illustrative model, not a completed deal; treat as "pitched, unproven" until a real outcome is documented elsewhere in this corpus.
- Success is explicitly gated on genuine two-sided engagement (a community needs both people posting and people responding) — the video notes a community that looks dead when a new visitor checks it out will immediately lose that visitor, making the early-seeding and first-14-days-reply-everything steps load-bearing, not optional polish.
