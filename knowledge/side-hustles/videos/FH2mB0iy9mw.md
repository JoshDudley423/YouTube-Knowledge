# watch this if you're tired of being broke
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FH2mB0iy9mw | Published: 2025-06-03

Interview with Shannon Jean (reselling/content creator, went from reselling online to multiple income streams). Episode is mostly mindset/psychology of entrepreneurship rather than a specific business breakdown — light on hard numbers — but contains several concrete, actionable tactics below.

## Concrete tactics and numbers

- **Reseller starter advice**: begin by selling items already in your own house (removes cost/inventory risk, teaches the mechanics — listing, permits, platform quirks — at low stakes) before building a dedicated resale business. Pick a resale niche based on personal interest/curiosity (e.g., a camping enthusiast sources and resells Blackstone grills) rather than a generic "what sells" category.
- **Poshmark reselling mechanic**: growth came partly from manually following large numbers of other users per day (a known platform growth tactic — following triggers notifications that drive profile visits). Jean did this manually at first (reportedly could follow ~1,000 people in ~10 minutes) specifically so he could observe which types of accounts he followed converted into buyers, then built a targeting system around that — his stated reason to avoid immediately automating: doing it manually first is what let him learn which follow-targets actually bought, a insight he says he'd have missed by jumping straight to automation/delegation.
- **eBay/reselling niche-finding tip**: look for obscure, low-competition items with real institutional demand rather than saturated categories (e.g., iPhones have no margin left). Example given: reading a news article that the FAA buys old/discontinued electronic components (e.g., 5.25" floppy discs) off eBay to keep legacy air traffic control systems running — sourcing and reselling into that kind of niche, low-competition institutional demand can carry real margin.
- **Rapid demand-validation process (told by Chris Koerner, using his own pickleball-club idea as the live example)**: before spending money on a private pickleball club build-out (converting a metal shop; would charge ~$100/month membership), he first posted the idea to Facebook Marketplace to gauge interest, then ran a self-made Facebook ad campaign (shot smartphone video himself rather than waiting on an editor, built the ad in ~10 minutes) geo-targeted to a 6-mile radius around the facility (tested and found 6 miles was the right radius — 7 was too far, 5 too narrow). Cost per lead: $1.78; got 2–3 leads within hours for about $6 total spent. Stated rule: validate real demand cheaply (with ads/organic posts) before spending money on facility costs (he'd been about to pay $39 for a septic-inspection quote before doing this test) — "buy with math, sell with emotion" (use data to decide what to build/invest in, but market/sell the finished thing on emotional appeal/story value).
- **Auction-flipping discipline**: set a maximum bid based on spreadsheet math before bidding (e.g., "I can pay $3,500 for this pallet"), submit that number (or a slightly-off number like $3,505) and walk away rather than emotionally chasing a bid upward. Expect to lose the majority of individual auctions (cited ~80% loss rate) — profitability comes from disciplined pricing on the ~20% won, not from winning often.
- **Self-published book as a passive-income stream**: after a software startup failed, repurposed the resulting content/experience into a self-published book; used a paid coach and specific self-publishing tactics to get "bestseller" placement. Now generates ongoing royalty payments (examples cited: an $800 payment and a $500 payment arriving via Amazon after a period away) — compared to owning a small rental property in terms of passive yield.
- **Monetized community**: runs a paid mastermind/community at $5/month, cited as approaching 500 members at time of recording.

## Framing / mindset points (kept brief — not the substance a note is meant to capture, but referenced repeatedly as "why" behind the tactics above)
- Uses the term "revenue stream" instead of "side hustle," arguing side projects should be built around genuine personal curiosity, and can range from $100/month to $50,000/month.
- Advocates treating each new question/curiosity as something to research or test within the hour rather than deferring it, as a deliberate practice for building a "bias for action."
- Warns that people who delegate/automate a new business immediately (without first doing the manual work) tend not to develop real operating knowledge of the fundamentals.

## Caveats
- This episode is explicitly framed by the hosts themselves as an atypical, non-tactical episode ("we didn't have a ton of numbers... this was a different kind of episode than we normally do") — most of the runtime is personal psychology/motivation discussion rather than business mechanics, and few dollar figures beyond the ones listed above are given.
