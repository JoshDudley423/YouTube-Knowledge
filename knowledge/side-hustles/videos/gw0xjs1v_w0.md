# Almost all Business Gurus are Fake. What Makes Me Different?
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gw0xjs1v_w0 | Published: 2024-08-07

This is Chris Koerner's origin-story/bio episode, not a how-to, but it names concrete numbers and mechanics for several businesses he's built.

## iPhone repair retail store ("Phone Restore", started ~2010 in Tuscaloosa, AL)
- Idea trigger: saw a campus flyer "I'll fix your iPhone $60"; watched a student fix a cracked screen with a $15 eBay LCD screen, a suction cup, and a screwdriver, learned via YouTube.
- Inspired by "iPhone MD" (Baton Rouge, LA, LSU students) doing $20k/month out of a single storefront with a line out the door; Apple forced them to rename to "My Phone MD" over trademark use of "iPhone."
- Startup mechanics: signed a personal-guarantee 5-year retail lease at $2,300/month next to a Subway/McDonald's at the busiest intersection in the county (505 East 15th St, Tuscaloosa) — in hindsight, an unnecessary risk since the space had sat vacant for years and a short-term lease with a termination option could have been negotiated instead.
- A local small-business advisor told him the idea and location were bad (rent too high, should open "in the ghetto" at $500/month); Koerner ignored the advice and says the lesson was: only weight criticism from people who know the industry as well or better than you do.
- Launch tactic: got the mayor of Tuscaloosa to host the grand opening, pitched local news (Tuscaloosa News) and the campus paper (Crimson White) for coverage — generated first real traffic.
- First sale was $60 (his own professor's wife's iPhone); business grew slowly at first.
- Expanded to 4 locations in 18 months (Tuscaloosa, Birmingham, 2x Huntsville, AL).
- A tornado (April 27, 2011) destroyed/disrupted the business; recovered by putting out a plywood sign again like a scrappy restart.
- Reached six figures revenue, sold two-thirds to partners (kept a third), grew to seven figures, ultimately sold ~2 years later for about $1M.

## LCD Cycle (started day after selling Phone Restore, ~2013)
- Business model: buy and recycle iPhones/iPhone screens, resell/ship components to China.
- Revenue trajectory: $2.1M year 1, $4.8M year 2, $9.6M year 3, then stagnated in year 4 at eight figures as the market got more saturated and Koerner paid less attention.
- Sold in year 7-8; proceeds funded his current house purchase (described as "millions of dollars").

## Mining Syndicate
- B2B/content business selling mining/crypto-adjacent products; bootstrapped and profitable.
- Did $10M in sales in its first three months (per his crypto-industry business mentioned separately, reaching $10M revenue in 3 months back in 2018) — note the video conflates two crypto-era ventures; Mining Syndicate itself has run ~3.5 years and reached eight figures total.

## Texas Snacks (exclusive online reseller for Buc-ee's products)
- Buc-ee's (Texas gas-station chain, ~55 locations, multi-billion-dollar company) does not sell its branded products online.
- Koerner "growth hacked" his way into becoming their unofficial/exclusive online seller.
- Now a seven-figure business, run day-to-day by his partner Kirk; growing ~30% year-over-year.
- Caveat: getting this kind of exclusive-distribution deal required forcing his way in — no simple replicable steps given, just the general pattern of "official brand has no online channel, become their online channel."

## Sends (3PL for food brands, with partner Kirk)
- Business model: third-party logistics management for online food brands.
- Reached seven figures in revenue but ran on single-digit margins — "really really tough business," profitable but not attractively so.
- Ultimately shut down / fired all customers to focus fully on the more profitable Texas Snacks/Buc-ee's business.
- Caveat: food logistics is a thin-margin, operationally hard business even at real revenue scale — don't assume top-line revenue implies a good business.

## RV parks / mobile home parks
- 2019: started buying RV/mobile home parks with partners; reached $10M valuation within 18 months, sold about a year later.
- A separate 2019 venture buying 20+ mobile home/RV parks across Kansas, Texas, and Georgia with two partners collected $80-90k/month in rent, reached eight figures in property value — but "crash landed very horribly" due to a bad decision by a partner (not detailed here, referenced as a separate video topic).
- Currently: co-general partner in "Blue Metric," a private equity real estate fund buying 1-2 large RV parks/campgrounds per month alongside investors; owns 21 parks, ~$80M in assets under the fund as of this recording.
- Caveat: real estate partnerships carry real downside risk from a single partner's bad call — one such venture failed badly despite strong top-line numbers.

## General patterns/advice stated
- Runs a personal spreadsheet tracking all ~72 businesses started; most failed or were abandoned, some succeeded/sold.
- States a repeatable personal process he loves most: "zero to one" — validating ideas via cheap marketing tests (Facebook ads, flyers on cars, ringless voicemail drops, cold calls/texts/emails, door-to-door) to get "the first dollar in the door."
- Philosophy: only take advice from critics who know the industry as well as you; otherwise use criticism as motivation.
- Also ran a concierge used-car-buying business ("Presto Cars" or similar) that was successful/profitable but he quit because he hated it — illustrates that a working business isn't worth keeping if you don't enjoy operating it.
