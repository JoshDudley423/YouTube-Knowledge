# I Tried the Costco Gold Hack (How Much I Actually Made)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w0756mtps1Y | Published: 2025-12-16

Chris Koerner films himself executing the "Costco gold arbitrage" hack: buy gold bars from Costco below spot price, resell online, and stack credit-card/cashback rewards for extra margin. Credits friend Brayden ("Nugget Hunters," nuggethunters.carrd.co) as the source of the strategy.

## The core mechanic
- Costco sells gold bars (1 oz, various mints e.g. "Lucky Dragon," "Double Dragon," "Lady Fortuna") to members at a price just above/near spot (market) price — notably below what local coin/gold shops or the open resale market will pay.
- "You make your money on the buy, not the sell" — the arbitrage exists because Costco's purchase price is favorable, not because the resale price is inflated.
- Local gold buyers pay significantly less than the achievable online resale price: in the video, purchase price was $4,209 for a 1 oz bar; local buyers quoted $3,976.41 (Dallas Gold & Silver Exchange) and ~$3,900 (Frisco Gold Buyers) — both well below what online resale nets (~$4,150-$4,160 quoted as achievable online). Conclusion: never sell locally, "it's a smaller pond that we're fishing in" — go to the larger internet market instead.

## Process, step by step
1. Call local Costco locations to check gold stock and quantity before driving there (staff can't quote price over the phone, but will confirm stock and inventory count).
2. Purchase limit is typically 5 bars/day per membership; a customer with a separate business membership can buy more (example: one customer bought 10 in a day using two memberships).
3. Best time to catch inventory: reportedly restocks tend to land Wednesdays; call Wednesday morning, or by noon at the latest — it's first-come-first-served and can sell out within an hour when in high demand.
4. Buy the gold bar(s) at the register/cage.
5. List/sell the gold on CollectPure (collectpure.com) — described as "eBay but only for buying/selling precious metals." You are not selling to CollectPure directly; you're selling to another individual buyer, with CollectPure acting as an escrow/authentication middleman.
6. Once sold, CollectPure emails a prepaid FedEx shipping label. Triple-pack the bar (packing materials are free), keep a receipt, and photograph the item before shipping, given the high dollar value involved. Delivery requires signature-on-delivery (protects against "never arrived" disputes).
7. CollectPure receives the package, unpacks and validates authenticity via testing tools, records video of repacking, then pays the seller and forwards the gold to the buyer. CollectPure keeps a small margin/fee for this service.

## Reward stacking / cashback math (the extra profit layer)
- Up to ~7% total cash back is achievable by stacking:
  - 3% back from a credit card that categorizes Costco purchases favorably (e.g., cards giving 3% back on groceries, IF the issuer classifies Costco as a grocery purchase — not all do; a "Venmo card" is mentioned as working well for this).
  - 2% back from Costco itself (their standard membership/purchase rewards).
  - An additional ~2% via a specific service that lets you pay off a credit card balance using a debit card, generating extra cashback in the process.
- Net after typically losing 1-2% on the resale-vs-purchase spread when selling the gold: net cashback/profit around ~5% of the transaction value is achievable, which compounds meaningfully at higher purchase volume.
- Correction noted in the video's description: Costco does not accept Amex; the presenter recommends Capital One Venture or any card maximizing cash back at Costco instead.

## Anecdotal color from the Costco gold buyer (staff interview)
- Reasons people buy: some to flip/resell, some to hold as a savings/investment vehicle, some to melt down and manufacture jewelry (including shipping overseas for jewelry manufacturing) — staff say a "surprising" share falls into this category.
- Demand is highly momentum-driven/psychological: "when it gets expensive everyone wants to buy it, when it gets cheap everyone's scared" — inventory sold out within an hour one week, then was slow to move (2nd buyer of the week) the very next week despite a lower price.
- Some repeat customers are described as serious collectors/"whales" who've held physical gold/silver for decades and specifically seek out a particular staff member ("our bullion guy") they trust.
- When Costco first started selling gold (~2.5 years before this filming), a 1 oz bar was priced around $1,749 and sold out immediately amid heavy news coverage; many first-time-ever collectors bought in purely because of the hype/opportunity.

## Caveats / risks
- Video opens with an explicit disclaimer: educational/entertainment only, not financial/business/legal advice; no guaranteed outcomes; conduct your own due diligence and comply with applicable laws.
- Reselling locally is a bad option — always undersells vs. the online marketplace; the arbitrage depends on finding a large enough resale market (CollectPure) rather than a local pawn/coin shop.
- Purchase limits (5/day per membership, though a business membership can raise this) and inconsistent, hype-driven stock availability constrain how much volume any one person can move without multiple memberships/locations.
- Not all credit cards categorize Costco purchases as "groceries," so the 3% cashback leg of the stack depends on picking the right card.
- Because bars are worth thousands of dollars each, shipping/handling requires care (triple-packing, signature-on-delivery, documentation) — a real (if small) operational/loss risk if mishandled.
