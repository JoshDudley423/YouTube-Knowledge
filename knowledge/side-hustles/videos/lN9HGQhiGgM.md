# I Thought I Understood Credit Cards Until I Talked to This Guy
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lN9HGQhiGgM | Published: 20260928

Interview with Brandon Elliott (Credit Council Elite / Capital Bootcamp), who teaches using 0%-interest business/personal credit cards as a funding source for real estate, business capital, and vehicles. On-screen disclaimer: "aggressive" method, talk to a CPA before applying for anything; educational/entertainment only, not financial advice.

## The business idea
- Not a business to start so much as a **funding technique** other side hustles/real-estate deals in this knowledge base can use: get approved for a stack of 0%-interest credit cards, then convert the credit limits into cash via a "balance transfer" or "cash advance" call to the bank (flat 1-5% one-time fee), invest the cash, and pay it off before the 0% promo period (commonly 18 months) ends.
- Separately, Brandon runs a "trade line" side business: sell spare authorized-user slots on your own long-standing, well-maintained credit cards to strangers online for $125-2,000/slot, which boosts the buyer's credit score (total accounts + average account age) without giving them any account access.

## Numbers
- A documented case: one client got 13 credit cards approved in 10 days via a bank relationship manager (bypassing normal 2-cards/30-days and 5-cards/24-months rules), yielding $150,000 in 0% funding plus $15,600 in stacked signup bonuses (doubled bonuses from the relationship manager) — potential 7-21x return if redeemed for travel instead of cash.
- Total addressable 0% funding cited: "up to $500,000," repeatable every 6 months per person, sourced roughly 40-50% from ~12 big banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citi, Capital One), the rest split between regional banks and small local credit unions.
- Big-bank no-documentation thresholds cited: Chase up to $150,000, US Bank up to ~$80,000, Bank of America up to ~$50,000 (varies with relationship history) — above these thresholds, banks require financials/bank statements.
- Chase-specific rules: "2 and 30" (2 cards approved in 30 days → auto-deny a 3rd) and "5 and 24" (5+ cards approved across any bank in 24 months → auto-deny); a bank relationship manager can override these.
- Liquidation ("balance transfer"/cash-advance) fees: typically 1-5% flat, one-time, to move a credit limit into a checking account as cash — common current rate cited as ~3%, rising toward 5% at some banks recently.
- Auto business loans: up to 4 loans of $50,000 each available from some banks (e.g. Bank of America) via a brand-new business account after ~2 weeks of activity, with minimal documentation.
- Credit-score mechanics: FICO is roughly 55% "build" factors most people ignore — total accounts (want 21+, across 6 product types: personal loans, auto loans, mortgages, student loans, retail cards, regular cards, 10% of score), average account age (want 9+ years, 15% of score), utilization (stay under 9%, 30% of score) — vs. 45% "fix" factors (late payments, derogatory marks, hard inquiries).
- Trade-line economics: sellers historically got ~$125-200 per 60-day authorized-user slot sold directly, or ~$2,000 when sold through a trade-line marketplace middleman; a card can support 5-10 simultaneous authorized-user slots, rotated roughly every 2 months.
- Credit repair examples cited: some derogatory items removed in as little as 24 hours; a legitimate bankruptcy case took 12 months to resolve. ~70% of Americans reportedly have at least one inaccuracy on their credit report.

## Process / steps
- Funding-source priority order: mortgages first (if buying real estate), then credit cards (for working capital), then lines of credit/loans, then auto loans last (easiest to get, so save it for when other financing is used up).
- Get pre-approved (e.g. via a Bank of America business account) before negotiating with an auto dealer — pre-approval changes the negotiating dynamic to a cash-equivalent purchase. Tactical dealer tips: buy on the last day of the month/quarter when the finance department is hungriest to hit quota; target the salesperson second-from-the-bottom on the internal leaderboard (motivated but not already leaving).
- Liquidating a credit limit into cash: call the bank, ask for a "balance transfer" or "cash advance" at a **promotional 0% rate** specifically (not a same-purpose payoff of another card, which is taxed at high "mafia rate" APRs) — request it be deposited to your checking account, not applied to another balance. Alternative liquidation platforms named: Plastiq, Bill Pay, Zil Money, Melio.
- Entity setup: an LLC isn't strictly required (Brandon started as a sole proprietor using his SSN for the EIN in 2015) but is recommended once volume grows — 1-3 LLCs max is enough, avoid "aged entity" purchase scams (people reportedly charged $40-50K on false 20-million-dollar promises).
- Credit repair "backdoor method": pull your credit report, identify inaccurate/negative items, upload disputes directly to free accounts on TransUnion, Experian, and Equifax (plus mail a physical copy) with proof of identity/residency; feed the credit report to an AI tool and ask it to draft a formal dispute letter. Follow up by phone in 2-3 days; keep certified-mail tracking in case a small-claims suit against a bureau becomes necessary (cited as cheap, ~$50-70 filing fee, and bureaus that get served often don't show up, resulting in an automatic win).
- Trade-line mechanics: as the seller, provide the buyer's full name, date of birth, and SSN as an authorized user on a card in good standing; the card ships to the seller's address; the buyer never gets account login access, so has no ability to spend on it. The credit-boosting benefit is on the buyer's total-accounts and average-age FICO factors, not utilization.

## Caveats / risks
- On-screen disclaimer flags this as an "aggressive" method requiring CPA consultation before applying for anything.
- Never spend on a 0%-promo card after liquidating it if any balance remains available — a single new transaction or subscription charge can trigger interest on the entire liquidated balance.
- As a trade-line seller, risk to the buyer (not the seller) if the authorized user defaults/declares bankruptcy on their own accounts elsewhere, since that could reflect on the seller's card if the buyer ever gets added the wrong way — properly structured (buyer never gets access), the seller bears no real risk and can remove a bad-acting buyer from the account at any time by calling the bank.
- Real estate/business use of leveraged 0% capital assumes the borrower can actually pay off principal within the 18-month promo window (via project cash flow, refinance-and-cash-out, or a CD/treasury-bill parking strategy) — not addressed is what happens if the underlying investment underperforms that timeline.
- It is explicitly stated to be legal, but the interviewer flags it as an aggressive/lesser-known strategy relative to a conservative Dave-Ramsey-style debt-free approach, which the guest says is still the right starting point for someone who can't already manage spending.
