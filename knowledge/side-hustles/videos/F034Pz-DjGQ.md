# The Most Underrated Way to Make $50K/Month
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F034Pz-DjGQ | Published: 2026-02-26

Interview with Matt and Joni of Sonoma Strong Hauling (junk removal, Sonoma County, CA). Matt started the business at 35 after prison/rehab for drug addiction, while working nights at Safeway for $15/hr.

## The business idea
- Junk removal / hauling service. Idea came from working Salvation Army donation trucks in rehab and fielding "if you can't take it, who can?" questions from people with unusable donations (pet hair, damage, etc.).
- Triple revenue stream from the same job: (1) charge the customer to haul the junk away, (2) resell usable items (furniture, etc.) on Marketplace, (3) scrap metal (copper, aluminum) found in loads.

## Numbers
- First job: underbid at $200 flat for a garage cleanout; ~$100 went to dump fees, netted very little but was the first job.
- First year (part-time, alongside the Safeway job): $87,000 revenue at ~70% profit margin (~$60K profit) — margin this high because there was no paid labor yet (wife helped for free).
- Current state (full-time, with employees): ~$50,000/month revenue, ~35% net margin (~$17.5K/month profit). Cost breakdown: payroll ~$15K/month, dump fees ~$4K/month, plus commercial insurance and credit card fees.
- Pricing today: $695–$995 per truck load, scaled by material type (heavier/nastier loads like hoarder houses priced higher; light stuff like cardboard/green waste priced lower).
- Biggest single jobs: two at $40,000 and one at $49,000, all hoarder-house cleanouts requiring ~50 loads each. One came from a Facebook ad, one from a realtor referral, one from a general referral.
- Equipment progression: beat-up pickup truck → pickup + utility trailer → pickup with wood-plank bed walls (charged $300/load) → bigger truck that could haul a dump trailer → NPR Isuzu dump trucks (same trucks used by 1-800-GOT-JUNK and Junk King) → added a box truck. Total team now: owners + 2-3 laborers, 1 marketing person (runs Facebook ads), 1 offshore VA (in the Philippines, scraping commercial email leads).

## Marketing process (built with zero ad budget/experience)
- **Candy dish tactic**: buy cheap glass dishes + candy (dollar store) and business cards (Vistaprint), personally drop them at every real estate office's front desk (learned this from Matt's father, an ex-car salesman). Refined from leaving dishes in every individual cubicle (wasteful) to just the front desk/staff room (self-selects interested realtors). Repeated at storage-unit facilities, apartment complexes, and senior living facilities.
- Systematic geographic coverage: break the target area into towns/zones and knock out a batch per day (e.g., ~10 offices/day per person); a determined push can cover an entire county's real estate offices within about a week.
- "Realtor stalking": friend every realtor met (in person, at open houses, via Facebook/Instagram/LinkedIn search) so they see ongoing job photos in their feed; grew to 2,000+ realtor Facebook friends, a large share of whom now use the business monthly.
- Posted daily in local Facebook groups (buy/sell, "free stuff," houses-for-rent groups) in the service area.
- Ordered yard signs (via "UZ Marketing," an online sign vendor) placed at busy intersections, near funeral homes, apartment complexes, storage units.
- Cross-referrals with adjacent trades: introduced themselves to movers, maid services, handymen to trade leads.
- Branding lesson: stopped posting plain before/after junk photos (didn't stop the scroll or build recall) and switched to showing their faces, uniforms, and a "family-owned business, started in 2018 after the fires" story — got far more engagement; started boosting those posts on Facebook.
- Started a YouTube channel documenting the business; even non-customer viewers (including competitors) searching for them improved local search visibility/SEO and generated direct referrals from other junk-removal operators who couldn't take a job.
- Did all marketing/SEO/website themselves early on (self-taught) specifically so they'd recognize what tactics worked (e.g., noticed site updates directly correlating with phone ringing) — argued this matters later for holding a hired marketing agency accountable.
- Business name ("Sonoma Strong Hauling") coincidentally helped local SEO by including the county name.

## Advice for someone starting this
- Show up looking professional from day one (uniform/branded shirt+hat) — early on Matt showed up in a plain t-shirt and couldn't charge as much / wasn't taken seriously.
- Have an actual price sheet rather than pricing off the cuff.
- Build rapport before quoting price: compliment the house/dog/etc., make small talk, then give the number.
- Grow reinvestment based on what the market/demand actually supports, not by pre-buying a nice truck/trailer on debt before the business justifies it (common failure mode cited: buying gear on payments after seeing a viral "$1,000 in two hours" video, without having the business volume to support payments).
- Just start — don't over-research waiting for a "perfect" launch.
- Revenue diversification: also hosted a "junk expo" with speakers for other operators/viewers as a side revenue/community-building activity.

## Caveats
- Growth here was deliberately slow/bootstrapped (no ad spend for a long time); Matt frames this as a good thing but it did mean it took years of nights/weekends part-time work before going full-time.
- The couch-hoarding revenue trick still requires effort (listing, showing, selling secondhand items) beyond just hauling.
- Liability/insurance and dump fees are real recurring costs baked into the 35% margin figure.
