# The Most Overlooked Online Side Hustle Of 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DlNWYzaL_F0 | Published: 2026-08-31

Interview with Mike Stewart (30-year music/radio veteran, runs localinternetpresence.com), who sells "SER term jingles" (custom earworm jingles built around a business's unique searchable brand phrase) plus broader local-marketing services to small home-service businesses (plumbers, pest control, roofers, HVAC, etc.).

## Core business idea: SER-term jingles run as skippable YouTube pre-roll ads
- "SER" = Search Engine Results (page). Core concept: identify a short, unique phrase (2-4 words) that a business can "own" the first page of Google for (e.g., "Pink Plumber," "West Texas Pest Patrol"), then write a 15-second jingle that repeats that phrase + a positioning statement in the first 5-8 seconds.
- Deployed as a **skippable YouTube pre-roll ad**, geo-targeted to a small radius (a single zip code or a ~1-20 mile radius around a city) — the strategy deliberately wants viewers to skip, because the SER phrase plays in the unskippable first few seconds, so it's essentially free repeated brand exposure. Cited as "the cheapest local TV advertising in the world."
- Concrete example/case study: West Texas Pest Patrol (Abilene, TX) ran this for ~4 years, generated ~8 million ad "skips" (impressions) plus 3 million full views, for "pennies on the dollar" — quoted rate of thumb: ~40,000 listens/views for a couple hundred dollars. Business reportedly tripled/quadrupled from this.
- Psychological basis: repeated exposure to marketing language set to melody embeds subconsciously — claim that by the "21st" hearing, an earworm jingle is permanently retained (unsourced but stated as the working theory).
- Historical pricing: sold the same 15-second jingle to 100+ pest control business owners at $2,000 each (same jingle reused across non-overlapping ~50-mile markets, since local market overlap doesn't matter for direct competitors far apart).
- Modern (AI-era) production process: no longer needs a recording studio or hired singer — uses **Suno** to generate/write the jingle music and **ElevenLabs** to voice-clone the business owner's own voice reading ad copy, mixed together in about 15 minutes total. Can cheaply generate market-specific jingle variants (swap in different city names) using the same prompt.
- Recommended jingle length: 15 seconds; recommended max full-ad length: under 60 seconds (30-60s typical).
- Reported ad watch-through/attention stats: 30-70% of viewers watch the whole ad despite it being skippable (up to 60% in some cases cited).

## Broader service package sold (called "Local Internet Presence")
- Five components pitched as differentiators vs. typical "digital marketing agencies":
  1. **Google Business Profile (GMB) optimization** — most small businesses neglect it (no review responses, no photos/video); getting more 5-star reviews is framed as the only real lever against bad reviews (can't remove them, must "drown them out").
  2. **Content marketing via blog + podcast** (not just social media) — argument: content on a domain the business owns gets indexed by Google and isn't subject to a platform's rules/algorithm changes, unlike Facebook/Instagram/TikTok posts (a "walled garden" that "doesn't live forever"). Workaround for business owners who "don't know how to write": have them talk/record answers to prompted questions (e.g., "what are subterranean termites and why are they bad?") and transcribe it into both a podcast episode and a blog post — cites the "it's a shorter distance from your brain to your tongue than to your hands" framing, credits Gary Vaynerchuk's daily-blogging-then-pillar/micro-content model as informing this. Concrete case study: ran a long-running niche-focused pest control podcast/coaching program with a partner that generated "millions of dollars" in recurring digital marketing customers over years, purely from a subscriber base of pest control company owners.
  3. **Paid advertising to a high-converting landing page** — most small business "hero image" websites (professional photography, minimal text, marketing/proof "under the fold") have ~90% bounce rates and don't convert; recommends the classic direct-response formula instead: Headline → Video (a "Video Sales Letter," a format he says he co-invented ~20 years ago with Jim Edwards, drawing on Dan Kennedy's direct-response teaching) → Social proof/testimonials → Call to action, kept to roughly 45 seconds. A single reusable headline template worked repeatedly across ~100 client sites: "We will give you the most [X] service guaranteed, or it's free" (plug-and-play across trades: pest control, HVAC, roofing, etc.) — customers almost never actually invoke the "or it's free" guarantee, but it lowers perceived risk enough to drive calls.
  4. **The SER-term jingle** (above).
  5. **Networking** — described as the actual bottleneck/growth lever for the whole service model: in-person one-on-one meetings with local business owners to explain (in plain language) why this matters, since these owners "don't know what they don't know" and are otherwise skeptical of digital marketing pitches.

## Concrete case studies with numbers
- **Nashville Party Boat Rental**: found a boat captain whose employer's business was shutting down; identified "party boat rental" as the dominant local search term (Nashville is a top US bachelorette-party destination), bought the matching domain, built a one-page website with a single call-to-action ("Book your boat"), used ChatGPT (with the client's cloned voice via ElevenLabs) to generate SEO articles, wrote a jingle. Result: went from 40 organic-only bookings ("charters") the prior year to an estimated $80,000 in bookings that summer, after eventually convincing the owner to spend $250/month on ads (geo-targeted by city only, not by demographic — "just geography," letting Google's algorithm handle targeting).
- **Local Nashville Honey** (a side project for his wife's retail junk/collectibles booth that also resells local honey): built on the same playbook (one-page site, blog post, jingle) purely to drive foot traffic to a physical booth, not e-commerce; described as having its "biggest honey months ever" since.
- General framing: "300 customers is a million-dollar business" at his historical pricing model — flat $500/month per client (fixing GMB, one podcast + one blog post per week, Google Ads setup) plus a one-time $2,000 jingle fee, requiring roughly 1 hour/month of his own time per client once set up on autopilot (he intentionally capped himself at ~15 clients so he could keep other side income streams and lifestyle flexibility).

## AI angle
- States that everything in his service (jingle composition, ad script writing, blog content, dubbing into other languages via ElevenLabs) is now AI-assisted/AI-automatable, making a previously skill-gated, unscalable service (needed a recording studio + Nashville session singers + hand-written copy) scalable for the first time. Trains clients to generate their own blog drafts via ChatGPT/Gemini (free) and send them back to him for the final production step.
- Runs a paid community (playmakersai.com) teaching AI implementation/vibe-coding/service pricing for small-business marketing; mentions successfully mentoring at least one other person (a full-time business built from 2 Zoom calls) to replicate this model in another market.

## Caveats
- Networking (in-person, repetitive one-on-one selling) is explicitly named as the actual constraint on scaling this business — production itself (jingles, content, ad copy) is now largely outsourceable/automatable, but finding and converting local business-owner customers still requires manual relationship-building.
- Jingle/ad tactic depends on geo-targeted small-radius ad buys — the model assumes a hyper-local service business (won't work the same way for a national or purely online brand).
- Several claims (8M skips in Abilene, "quadrupled his business," honey shop's "biggest months ever," Nashville Party Boat's $80K summer) are anecdotal, self-reported by the guest/his own clients, without independent verification.
- Video is sponsored in part by Beehiiv (newsletter platform) and includes a domain-registrar affiliate promotion (get.online) — those endorsements should be read as sponsor content.
