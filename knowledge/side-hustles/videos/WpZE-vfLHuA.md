# I Asked 5 AI Tools to Make Me Rich. Then THIS Happened…
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WpZE-vfLHuA | Published: 2025-08-10

This is an AI-tool comparison/stunt video (ChatGPT-5 vs Claude Opus 4.1 vs Grok 4 vs Gemini 2.5 Pro), but it surfaces several concrete side-hustle/consulting niche ideas and a real-money options-trading experiment with numbers.

## Experiment 1: "$100/hour consultant in 72 hours, from scratch, $500 budget, no experience" prompt
Same prompt given to all four models; each proposed a different niche/business idea:
- **ChatGPT-5's idea:** create and sell custom GPTs/chatbots for small local businesses (realtors, therapists, coffee shops). Host is skeptical small businesses actually need this (compares it to the "every business needs its own app" hype from ~15 years ago that mostly didn't pan out). Scored lowest overall (2/5 quality-of-idea likelihood to work) — only 625 words of output, seen as too light/generic.
- **Grok 4's idea:** digital marketing/paid-ads consultant for local service businesses (Facebook/Google ads setup). Concrete plan included:
  - Skill crash course: Google Skillshop certification, Facebook Blueprint courses, free YouTube tutorials.
  - Client acquisition: build a target list via Google Maps, door-to-door outreach with a provided script, track progress in a Google Sheet.
  - Pricing tiers suggested: $500 starter package, $2,000 "transformation" package, $1,000/month retainer.
  - Cited stat: ~200 outreach touches yield 1-2 client wins.
  - Outreach doesn't start until day 3 of the 3-day plan. Scored 13/20 in host's grading.
- **Gemini 2.5 Pro's idea:** "no-code automation and productivity consultant for service-based SMBs" (essentially a Zapier/automation agency). Concrete tactics:
  - "Trojan horse" / foot-in-the-door strategy: don't pitch a full operational overhaul; offer one small, high-impact automation (e.g., auto-transferring website form leads into a CRM/spreadsheet) as a low-friction initial project to get in the door.
  - Recommended tools to learn: Zapier, Airtable.
  - Upwork positioning guidance: avoid technical titles like "Zapier developer" — use value-oriented titles like "productivity consultant: I automate repetitive tasks for service-based businesses." Lead with pain points, not tools.
  - Also recommended LinkedIn outreach and community/forum engagement as acquisition channels.
  - Scored 15/20 (2nd place) — very thorough (4,531 words) but criticized as dense/academic, "reads like a white paper."
- **Claude Opus 4.1's idea (host's overall winner, 16/20):** "AI implementation consultant for local service businesses" (framed around home-service industries — HVAC, plumbing, contractors), pitched with the line "I help contractors work on their business, not in their business."
  - Rationale given: home services are an underserved, high-adoption-potential niche for AI; a pest-control/blue-collar background (host's stated backstory in the prompt) gives instant credibility; local service businesses already pay $100-200/hr for basic consultants, justifying the $100/hr rate.
  - Day 1 (hours 1-12): learn core AI tools (Zapier, Voiceflow/Botpress for chatbots, Cal.com for AI scheduling) via free trials/YouTube; research industry-specific software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro) and join HVAC-owner Facebook groups; build a demo "arsenal" using free tools; build authority via a simple Carrd.co website with 3 fictional-but-realistic case studies; set up a Google My Business profile as an AI consultant.
  - Also starts outreach on day 1 (unlike Grok's plan), which the host liked.
  - Four "credibility shortcuts" given: (1) Beta tester play — offer 3 businesses free setup in exchange for video testimonials; (2) Association angle — offer to speak at a local trade association meeting (e.g., PHCC — plumbing/heating/cooling contractors) about AI; (3) Case study hack — document all work, even unpaid, as case studies; (4) Partner power move — partner with one established contractor as a flagship/showcase client.
  - Red flags to avoid per Claude: don't overpromise, don't talk tech (talk time/money saved instead), don't chase residential contractors initially, don't negotiate hourly rate into package deals.
  - Day 1 success metric: 3 working demos, live website, 10+ contractor connections. Day 2: 30+ outreach messages, 10+ businesses engaged, 5+ demos scheduled. Day 3: 3 demos delivered, first paying client secured, pipeline of 10+ prospects.

## Experiment 2: "$10,000 net in 7 days, $200 budget, no audience" prompt (ChatGPT-5 only, brief second test)
- ChatGPT-5's suggested money paths: flipping high-value items (Craigslist/Facebook Marketplace), rapid freelancing, local service gigs (decluttering/cleanup), affiliate promotion, pet sitting/dog walking. Host found this response fast but shallow/generic compared to its earlier answer.

## Experiment 3: Real-money options trading test ($1,057 total invested, live)
- Prompted Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, and ChatGPT-5 (not Claude — no real-time data access) to each recommend a single options trade (calls/puts on liquid tickers: SPY, QQQ, AAPL, TSLA) expiring the next day, capped near $500-700 per trade, executed live on Robinhood.
- Trades placed: Gemini recommended a QQQ put at $565 strike (cost ~$475 for 5 contracts) — this trade LOST $440. Grok recommended an AAPL call at $217.50 strike (cost $325) — this trade WON, sold for $596, profit of $271. ChatGPT-5 recommended a QQQ call at $567 strike (cost $277) — this trade WON, sold for $633, profit of $356 (wait, transcript shows QQQ call profit as part of net figures below).
- Net result after closing all three positions the next day: total invested $1,057, ended with $1,244 → net profit of $187, a 17.69% day-over-day return.
- Notable: because Gemini and ChatGPT-5 gave opposite directional calls on the same ticker (QQQ) — one bullish, one bearish — the host effectively hedged both sides of the same underlying asset. Had he only followed Grok's and ChatGPT's picks (skipping Gemini's losing QQQ put), he calculates he would have made $627 profit — a 60% day-over-day return instead of 17.69%.
- Explicit caveat from the host: "sample size of one day, results not guaranteed... this isn't investment advice." Presented as a stunt/entertainment demonstration, not a repeatable income strategy — no claim that AI-driven day-trading of options is a viable recurring side hustle.

2. Gemini 2.5 Pro — 15/20 (highest quality/detail but too dense/slow, burned through free usage quota quickly)

## General takeaways/caveats
- Different AI models can produce meaningfully different business-idea recommendations from the identical prompt/inputs — worth cross-checking multiple models rather than trusting one.
- A well-crafted prompt should explicitly ask the AI to first ask clarifying questions before generating the plan, to get a more tailored output.
- The specific niches proposed (AI/automation consulting for local service businesses, ads management for local businesses) are framed as low-barrier-to-entry, high-demand opportunities because local service businesses are underserved on AI adoption and already used to paying $100-200/hr for consultants.
- Skepticism flagged about the "sell custom GPTs to small businesses" idea specifically — host doubts most small businesses (e.g., coffee shops) actually need or will pay for a dedicated GPT, drawing a parallel to overhyped "every business needs its own app" predictions from ~15 years ago that mostly didn't materialize.
