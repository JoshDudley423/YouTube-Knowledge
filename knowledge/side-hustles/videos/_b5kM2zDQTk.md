# The Best Way to Clone These $1M+ Online Businesses (Legally)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_b5kM2zDQTk | Published: 2025-10-07

- Business idea: build a white-label SaaS/software-reseller business on top of **GoHighLevel (HighLevel)** — resell HighLevel's existing tools (CRM, SMS/text automation, funnels, AI voice agents, appointment booking, form builders, etc.) rebranded as your own product, charging monthly fees, without needing to code anything yourself.
- Core mechanism: HighLevel runs an annual "SaaSpreneur Awards" program that publicly lists companies by how many "sub accounts" (paying customers) they've built on top of HighLevel — Gold = 100+ customers, Platinum = 500+ customers, Diamond = 1,000+ customers. Nearly all these businesses charge customers $50–$5,000/month per customer, while paying HighLevel itself only $200–500/month for the white-label privilege — meaning near-zero marginal cost per additional customer.
- Method for reverse-engineering any of these award-listed businesses (repeatable playbook):
  1. Google the company name to see their website, positioning, and pricing.
  2. Check the **Meta (Facebook) Ads Library** for the company/brand name to see if/how they run paid ads, how many active ads, and how long ads have been running (an ad running 12+ months = strong signal it's a high-converting "winner" ad worth studying/copying the concept of).
  3. Check the **Wayback Machine** (web.archive.org) for the company's domain to see their original site/pricing/positioning from launch, to understand how their offer evolved (useful for deciding what to imitate vs. what was just an early experiment).
  4. Ask ChatGPT to research how the company grew/found customers (e.g. "tell me everything you know about how [Company] grew and found customers").
- Concrete examples reverse-engineered in the video:
  - "Astro Blaster" (SMS/automation platform for real estate agents): Platinum tier (500+ customers), charges $1,000 upfront + $100/month → at minimum $50,000/month recurring plus $500k+ in upfront fees historically, on top of near-zero marginal costs. Founded by someone with a wholesaling audience; growth included a "done-for-you" model (they scrape/provide data alongside the software to remove friction) and an affiliate program. Pricing has stayed flat since launch (evidence pricing found the "sweet spot" — don't underprice when copying).
  - "Event Rental Systems" (booking software for bounce-house/mobile-kitchen/catering rental businesses): Platinum tier, ~$150–400/month tiered pricing, est. $100k/month at 500 customers and $200 average. Uses Google Ads and Meta ads (one static image ad had run 14 months — rare and a strong signal it converts well). In business 15–16 years, migrated from custom software to white-labeled HighLevel. Offers a guarantee: refund $100 or donate $150 to charity of choice if unsatisfied after 90 days — flagged as a consistently high-converting offer type regardless of industry.
  - "Pipeline Pro" (Diamond tier, 1,000+ customers): uses a lifetime-price model instead of monthly ($67 lifetime access, up from an original $35, plus a ~$13 upsell PDF at ~100% margin) — rationale given: since white-label software has a flat monthly cost to the reseller regardless of customer count, a one-time lifetime fee can differentiate you from competitors all charging $100s/month, and is increasingly viable as AI/vibe-coding lowers software-building costs generally.
  - Other named examples: REI Reply (real estate automation, poor website but large customer base), Sierra M Voice (AI calling/voice agent feature), FG Funnels (funnel builder, 1,000+ customers at $129/month = 6-7 figures/year).
- Reverse-engineering angle for businesses founded by an influencer/creator with an audience: you don't need your own audience — find creators who *don't* already know about HighLevel/white-labeling, build the same tool, and pitch a revenue-share partnership (you handle software/fulfillment/customer service, they just promote it to their audience; example split proposed: 5–10% of their audience converts at ~$100/month + $1,000 upfront, split revenue). Best way to reach such creators: DM them on platforms they're not actively posting on (e.g., Strava, personal Facebook, X) where their inbox isn't flooded, rather than their main platform.
- Extensive list of research/spy tools mentioned for reverse-engineering any business (not HighLevel-specific):
  - Tech stack detection: BuiltWith (Chrome extension free; full scraping ~$450/month — or hire someone on Upwork/Fiverr who already has a BuiltWith Pro account, e.g. pay ~$20/spreadsheet), Wappalyzer (cheaper alternative), CRFT Lookup, Stack Crawler.
  - Traffic: SimilarWeb.
  - Database/stack details: WhatRuns, NerdyData.
  - Market intelligence/company reports: IbisWorld ($3,000 reports — get cheaper/free via "Search Funder," roughly $100 flat for access to many reports), Dealroom (cheaper Crunchbase alternative), CB Insights, PitchBook (both expensive; can rent access via Fiverr/Upwork).
  - Competitor discovery: Owler, AlphaSense, Data Briar.
  - Franchise financials: search "[State] franchise search" (e.g. "WI franchise search" for Wisconsin) — some states legally require public disclosure of franchisor financials.
  - App revenue estimates: Sensor Tower (free tier shows app download/revenue estimates).
  - Product feature gaps: G2 reviews fed into ChatGPT ("what features is this product missing that I can copy"), Bombora.
  - Website change tracking (e.g. competitor pricing changes): Distill, Visual Ping, Change Tower.
  - Ad spying beyond Meta Ads Library: AdEspresso, Big Spy, Social Petta.
  - Also: read a company's job listings for hints at new products/initiatives in the works.
- Explicit ethical framing: the host repeatedly says to copy the business model/pricing/positioning but not to infringe trademarks, IP, or trade secrets, and argues that competitors copying a niche (his cited examples: porch-pumpkin decorating business, appliance rental) tends to grow overall market awareness and can even help the original business rather than hurt it.
- Caveat: figures for competitors' revenue (e.g. "$50,000/month," "$100,000/month") are estimates derived from public pricing and customer-count tiers, not confirmed financials — host explicitly frames the video as educational/inspirational, not verified data, after a previous version of the video was removed by YouTube and had to be re-posted with a disclaimer.
