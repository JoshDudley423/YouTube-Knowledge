# I Tried to Make $10K in 10 Days With Costco Returns
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kEV86fy-Rvc | Published: 2026-08-12 (video appears to actually cover a challenge conducted earlier; upload date as given in metadata)

Chris Koerner + Shannon Jean (reselling mastermind operator) buy liquidation pallets/lots via B-Stock's Costco auction channel and attempt to flip them for profit in 10 days, reselling primarily on Facebook Marketplace.

## The business idea
- Buy customer-return/liquidation lots of retail merchandise (originally sold at Costco, Target, etc.) at steep discounts through B-Stock's auction platform (bstock.com, specifically bstock.com/costco), then resell individual items locally via Facebook Marketplace (or live-sell via Whatnot) at a large markup vs. retail.
- Works out of a home garage or a small storage unit — no warehouse needed. "More fortunes are started in the American garage than anything."

## Numbers
- Bought a lot of ~12 LG appliance units (washers/dryers/wash towers/garment steamers, mostly "used good" = customer returns) for $243/unit including freight; comparable new retail: $1,000-$2,500+ per unit at Lowe's (e.g., wash tower retailing $1,300-$2,400, sold new for $879 in the flip).
- Bought a 12-unit outdoor/patio lot (gazebo, patio furniture, Blackstone griddles, sheds) for ~$222/unit including freight; retail values cited: $3,400 gazebo, $2,600 gazebo, $1,800-$2,400 patio sets, $1,200 sheds, $400 Blackstone griddles (sold "as-is/needs repair" around $250).
- Total spend across both lots: roughly $5,600.
- Total challenge result (10-day goal): took 12.5 days total to sell through inventory (missed the 10-day goal), still called "a huge success" — exact final revenue/profit figure not stated numerically in the transcript beyond noting profit made.
- Example listing prices used: LG stackable washer (retail $1,300) listed at $879; a scratch-and-dent black garment steamer (retail $1,300) initially listed at $884/$917, ultimately sold around $850 with free delivery; Thomasville patio set (retail ~$1,000) listed at $767.

## Process / steps
- Platform: B-Stock auction site, specifically its Costco-manifest channel — lets you filter by category (appliances, patio/home & garden, electronics, apparel, food, etc.) and see number of bids, average per-unit price, and how many units are in a lot.
- Condition grades on B-Stock: New (never sold) > Like New (may have showroom stickers) > Used Good (customer returns, often nearly unused — sweet spot recommended by the expert for best profit-to-risk) > Used Fair (avoid — has real issues) > Mixed/Salvage (avoid — highly variable, could be mostly junk).
- Before bidding: cross-check the Costco manifest price against real-world resale comps (Lowe's/Home Depot listing price, Facebook Marketplace "sold" search, local market search) to estimate a floor resale value; target paying roughly $200-250/unit or less to leave a wide margin/safety net (aim for at least 2x return as a conservative floor, more realistically 3-4x).
- Payment options on B-Stock: credit card, direct ACH, or apply for Net-30 terms.
- Look for "barrier to entry" categories with less bidding competition: bulky/heavy items (appliances, patio furniture) get fewer bids because delivery logistics deter casual bidders — that barrier is the opportunity. Avoid categories like computers/electronics (recommended against) and women's apparel (brand-dependent).
- Look for lots where the manifest photos show items still in original box/factory packaging/with tags — a positive signal of real condition.
- The "FLIP system" (acronym referenced but not spelled out in this episode) is Shannon Jean's process, covered more/its own episode.
- Logistics: don't need to own a truck — rent one (Home Depot ~$20) or pay a local delivery driver ~$50/run; delivery-included pricing is a selling point.
- Listing process per item: take a ~60-second walkaround video (helps Marketplace search ranking) plus use all available photos (12 on FB Marketplace) showing both positive and negative details (scuffs, missing parts) — transparency framed as a value/pricing justification, not a defect to hide (e.g., "priced aggressively because of the missing handle" converts better than downplaying it).
- Use ChatGPT to draft SEO-optimized Facebook Marketplace titles and descriptions (include model number, color, condition, "delivery available"); then add a personal, human sentence to the post (e.g., "I'm doing this to save for my kids' college") to build buyer trust/connection.
- Prioritize listing and selling first ("get momentum") over fixing/cleaning problem items first — sell the easy wins immediately for cash-flow/dopamine, deal with repairs (broken doors, missing parts) later or discount/liquidate them as-is. Explicit advice: "focus is overrated, momentum is underrated."
- Respond to Marketplace inquiries fast, with a call-to-action (e.g., "I'm available at 3pm, can I meet you then?"); use phone text-expander/templates for common questions; expect heavy "still available?" ghosting — treat it like an abandoned shopping cart, not personal.
- Negotiation tactics: use urgency ("I have other inquiries," "it has to be delivered today at X time") and small discounts (e.g., $20-35 off) to convert a hesitant-but-motivated buyer without a big price cut.
- If an item isn't getting inquiries: two levers are (1) pay for a Marketplace "boost" (mixed results depending on category/demand) or (2) cut the price — be willing to accept lower profit or even a loss on slow movers since storage/holding also costs money.
- Offer a return/service guarantee (14-30 days) even though not formally required — framed as a trust-building, community-reputation play ("I want to be the liquidation guy" people refer others to), not a full warranty.

## Tools / platforms named
- B-Stock (bstock.com, bstock.com/costco) — liquidation/returns auction marketplace.
- Facebook Marketplace — primary resale channel.
- ChatGPT — for writing listing titles/descriptions and diagnosing/repairing broken items (e.g., looking up how to reattach a dryer door).
- Shannon Jean's Reselling Mastermind (shannonjean.com/mastermind, ~2,200 members) — community/training for this business model.
- Whatnot — mentioned as a live-selling venue other resellers use for moving bulk lots (e.g., candy/energy drinks/liquid IV sold "$1,500 worth of stuff in an hour").

## Caveats / risks
- Not passive/hands-off: requires testing items (electrical function at minimum — water hookups not tested), cleaning, sourcing replacement parts (e.g., an Amazon-ordered Blackstone handle), and physically loading/unloading heavy items.
- "Used" lots are a mixed bag by nature — expect at least some units with real defects (missing parts, broken doors) even in Used Good lots; this is explicitly framed as "a problem-solving business."
- Missed the stated 10-day goal (took 12.5 days) — attributed to the presenter's own procrastination in listing/responding/cutting prices promptly, not a flaw in the model itself.
- A shed included in one lot did not sell during the challenge window.
- Requires enough capital to buy a lot upfront ($2,600+ in this case) and space to store and stage items (garage or ~$130/month storage unit cited as sufficient).
- No sales tax was noted as a side benefit specific to their state (Texas) — tax treatment will vary by location and is not general advice.
