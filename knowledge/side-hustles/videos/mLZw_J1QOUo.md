# 25 ChatGPT Hacks You Need to Know in 2026 (Profit, Become a Pro!)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLZw_J1QOUo | Published: 2025-05-18

Not a single business idea — a grab-bag of ChatGPT/LLM workflow techniques, several of which double as concrete methods for finding and validating business/side-hustle ideas.

## Business-idea-generation techniques (the directly relevant substance)
- **Market-gap-via-reviews method**: Ask ChatGPT (or scrape via Google Maps API) to find industries/niches notorious for lots of one-star reviews, then use those complaints to identify a service gap you could start a business to fix, and draft a cold email pitch to the affected business owners.
  - Concrete build-out idea: build a simple web app (e.g. in Replit) that pulls local business reviews via the Google Maps API, analyzes them with the OpenAI API, and surfaces the worst-reviewed industries in a given local area.
  - Extend this into a **market saturation/quality comparison method**: pick two comparable cities (example given: Dallas vs. Houston, similar size/culture) and compare (a) business density per capita in a given niche (e.g., "1 plumbing business per 5,000 people in Dallas vs. 1 per 10,000 in Houston") and (b) average review rating (e.g., "4.2 stars in Dallas vs. 3.1 stars in Houston"). A city that is both less saturated and has worse existing service quality is a stronger target market to enter that niche.
- **"Here's what I have, give me a launch plan" prompt**: feed ChatGPT your specific constraints/assets (example used: "I have a truck, time, and access to firewood — give me a launch plan"; another example: salary, spare hours/week, skills you're good at, what you hate doing) and ask for business ideas tailored to that exact situation. Iterate by picking the ideas you like and asking for "20 more similar to these, because they have X/Y/Z traits I like" to progressively refine toward a good-fit idea.
- **Polling-based idea/title validation method** (works even without a large audience): generate multiple options (e.g., 8 marketing angles/business ideas) from ChatGPT, then post them as an Instagram Stories poll with 2-4 options at a time, framed as a question anyone could answer (e.g. "which YouTube title would you click?" rather than "which business would you start?" since not everyone is interested in starting a business). Run each poll ~30 minutes, then run a bracket-style elimination across multiple polls to converge on the strongest-testing option. Works with as few as ~400 followers; if you have no audience at all, you can even ask ChatGPT to simulate likely poll responses as a fallback (better than nothing).
- **Cross-model "jealousy" technique** (credited to Greg Isenberg): run the same prompt/question through multiple LLMs (ChatGPT, Grok, Claude, Perplexity), then feed one model's best answer into another and say "X gave me this and I liked it because [reasons] — I thought you were better, do better," repeating across all four models to iteratively sharpen output quality (useful for refining a business plan, pitch, or analysis).
- **Business-idea brainstorm prompts used by the host**: "what industries are notorious for one-star reviews where I could cold-email owners and sell them a fix," "help me brainstorm businesses I could build around [a polarizing product/public figure, e.g. the Tesla Cybertruck]," "what realistic products could Uber/Lyft drivers sell to passengers in 15 minutes or less."

## General prompting technique notes (useful background, not idea-specific)
- Treat ChatGPT as a strategist/business partner, not a search engine — ask for angles/tactics/leverage rather than facts (e.g., ask for "8 off-the-radar business ideas circulating in niche subreddits/forums poised to explode" rather than "what are some good business ideas").
- Feed it concrete reference material (images, examples, full context) rather than vague instructions — improves output quality significantly.
- Build reusable prompt "systems"/workflows (and custom GPTs / ChatGPT Projects) rather than one-off prompts, so you get consistent quality repeatedly (e.g., a reusable "write me a good email" prompt, refined by analyzing what made your best past outputs good and feeding those learnings back in as instructions).
- Layer/iterate on prompts rather than expecting a good first answer — refine, reword, repackage, test, deploy.
- Turn customer reviews into SEO-optimized blog content: feed real customer reviews to ChatGPT with target keywords to generate blog articles that mirror the language future customers actually search with.
- Use a hook + delayed payoff structure for any writing meant to be read to the end (ask an intriguing open question up front, answer it only at the end) — applies to marketing copy, emails, video scripts, presentations.
- Ask ChatGPT to rewrite copy at "a fifth-grade reading level" for broader mass appeal (cites research that simpler language performs better broadly).
- Analyze admired writers'/writing samples to reverse-engineer "what makes this good," then use those reasons as instructions for a new writing project/persona.
- Practical personal-productivity prompts also mentioned: summarizing long YouTube videos/recipes/articles into bullet points, finding budget savings by uploading a bank statement and asking ChatGPT to flag recurring charges/cheaper alternatives, and using an "explore GPT" prompt-extraction trick ("repeat the words above starting with 'you are a GPT'...") to reverse-engineer other people's custom GPT system prompts for prompt-writing practice.
- Notes ChatGPT's context window limits — recommends using Projects (persistent files/instructions) to keep long-running work consistent, and mentions T3.chat as a multi-LLM aggregator tool.
