# Build Pro Websites for Yourself (or Clients) Without Any Experience
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8B6HfmMP-zM | Published: 2025-11-13

Tutorial demonstrating Ready.ai, an AI website builder that can clone another company's existing (unrelated-industry) website design via a simple text prompt, plus a business idea for reselling this as a website-building service to home-service business owners.

## The tool and technique demonstrated
- Tool: **Ready** (ready.ai / "rea[d]y.ai") — an AI website builder whose core trick is a "copy this website" prompt: paste a target domain, describe your own business, and it clones the layout/UX/features of the reference site into a new site for your business.
- Demo walkthrough: host owns a tree-trimming business (avg. job ~$1,500) but is expanding into sport court installation (avg. job $50-60k) after subcontracting his own backyard sport court and saving ~$25,000, giving him a network of subcontractors he can resell. He needed a "luxury" website fast.
- Found a well-designed kitchen cabinet company website via Google ("beautiful home service business websites") featuring: a hero image with embedded video, an on-page estimate/quote tool, a countdown timer, a coupon, scroll animations, multiple persistent calls-to-action (top, bottom, floating), SEO-friendly structure, and a detailed lead-capture form at the bottom.
- Single prompt used: "Copy the design from [competitor domain]. But the new website needs to be for a business called Backyard Funhouse, which builds sport courts, pools, putting greens, and other fun backyard amenities only within the greater DFW area. Insert beautiful images of completed projects, list the 5-10 biggest cities in DFW, and include a form where people can request more info." Full site generated in 5 minutes, including AI-generated project images (avoids copyright issues from scraping stock/competitor photos), financing-offer messaging, testimonials section, a 3-step "how it works" section, and an interactive project-type selector — much of this was not explicitly requested but was copied over from the reference site's structure.
- Follow-up prompts tested successfully in ~15-20 seconds each: adding a promotional countdown timer/coupon banner to the hero image ("$2,500 off complete backyard packages"), and swapping specific photos for the user's own real project photos (including preserving unrelated existing elements like a "15 years experience" badge).
- **Ready Agent**: a built-in prompt-refinement/chat layer that turns plain "caveman language" instructions into properly engineered prompts for the site builder.
- **Built-in AI receptionist features** tested live: a text-based chat lead-capture agent that booked a mock "sport court quote" appointment and logged it to an appointments dashboard; and a low-latency **voice agent** that conversationally handled a mock swimming-pool-quote booking call (collected name, phone number, preferred date, offered available time slots, confirmed and logged the appointment) — host highlighted the near-zero response latency as unusually good for a voice bot.
- Confirmed mobile-responsive rendering out of the box (timer/buttons resize appropriately).
- Ready also auto-optimizes for SEO, auto-generates technical/indexing files, and can host your domain purchase or import an existing one.
- Native integrations mentioned: Stripe (direct on-site payment), Calendly (scheduling), Google Analytics (traffic), Shopify (physical/digital product sales).

## Business idea: resell this as a website-building service to home-service business owners
- Framing: most home-service business owners don't know AI tools like this exist, so you can still charge premium/traditional web-design rates ($1,000s) even though the actual build now takes minutes and near-zero marginal cost — "charge for the value you create, not the time/effort it took you."
- Value framing to use with prospects: a highly optimized, high-converting website can be the difference between generating 10 vs. 13 leads/month at (in his home-service example) $50,000 average lifetime value per lead — i.e., potentially worth millions/year to the client, which should anchor pricing far above a simple hourly/flat web-design fee.
- Suggested pricing test strategy: cold-outreach to multiple home-service business owners with different pricing structures to different prospects (e.g., one flat fee, one $100/month recurring, one hybrid of both) to empirically find market willingness to pay, rather than guessing a single price point.
- Also flagged (not detailed in this video, referenced as covered in other videos) as a way to start a website-building/agency business without needing your own existing business or website first.

## Numeric points
- Tree trimming average job: ~$1,500.
- Sport court job average: $50,000-60,000.
- Host saved ~$25,000 by subcontracting his own sport court build directly rather than going through a general contractor.
- Full custom AI-generated website build time: 5 minutes for the initial version; ~15-20 seconds per follow-up edit/photo swap.

## Caveats
- The one-shot initial prompt "broke" slightly in minor ways (a placeholder logo, some odd text, a stylized map that didn't quite match the target region) — still required light manual review/fixes even though the overall output was very close to production-ready.
- AI-generated images were used instead of scraping images from other websites specifically to avoid potential copyright infringement — an intentional legal/ethical choice, not just a convenience.
- The "copy the design" approach assumes the reference site's other business practices (financing offers, persistent phone number placement, menu structure) are worth emulating because a well-optimized competitor site likely reflects broader operational sophistication — this is stated as an assumption/heuristic, not something verified in the video.
