# Polson, Montana RV Park Investment Webinar
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h0ocdaRmB4Q | Published: 2024-10-23

Investor webinar (not a general audience video) pitching a specific RV park acquisition deal to LPs. Useful as a worked example of how RV park syndication/investing works as a business model.

## The business/investment idea: syndicated RV park acquisition
- Chris Koerner's company (Blue Metric, via "co-founders") raises capital from limited partner (LP) investors to buy and operate RV parks/campgrounds, then improves and eventually sells or refinances them.
- This specific deal: a KOA-branded park ("KOA Holiday" tier, mid-tier of KOA's Journey/Holiday/Resort tiers) in Polson, Montana near Flathead Lake, plus an adjacent 10-pad "Motorcoach Resort" parcel with individually deeded lots.
- This would be the company's 23rd RV park acquisition; a second park in Montana (an existing park, "Moose Creek," is about 1.5 hours away and shares management to avoid adding a new on-site management layer).

## Deal economics (concrete numbers)
- Asking price $5.8M; negotiated purchase price $5.5M — roughly a 10 cap (cap rate).
- Deal includes 6 of 10 individually-deeded motorcoach pads (valued ~$150,000 each) transferred at close; rights (not obligation) to buy the remaining 4 post-close at ~$150,000 each, subject to a 90% supermajority HOA vote that could block the sale.
- Capital raise: $5.5M total, including funds needed for a planned 64-pad expansion.
- Investor terms: 10% preferred return (LPs get 100% of returns up to 10%); above 10% split 70/30 in investors' favor; above 15% split 60/40; above 20% split 50/50.
- Minimum investment $50,000, accredited investors only.
- Targeted 24.3% IRR; ~50% bonus depreciation expected to be taken in 2024 before that tax provision was set to be reduced starting Jan 1, 2025.
- Modeled exit at an 8% cap rate (vs. the 10 cap purchase), assuming exit happens bundled with other parks in the portfolio (bundling multiple parks drives the achievable cap rate down/valuation up).
- Sponsor/GP alignment: the company invests 10% of every deal as co-GP, at LP terms; Koerner personally invests a five-figure amount (not fixed, varies by deal and available capital).
- KOA franchise fee: 6% of topline revenue; the KOA license on this park expires in ~1.5 years (Feb 2026), giving the buyer optionality to drop the franchise if it's not adding value (contrasted with another of their parks, in Shreveport, where they did drop KOA after finding it didn't drive meaningful bookings; other parks in their portfolio keep KOA because it does help).

## Expansion plan and returns
- Expansion plan: add 64 new pads, using existing city water/sewer utilities (cheaper than parks requiring septic/well, which the host notes can be 2x+ more costly), plus adding cabins/tiny homes (no tiny homes in the original plan, but the company plans to add them based on strong performance of cabins/tiny homes at their nearby Moose Creek park).
- Estimated expansion cost: ~$12,000/pad (~$770,000 total for 64 pads) — flagged as a rough, unconfirmed estimate.
- Projected topline revenue increase from the expansion: ~$500,000/year; bottom-line (net) increase ~$400,000/year, assuming a similar expense ratio; expense ratio is projected to improve over time via economies of scale (currently modeled around 43% on day one).
- First-year occupancy assumption for the new pads: ~18-20% annually (as low as 5-10% in winter, 30-50% in summer) — described as conservative.
- Growth pattern in their financial model: large revenue/income jumps in years 1-2 (as new pads come online), then stabilizing to smaller (~$70,000/year) increases in years 3-4.
- Rationale for expanding even when not always fully booked: cited a "Parkinson's law" pattern from prior parks — adding more pad sites lets you lower per-pad pricing across the board and typically nets out ahead on revenue; cabins specifically are highlighted as outperforming standard pads at their other park.
- Nightly rate context: comparable RV pads in the area rent for $87-$119/night; short-term rental target range across their portfolio generally is $50-$150/night, with long-term rental as a fallback ("Plan B") if the economy weakens, trading profitability for stability.

## Track record / operational claims
- Never missed an LP distribution payment across their portfolio.
- Standard strategy: aim to return most/all LP capital via refinance within 2-3 years, but keep investors in the deal for a total hold of 5+ years; they had not yet needed to refinance any park due to current interest rates (favorable refi term sheets received but not used because it wasn't financially advantageous yet).
- ~5 prior pad-site/unit expansions completed successfully, with ~12 more underway across the portfolio at the time of recording.
- Cited one past issue: on their second-ever park expansion, the existing electrical service was under capacity, requiring an unplanned $20-30K fix — now they do more thorough electrical inspections upfront as a result.
- Management: parks are managed by their operating company "Blue Metric" (30-40 employees in Nashville); this specific park currently has 28 employees and no additional headcount is planned post-acquisition.

## Sourcing/negotiation color
- Deal originated in an unusual way: Koerner tweeted a casual, admittedly under-researched ranking naming this park the "4th most beautiful in the US" with no prior connection to it; the actual owners (whom he'd previously spoken with on other RV park matters) emailed him hours later, leading to this deal.
- Sellers wanted the deal kept quiet (not listed publicly, webinar recording marked private) because of the individually-deeded-lot complication — they feared the HOA-eligible lot owners might organize a supermajority vote to block the transfer if they found out too early.
- Reason the sellers are selling: their capital partner disliked having individually-deeded parcels within an otherwise uniform portfolio and wanted to standardize.

## Caveats / open risks acknowledged during Q&A
- Uncertainty at recording time whether the park sits on a Native American reservation (per Google Maps) — unresolved, but host noted other parks they've evaluated on reservations were "not a dealbreaker."
- Not fully clear which specific motorcoach sites/how much minority-ownership exposure comes with the 10-pad parcel; host admitted he didn't have full clarity on this himself.
- Airport near the park was initially a concern but was found via reviews to be low-traffic/small-plane only, deemed a non-issue.
- General expansion risk: unforeseen delays/costs are common (see electrical example above); mitigated by an experienced expansion contractor (30 years in RV park land development) but not eliminated.
- Demand risk for the expansion: nearby competing parks were reported full with waiting lists per the sponsor's own due diligence calls, and existing owners hadn't been running paid marketing (relying solely on KOA), which the sponsor sees as underexploited upside — but this is their own optimistic read, not guaranteed.
- This is an accredited-investor-only, minimum $50K commitment, illiquid, multi-year syndication — not a hands-on side hustle but a passive capital deployment vehicle.
