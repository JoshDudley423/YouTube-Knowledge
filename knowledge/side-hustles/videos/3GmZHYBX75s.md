# He Built This Business in One Weekend for $40
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3GmZHYBX75s | Published: 2026-07-27

Interview with Spencer, founder of Print Our Pet (printourpet.com) — a custom pet-portrait/gift business (pet's face printed on posters, framed portraits, canvases, digital files) built from a ~$40 initial investment into a seven-figure business, followed by a joint brainstorm on reviving stalled growth.

## The idea
- Custom pet portrait/gift e-commerce: customer uploads pet photos, an artist (or, for one newer SKU, an automated AI-assisted process) creates stylized artwork (e.g., Renaissance-style, "block style," minimalist line art, "Vogue" style), printed on posters, framed prints, canvases, or sold as digital files.
- Origin: pivoted from a failing pet bandanna/scarf manufacturing business after noticing a competitor's Renaissance-style pet portraits going viral in his Instagram feed; recognized print-on-demand (no inventory) made this instantly launchable.
- Highly giftable product: ~30-40% of sales are self-purchases, ~60% are gifts (new puppy, pet's birthday, memorializing a pet that passed away) — this gift-orientation is explicitly why the business over-indexes on November/December sales.

## Numbers
- Initial investment: ~$40 (a $30 Shopify subscription + incidental costs); built entirely via "University of YouTube" with zero coding or Photoshop experience — used Canva for design, sourced early photo needs via Fiverr/Upwork.
- Built and launched the entire website in one weekend; launched publicly November 2019.
- Month 1 revenue: ~$1,000, almost entirely friends/family, boosted somewhat by launching right at Black Friday/Cyber Monday.
- Month 2: started running Meta/Facebook ads → revenue climbed to $3,000-5,000.
- Month 3: added Etsy as a sales channel → revenue jumped to $10,000-20,000/month, sustained through month 10 (business is highly seasonal: 65-70% of annual revenue comes from November and December alone).
- Year 1 (first ~10 months at steady state, then holiday surge): ended just over $1,000,000 in revenue for the first full year; November-December 2020 alone generated over $800,000 in revenue in those two months.
- 2020 (second calendar year) was a 7-figure revenue year; by year-end 2020 the company had scaled to 80 non-full-time commission-paid remote artists (paid per completed artwork, primarily based in the Philippines) plus 3 full-time remote employees.
- Margins: overall gross margin ~60-70%; digital file products have very high margin (labor-only cost); physical framed portraits run 40-50% margin depending on size. Net/gross profit margin cited as ~20-30% (e.g., ~23% in a representative month) — translating to roughly $3,000-4,000 profit in a slow month, $8,000-9,000 in a good month, before the big 2020 scale-up.
- Manufacturing/fulfillment outsourced to third-party screen-printing/print-on-demand facilities at a flat blended rate covering fulfillment and shipping — accepted a lower margin than in-house production in exchange for zero inventory risk and no in-house operations burden.
- Current average order value (AOV): ~$90 on the direct Shopify store; ~$65 on Etsy (lower because Etsy sales skew toward cheaper digital files rather than framed physical portraits); a two-pet portrait runs ~$105. Redesigning the checkout flow (adding upsells: frame style upgrades marked up ~$2 profit margin per upgrade, e.g. an $8-cost red oak frame priced as a $10 upsell; a "skip the queue" paid priority option; premium hand-designed backgrounds) raised AOV by 20%.
- Email list: ~24,000 subscribers, actively pruned via inactivity/"flow" automations.
- Etsy ads: pay-per-click with no guaranteed spend — even with a $1,000/day budget set, actual daily spend often lands around $100-200/day outside peak season because Etsy controls allocation/targeting entirely (no granular targeting control); spend and conversion both surge dramatically in November-December.
- Facebook ads became "non-existent" for this business once Etsy scaled up, since Etsy ad economics (pay only for actual listing visits) outcompeted Facebook at their margin level.

### AI/automation product line detail
- Added a "Vogue" style SKU: fully automated (no artist labor) — an employee processes the uploaded pet photo into a raw print file in a couple of minutes at a cost of under $1, using the photo directly rather than commissioned artwork.
- Deliberately has NOT fully switched to AI-generated artwork for the core hand-drawn-style portraits, treating "hand-drawn by real people" as a market differentiator versus AI-only competitors — cites a specific practical limitation (AI struggles to make precise, surgical edits like "remove the bandana but keep everything else the same" without visibly degrading the rest of the image) as a functional reason alongside the brand-positioning reason.
- Notes rising competitive pressure from Etsy sellers offering pure AI-generated pet portraits as digital files for as little as $10, with near-100% margin since there's no artist labor cost at all.
- Uses AI image generation already for blog content; considering it for email marketing, customer support (chat/email), and influencer sourcing.

## Growth ideas discussed for reviving stalled sales (co-brainstormed with Koerner)
- **Comparable competitor benchmark cited**: "Custom Captain" (a friend's boat/car custom-artwork business, ~$10M/year) drives virtually all its business through Facebook ads.
- **Creative volume/freshness as the core Facebook-ads lever** (from Koerner's own e-commerce business, Texas Snacks): historically ran Google Ads only; Facebook ads were unprofitable in prior attempts (a $7 CAC for a $50 customer years ago) but became profitable again recently purely by dramatically increasing ad-creative volume and freshness — currently spending ~$500/day at a 10x ROAS, using zero AI-generated content and zero formal UGC creators; instead, employees film raw, casual product-demo content in-store/warehouse in varying lengths (10 seconds to 10 minutes), all spend gets shifted to whichever creative is currently performing, and creative gets rotated out every 1-2 weeks as performance decays — described as requiring low cash spend but high ongoing time/effort ("the harder way is the better way... you need more creative, you need to test more things"). Uses CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization) ad structure and lets Meta's algorithm handle targeting entirely rather than manually targeting by interest/age.
- **Competitive intelligence tactic**: mine the Meta Ads Library for a close competitor (mentioned: "Custom Willow," a near-identical competitor product) to see and copy/adapt their running ad creative and messaging.
- **Pricing/positioning test suggested**: directly A/B test customer preference between a human-drawn portrait at a higher price (~$90) versus an AI-generated equivalent at a lower price (~$65) to check the assumption that customers value "hand-drawn" enough to justify the premium, rather than assuming it based on internal brand philosophy.
- **AI tools named for potential use**: HeyGen (AI avatar video generator — used to mass-produce UGC-style video ad variations without hiring real people or creators, cited as something Koerner has personally been retargeted by on Instagram); Nano Banana (Google's image generation tool, described as very strong quality) for image generation tasks.
- Tangential business ideas mentioned by Koerner (not Print Our Pet's own plans, but discussed as adjacent applicable models): (1) a lead-gen side-business idea — pull home photos via Zillow/Redfin/Google Maps APIs, use Nano Banana to generate a photorealistic "improved landscaping" version of the actual house (not a cartoon/render), then sell the resulting leads to local landscapers; Koerner claims people who saw his prior video on this reported successfully launching it. (2) A similar model applied to real estate: AI-"stage" unstaged real-estate listing photos and pitch listing agents a low-cost ($1/photo) staging service, citing the (unverified, anecdotal) claim that staged homes sell for ~7% more and in ~23 days vs. 42 days on market.
- **Live-selling channels suggested as a new customer acquisition angle**: TikTok Live auctions (newly launched at time of recording — early-mover content is reportedly heavily boosted by TikTok's algorithm) and Whatnot (an established live-auction shopping platform, popular in collectibles like Pokémon cards). Cited example: a friend (Braden) sells protein bars (Built brand) live on Whatnot/TikTok Live, buying them at $0.75 and reselling at or above retail, with TikTok specifically yielding higher prices currently due to novelty/buyer excitement. Proposed application to Print Our Pet: live-auction physical or purely-digital ($5-10, AI-generated, no artist labor needed) pet portraits in common breed/style templates (e.g., "golden doodle," "Frenchie") as a low-friction way to both generate direct sales and drive traffic back to the main website.

## Caveats / risks
- Extreme revenue seasonality (65-70% of annual revenue concentrated in Nov-Dec) makes off-season cash flow and staffing planning difficult; the founder had to temporarily shut off the Etsy shop during a holiday order surge because artist capacity couldn't keep up.
- No natural repeat-purchase/subscription mechanic — it's a one-off gift-style purchase, not a consumable/recurring product, limiting customer LTV levers.
- Etsy's ad platform gives sellers very little control over targeting/spend pacing, capping how aggressively the business can scale that channel even with a larger allocated budget.
- At time of interview, the business had "proven demand" but lacked a reliable, evergreen (non-holiday-dependent) customer acquisition channel — founder explicitly stated he had not found a consistent way to drive traffic outside the holiday season, had not seriously tested influencer marketing, and had grown personally reluctant/uncomfortable promoting the business himself on social media ("I don't like selling to people").
- Rising AI-generated-art competition on Etsy (near-zero marginal cost sellers) is compressing pricing and margins in the category and creates brand-perception risk (customers sometimes wrongly accuse the business of secretly using AI when they don't like an artist's stylistic interpretation).
