# Craigslist is a Goldmine. Here's Where the Gold Is
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ-OSmDMsFI | Published: 2025-12-28

How to use web scraping (specifically Craigslist) plus AI tools to find arbitrage, leads, and business opportunities. Tool-focused walkthrough using Oxylabs AI Studio.

## Origin story / proof of concept (Chris's own past businesses)
- **iPhone unlock arbitrage (~17 years ago, in college)**: noticed a locked iPhone sold for ~$300 on Craigslist but unlocked ones sold for $350-$400, and eBay sellers offered unlock services for $5. Built a scraper to find every locked iPhone listed on Craigslist worldwide (by carrier: Verizon, T-Mobile, Sprint, AT&T), scraped seller emails, and auto-emailed/texted them an unlock offer. Ran two competing domains ($10unlocks.com and $20unlocks.com) as an A/B price test. Fully automated: customer entered their IMEI on checkout, the request was forwarded to a single eBay unlocker who was billed weekly at $5/unlock; unlocked within an hour with zero customer support needed. Made "hundreds of dollars a day in pure profit" until the hack eventually died out.
- **iPhone parts wholesale business**: scraped ~20,000 iPhone repair shops across the US (in 2012), then had virtual assistants cold call them as customers. Grew into a business worth over $10 million, sold in 2019.
- **Presto Cars**: scraped Manheim and Adesa car auction data the night before auctions to identify likely-mispriced vehicles based on historical sale results, then bought cars for clients.
- **Zillow FSBO scraper**: scraped daily "For Sale By Owner" Zillow listings and auto-texted owners a lowball offer (40% below the Zestimate). ~97-98% ignored or rejected it; 1-3% responded and were willing to talk -- framed as "a numbers game," and the higher the ticket size of the item, the fewer responses you need for the model to work.

## Business models enabled by Craigslist scraping
- **Retail arbitrage**: buy underpriced items on Craigslist (theory: sellers only posting to a shrinking platform get less demand/lower prices than the same item would get on Facebook Marketplace) and resell on Facebook Marketplace or elsewhere without modifying the item. Example: buy a lens for $900 (negotiated down from $1,000 asking), relist at $1,200-1,400 on Facebook Marketplace, sell around $1,200 for ~$300 profit. Compared to classic Walmart-clearance-to-Amazon retail arbitrage (buy at $113, resell at $225).
- **"Need gone" and "wanted" keyword mining**: search listings containing "need gone" to find desperate/motivated sellers (often free or below-market items); search "wanted" to find people actively wanting to spend money on something, then go find them a seller (you already have the buyer, the hard part solved) -- can be automated at scale once you scrape the whole supply side of a marketplace.
- **Lead-selling / referral fee model**: find posts like "small and large motorcycles wanted, any condition" (implies a flipper/reseller) and scrape/cold-outreach across other marketplaces to find matching sellers, then sell those leads to the buyer for a flat fee (e.g., $5/lead) rather than getting into the buy/sell business yourself. Same logic applies to car leads: sell scraped/flagged good-deal leads to car flippers or dealerships instead of flipping cars personally.
- **Niche job board**: scrape job postings from Craigslist, populate a niche job board, drive traffic to it, and charge businesses to list jobs -- described as a source of "a lot of passive income" for others doing it.
- **Higher-ticket flipping**: apply the same underpriced-listing detection to higher-value items (e.g., cars bought for $2,000, sold for $3,000 without repair work) rather than small items, since fewer profitable transactions are needed at higher ticket sizes.

## General framework/mindset points
- Craigslist is framed as declining in usage but still receiving "millions of views every month" -- its decline itself is framed as the opportunity (fewer eyeballs on a listing = lower price = arbitrage opportunity), not a reason to ignore it.
- "More eyeballs equals more money" principle -- illustrated with a rental-pricing example: showing a $1,000/month rental to 100 prospective tenants (vs. just a couple) can surface someone willing to pay $1,300/month because they specifically value some feature more than the ordinary market price reflects.
- Encouragement to connect everyday tools you already use (e.g., Facebook Marketplace for personal use) to business ideas you're hesitant to start (e.g., finding customers for an equipment rental business) rather than assuming you need to learn new specialized tools/skills.
- "Trough of despair" caution: initial excitement from scraped data often doesn't immediately translate into results when you act on it -- expected and normal, push through rather than quitting.

## Caveats
- Retail arbitrage/relisting is presented as legitimate market-making (buying at a real price, reselling at a market-clearing price), not scamming; video does not address legal/ethical nuances of scraping personal contact info (emails/phone numbers) for unsolicited outreach, beyond noting it worked well historically.
- Results (response rates, specific price spreads) are anecdotal/historical, not guaranteed outcomes.
