- Service: full-house exterior pressure washing (vinyl and aluminum siding, two-story sections, brick).
- This video functions as social proof/marketing content for the power-washing home-service niche generally (and implicitly for the franchise/company shown, Rolling Suds), rather than a tutorial with a distinct business model — but it does surface some concrete operational and pricing data points relevant to the trade.

## Numbers
- House size: 4,825 sq ft (measured by the crew as roughly 35 ft x 85 ft footprint, much of it two stories).
- Job completion time: 22 minutes flat, using a 2-person crew plus a truck-mounted rig, each running a 1,000 ft hose reel from opposite sides of the house.
- Price charged for this specific job: $400.
- Water usage estimate for the job: roughly 450-900 gallons (crew estimated "close to 900," host suggested "probably 450," acknowledged imprecision).
- Equipment value referenced on camera: the pressure-washing truck/rig used was valued at approximately $150,000.

## Process notes (brief, incidental to the demo)
- Two-step method used for painted/dirty siding: apply a soaping/chemical solution first and let it dwell/"soak" to break down grime, then do a final high-pressure rinse pass — described on camera as a simple two-step process, not a one-pass spray.
- Crew rinses top-down on multi-story sections deliberately, to ensure chemical doesn't dry on the siding before being rinsed off.
- Different materials require different treatment: e.g., aluminum siding with rust staining won't fully restore to like-new (rust isn't simply washed away), whereas siding with ordinary dirt buildup cleans up dramatically; brick sometimes needs an additional/different chemical process not used on siding, applied as a separate step.
- Equipment simplicity is intentional: crew noted they deliberately avoid over-engineering their setup (e.g., no electric-only rig) to keep operations as simple as possible and minimize points of failure.
- On-camera joke referencing franchise economics: a crew member mentions seeing new franchisees in the space pull "$30,000-$40,000" in their first month — presented anecdotally/enthusiastically on camera, not verified or sourced, and should be treated as unverified marketing color rather than a documented case study.
