# KOA RV Park Business Investing Webinar
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcbhUyFs68w | Published: 2024-05-02

A live investor webinar (Chris Koerner + partner "John," presenting through their firm "Blue Metric") pitching a specific accredited-investor real estate syndication deal: acquiring a 56-site KOA-affiliated RV park in western North Carolina. This is a capital-raise pitch for a specific deal, not a general side-hustle how-to — but it lays out their full RV/campground park acquisition investment model and underlying numbers in detail. Note: the video description says this specific park has since closed, so the deal terms below are historical/illustrative of their model, not a live opportunity.

## Investment thesis / "buy box" criteria (their general acquisition model, not just this deal)
- Only buy parks already performing well: under-market rents and already near full occupancy (they view these as correlated — under-market rent tends to correlate with high occupancy).
- Require the park to have a credible path to $750,000 top-line revenue after expansion — will not buy a park lacking real expansion potential, because they are confident in their ability to fill added capacity.
- Preference stack: under-market rents, good location, waterfront when possible, amenity-rich when possible, and room to physically expand (add pad sites/units).
- Target expense ratio ~45%, yielding projected net profit in the $300K–$400K range on a park reaching the $750K top-line target.
- Will consider larger parks but finds them harder to acquire at their target cap rates.
- Deliberately targets the "messy middle": parks valued $2M–$10M. Below $2M is dominated by individual/small investors; above $10M attracts institutional private equity competition. Mobile home parks got heavily chased by PE ~7-8 years prior to this recording; marinas ~3-5 years prior; campgrounds above $10M are being chased now — but the $2-10M amenity-rich RV/campground segment has comparatively little competition, partly because managing amenity-rich parks requires a bigger, more capable management team than most small investors can field (which Blue Metric already has built out).

## The specific deal terms (this North Carolina KOA park)
- 56 total sites: 2 riverside campground sites + 54 RV pull-through sites (30/50 amp service, 70-foot max length — longer than typical, a competitive advantage). Amenities: pool, river deck, game room.
- Sourced off-market: found via a referral chain — their acquisitions lead found another park in Georgia, whose owner knew of this North Carolina owner planning to list; they secured it about a week before it would have hit the broker market.
- Location: rural western NC, roughly equidistant from Chattanooga, Atlanta, Knoxville, and Dollywood — high RV drive-through traffic corridor; also in a growing "halfback" migration area (retirees who moved from the Northeast to Florida, found Florida too hot, and relocated partway back to the Carolinas).
- Occupancy pattern: highly seasonal/transient (~80% short-term stays). Summer occupancy 80-100%+ (especially July 4th, Memorial Day, Labor Day); shoulder/off-season occupancy roughly 40-60%. Annualized occupancy blended around 50-60%. Their strategy: maximize nightly rates during the ~30%-of-the-year summer peak (which alone can hit most of the revenue target), then convert 30-40% of units to monthly long-term stays in the off-season to backfill occupancy.
- Financial structure of the raise: $750,000 total capital raise (one of their smaller raises). Preferred return structure: 8% preferred return to limited partners (LPs) first, then a 70/30 LP/GP split up to a 30% IRR, shifting to 60/40 thereafter. Target 3x (specifically ~3.05x) equity multiple over a 5-7 year hold. GP (general partner/sponsor) commits 10% of the capital themselves on every deal.
- Debt structure: first mortgage at 70% LTV (30% down), fixed interest rate 8.25% for 5 years on a 25-year amortization; plus an optional unsecured line of credit (~$500K in this case) at 6% interest-only for 5 years, used opportunistically to increase leverage and fund capex/expansion without giving up more equity — not always available, dependent on what the seller offers.
- Return timeline / cash flow: quarterly preferred distributions beginning the quarter after closing; formal investor updates quarterly; K-1 tax documents issued by mid-March. Strategy: refinance within 2-3 years (once value-add improvements are complete) to return most/all LP principal, after which LPs remain in the deal for ongoing "perpetual" cash-flow-only returns; full exit (sale, potentially to private equity) targeted at 5-7 years.
- Uses of capital in this deal: ~$615K purchase-related down payment, ~$61K closing costs, ~$22K capex (unusually low because of the available credit line), ~$50K reserved for the operating account.
- Expansion plan: add 10 new RV pad sites and ~5 cabins/tiny homes; existing power/sewer capacity supports this scope of expansion without a costly sewer pump station. No deferred-maintenance capex needed — inspection found the park well-maintained (only minor cosmetic touch-ups like paint).
- Tax structure: property depreciation treats roughly two-thirds (60%) of the purchase price as depreciable in year one (bonus depreciation) — flagged as subject to potential future changes in federal tax law.
- Track record cited: Blue Metric (16 months old at time of recording) had by this point acquired ~$30M of parks (against a ~$50M current portfolio basis); reports having never missed a preferred distribution payment to investors and averaging 8%+ cash-on-cash returns across their portfolio. Typical acquisition cap rates 10-13% (vs. this deal's atypical 9% cap, appraised at a 7% cap — still considered a strong buy due to a supply-constrained area).
- Sourcing method: acquisitions lead makes cold calls to 5 park owners/operators daily as an ongoing pipeline-building habit; most deals sourced this way are "pocket listings" (not publicly marketed) rather than fully off-market or on-market.

## Value-add / operational playbook themes (echoes concepts from Koerner's other RV/mobile-home-park videos)
- Their model leans much more on capacity expansion (adding pad sites/units) than rent increases for driving returns — stated explicitly: adding a new pad site nets roughly $60/night vs. a 10% rent increase yielding only ~$6/night on an existing site, so their financial projections barely budget for rate increases (treated as a bonus, not core to the model).
- KOA (the franchise brand) provides centralized pricing guidance/dynamic pricing support, competitive rate benchmarking for holiday weekends, and even guidance on ancillary items like firewood/propane pricing; Blue Metric staff have attended "KOA University" training.
- Off-season occupancy optimization: converting cabins/tiny homes to monthly long-term rentals in the off-season (e.g., ~$1,200/month) while keeping them available as high-margin nightly rentals in peak season (e.g., $150-200/night, where as few as ~8-10 nights can outperform a full month's rent) — claimed this seasonal rate/length-of-stay optimization can nearly double a park's revenue if executed well, but typically takes 1-2 full seasons to dial in (building relationships with local contractors, military bases, and Facebook groups for consistent off-season tenants). Explicit observation: most prior mom-and-pop park owners they've acquired from ran the park with no seasonal pricing strategy at all.
- In-house team structure built specifically to run an amenity-heavy, multi-park roll-up: a director of land development (maximizes each park's land value — e.g., site-building tiny homes for ~$25,000 each with fast payback via rental income), a director of personnel (oversight/leadership, background from Darden Restaurants), and regional on-the-ground managers overseeing clusters of parks (one manager described as retired military, effectively "enforcing brand standards" across a regional cluster).
- Nearly all acquisition targets are single-park "mom and pop" owners (occasionally owning 2-3 parks) — considered a large, fragmented, under-professionalized seller base.
- Explicit lesson on post-acquisition transition: they deliberately do NOT keep the prior owner on staff/operating after closing (beyond a small amount of paid transitional consulting for logistics/utilities/access) — cited as generally more trouble than it's worth, especially in long-term parks where tenants have personal relationships with the departing owner, which can create friction for the new operator.

## Risk factors discussed
- Biggest risk framed as basic operational execution risk (not natural disaster or demand risk): whether the business can actually be run to the underwritten pro forma — expenses running above projection or income below projection reducing distributions below target, rather than catastrophic failure.
- Downside scenarios modeled as reduced distribution yield (e.g., 3-5% instead of target returns), not principal loss, in their framing.
- Insurance covers casualty/damage and up to one year of lost rental income.
- Argued this asset class carries less occupancy risk than multifamily housing, because RV/mobile home parks can flex between short-term transient and long-term monthly tenancy models depending on the economy — framed explicitly as a hedge against both a strong economy (higher-margin short-term RV travel demand) and a weak one (parks become one of the cheapest forms of housing, shiftable to full-time monthly tenancy).
- Deal structure: each acquisition is a standalone LLC/deal with its own investors — no cross-collateralization or shared "fund" entity across deals; LPs and GPs are equity holders (Class A shares) in that specific park's LLC, not simply lending money or buying tech-style priced equity rounds.

## Caveats
- This is a paid capital-raise pitch for accredited investors only, not general educational content — treat return projections (3x multiple, 30% IRR, 8%+ cash-on-cash) as sponsor-provided targets, not guaranteed or independently audited outcomes.
- The specific park discussed has since closed per the video's own description — the deal itself is no longer available; the lasting educational value is the acquisition criteria, deal structure, and operational playbook described.
