# The Most Profitable Solo Business You've Never Heard Of
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bAzg8BugEVY | Published: 2026-04-21

Guest: Brett Williams, founder of DesignJoy, a solo-operated "productized" design agency running since 2017.

Business idea: "productized services" — selling a professional service (design, copywriting, video editing, thumbnails, dev work, etc.) as a flat-rate, no-consultation, subscription/off-the-shelf product rather than a traditional custom-quoted agency engagement. Core value proposition is fast "time to value" (TTV): customer subscribes and gets output back quickly (e.g., next-day), with no contracts, no sales calls.

DesignJoy specifics and numbers:
- Run entirely solo since 2017; does roughly $1M/year in revenue, minimal expenses.
- Origin: built the idea in a weekend (idea Friday, built in Webflow Saturday, launched on Product Hunt Sunday) with zero prior audience — got friends/family to upvote it on Product Hunt, reached #4 that day, drove ~36,000 unique visitors, and generated roughly $10,000 in (nominally recurring) revenue on day one.
- Kept his full-time job (paying $75K/year) for 4 years while running DesignJoy on the side, doing DesignJoy work during work meetings; quit only once DesignJoy hit $80,000/month in revenue. Two months after quitting, revenue doubled to $160K/month.
- Pricing evolution (unlimited design, one request at a time): started at $449/month, then raised repeatedly as demand grew rather than shrank at each price increase — $849 → $1,299 → $2,500 → $3,200 → $4,000 → $5,000 → $6,000 → $8,000, then dialed back down. Lesson: doubling price didn't curb demand, it "put me into another category."
- Current: works about 30 hours/week; monthly recurring revenue swings significantly ($40K-$120K+ months) due to churn/pausing, averaging $70-80K/month net profit; peak month was $200,000. Total monthly expenses (excluding tax) are only about $1,000.
- Model differentiator vs. traditional agencies: traditional agencies carry large headcount overhead (PM, CEO, CFO salaries baked into pricing) and multi-week/month/year delivery timelines; a productized solo/small-team agency is far cheaper and much faster (next-day delivery), and structured as a pausable subscription (client can pause when they have no active need and resume later, unlike paying a retained contractor with no work).

Other productized service business ideas named as copyable models (with rough figures):
- Off Menu — AI-driven productized design and development agency; addresses the gap where non-technical people can vibe-code an app's front end with tools like Claude but get stuck connecting a database, Stripe, or hosting/distribution. Suggested pricing range for this kind of AI-build/host/distribute retainer service: roughly $10,000-$20,000/month.
- A productized copywriting agency (mentioned example: entrepreneur Alex Lieberman built one after a single conversation with Brett and was "crushing it" within about 2 months) — flagged as a strong opportunity because AI has gotten very good at copywriting, allowing the work to scale without hiring many people, and "everyone needs it."
- Viral Cuts — a productized short-form video editing subscription (clips long-form podcast/YouTube content into short-form clips for creators); run by the same person (Hunter Hammonds) behind Off Menu.
- YouTube thumbnail design as a subscription — flagged as one of the best productized service models because thumbnail demand is recurring (unlike one-off branding work) and has grown further because YouTube now natively supports thumbnail A/B testing, driving creators to need many thumbnail variants per video, including new ones generated a month after publishing to try to "refire" a video's performance.
- General framing: recurring-need services (video editing, podcast editing, thumbnail design) make better productized business models than one-off services (like branding) because branding revenue doesn't naturally recur.

Guest's view on AI's effect on the design/productized-service business model:
- Net positive for his business: (1) AI has caused an explosion in the number of new businesses/startups being built (more potential clients needing design), and (2) AI expanded what he personally can produce and how fast, letting him take on projects/asset types he'd previously have had to turn away for lack of a team, and he now uses Claude to gather requirements/UX-flow-plan complex software projects without lengthy client meetings.
- Believes distribution and branding/design are the two enduring competitive moats as building product gets easier via AI — "the second you build a successful product... there are 300 people behind you that could spin up something like that within a week."
- Believes most non-technical founders can get by with AI-generated design (e.g., from Claude) that is "good enough," but sees an increasingly important tier above that for brands that want genuine differentiation.

AI tools named and specific uses: Midjourney (image/video-adjacent generation, favored for artistic/stylized imagery), Krea/"Criya" (aggregator that plugs into multiple image/video generation models), Claude (his primary tool for coding/prototyping and requirements-gathering; also considered best for structured/technical prompting), ChatGPT (favored for more conversational/creative prompt writing — he recommends using ChatGPT to write prompts for other tools like Midjourney or Runway rather than writing prompts yourself, and setting up a persistent "rule sheet" in a ChatGPT project so recurring parameters, like Runway camera/lens/color-grading settings, are auto-included in every prompt), Nano Banana Pro (recommended for generating realistic images with specific composed elements, e.g., a model holding a product), Magic Path / Magic Patterns and Claude (recommended tools for non-designers wanting near-professional design output without hiring a designer), Figma (used downstream of Claude for detailed design work), Runway (video generation).

Practical launch guidance: this style of business is framed as "low effort, low cost, low risk" — spin up a website, then focus most of the effort on distribution/customer acquisition since the productized service itself can start simple. Look for a service with genuine ongoing/recurring demand rather than a one-off need to build a subscription-style recurring revenue base.

Caveat raised: the guest notes he worked himself into a hospital visit ("almost to death") during a period of overwork/scaling without adequate systems — emphasizes that growth eventually required better systems, more selectivity with clients, and reduced hours, not just increasing volume indefinitely.
