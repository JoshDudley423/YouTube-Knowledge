# The Most Profitable Side Hustle You've Never Heard Of
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8-FG4NJ39s | Published: 2026-07-02

- Idea: "Plant Bingo" -- host live bingo events (in bars, breweries, restaurants, or any venue with seating for 50+) where attendees pay a ticket price and win potted plants as prizes instead of cash/beer. Interview with Ben & Stephanie of The Social Greenhouse (Dallas, TX), who made ~$30,000 profit in their first two months.
- Origin story: idea copied from a viral TikTok of a California group already doing plant bingo (that group had 10 upcoming events, half already sold out -- used as market validation before copying). Founders already had a home greenhouse/plant hobby. Also validated demand by attending a regular brewery bingo night (free beer prizes only) that still drew 200 people -- proof people will show up for bingo with a low-cost incentive.
- Business model / venue partnership: partner with breweries/restaurants/venues that already run trivia/bingo nights. No money changes hands between host business and Plant Bingo operator ("we pay zero to them, they pay zero to us") -- the venue gets foot traffic/drink+food sales and exposure to their regulars; the operator keeps 100% of ticket + extra-board revenue and handles all marketing. Other example venues: Chicken N Pickle (pickleball venue), PTA groups, HOA associations, private events.
- Startup capital: effectively $0 needed to start -- initial equipment was just a speaker, microphone, bingo boards/daubers, a fold-up table, and some stickers (a few hundred dollars total, and many venues already have speaker/mic since they run trivia). Because events are pre-sold weeks/months in advance, ticket revenue funds the plant purchases before the event happens.
- Pricing evolution: first event priced at $10/person (intentionally break-even/loss-leader to test the model) with free pizza, 25 attendees, mostly strangers reached via personal TikTok/Instagram (only ~200-1,000 followers at the time). Current price is $30/person for one sheet of 10 boards (10 rounds of bingo); they also sell extra bingo boards a la carte (~30 extra boards sold per 50-person event).
- Plant costs: budget ~$10-12/person on average for prize plants, mixing cheaper ($5-10) and pricier ($25-60) plants; consolation prizes run $5-10. Plants require a state license to resell directly (regulated agricultural/nursery licensing) -- the business avoids this by giving plants away as bingo prizes rather than selling them.
- Margins: gross margin (before plant/prize costs) targeted at 60-70% with no paid staff. Net take-home margin cited as "50% at least," sometimes "more than half" once hosts are paid (~$200/event per host). Example big event: 150-person event (oversold to 160 tickets since some no-shows are expected) grossed ~$5,300 (incl. ~$500 extra board sales) and netted ~$3,000 profit after paying two $200 hosts -- roughly $1,000/hour of profit for a ~3-4 hour event including setup/teardown.
- Revenue trajectory: Month 1 (1 event): ~$2,700 revenue. Month 2 (4 events): ~$21,000 revenue, more than half profit. Month 3 (projected, 9 events planned in the following month): ~$25,000. Typical event size ~50-75 people; their biggest event (5th event) was 150 people.
- Scaling plan: targeting ~8 events/month per city (one weekday, one weekend recurring) plus 3-4 private events/month (which come pre-booked, no selling required). Believe a single metro area could plausibly support an event almost every night across many venues/nights, which the host calculates could scale toward $1M/year in one city alone if $3,000 profit/event held at that pace (explicitly caveated as not necessarily realistic at full scale). Received inbound interest from people in 50+ different cities wanting to replicate it; considering expansion and other bingo themes (mystery plant night, "Book Bingo" floated as a spinoff concept for a different niche/hobby audience).
- Customer acquisition: organic TikTok/Instagram content -- video views directly correlated with ticket sales in their experience. Also directly cold-calling venue owners (restaurants, breweries, event spaces, pickleball venues). Recommends engaging existing local plant-hobbyist Facebook groups/communities (present in nearly every city) by contributing genuine value/education, not just self-promotion. No large following needed to start (founders had ~200 and ~1,000 followers respectively).
- Audience: skews 85-90% women aged 25-55; only ~10% of attendees are genuine "plant people" -- most just want a fun night out (date nights, girls' nights). Comparable/adjacent concept referenced: "Bingo Loco" (a more established novelty-bingo brand) as inspiration for making traditional bingo more entertaining/incentivized vs. the old "dark room full of grandmas" stereotype.

Caveats/risks:
- First event was run at a loss/break-even intentionally to work out logistics and gauge demand before charging full price -- expect to not profit on your pilot event.
- Selling plants directly (rather than giving away as prizes) requires a state horticulture/plant-dealer license -- a real regulatory hurdle if you want to add a retail component.
- Revenue figures are self-reported by the founders in an interview (not independently verified) and reflect an unusually fast/favorable outcome (viral proof-of-concept niche, founders already had domain interest and existing relevant assets like a home greenhouse).
- Founders explicitly note they intentionally keep staffing/hours modest ("we don't run it because we need the money... I don't mind making a little bit less to make it easier") -- the ~$1,000/hour profit figure is a peak/best-event data point, not a guaranteed average across all events.
