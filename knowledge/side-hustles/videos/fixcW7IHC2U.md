# The Easiest Way to Make Money with No Code AI
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fixcW7IHC2U | Published: 2025-03-04

Co-hosted brainstorm episode ("HoldCo Bros") between Chris Koerner and Nik (Cofounders/Nikonomics), riffing on AI-adjacent business ideas.

## Business ideas discussed
1. **Digital detox / device-addiction retreats or centers**: framed broadly (not just drugs/alcohol) — retreats where phones are locked away (e.g., in a cooler/tent) so attendees disconnect; targets a growing "digital addiction" market (phone/social media/porn/gambling addiction) analogous to how "organic food" became a reaction to processed food in the prior generation.
2. **Add-on retreat feature — emergency-contact-only notification system**: a questionnaire-driven software tool where attendees pre-select up to 3 emergency contacts; if one of those contacts tries to reach them during the detox, an on-site staff member is alerted and relays the message — addresses parents'/attendees' top objection to going phone-free.
3. **Post-retreat "phone hygiene" info product**: a course or AI chatbot/prompt that teaches people specific iPhone/Android settings changes to reduce compulsive use (screen time limits, notification filtering) since most users don't know these settings exist or won't bother configuring them alone.
4. **Selling AI prompts as a standalone product** (via PromptBase.com): prompts for Midjourney, ChatGPT, etc. sold for $4-8 each (e.g., a "personal financial advisor" prompt, a "real estate listing description generator" prompt, Midjourney art prompts).
5. **Prompt → SaaS pipeline**: buy a cheap prompt from PromptBase, feed it into Replit to build a working chatbot, use Cursor to turn that into an iPhone app, and publish it to the App Store as a paid app/subscription (e.g., $2.99-4.99/month) — effectively converting a $4 one-time prompt purchase into a recurring SaaS product.
6. **"AI wrapper" businesses generally**: building a simple, branded front-end/app on top of an existing LLM (ChatGPT, etc.) for a specific niche use case — e.g., a sourdough-baking coaching app that is really just a well-crafted ChatGPT prompt with nicer branding/UX, sold to non-technical users who don't want to write their own prompts.
7. **Idea validation/"philosophical fit" AI tool**: a proposed (not yet existing) product that helps an entrepreneur validate a business idea (using signals like Google Trends, Google Keyword Planner, Wayback Machine, Reddit/subreddit growth via a tool called Gummy Search) AND cross-references it against the person's own past experience/data (emails, tweets, work history) to help them realize which ideas they're personally suited to execute on — pitched as more valuable than generic idea lists because "you have to be philosophically bought into an idea for it to work."
8. **"Prompt engineer for hire" / prompts-as-SEO framing**: analogy that prompt engineering today is like SEO was ~20 years ago — early expertise (e.g., from working inside OpenAI) could be leveraged into an agency or productized service, the same way early Google/SEO expertise spawned entire agencies and industries.
9. Passing reference to **online trade schools** funded by government tuition dollars as a high-margin, free-lead-flow business model (cited as inspiration, not detailed as a hustle to copy directly).

## Numbers cited
- Referenced example (not detailed in this transcript, from a guest named Hector Rendes mentioned in passing): an online trade school business running 80-85% margins on ~$3M topline revenue, funded by government-paid tuition, with free/organic lead flow.
- PromptBase pricing observed live: Midjourney art prompts $4-8 each; a personal finance advisor ChatGPT prompt $4; a real estate listing generator prompt $5.
- Example of a "wrapper" already succeeding: an Etsy seller offering an $8 personal finance spreadsheet reportedly sells "a hundred a day" ($800/day) fully automated/passive.

## Process / steps described
- Prompt-to-SaaS build path spelled out step by step: (1) buy a prompt on PromptBase for a few dollars, (2) paste it into Replit to generate a working chatbot, (3) use Cursor.ai to wrap that into an installable iPhone app, (4) submit to the Apple App Store, (5) charge a small one-time or subscription fee.
- Framing device repeated for validating any niche AI idea: check Google Trends, Google Keyword Planner, Wayback Machine (historical interest), Reddit/subreddit growth trends (via Gummy Search) before building.
- General principle articulated: "there's always a business in making something a little bit easier" — cited example of a $10M product being just a metal bar simplifying motocross-bike truck loading versus tie-down straps, as a template for identifying small-friction-removal products broadly (not AI-specific, but used to justify the AI-wrapper thesis).

## Tools/platforms named
PromptBase.com (buy/sell AI prompts), Replit (turn a prompt into a chatbot), Cursor.ai (turn a chatbot into an app), ChatGPT/"Tasks" feature (scheduled recurring AI reminders/prompts), Opal (app-blocking/screen-time tool mentioned anecdotally), Gummy Search (subreddit growth tracking), Apple App Store.

## Caveats / risks
- Both hosts are explicit that they consider this space "still super early" — implying execution/timing risk is on the upside (opportunity), but also that tooling for some of the described ideas (e.g., a fully no-code "prompt into SaaS product in a few clicks" builder) doesn't fully exist yet as of recording; today's version still requires real, if minimal, technical iteration through ChatGPT/Replit/Cursor rather than true one-click deployment.
- On the digital-detox retreat idea: acknowledged this concept came from a prior guest (James Camp) rather than being original, and is explicitly framed as a long-term play, not a quick "cigarette butt" (fast, low-effort) idea.
- On chasing generic "genius ideas": Chris notes that most people (himself included) hear a great idea and never act on it because it isn't personally/philosophically theirs — cites a listener (Tyler Mumford) who successfully acted on a stump-grinding business idea specifically because he had personal relevant experience (worked on a tree-trimming crew, did B2B enterprise sales) — implying generic AI-idea tools are only useful if paired with genuine personal fit, not idea quality alone.
- No concrete revenue numbers, unit economics, or execution timelines are given by the hosts themselves for any of the AI-wrapper/prompt-selling ideas — this episode is speculative brainstorming rather than a tested playbook.
