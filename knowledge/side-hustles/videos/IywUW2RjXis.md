# How to Get People to Pay You (Free, No Audience)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IywUW2RjXis | Published: 2026-06-28

Interview with Caleb Panza, co-founder of Post For Me (postforme.dev — a developer-focused social-media-posting API/SaaS) and Day Moon Development (software dev agency). Covers building a SaaS from zero audience to $10K+ MRR.

## The business idea

- **Post For Me**: a social-media auto-posting tool/API aimed specifically at developers/technical users (competing conceptually with Buffer, Post Bridge, but targeting a technical niche those tools don't focus on) — lets other apps/agents integrate social posting. Pricing starts at $10/month, scales unlimited via upgrade tiers ($25, $50, $100+ plans as usage grows).
- **Day Moon Development**: the founders' underlying software dev agency/services business — used to fund and build the SaaS while it was pre-revenue. Started by saying yes to nearly any paid work (design, marketing, WordPress/Webflow site builds) and outsourcing what they personally couldn't do, to stay afloat.
- Origin story: both co-founders lost their jobs/companies around the same time (one company laid off 20% unannounced; the co-founder's other company went bankrupt). They spent about 2 years doing client/agency work for others before finally launching their own product.

## Concrete numbers / growth timeline

- Launched Post For Me around late July/early August 2025.
- Initial launch tweet: ~200 views, essentially flopped; only friends/family and a couple Twitter contacts signed up.
- Took 5 months to reach $500 MRR (by January 1, 2026).
- Starting January 1, began publicly posting a Twitter screenshot every time MRR increased by $100 — despite having virtually no following (~500 Twitter followers at the time, growing to only ~1,200 followers by the time of this interview, yet still described as driving significant business).
- Growth accelerated after the transparency posts began: weekly MRR adds of roughly $500-$1,000/week at points; one jump from $4,500 to $5,000 MRR took only 5 days; best single week reportedly added $1,000 MRR.
- By ~8 months post-launch (April 8, 2026 tweet), crossed $5,000 MRR — that milestone tweet got 24,000 impressions, 114 replies, a 6.4% engagement rate (vs. a much smaller initial post).
- Crossed $10,000 MRR shortly before this interview; only then started running (light, experimental) Google Ads — previously 100% organic.
- Adoption cost example given elsewhere in the conversation: unrelated life context — the founder's family was mid-process on a $50,000 second adoption when he was laid off, underscoring the financial pressure behind starting the business.
- Churn: lowest recorded churn was 10%; in the ~2 months before this interview it rose to 13%, then 16%, then over 17% — became the team's primary focus area (paused new-feature roadmap work to instead investigate why customers leave).

## Process / growth tactics (the "playbook")

1. **Build in public via incremental milestone screenshots** (every +$100 MRR posted to Twitter/X), regardless of follower count — modeled directly on the public build-in-public habits of indie hackers Peter Levels, Marc Lou ("Mark Lou" in transcript), and "Jack." Key insight: those creators built audience trust specifically *because* they posted honestly when their numbers were still small/unimpressive, not only once they were already big — so a new founder doesn't need years of built-up followers before people start paying attention or converting.
2. **Personally staff customer support/live chat** rather than automating it with AI chat features early on — the two co-founders manually answered every website chat message themselves (no 24/7 coverage, but fast personal responses) to build trust and catch real product problems firsthand.
3. **Turn every repeated support question into public SEO/AI-discoverability content**: whenever the same type of problem was reported by a customer at least twice, they logged it in Linear (project-tracking tool) and used it as the basis for a "resource article" published on their site's Resources section. Rationale: a customer who goes out of their way to report a problem (multiple clicks to open a chat) likely represents 10-30 other users experiencing the same issue silently who never bothered to ask — worth building content around even from an n=1 signal.
4. **AI-SEO / LLM-discoverability strategy**: structuring these resource articles/metadata so they get indexed not just by Google but scraped/cited by AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok) — multiple new customers have explicitly said "ChatGPT sent me" or "I was chatting with Claude and they sent me your way." Growth from this channel was described as steady/compounding rather than a spike.
5. **Content production workflow**: co-founder Matt wrote the first batch of resource articles by hand, documented his own process/notes, then used Google's NotebookLM to consolidate his notes/findings, fed in commonly-reported problems pulled from support chat and Discord, and had it generate draft articles which he lightly edited — publishing at least 1/week, sometimes 2-3/week during the highest-output period.
6. **Domain-name tactic (sponsor-adjacent but stated as genuinely used advice)**: since most good `.com` domains are taken (expensive to buy secondhand, or you settle for something forgettable), a `.online` domain is suggested as a memorable, available, and searched-for alternative (the word "online" reportedly gets 500M+ searches/month) — applicable to any small business's web presence, not just SaaS; specifically recommended for physical/local service businesses too (example given: a local piano repair shop that could similarly post educational content — how to maintain/play/design around a piano — every time a repair job happens, applying the same "answer real customer questions publicly" content strategy to a brick-and-mortar business).
7. **Recommended playbook for starting any SaaS**, technical or not: identify a problem you've personally felt, brainstorm solutions freely, then cut it down to the smallest possible first shippable version and ship as fast as possible; iterate daily based on how actual users use the product, staying obsessively focused on real usage data over a big roadmap.
8. **Tech stack named**: Stripe (payments), Unkey (API/auth hosting/tooling), React Router (frontend), shadcn/ui (customizable UI component baseline), PostHog (analytics), Vercel (hosting, especially quick marketing sites). Strong recommendation: put all code on GitHub from day one (even non-technical founders can just tell an AI coding assistant "I have a GitHub account, push this there") to avoid catastrophic local-machine data loss.

## Caveats / risks

- Explicitly frames the founders' initial motivation as forced, not chosen by ambition: both lost employment/income unexpectedly and had families to support — described by the host as a "rock bottom" that pushed them to act, not a case of confident, planned entrepreneurship.
- Rising churn (10% → 17%+ in recent months) is presented candidly as an ongoing, unsolved problem, not a success story detail — the team paused feature development to investigate root causes (onboarding failures in the first ~10 minutes flagged as the most common churn driver by the host's general experience, though not confirmed as Post For Me's specific cause at time of filming).
- Growth numbers are self-reported from the founder's own Stripe dashboard screen-share, not independently audited.
- The "AI SEO" discoverability wins are anecdotal (customers self-reporting "ChatGPT/Claude sent me") rather than measured with hard attribution data.
- Two years of unrelated agency/client work preceded the SaaS launch — the "zero to $10K MRR" story is not literally a zero-experience, zero-runway start; the founders had accumulated technical/business skills and some financial runway via Day Moon Development first.
