# The Secrets to Unlocking Facebook Marketplace
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QOOxSYk0HWM | Published: 2025-01-30

Clip pulled from a full episode on starting a firewood business; focuses on Facebook Marketplace/Groups tactics applicable to any local-lead-gen business (used firewood as the working example).

## The idea
- Use Facebook Marketplace + Facebook Groups as the primary lead-gen channel for a local service/product business (example used throughout: a firewood business run by two teenage boys to save for college, branded "College Fund Firewood").
- Core technique: competitive market research to pick a launch city, then listing optimization + community engagement to win the Marketplace algorithm.

## Market research method (before launching)
- Pick your target city plus 2 comparable "benchmark" cities with similar population/demographics (example: Plano TX [~290k], Frisco TX [~225k], McKinney TX [~215k]).
- On Facebook Marketplace, set a tight search radius (e.g. 5 miles) so you don't count competitors who merely deliver into the area — only count truly local listings.
- Search the product/service keyword + city name (e.g. "firewood Plano Texas"), count only listings with the target city literally in the title (ignore listings from other cities that mention delivery); stop after page one for 80/20 speed rather than exhaustively scrolling.
- Example counts found: Plano 14 listings, Frisco 21, McKinney 28.
- Compute competitors-per-capita: Frisco 1 per 10,700 people, Plano 1 per 20,700, McKinney 1 per 7,600. Benchmark cited from prior research: ~1 tree-trimming business per 10,000 people is "pretty good" density; a market at 1-in-20,000+ is comparatively underserved.
- Use local knowledge (not just raw numbers) to interpret results — e.g., McKinney has more rural/vacant acreage and cheaper land, which independently explains more firewood competition there than in denser, built-out Plano; don't launch somewhere you're not native without adjusting for context like this.
- Fewer competitors per capita = easier entry, but does not by itself guarantee the business will work.

## Listing optimization (applies to Marketplace, eBay, Airbnb, "any marketplace")
1. **Pictures/video**: use the maximum number of photos/videos the platform allows (if a platform caps uploads at 24, that's their studied "optimal" number — use all of them, even near-duplicate angles). Lead with a video (even a 1-second loop) because Marketplace algorithmically prioritizes video-first listings and it stops the scroll. Don't rely only on stills — use "multimedia" (mix of photos and short videos from multiple angles). Photos must look professional (a phone is fine) — not blurry/dark like the poor competitor listing critiqued in the video.
2. **Description/keywords**: cram in every relevant keyword, including adjacent product terms buyers might search even if you don't sell that exact variant (example: list "Hickory, Oak, Post Oak, Scrub Oak, Ash, American Ash, Elm" even if not all are in stock) — this is purely for search visibility.
3. **Responsiveness**: fastest responder wins the lead; turn on all notifications. Facebook's audience is described as more impatient/finicky than other marketplaces, so speed is a bigger edge there than elsewhere.
4. **Never put a phone number in the listing/description.** Facebook detects it (even with disguised line breaks/spacing) and will suppress the listing's reach because Facebook doesn't want traffic leaving the platform. Instead, funnel leads to Facebook DMs — this also gets your listing algorithmically boosted since you're keeping users on-platform.
5. **List your price.** Omitting price gets more (low-intent) clicks but wastes time on unqualified leads; showing price filters for higher-intent buyers.
6. **Title strategy**: use every available character — don't waste the title on a generic term like "Firewood." Put your differentiator/story in the title itself since people don't have to click to read it (example title used: "Cheap Firewood Free Delivery Help Us Save For College — College Fund Firewood").
7. **Storytelling sells for home-service/local businesses**: naming real people involved (the two teens, "Devon and Cade") and stating a relatable motive (saving for college) increases willingness to pay/support vs. competing on being "just another business." Caveat: this framing is business-type dependent — the video explicitly says a professional like an orthodontist should NOT use this "amateur/backyard" framing; it works for home-service/community-goodwill businesses, not credentialed professional services.
8. Use real photos of the actual product/people, not stock/Google images — builds trust that the listing is genuine.

## Facebook Groups tactic (separate channel from Marketplace)
- Search your target city name under Facebook Groups; expect dozens of hyper-local niche groups (e.g., "Plano Foodies," city buy/sell/trade groups, job-hunting groups) — quality varies with how strictly each group is moderated.
- Process: join relevant local groups and post genuine, non-salesy "engagement bait" questions unrelated to your product (e.g., "best tacos in Plano?" or "best school-district neighborhoods?") to build karma.
- After roughly one week of authentic participation, Facebook's algorithm typically tags an active poster as a "top contributor" (a trust badge); after earning that, you can post more deliberately salesy content referencing your actual business/story and it will land better because you've already added value without asking for anything.

## Caveats/risks
- This is tactical/algorithm-specific advice (Facebook Marketplace/Groups behavior) — could change if Facebook alters its algorithm.
- Lower competitor density doesn't guarantee success; it only reduces one variable (competition), not demand or execution risk.
- Home-service "authentic story" framing is not universal — wrong fit for licensed/professional service businesses.
