# He Gets These Free. Makes $1,000 With Each.
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a8P2K9DYJp4 | Published: 2026-09-08

- Business idea: pick up free/discarded washers and dryers (people throw them out for minor fixable faults), repair them cheaply, then either flip them for quick cash or rent them out for recurring revenue. Guest: Kyler, 24, appliance repair "guru," reportedly makes $10,000+/month from rentals plus ~$5,000/month from flipping.
- No formal training — Kyler learned entirely from YouTube tutorials and ordering parts from Amazon; not technical, no appliance school.
- Framework name: "PFS" — Post, Fix, Sell/rent: list the appliance for sale/rent (or start repairing) before even confirming what's wrong or that it's fixable, because listing costs only ~5 minutes either way ("if it sells before you fix it, you save an hour; if it doesn't sell, you only wasted 5 minutes").
- Concrete repair walkthrough on one free dryer:
  - Dryer had no power: replaced power cord (~$5, from Amazon).
  - Used a multimeter to test the thermal fuse (part that shuts off the dryer if it overheats) — beep = good, no beep = bad. Bought a $20 parts kit off Amazon that included thermal fuse + heating element + other common-failure parts.
  - Drum wasn't spinning: broken belt (~$5–10) and worn/broken roller wheels/bearings (included in the same kit) — also cleaned out heavy lint buildup, which causes overheating.
  - No heat: tested the heating element the same way (multimeter beep test) — bad, replaced with the part from the same $20 kit.
  - Total parts cost for this dryer: ~$20 (covers thermal fuse + heating element + rollers/belt kit). Total time: ~45 minutes of hands-on repair (plus ~45 minutes of driving in this specific case).
- Listing/selling process:
  - Used ChatGPT to clean up "dirty" listing photos (prompt: "make this look clean, keep the lighting and background the same, add a cable to the back of it").
  - Facebook Marketplace listing title formula for sale: e.g. "Electric Dryer *Works Perfectly*" — condition "Used - Good."
  - Pricing for a same-exact used dryer at an appliance resale store: $175–200; if you want it to sell same-day, price aggressively at $140–150 instead (Kyler's own approach: high volume, lower price).
  - Always mention: cleaned, tested, works perfectly/heats up great, cord type note, 30-day warranty/guarantee, and (the "real trick") free delivery within 30 minutes — this framing is highlighted as the single biggest driver of fast sales.
  - Bonus mechanic ("infinite money glitch"): ~90% of the time when you deliver a replacement dryer to a buyer, they give you their old (broken) unit for free — creating a continuous free-inventory pipeline.
  - Separately post the same unit as a **rental** listing (different photos/wording to avoid Facebook flagging duplicate listings — include the brand name, e.g. "Admiral Dryer for Rent," to look like a legitimate separate post): rental pricing $35–50/month (aim ~$40–45); pitch lines like "stop going to the laundromat, stop dealing with broken machines," include free delivery/install/maintenance.
- Rental math: a $45/month rental retained for an estimated ~2+ years (based on Kyler's 3 years of experience) = ~$1,080 lifetime value from a single free/near-free unit; you get the unit back afterward and can re-rent it again, indefinitely, repairing as needed.
- Two complementary revenue streams: rentals = recurring/passive-ish income (Kyler nets $10k+/month from rentals); flipping/selling = active, faster cash (~$5k/month) — demand for each tends to offset the other.
- Scaling/sourcing tool: **TakeMyAppliance.com** — a website Kyler built connecting homeowners who want appliances removed (working or not) with people who want to flip/rent them; generates "tens of thousands" of leads/month across US cities. Individuals needing pickup use the main site; people wanting to source appliances to flip/rent sign up at takemyappliance.com/partners to buy/receive leads.
- Sponsor plug (not core to the business idea): HighLevel CRM pitched as an all-in-one replacement for multiple paid software subscriptions (CRM, email, SMS, funnels, calendars, automation, reputation management) starting at $97/month, with white-label resale potential at $200–500/month to other businesses — tangential to the appliance business itself.
- Caveats: appliance condition/faults are unpredictable going in (video stresses fixing before knowing what's wrong is normal); rental timeline/value (the "$1,080" figure) is an estimate based on average retention, not guaranteed; a basement-apartment delivery in the video shows real friction in transport/installation logistics.
