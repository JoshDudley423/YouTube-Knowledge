# How to Find the Best Undervalued Deals (For Beginners)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QghV97IsDpI | Published: 2025-08-29

Live screen-recorded walkthrough by Chris Koerner of an off-market lead-generation system (scrape → validate → cold-text → cold-call) demoed live on RV parks in South Dakota, but framed as applicable to any niche: storage facilities, restaurants, medspas, contractors, mobile home parks, etc. Not a single "idea" video — it's a repeatable deal/lead-sourcing method for buying real estate or existing businesses off-market.

## The idea
- Use Google Maps business-listing data (scraped in bulk) to build a cold-outreach list of business/property owners in a chosen niche and region, then text them (not cold call first) to find owners willing to sell — enabling off-market acquisition deals, including ones with seller financing / no-money-down potential.
- Applies to buying real estate (RV parks, mobile home parks) or acquiring existing small businesses in any niche the scraper can target.

## Numbers (from the live South Dakota RV park demo)
- Outscraper cost: ~$10 to scrape up to 500 listings (job estimated to take 7-25 minutes).
- Of 500 scraped RV park listings: roughly 50/50 split between Google-"owner verified" (241) vs. unverified (259) listings — unverified/never-claimed listings are called out as often "under the radar" opportunities. (Contrast note: a search for dentists would typically run ~98% verified, so RV parks/campgrounds skew unusually unclaimed.)
- After phone-number validation and de-duplication: 500 → 452 numbers with data, of which 129 were confirmed cell phones (~26% of total scraped — Koerner says to expect roughly a quarter of any scrape to be cell numbers, fairly consistent nationally).
- Texting only the 129 cell numbers, sent all at once: 4 responses within moments, eventually 23 total responses out of 129 texted — roughly an 18% response rate mentioned in dialogue ("almost 50%" was said about a smaller batch at one point, and "10% response rate" was also mentioned early — the actual figures fluctuated as replies came in over time, settling around 23/129 ≈ 18%).
- Buying criteria stated on the live call: targets a minimum 10% cap rate (net operating income ÷ purchase price) — e.g., willing to pay under $1,000,000 for a park netting $100,000/year in profit; will pay more if rents are under market and can be raised, or if infrastructure (paved roads, city utilities) is already in place.
- Example park detail surfaced via research before the call: Buckshot Resort — $2,500/year for a lakefront/shoreline RV pad, $2,000/year for an off-shore pad (water/sewer/garbage included, electric metered to camper); ~80-85% full, up "plus six" spots over the prior year; owner operates seasonally (~6 months/year) on family land held 100+ years, on 12 acres (expanding to 20), within a larger 2,000-3,000 acre family farm/ranch property.

## Process / how to do it
1. **Scrape**: Use Outscraper's "Google Maps Data Scraper." Search a business category (e.g., "RV park") — an actual Google Maps category — and filter by state/city/zip (state-level filtering available, no county-level filtering). Cap the result count (e.g., 500) to control cost.
2. **Choose data columns deliberately** to keep the file clean: include name, name-for-emails (secondary name source), category, phone, address/street/city/postal/state, rating/reviews (signal of business quality/longevity), number of photos (signal of owner engagement with their Google Business Profile), "owner verified" status (an unclaimed/unverified GMB listing often signals an under-the-radar, less sophisticated owner — a good acquisition signal), owner ID/title/link, and phone carrier type (landline vs. cell — critical for texting). Skip irrelevant columns (lat/long, social handles like Reddit/Medium/Skype) to reduce noise; the stated 80/20 core fields are business name, phone number, and email (owner name is often missing/inaccurate).
3. **Clean the data** in Google Sheets/Excel: strip "+1" prefixes and dashes from phone numbers via find-and-replace; remove duplicate rows by phone number (critical — duplicates otherwise cause double-texting and misalignment when merging validation data back in).
4. **Validate phone numbers/carrier type** using either SearchBug (paid, ~$10, fast) or SlyBroadcast (a ringless-voicemail provider that validates phone lists for free as part of its normal service, with no obligation to buy voicemail broadcasts) — needed because Outscraper's own carrier-type data isn't reliable. Only cell numbers are texted (landlines can't receive SMS).
5. **Filter down to cell-phone-only contacts** before uploading to a texting platform (~26% of a scrape, per this example).
6. **Set up personalization variables** for the text campaign — city name is the most reliable variable (nearly always populated cleanly); street name is usable but sometimes has formatting glitches (stray "+" characters) needing cleanup. Use the word "park" generically instead of "RV park" specifically, since some listings may actually be mobile home parks and "park" covers both without looking wrong.
7. **Send a short, low-friction first text via an SMS platform** (Sinch/MessageMedia in the demo). Keep it under 160 characters (extra length costs 2 credits instead of 1, and shorter messages perform better anyway). Add "here" if you're actually local to the area (increases perceived legitimacy) — Koerner skipped it since he wasn't local. Don't ask directly "are you interested in selling?" in the first message — instead open a low-pressure conversation; playing "the long game" gets better results than an upfront sell-ask.
8. **Review the personalization preview before sending** to avoid a broken merge-field.
9. **Send to the whole validated list at once** rather than a slow drip, for a fast read on response rate — but Koerner notes he "should have throttled it" (implying some risk of looking spammy or overloading the platform / getting flagged when blasting all at once).
10. **Triage responses manually, not via full automation** — Koerner explicitly does NOT recommend auto-reply automations for this kind of high-value, low-volume outreach (useful only if casting an extremely wide net and buying many properties per month, which isn't the target use case here); he recommends staying high-touch and personally reading/answering each reply. Caution: forgetting to pause an automation before sending a second, different campaign can result in bots sending nonsensical follow-ups to old conversations.
11. **Before calling a promising lead, do reconnaissance**: check the business's website, pricing, social media follower count/posting frequency (signals occupancy/health — e.g., a park that "stays full" if actively posting with meaningful followers), and review count (absence of reviews here was read as a signal the seller is likely an individual/family, not institutional/PE-owned).
12. **Call from a personal phone number (not the texting platform's number)** once ready to talk seriously — puts the conversation on a more personal, trustworthy footing; be ready to explain who you are, credibly (track record, niche focus, personal site/newsletter) since sellers will vet you back (e.g., the RV park owner said he'd Google the caller before sharing sensitive numbers).
13. **On the call, build rapport and gather diligence data conversationally** (ownership history, occupancy trends year-over-year, rent roll basics, utility inclusions, land size, zoning/expansion plans) before pushing for a number; state your buy-box/cap-rate criteria plainly once trust is established, and leave the door open even after a "no" or hesitation ("let me know if that changes") since sellers who don't ghost you remain warm long-term leads.

## Tools/platforms/resources named
- Outscraper — Google Maps data scraper; primary lead source (not real-estate-specific, but cheap/fast and works for any business category on Google Maps).
- Reonomy — alternative, real-estate-first data source; cleaner focus on real estate but a smaller/less rich dataset than Outscraper's for this purpose.
- SearchBug (searchbug.com) — phone number validation/enrichment, ~$10.
- SlyBroadcast (slybroadcast.com) — ringless voicemail provider; used here purely for its free phone number/carrier validation feature.
- Sinch MessageMedia (messagemedia.com) — SMS/texting platform used to send the bulk campaign; Koerner calls its interface "clunky" for tracking read/unread conversations.
- SlyText (slytext.com) — alternative texting platform suggested as a backup/hedge.
- Porterhouse (porterhouse.app) — app enabling texting via iMessage-style "blue bubbles," suggested for those who want to start texting immediately without waiting on a new platform account.
- Beehiiv (beehiiv.com/chris) — newsletter platform plugged mid-video (sponsor mention), not directly part of the lead-gen workflow.
- Google Sheets — used as the working spreadsheet/CRM throughout for cleaning and merging data.

## Caveats / risks
- Data quality is inconsistent: Google Maps/Outscraper data can be outright wrong (example: "Buckshot Resort" location looked geographically off — Koerner speculated it might be stale Google Maps data) — always sanity-check listings before investing outreach or call time.
- Response/lead volume skews toward property managers or front-desk/reservation lines rather than true owners on some numbers scraped — not every contact reached is a decision-maker.
- Blasting an entire validated list at once (rather than throttling) risks looking spammy or triggering platform/carrier spam flags; Koerner explicitly says in hindsight he should have throttled the send.
- Full automation (auto-reply sequences) is discouraged for this use case — deal-sourcing outreach is described as needing a high-touch, personal approach rather than set-and-forget bots, and mismanaged automations can send embarrassing or nonsensical follow-ups if not paused between campaigns.
- Real acquisition still requires normal small-business/real-estate diligence (occupancy history, cap rate math, land/utility specifics, seller's true motivation) — the scrape-and-text system only generates the lead/conversation, not the underlying deal quality.
- No completed transaction shown in this video — it demonstrates the top-of-funnel lead generation and first qualifying call only, not a full offer-to-close cycle.
