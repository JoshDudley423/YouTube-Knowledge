# Watch Me Clone a $200K/Month Business in 5 Minutes
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VOo3M3ZHUoA | Published: 2026-08-14

Solo episode: reverse-engineering the GoHighLevel (GHL) white-label SaaS-agency business model using GHL's public "SaaSpreneur Awards" leaderboard, then live-building a starter missed-call-text-back product for roofers.

## The core business model
- White-labeling: take GoHighLevel's all-in-one software (CRM, marketing automation, booking, phone system, AI) and resell it under your own brand/name/pricing to a specific niche of local service businesses — customers never know GoHighLevel exists underneath (analogy: Walmart Great Value spaghetti made in the same factory as Bertolli).
- GoHighLevel's white-label reseller cost is a flat $497/month regardless of how many end-customers you have (10 or 10,000) — GHL's rule requires reselling at a minimum of $97/month per customer. Most operators actually charge $200-$500/month, some $1,000+.
- GHL publishes a public "SaaSpreneur Awards" leaderboard with three tiers based on paying-customer count: Gold (100+ customers), Platinum (500+), Diamond (1,000+) — used as a public proxy to estimate any given operator's revenue (pricing page × tier-implied customer count).
- Napkin math examples given: 101 customers at $297/month = ~$30,000/month revenue against $497/month software cost = ~98% gross margin before other overhead (payroll, ad spend); a diamond-tier operator with 1,500 customers at $97/month = ~$145,000/month revenue.
- There are 2+ million businesses on GoHighLevel total — described as a large, "boring," underexploited opportunity space.

## Reverse-engineering toolkit (the 5 free/cheap tools used repeatedly)
1. **Pricing page × leaderboard tier** — estimate revenue directly from public pricing and the award tier's customer-count floor.
2. **Wayback Machine** (web.archive.org) — view a competitor's historical website versions to see how their positioning/niche evolved over time (e.g., a company might have started as a generic "we do marketing" agency and narrowed down to one niche after it worked) — lets you skip years of trial-and-error niche-finding.
3. **Meta Ads Library** (Facebook/Instagram ad transparency tool) — search a competitor's name to see all their live/historical ads; ads still running are the ones that are profitable (an ad that doesn't convert gets turned off, so a long-running ad = validated). Sort by most recent start date, or by impressions, to find their top-performing/highest-spend ad to copy the format of. The UK version of the ad library additionally discloses ad targeting parameters (age/location/interest) due to regulation there. Tip: screenshot competitor ads and feed them to an AI tool (Claude/ChatGPT/Grok) to generate similar ad copy/variations for your own business.
4. **BuiltWith.com** — reveals what tech stack/plugins a competitor's website runs on (CMS, pixel tracking, analytics, page builders) — used to show that even $200K/month businesses are built with ordinary off-the-shelf tools (WordPress/Elementor, HighLevel, Facebook Pixel, Google Analytics/Tag Manager), not custom engineering.
5. **LinkedIn** — company page follower/employee counts (self-reported, so can be inflated or deflated) and the "People" tab to see actual named employees (used to infer headcount, e.g., counting real associated profiles rather than trusting the "11-50 employees" range field); many resellers staff with low-cost virtual assistants (Philippines-based, $4-12/hour) doing most of the work remotely with no office.

## Business examples profiled with estimated economics
- **Event Hawk** (event rental industry software/marketing — bounce houses, tables/chairs, photo booths, tents): Platinum tier (500+ customers), pricing $297/$597/$1,000 tiers, estimated average customer ~$400/month → ~$200,000/month revenue against $497/month GHL cost. ~9,800 event rental businesses in the US; Event Hawk holds ~5% penetration. LinkedIn showed ~26 employees, mostly low-cost VAs, implying ~$50,000/month payroll (~25% of overhead) and an estimated 35-40% net margin (owner take-home guessed at $50,000-$100,000/month). Originally launched under a generic ad-agency name ("MyAdAcademy") before narrowing into the event-rental niche specifically.
- **"Asthetics" (sic)** — medspa booking/lead-capture software: Gold tier, $300-$500/month pricing (custom for multi-location), ~200 customers estimated → ~$80,000/month revenue. Runs Google Ads (confirmed via sponsored search results) rather than visible Meta ads — illustrates that not every niche's leader relies on Facebook; test both channels.
- **PushPress Grow** (gym/fitness studio all-in-one software): Diamond tier (1,000+ customers). Monetizes partly via a payment-processing markup (charging ~5% vs. paying ~2.75-2.8%, netting ~2.25% per transaction) plus flat monthly fees ($159/$229 tiers); "free" tier still nets ~$50/month per customer via processing. Estimated ~$100/month average customer × 1,000+ customers → $100,000+/month. Runs ~49 active Facebook ads (roughly double Event Hawk's ad volume) — lower price point per customer offset by much larger volume and lower service complexity (reselling a CRM is easier than building full marketing funnels).
- **Dental Implant Machine** (lead-gen/marketing agency targeting dentists for implant cases): Gold tier (750+ customers). Doesn't post pricing publicly ("book a free strategy call" = high-ticket signal); estimated agency pricing found via a Google search: $2,500-$5,000+/month. Justified by dental economics: single implants cost $3,000-$6,000, "all-on-4" full-arch cases run $15,000-$36,000 — one new dental customer easily pays for a month of the service. Runs 17 active Facebook ads even in a niche B2B market with only ~202,000 dentists in the US; estimated ~$1,000,000+/month revenue (500+ customers × ~$2,000/month minimum).

## Live-built example: missed-call text-back for roofers (the recommended "wedge product")
- Rationale: roofers are frequently on ladders/roofs or with a customer and can't answer calls; missing a call on a potential $30,000 roofing job is a direct, quantifiable loss — an easy, high-value "no-brainer" first sale that opens the door to selling additional services (ads, website, full marketing) once trust is established.
- Step-by-step build shown live inside GoHighLevel (agency view):
  1. Create a "sub-account" for the new client by searching GHL's built-in business database (has a "Home Services - Roofing" template) and picking/creating the roofing business record.
  2. Switch into that sub-account's view (as if operating as the client) → Settings → Phone System → Phone Numbers → buy a new local number matching the client's area code (cost: ~$1.15/number) for SMS/calls.
  3. Forward the business owner's existing personal cell number to the new GHL number (20-second ring timeout, voicemail off — voicemail being "on" makes the call register as answered and the missed-call-text-back automation won't fire).
  4. Automation → Create Workflow → Select from Template → "Missed Call Text Back" (a prebuilt template with 13,000+ prior uses in the marketplace, chosen over lesser-used alternatives) — auto-texts the caller after a short delay (to avoid feeling robotic) when a call is missed/busy/unanswered, tags the contact, and optionally notifies the business owner.
  5. Calendars → New → select an appointment type from the template (e.g., "roof inspection") and get the shareable booking link.
  6. Edit the automated SMS response to include that calendar link (e.g., "Sorry I missed your call... Would you like us to come inspect your roof? Book here: [link]") so a missed call converts directly into a booked appointment without any human intervention.
- Entire build demoed as completable "in like 90 seconds" for a live sales demo to a prospective roofing client.
- Suggests using AI (via a prompt shown live: "give me 10 industries similar to event rentals from a first-principles perspective") to brainstorm adjacent niches if a given industry doesn't pan out — examples generated: dumpster rentals, porta-potty rentals, skid steer/mini excavator rentals, storage container rentals, restroom trailers, party bus/limo rentals, temporary fence rentals, trailer rentals.

## Caveats / risks raised
- Award-tier customer counts and estimated pricing are approximations (a "Gold" tier could be 101 or nearly 500 customers) — the revenue estimates are deliberately conservative napkin math, not verified financials.
- Employee counts self-reported on LinkedIn can be deliberately inflated or deflated by competitors, so treat as directional only.
- Whether a given niche responds better to Facebook/Meta ads or Google ads varies (e.g., the medspa example showed no active Meta ads, implying Google Ads or another channel was their real driver) — test both rather than assuming one channel works universally.
