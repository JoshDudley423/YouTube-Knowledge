# The Simple One-Man Side Hustle That Runs Itself
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68x7jFWbaE8 | Published: 2026-09-06

Guest: Nathan, owner of Lanco Trailer Rentals (Lancaster, PA), runs it alongside a full-time job.

## The idea
- Buy utility/dump/enclosed trailers and rent them out (daily rental), highly automatable, minimal moving parts (no engine) so maintenance is low compared to vehicle/equipment rental.

## How he got started
- Found an existing trailer rental business for sale on Facebook Marketplace (owner retiring, moving away).
- Bought just the 10 trailers (a mix: 5 enclosed, several open/utility/equipment trailers, no dump trailer) for $35,000 — didn't pay for the "customer list" the owner wanted $20,000 extra for; instead negotiated to pay the seller a 35% referral fee on the first rental from anyone the seller refers or forwards. Over 15 months, only ~$1,200 total paid out this way (~20 referrals) — not a big factor in the business's success.
- Seller's business had done $28,000/year total revenue across those 10 trailers, with an unsophisticated setup: WordPress website with backlinks (ranked organically for "trailer rentals" in the area), Google Business Profile with mixed/decent reviews (4.2 stars), no real CRM, no insurance on the trailers themselves.
- Seller kept his own phone number, website, and business name; forwarded calls/leads to Nathan. Nathan built a brand-new business name (Lanco Trailer Rentals — "Lancaster County"), new website (lancotrailers.com), new Google Business Profile from scratch.
- Financed the $35K purchase with a HELOC against his house (initial rate ~2.5% for 9 months, then jumped to ~6.5%). Since then, replaced 9 of the original 10 trailers using business cash flow (not new financing).

## Revenue / numbers
- First (partial) month revenue: $2,300. First full month: ~$4,000 (about 20 rentals that month; only 3-5 of those came from the seller's referrals — the rest from Nathan's own new website/Google listing).
- Steady-state monthly revenue: fluctuates $5,500-$7,500 (called ~$6,500 average); seasonal — dropped to just over $2,000/month in winter (open trailers barely move in snow; enclosed trailers still rent for moving/road trips).
- Average rental price: ~$90/day (range $50-$150 depending on trailer). Enclosed trailers average ~4.5-day rentals (moves, road trips like Sturgis); open trailers average ~1.5-day rentals (quick jobs, towing a broken-down car, picking up a mower).
- Monthly fixed overhead: lot rental $800/month (half-acre gravel lot, no road frontage — cheaper because it's not visible from the road) + insurance ~$300-$320/month (general liability + trailer insurance, $3,800/year for 13 trailers, ~$350/trailer/year) + website/software ~$200/month + cameras $50/month for 4 cameras (GPS trackers are a one-time $150 purchase per unit with 4-year batteries, no ongoing cost) ≈ ~$1,100-$1,300/month fixed, plus maintenance.
- Maintenance: ~$8,000 in the first full year (elevated because the trailers were bought used and he front-loaded spare parts purchases); works out to roughly $650-$700/month. Main wear items: tires, bearings (replaced roughly every 12,000 miles, tracked via GPS mileage), suspension — no engines/hydraulics to speak of (dump trailers have minor hydraulics).
- Total overhead including maintenance: ~$2,000-$2,500/month.
- Net profit: ~$4,000/month (~$40,000/year), working only 2-4 hours/week (sometimes up to 5) — works out to $200+/hour of his time.
- New trailer example: an $8,300 dump trailer rents for $95/day; pays for itself in ~75 days of rental and lasts 10+ years with maintenance. Rule of thumb pricing: ~1.5% of purchase price per day (e.g., $10K trailer → ~$150/day, though he actually prices closer to 1% for his cheaper trailers). Used trailers depreciate slowly (resell for only 20-40% less than paid).
- Security deposit: 2x the daily rental rate (e.g., $100-$250) to cover minor damage; no claim yet has exceeded the deposit in 15 months.

## Process / automation stack (build order)
1. Phase 1 (manual): meet every renter in person at the lot — biggest downside was being stuck waiting on renters who show up late.
2. Phase 2: lock boxes with codes on each trailer — renter gets pickup instructions with the lockbox code, no in-person meeting needed.
3. Phase 3 (current, fully automated): each trailer has a numbered, dedicated spot with its own lock/lockbox on a sign/message board. Renter books online or calls in; card is processed; automated system sends the rental agreement to their phone to e-sign; renter uploads driver's license + insurance before getting pickup instructions; system texts the lockbox code 24 hours before pickup; renter takes before/after photos via the system to check the trailer in/out. Nathan's own time is now just fielding occasional calls and driving by ~2x/week (~30 min swapping a broken part occasionally).
4. Setup cost for Phase 3: lockboxes ~$30/piece (Amazon), locks ~$65/trailer, ~$350 for a printed sign plus a small overhang board to protect it from rain, cameras.
5. Lead sources: ~55% Google (organic SEO/Google Business Profile), ~14% Facebook Marketplace, rest organic/word-of-mouth/referral/repeat customers. Spent only ~$100 total on boosted Facebook listings over 15 months; no paid Google ads at all.

## Tools/platforms named
- Rent Chek Solutions ("Rent Close Solutions" per transcript) — trailer-rental-specific CRM/website builder that handles website design, SEO, and automation setup.
- GPS trackers (generic, pings twice daily, used for mileage tracking and theft mitigation).
- Cameras (4 units, $50/month total) at the lot.
- No-upfront-cost trailer rental listing platforms to test demand without owning trailers or a website: Neighbor, Big Rentals ("big loads" mentioned too) — these take a commission but let you list for free and can test a market with zero trailers owned.
- Facebook Marketplace for demand testing and low-cost customer acquisition.
- trailerrental.guide — Nathan's free starter guide (email opt-in).

## De-risking / how to test before buying
- Chris's suggested "de-risk" trick: list a trailer you don't yet own (or one you can rent) on Neighbor/similar sites or Facebook Marketplace using a stock photo, just to gauge inbound demand before committing capital — you can even go rent a trailer yourself to fulfill an order and pocket/lose a small amount just to validate demand.
- Suggested market-research method before choosing a location: find demographically comparable "sister cities," count existing competitors' total trailer inventory (by calling/checking their sites), and compute a rough "residents per trailer" ratio to identify underserved markets — doable in about an hour of research.

## Growth options discussed
- Add more trailer types/inventory to the existing lot (currently only ~50% lot capacity used) — potential to reach ~$100,000/year profit just by filling the existing half-acre lot at current occupancy rates, according to Chris's estimate.
- Open a "transplant" location: replicate the exact same lot/trailer/lockbox system in a nearby town (Nathan already drives through the region for his day job).
- Expand into other unpowered no-moving-parts rental categories — hard to find many (that's part of why trailers work); golf carts, RVs/pull-behind campers, and equipment (skid steers, excavators) are options but come with real downsides: golf carts/campers are usually requested with delivery (95% of golf cart inquiries wanted drop-off, which breaks the hands-off model); equipment needs a walkthrough/tutorial for the renter and has far more that can go wrong (more moving parts = more breakage).
- Recommended priority (per Chris): first max out the current lot, then expand to the next-best nearby city, rather than diversifying into more complex categories.
- Suggested attracting repeat/bulk local business: offer landscaping and other local companies a 10% discount for 3+ day rentals and easy booking by text — once someone rents once, they tend to become repeat customers.

## Caveats / risks
- Trailer insurance is not always legally required — in Pennsylvania, the towing vehicle's own insurance must cover liability for whatever it's towing, so some competitors (like the original seller) operate with zero trailer insurance; Nathan chooses to carry it anyway (~$3,800/yr for 13 trailers).
- The business is seasonal in colder climates — revenue can drop by more than half in winter months (open trailers barely rent when snow is on the ground).
- Physical location matters a lot: a cheap lot without road frontage saves money but likely reduces walk-up/passive visibility; road frontage would probably increase rentals but is harder to find and costs more.
- Damage/liability risk is real but manageable — security deposits have covered all damage claims so far, but Nathan expects "there will be a day" he has to eat a cost.
- Anti-theft protections aren't foolproof: trailers are locked and tracked via GPS, and renters must upload license/insurance before getting a pickup code, but theft is still physically possible (hasn't happened yet).
- Expanding into powered/complex rental categories (equipment, golf carts) meaningfully changes the business — requires an operator/employee and more hands-on time, undermining the "runs itself" appeal that makes trailers attractive.
