# Find Your Passion in 20 Minutes, Not 20 Years
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NbAqXzS7Q44 | Published: 2026-07-12

Solo episode by Chris Koerner (not an interview) — a methodology for identifying which business/niche to pursue, not a specific business idea. Relevant as a "how to pick your idea" framework rather than an idea itself.

## Core thesis
- "Follow your passion" is bad advice because most people don't know their passion and assume they only have one. Passion (Latin: "suffering") should mean finding what you're willing to suffer for, not what feels good on a relaxed Saturday.
- Passion follows progress, not the other way around — you get obsessed with things once you get good at them, not before. Don't wait to feel passionate before starting.
- Koerner's own throughline across 80+ businesses (iPhone repair, RV parks, tree trimming, online snack business, etc.) was never the industry — it's the "zero to one" process of building/launching itself (he calls himself a starter, not an operator, and often hands off businesses once running).

## The "Obsession Audit" — 8 tactics for finding a direction
1. **Receipts**: Review YouTube watch history (last 90 days), Google search history, Amazon order history, and bank/discretionary spending — these reveal real interests, especially spend on things you can't really afford. Tool: export YouTube history via Google Takeout (takeout.google.com), export Amazon orders as a spreadsheet, feed to an AI (ChatGPT/Claude) with a prompt like: "Here's my YouTube watch history — ignore outliers, show me the three obsessions hiding in here I'd probably never admit to myself." Do the same with bank statement / Amazon export ("categorize my discretionary spending and tell me what I clearly care about").
2. **Hijack test**: Text 5 people who know you well and ask "What do I not shut up about?" — answers will cluster. AI variant: feed 6+ months of texts/notes/voice memos to AI and ask "what topics do I keep circling back to?"
3. **Childhood callback**: What did you obsess over unprompted at ages 8-15 (before high school/social pressures took over)? What did you get in trouble for as a kid (often signals an underlying strength — talking too much → sales/content; taking things apart → building; bossing kids around → leadership).
4. **Energy log**: For two weeks, rate every activity 1-10 on "how much energy did this give or take after I finished it" (not during). Look for the rare activities that are net energy-positive and where time distortion occurs (3 hours feels like 20 minutes = flow state). Paste two weeks of logs into AI and ask it to find the pattern.
5. **Envy map**: Identify 5 people whose life/work triggers a genuine jealous sting ("that should be me") — not admiration, actual envy. Look for the pattern across all 5; envy indicates a life direction some part of you already wants.
6. **100 questions dump**: Voice-memo or write 100 questions you want answered before you die (not business-focused). First ~20 are surface-level; real signal shows up in questions 60-100. Feed transcript to AI: "cluster these into themes and tell me which theme shows up most."
7. **Teach-it-for-free filter**: What would you happily explain to a stranger for zero dollars because you can't help talking about it? That's the thing people will eventually pay for.
8. **Generate new ideas by trying things** (the big one): Try 2-3 small things a month rather than 2-3 things in a lifetime. Give yourself permission to quit fast without guilt. Key tactic — "sell it before you have it": post the offer/ad before building the product or buying equipment, and let market response (or lack of it) tell you the truth. Counter-example given: a guy who spent $113K financing a forestry-mulching machine before ever talking to a single customer, instead of posting a Facebook Marketplace ad first to test demand.

## Supporting tactics
- Post experiments publicly (including failures) — free "lottery tickets," builds accountability, and puts you in front of potential customers/partners without having to chase them.
- Use self-imposed stakes/accountability (example: Koerner told his team he'd pay each of them $100 if he didn't finish filming 3 overdue videos by midnight — it worked).
- Once a direction is chosen, pursue the smallest fastest win first to build momentum (Dave-Ramsey-debt-snowball logic) rather than over-optimizing for "focus."

## Anecdote referenced
- A near-unknown Twitter user (~500 followers, "Ethan") posted an AI-generated video of a miniature-house build process; Elon Musk commented, it hit 7M views, and he raised $50K on Kickstarter with zero dollars in funding and no prior audience — cited as proof that testing/launching an idea now costs nearly nothing.
