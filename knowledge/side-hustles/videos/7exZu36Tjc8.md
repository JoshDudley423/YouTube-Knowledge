# How to Import and Sell Profitable Products from China
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7exZu36Tjc8 | Published: 2024-06-04

Coaching-call format episode with a guest ("Clayton"), an aerospace consultant/engineer/"tinkerer," brainstorming a physical-product e-commerce business: combine two existing generic/white-label products into one branded bundle and sell via Amazon FBA, Shopify, and social media.

## The core idea discussed
- Guest's original concept: a programmable smart outlet/timer wired to a space heater, used as a "wake up warm" alarm clock (inspired by hacking together an off-the-shelf smart plug + space heater at home).
- Key insight from host: you don't need a patentable/proprietary invention. Combine two already-mass-produced, generic (unbranded) components sourced from Chinese manufacturers into a single branded product/box — you can sell a meaningful volume (framed as "a couple hundred thousand units") before imitators catch up, even with zero IP protection.
- Reframe the value proposition around eliminating a specific friction point (e.g., "you're not just an alarm clock, you're a better space heater that's also an alarm clock") rather than inventing new core technology.

## Concrete step-by-step process recommended
1. Go to Alibaba.com and DM multiple manufacturers of each of the two component products (e.g., smart plug/timer + space heater); most will send free samples.
2. Test a dozen or so sample units, pick the two you like best (most generic-looking, no existing branding/logos).
3. Find a US custom-box/packaging manufacturer (cited estimate: $1-2 per box) and order their minimum order quantity (roughly 100-200 units) to get custom branded packaging.
4. Order matching quantities of the two components, assemble at home (garage/closet-scale), print simple included instructions.
5. Set up an Amazon FBA seller account, learn their labeling/shipping requirements (a "headache" the first time but reusable for every future product), and ship inventory to Amazon distribution centers.
6. In parallel, list the assembled product on Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist immediately (even before scaling) — free way to validate real demand since sellers of "used" versions of the underlying products will surface the new listing to interested searchers by keyword; not scalable long-term (mostly local reach) but useful early signal.
7. Use the ~6 month lead time before the target buying season (space heaters = winter) to properly time inventory and marketing.

## Numbers / costs cited
- Alibaba samples: typically free.
- Custom box order: ~$1-2/unit at ~100-200 unit minimum order quantities → total starting capital of roughly "a few hundred bucks" for packaging, plus the cost of the underlying component quantities.
- Guest was comfortable risking up to ~$5,000 in inventory if the idea flopped.
- Explicitly advised AGAINST ordering a full shipping container up front — "way too much money" for an unvalidated idea; start with small sample-driven batches instead.
- Paying a Shopify freelancer (Upwork) for a single-product, conversion-optimized landing page: cited $400-500.

## Marketing / channel strategy
- **Shopify one-product site**: hire from Upwork specifically for a one-product Shopify theme (different design/UX needs than a general multi-product store — needs countdown timers, urgency widgets, apps) rather than reinventing it yourself; this is the version optimized for driving cold Facebook ad traffic.
- **Price-point A/B testing**: use a Shopify app that lets you split traffic to different price points on the same landing page/ad copy (e.g., test $30 vs $80 vs $150 simultaneously). Rationale: if you sell a $3-50 product, Facebook ad customer acquisition cost tends to be similar regardless of price, making it "almost impossible to scale" at low price points — test materially higher prices to find one where margin actually supports paid acquisition.
- **Amazon FBA angle**: appealing because it's hands-off logistics (no need to personally store/ship inventory) and because buyers have an existing trust/payment bias toward Amazon.
- **Micro-influencer seeding**: create a short (20-30 second) demo video (host suggested an "Office"-style bacon-alarm-clock gag reframed for a space heater) and pay a micro-influencer (50k-200k followers) a per-sale commission (example: $10/sale) to post it; expect most influencers approached to ignore the pitch, but a "yes" from even one can drive real volume. Explicit survivorship-bias caveat: plenty of people who tried random-product-to-Amazon plays made real money, but plenty of others tried and made nothing — you don't know in advance which you'll be.
- Recommendation to run Amazon + Shopify + social/influencer channels together rather than picking just one, once the base product/logistics are set up.

## Tools/platforms named
Alibaba.com (sourcing/sampling), Amazon FBA (fulfillment + distribution), Jungle Scout (competitive research tool — estimates sales volume of existing Amazon products/competitors), Shopify (one-product landing page), Upwork (hiring a Shopify site builder), Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist (free demand validation), ThomasNet (directory for finding US manufacturers, e.g., for custom enclosures/casings — suggested as an alternative to searching "injection molding with direct shipment," which the guest found expensive/high-minimum-run), Facebook ads (paid traffic to the Shopify page).

## Caveats
- Low-price physical products ($3-50) are hard to scale profitably via paid Facebook ads because acquisition cost doesn't scale down with price — test higher price points instead of assuming a product must be cheap to sell.
- No IP/patent protection means competitors can copy quickly — the strategy is to move fast and capture early volume/brand recognition, not to build a durable moat.
- Trying to run multiple very different product ideas simultaneously (e.g., an imported/assembled product plus a 3D-printed/injection-molded product) adds a lot of complexity; better to nail the process with one product first, since subsequent products go much faster once the sourcing/Amazon/Shopify process is learned. If there's enough runway before the seasonal selling window (e.g., 7 months), running two ideas at once could work if neither is too complex.
- A quick "5-minute Google test" for existing competing products is a useful gut check before committing, though not exhaustive.
