# How to Validate a Home Service Business Idea in 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=796U9nZPINI | Published: 2024-07-14

Live-demo of a market-validation methodology, run against a dumpster rental business, comparing 6 midsize cities (Cincinnati, Cleveland, Toledo, OH vs. Madison WI, Grand Rapids MI, Fort Wayne IN, all pop. 2-400k).

## The validation process (generalizable to any home-service business/city)
1. **Build a comparison spreadsheet** across candidate cities: population, % real estate growth, % economic growth, average home value, average household income, home ownership rate, crime rate, zip code (used ChatGPT to source most figures, spot-checked a few for accuracy).
2. **Count competitors via Outscraper** (Google Maps scraper): scrape "<business type> <city>" per city, standardize phone numbers, do NOT dedupe multi-location chains (they represent real competitive density). Divide competitor count by population/households for a saturation %.
3. **Check Google LSA (Local Services Ads)**: search "<business> <city>" and count LSA/Google-Guaranteed listings (max 2 shown even if more exist — use as a rough signal only). Very low LSA presence = "arbitrage opportunity" since LSA is newer and has less market share/competition than SEO. Cited example from author's own tree business: LSA tree lead costs ~$50, ~50% close rate, avg job $800, ~50% margin → roughly $100 customer acquisition cost, ~$300 net profit per closed job.
4. **Check Thumbtack** by city zip code, but do NOT click through to "request a quote" — that charges the business being researched real money just for a fake research lead.
5. **Check Angie's List** landing page count per city (Angie's List scrapes Google Maps/Manta too, so it inflates the true count but is directionally useful; only paid listers show near the top).
6. **Cold-call every competitor with a phone number** in the shortlisted city, using a script-free "does anyone even answer?" test. Categorize as: answered live, answering service, forwarded to voicemail, no answer, no phone number listed at all (counts as a "no"/red flag). Rationale: in the author's own tree-trimming business, live phone-quote close rate ≈ 70% vs. cold call-in close rate ≈ 30%; missing a call on a $1,000 job at 30% close / 50% margin costs ~$150 in lost net profit — so a market full of no-answers/answering services signals opportunity (bad competitor service) or overload.
7. **Availability stress-test call**: call the top 2-3 remaining competitors and ask for a same-week/weekend booking (e.g., "3 dumpsters this weekend"). If they say yes readily during what should be peak season, that's a red flag the market isn't as busy as the other data suggests; if they say no/booked, that confirms demand.
8. **Mass-text remaining competitor phone numbers** to sanity-check via a different channel: upload scraped numbers to Sly Broadcast (or similar) to filter cell vs. landline, then use MessageMedia (an SMS platform) to send a single simple factual question (not a sales pitch) to ~27 numbers. Got a 20-40% response rate (7-10 of 27 replies), revealing real-time booking status (e.g., "fully booked," "no 20-yards available," "can you book online?").

## Key numeric findings from the demo (dumpster rental)
- Cincinnati: most saturated (17 Google Maps competitors, 52 Angie's List listings) — one company ("Rumy") has a strong multi-location foothold.
- Toledo: second most saturated.
- Madison, WI: only 4 Google Maps results initially, but one was actually a recycling center (not a real competitor) — true count 3. Best stats otherwise: highest average home value by far, lowest crime rate by far, highest household income, #2 real estate growth (12%) and #2 economic growth (5%), only one LSA advertiser city-wide, decent home ownership rate. Biggest suburb only 36k (1/8 the population) — no fragmented multi-city market effect.
- Fort Wayne, IN: 66% home ownership rate (vs. ~65% national average) — surprisingly strong; also looked attractive on saturation-per-population.
- Of Madison's remaining 2 real local competitors, one ("Kars Dumpsters") answered immediately, quoted openly on the call, and admitted low weekend demand (no weekend deliveries, Friday open) despite supposedly being "prime construction season" — a red flag suggesting the market may not be as tight as the desk data implied. The other ("Tread Lightly") went straight to voicemail (busy signal, i.e., a good sign for market demand).
- A company found in the search results ("Countrywide Dumpster Rental") turned out to be a multi-city lead-gen site built on programmatic SEO (same city-page template reused across Lakeland FL, San Bernardino, Madison, etc.), with fake AI-written Google reviews (recognizable by nonsensical repeated phrasing and full-name reviewer patterns) and an AI auto-responder — a pattern the author says his own company also uses (for lead-gen, not dumpsters) via programmatic SEO.

## Caveats / qualifiers
- Colder-climate cities (Madison, Toledo) have seasonality — dumpster/construction demand drops in snow season.
- Don't fully trust ChatGPT-sourced economic/demographic stats without spot-checking a few against real sources.
- Competitor counts from scrapers/Angie's List can include irrelevant businesses (recycling centers, junk removal companies that also offer roughly the same service) — manually vet before drawing conclusions.
- Beware confirmation bias if you're not location-agnostic and already want to live/operate in a specific city — "follow the data" rather than rationalizing a preferred location.
- This same 6-city dataset can be reused for validating multiple different home-service ideas in ~2.5 hours total after the first pass (don't get attached to one industry or one city — stay flexible on both).
- Roofing is called out as a much harder market to break into than dumpster rental/tree work: jobs are ~$20k vs. $200-$2,000, which attracts sophisticated operators and heavy marketing agencies, making it far more competitive.
- Whole process (research only, not launch) took about 1 hour for the dumpster example.

## Tools/platforms named
Outscraper (Google Maps competitor scraping), Google LSA / Google Guaranteed, Google Ads/performance/search ads, Thumbtack, Angie's List, Google Earth / Street View (to check how long a competitor's yard/lot has been in use, business age/legitimacy), Sly Broadcast (cell vs. landline number validation), MessageMedia (bulk SMS outreach), ChatGPT (city-level economic/demographic research), Google Sheets.
