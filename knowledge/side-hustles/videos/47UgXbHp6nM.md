# Quit Waiting. Start This Simple Business with $0
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=47UgXbHp6nM | Published: 2025-05-19

Part 1 of a 2-part episode with Brandon Doyle (owns an 8-figure marketing agency). Focused on zero/low-cost local marketing and lead-gen "growth hacks" for home service businesses, not a single business idea. Part 2 is video 4JgLTWSzGgc.

## Yard-sign QR code lead magnet
- For home service businesses that leave a yard sign after a job (landscaping, window cleaning, etc.): add a large QR code + "scan for a prize" in bold text.
- Mechanism: scanning captures name/email/phone in exchange for entry into a prize drawing (Amazon gift card, or an aggregated multi-business prize bundle from local partners).
- Debate on branding: including your logo builds brand recognition but showing *just* a bare QR code with no branding may drive more curiosity-driven scans ("why does Maryanne have this in her yard?") — trade-off is QR code alone gets more scans but lower buyer intent, vs. an explicit offer (e.g. "20% off at Applebee's") gets fewer but higher-intent scans.
- Make the QR code a trackable/custom affiliate link so you can attribute conversions per customer.

## "Neighbor Blitz" — coupon/referral hand-out program
- After completing a job, give the customer a stack of personalized coupons/door hangers (e.g. 5-6 copies) to hand to neighbors: "Mention you got this from [Customer Name] and get 10-20% off."
- Also drop door hangers directly on the 6 closest neighboring homes after finishing a job.
- Framed as bringing affiliate marketing into home services — low-lift, $0 out of pocket.

## Cash-incentivized social media referrals
- Directly ask satisfied customers (e.g. after a bounce house rental) to post about the service on their personal social media / neighborhood Facebook groups in exchange for a flat cash/gift-card incentive (example figures discussed: $100 Amazon gift card, plus an optional $20 per signup via a personal trackable affiliate link).
- Rationale/math example given: an average mom has ~700 Facebook friends, ~200 local, of which ~5-10% (10 people) might book a $500 bounce house rental = $5,000 revenue for a $100 gift card cost.
- Tactic: ask customers directly "are you in any local Facebook groups?" (e.g. "Dallas Moms Unite") and offer to hand them the incentive on the spot if they post while you're still there — described as needing the field rep to be comfortable sitting through ~20 seconds of initial awkwardness when asking.
- Tip: tell the poster to put the trackable link in the top comment (not the main post) so Facebook's algorithm doesn't deboost/suppress the post for containing a link.
- Once you have one success story/case study (e.g. "we paid Shelly $450 for one post"), use it to recruit other customers into the same referral program.
- Related idea: buy discounted restaurant gift cards directly from local restaurant owners (e.g. buy a $50 gift card for $30) to use as the referral/prize incentive, creating a local business cross-promotion.

## "Rock in a flyer" door-to-door marketing (credited to guest "Daniel," a pressure-washing/home-services business owner, referencing his own earlier appearance on episodes 123 and 127)
- Method: buy a bag of small landscaping rocks (~$10 at Home Depot), an open (not sealed/ziplock) small paper bag sized to match a folded flyer, put one rock + one flyer in each bag, staple shut, and throw it onto driveways in a targeted neighborhood (not knock on doors).
- Cost: roughly $0.10 per household plus ~10 seconds of labor to assemble each one; described as easy to batch-produce at home (e.g. at a folding table).
- Critical execution rule: do NOT answer/return calls that come in on day 1 — those tend to be angry recipients; start responding from day 2 onward.
- Reported results (self-reported by the guest, not independently verified): ~10-20% response rate and double-digit percent *close* rate when the right neighborhood is matched to the right service (e.g. specific neighborhoods targeted for Christmas lights vs. pressure washing).
- Key success factor: correct neighborhood targeting per service type.
- Suggested extensions: could be run as a standalone service offered to other home-service businesses, or reframed as a "giveaway/PSA" bundling multiple local business prizes (free bid + $100 to a local restaurant + $100 trampoline park + $50 flowers) rather than a direct sales pitch. Also suggested for non-home-service local business (e.g. a new restaurant flyering the immediately surrounding houses with a free-appetizer offer, A/B tested against direct mail).
- General framing: physical/unconventional marketing (a rock-weighted flyer on the driveway) stands out against the "endless" volume of generic direct mail people already ignore; direct mail is still claimed to work well when it hits someone at the right moment of intent (anecdotes: got direct mail for homeowners insurance and lawn spraying right when shopping for those services and called the company).

## LLM/ChatGPT SEO ("answer engine optimization")
- Thesis: most small businesses are not yet optimizing content to be surfaced by ChatGPT/LLM search, so being early is a low-effort competitive edge.
- Exact method demonstrated: prompt ChatGPT with something as simple as "I own [X].com landscaping. Can you write me quality in-depth blog posts for my website that will rank well in LLM searching on ChatGPT, tailored to specific and relevant searches?" — ChatGPT returns topic suggestions, a structuring approach, and (on confirmation) full draft blog posts to copy-paste onto your site/blog. Also suggested: have it draft an FAQ page based on your business's most common customer questions.
- Caveat raised by the host himself: it's unverified whether ChatGPT's stated content-preferences reflect its actual retrieval method — treated as a reasonable-but-unconfirmed heuristic, loosely compared to how Google surfaces structured "People also ask" content.
- Anecdote on LLM traffic: host's own YouTube channel now gets referral traffic from ChatGPT (small but growing volume); average view duration from that traffic was 3:54 vs. 3:00 channel average (~30% more engaged), and no other LLM (e.g. not other assistants) was sending him traffic at the time.
- Side anecdote: host asked ChatGPT to confirm a rumor that it was building a Shopify e-commerce checkout integration; it confirmed the rumor before OpenAI/Shopify made it public — cited as a viral tweet, offered as a curiosity, not a repeatable business tactic.

## Non-competing local business partnerships (cross-referral)
- Idea: businesses serving the same customer base but not competing (e.g. roofers + painters, Christmas light installers + landscapers, plumbing + HVAC) should formally partner to cross-promote to each other's customer lists via text/email — framed as low-lift (a few hours of setup) with high ROI, since customers are already used to having a service provider come to their home.
- Historical case study for "own the channel until negative ROI" marketing persistence: **AOL's free CD mail campaign** — at peak, AOL CDs made up ~50% of all CDs manufactured worldwide; total campaign spend >$300M; converted 1 new subscriber for every 3 CDs sent (33% "close rate"); grew from ~10,000 to ~35 million users over 3-4 years via this single channel; cost ~$1 per CD (incl. shipping) to acquire a customer paying ~$50/month; AOL as of 2021 was still generating ~$180M/year from a legacy "AOL Advantage" internet-security product sold to that original customer cohort. Distribution channels used: Blockbuster video sleeves, under Super Bowl seats, bundled in every new modem/printer sold, software boxes, cereal/popcorn boxes, grocery checkout lines/end caps.
- Modern analog cited: Crumbl Cookies' cross-brand marketing (Crumbl-scented lotion, Crumbl cereal, celebrity/influencer collabs e.g. Kylie Jenner) as an example of aggressive non-obvious brand partnership tactics worth copying.

## "Launch a business with $0" — piggyback-first launch strategy
- Explicit strategy for launching a brand-new home service business (e.g. painting, window cleaning) with no capital and no existing customer base: throw up a basic website, get a phone number staffed and ready, then approach an *already high-volume, non-competing* home service business (e.g. a landscaper serving ~50 customers/day) and offer to pay them a flat referral fee (example figure: $300) per customer they refer/mention to homeowners.
- Framed as capable of generating customers "starting on day two" with zero out-of-pocket product/inventory cost — the business is essentially built entirely on borrowed distribution from an established local provider's customer base.

## Caveats
- All ideas are framed by the hosts as untested-by-them "growth hacks" to try, not proven case studies with independent verification (except AOL's numbers, sourced from public reporting) — the pressure-washing "rock and flyer" response-rate figures are self-reported by the guest referenced, not independently verified.
