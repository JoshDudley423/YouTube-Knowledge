# The Most Overlooked $10K/M Business Anyone Can Start
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UCdWRg1KQLY | Published: 2025-08-03

Interview/case study with Kyler, 23, who rents washer/dryer sets to renters via Facebook Marketplace. Runs it ~5-10 hrs/week, zero employees, started Nov 2023.

## The business
- Buy or acquire used washer/dryer sets, install them in a renter's home/apartment, charge a flat month-to-month rental fee, handle all maintenance. Customer never owns it; it's not rent-to-own.
- Pitch to renters: cheaper and more convenient than a laundromat, no upfront cost, free delivery/install/maintenance.
- Why washers/dryers specifically (vs. seasonal rentals like boats, paddleboards, hot tubs he tried first): they're needed **year-round** regardless of season, and once installed they stay in place — no re-collection/cleaning cycle like paddleboards required.

## Numbers
- **Pricing**: used sets rent for $60-85/month; new sets $85/month. Minimum rental term: 6 months. First payment due day of install; auto-billed via Stripe on a fixed day (27th) each month.
- **Startup capital**: technically **can be started with $0** (using free/scavenged units + a borrowed truck/trailer), though Kyler's own first purchase was 3 new sets at ~$1,000/set (~$450 washer + $450 dryer + tax) = **~$3,000**, funded by "half or more" of his life savings.
- **Unit economics**: used sets typically cost $0-$200 (many effectively free — see sourcing below); a used set pays for itself in **4-5 months** of rental income.
- Revenue trajectory: 4 installs in month 1 (Nov 2023), 17 in month 2, 13 in month 3 (~35 sets total in first 3 months) → roughly **$2,400/month recurring revenue** within the first two months (calculated: ~$80/set avg × ~32 sets).
- Current scale (~2 years in, at time of filming): **~120 active rented sets**, ~170 total ever delivered, working **5-10 hours/week**. Referenced comparable operator with **900 sets** doing ~$60,000/month while working only 2-3 days/week.
- **Churn**: extremely low — ~50 canceled sets over 24 months on a base that grew to 120+, calculated at roughly **1-1.5% monthly churn** (vs. ~10% considered normal/doable for subscription businesses). Main cancellation reason: customer moves out.
- **Service calls**: ~1-2 per week (roughly 3% of the installed base per month); a service call takes the same 30-45 minutes as an install. Many issues are resolved by phone (e.g., "check if your dryer vent is clogged").
- **Insurance**: $700/year (~$60/month) — operated 3 months without insurance before getting it.
- **Door-hanger marketing math**: door hangers cost ~5 cents each; hanging ~200 in an apartment complex takes ~30 minutes and reportedly generates 3-4 leads. At a planned 10,000 hangers (~$500 total spend) with an assumed conservative 1% conversion, projected to add ~100 sets and roughly double the business (goal: go from $10K/month to $20K/month for ~$500 in materials).

## Sourcing units (how to get them cheap or free)
1. **Search Facebook Marketplace for "free appliances" or "broken appliances"** — high turnover market (people moving, upgrading, sets breaking) means 5-6+ free/near-free listings appear regularly in any given area; often only one washer or dryer in a "free" pair is actually broken.
2. **Partner with used-appliance delivery/liquidation businesses**: Kyler did paid deliveries for local used-appliance shops (charging the shop's customers directly, e.g., ~$150 for a washer delivery/install, ~$300 for a fridge that "only takes an hour" to plug in) and in exchange got early access to inventory and picked up old units being replaced, which were "technically free" after fixing the one that wasn't already working.
3. **Big-box store delivery drivers** (Costco, Lowe's, Home Depot) will often sell customers' old appliances they're hauling away for ~$15-20 each as side income — giving your phone number to a driver you meet can turn into an ongoing supply relationship (drivers proactively texting when new appliances come available).
4. **Buy wholesale from small "mom and pop" used-appliance resellers** who already have relationships with big-box delivery contracts — approach them, tell them you're a "property manager" needing many sets (to avoid tipping your hand and getting a markup or unsolicited "this won't work" pushback), and negotiate a bulk handshake deal (Kyler's: sets guaranteed for 2 years, paid off in 4-5 months of rental income — described as "zero risk").
5. **Older, pre-2013 "Whirlpool direct drive" washers/dryers** are specifically recommended over new units: built before efficiency-mandate design changes, extremely durable/simple (no suspension, doesn't wobble), parts cost $30 or less and have been standardized for ~30+ years, most non-Whirlpool-branded units (Kenmore, etc.) are actually built by Whirlpool on the same platform (~70% of the market). Average price for a set: ~$200 ($100 each); a well-warrantied set: ~$300. New machines by contrast are described as designed with planned obsolescence and don't last.
6. A dedicated appliance-scrapper contact (found via local classifieds like KSL, a Utah site) supplies daily photos of incoming units at a storage yard; front-load washers are too heavy for this business model and are instead resold as-is (~$200, sell within a day); top-load units are either cleaned/rented (~$35 all-in cost) or parted out for spares (e.g., one broken dryer can supply parts to fix ~8 others).

## The "PRB" process (Post, Rent, Buy)
1. **Post** the unit for rent on Facebook Marketplace *before you own it* — use a real (or borrowed) photo of a washer/dryer set that "looks real" (not a blurry stock screenshot, since users/the algorithm can tell), list at the lowest end of the $60-85 range, title like "Washer and Dryer" or "Washer and Dryer for rent," description built around a pain point ("Never go to the laundromat again... rent month-to-month, free delivery, install and maintenance").
2. **Rent** — respond to inquiries FAST (same day; delayed response loses the lead to a competing listing) and pre-write templated answers to the common questions (price, minimum term, delivery/install/maintenance included, first payment due at install).
3. **Buy** only after you've secured a paying renter — validates demand before spending capital, described as making the business "zero risk."
- Uses multiple Facebook profiles (different emails, one pseudonym used) to post the same listing across different ZIP codes/cities and in local buy-sell-trade Facebook groups (e.g., a 30,000-member county classifieds group), which reportedly increases visibility (unclear exact mechanism, attributed to algorithm boosting). Pays Facebook's ~$12/month "verified badge" fee, which reportedly makes account bans easier to appeal/reverse (especially if you mention you also run paid ads).
- Additional lead-gen channel: **door hangers** targeted at apartment complexes known not to already provide in-unit laundry.
- Install process: knock on door, explain 30-min install, have renter sign a (non-legally-binding, Google Docs) contract, install and test both machines (run water, run dryer 30 sec, check for leaks), explain maintenance is free but not misuse damage, take a credit card for autopay, give a 2-minute usage walkthrough including load-size limits and lint-trap maintenance.

## Scaling / logistics
- Equipment needed: only a drill and channel-lock pliers as tools; a vehicle with a trailer hitch (~$250 to install at U-Haul) or truck/trailer rental (~$25/day from Home Depot) — no need to own a dedicated truck. Kyler's actual rig: a 260,000-mile Toyota Highlander towing a small ~10x6 ft trailer that holds 3 sets (6 units) at a time, sufficient to have grown to 120+ sets.
- Removed units are pressure-washed and typically re-rented within 2-3 days.
- Business is location-agnostic per the guest: works in small towns (~2,000-10,000 population — example given, Burley, Idaho, where a friend has 25 sets rented with strong demand) up through major metros; framed as "anywhere there's people, this business will work."

## Caveats / risk notes
- No formal legal contract or insurance for the first several months (host flags this explicitly as risky, though it worked out).
- Being unresponsive to Marketplace messages loses leads almost immediately — speed-to-respond is described as the single most important factor in conversion.
- Guest explicitly disclaims a "silver spoon" narrative: while he did use family-owned garage/storage space, he states none of the actual customers came from family connections and the model works from a rented $80/month 10x10 storage unit if you don't have free storage.
- Guest sells a paid "school community" (mentioned but not the focus of this note) covering deeper tactics (paid Facebook ads, door-hanger templates, Stripe/Google Business setup, building a website via Replit, sourcing/repairing/flipping machines).
