# My 3 Favorite Business Ideas this Week
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pWtoX0HWXAE | Published: 2024-12-28

"HoldCo Bros" episode: Chris Koerner and Nik (Cofounders/Nikonomics) riff on three business ideas.

## Idea 1: College tuition negotiation service
- Premise: most people don't know tuition is negotiable (panel guesses ~4-5% negotiate, ~5% even know it's possible).
- Business model: charge $0 upfront to families/students; take a percentage of whatever tuition savings you negotiate (pure alignment of incentives, "I only eat when you eat").
- Comparable/adjacent proven model: medical bill negotiation-as-a-service already exists (Andrew Wilkins ran one successfully, sold it, said he'd do it again).
- How the negotiation is framed: position yourself as an advocate for a specific admitted student ("she's got into 4 schools, this is her top choice, but budget is X, your price is Y — what can you do?") rather than a bulk/group negotiator; leverage that university enrollment is falling while tuition keeps rising, so admissions offices are more receptive near enrollment deadlines when they have quotas to fill.
- Customer acquisition approaches discussed:
  - Direct/local: same "warm knock" logic as a line-striping business owner cited elsewhere on the show — high school seniors are publicly identifiable (school websites, sports events), so cold outreach is actually "warm."
  - Piggyback strategy: partner with existing college admissions-prep/test-prep companies (SAT/ACT prep, essay coaching) who already have relationships with admitted families but don't offer negotiation; offer to work their referred clients for free and give them a cut (e.g., 1/3) of your negotiation fee.
  - Build initial testimonials for free/at minimal charge with friends/family who have high-school seniors, then use those testimonials to start charging locals 10-30%.
- Adjacent idea mentioned: cold-emailing parents to help kids get athletic scholarships (an Instagram-based operator reportedly does this profitably).
- Caveat: requires being a genuinely strong/aggressive negotiator; Koerner says he personally wouldn't be good at this because he'd cave too easily against admissions staff.
- Verdict: Koerner would "marry" this idea (low cost to start, scalable, proven negotiation-as-a-service pattern applied to an underserved niche).

## Idea 2: Liquidating $200K of dead apparel inventory (a friend's failed launch)
- Backstory: a friend invested $200k into launching an apparel brand (had it manufactured in China), then the co-founding relative backed out, leaving him with ~$200k cost basis / potentially $800k-$1M retail-value worth of shirts, hoodies, socks, sunglasses sitting in his garage.
- Core lesson/framework: when starting any business, people gravitate to the "easy/fun" tasks first (buying inventory, designing a logo, filing an LLC) instead of the hardest task — getting revenue/customers. Test sales viability *before* buying $200k of product.
- Options considered for the dead inventory:
  1. Sell to a liquidator (estimated payout ~$10k for all of it — low, but zero effort).
  2. Give products to a few influencers to wear (paid placement) to try to generate sales.
  3. Turn it into a marketing exercise: give inventory to 5 people, whoever sells the most wins a prize — builds brand/audience via competition.
  4. Give away free (like the "God Is Dope" model — see below) at a targeted local venue (e.g., a football game) to create organic buzz, since it's dead inventory anyway.
  5. Hand the inventory to a hungry local high schooler/young entrepreneur to sell and let them keep the proceeds/learn the ropes.
  6. Attach a $10 tag-printer QR code (Amazon) to each item linking to a Shopify page telling the brand's backstory, to try to manufacture the "meaningful brand story" the product currently lacks.
- Case study cited: "God Is Dope" apparel brand — founder in Atlanta designed one T-shirt, gave away hundreds for free at a university, then sold at $5 cost (no profit) once demand appeared; went organically viral; grew into a ~$30M/year business. Success attributed to: affordable price, good design, polarizing/resonant message, mass-market appeal, and a strong distribution/marketing strategy. Explicit caveat: the friend's inventory *can't* replicate all of this because it's already manufactured/branded with a fairly generic logo (a single letter "M") and no attached story — so only 1-2 of the "success factors" are recoverable after the fact.
- Verdict: Koerner would "marry" this idea for someone getting into entrepreneurship (near-zero cost since it's already sunk, good sandbox for learning Facebook/Google ads and e-commerce).

## Idea 3: University "parent concierge" service ("Dean List")
- Origin story: a University of South Carolina student got an MIP (minor in possession) charge; was advised that visiting a hospital instead of an on-campus urgent care avoids mandatory reporting to the university (avoiding expulsion). He later built a website called "Dean List" around monetizing this kind of insider knowledge.
- Concept: subscription concierge service for wealthy/anxious ("helicopter") parents of college students, positioned like an insurance policy — local insider knowledge (where to eat during parents' weekend, which bars to avoid, what to do if a kid gets an MIP, occasional "check on my kid" requests) for a flat annual fee (~$500/year floated).
- Alternative, lower-effort framing: aggregate existing scattered parent Facebook groups (e.g., "Parents of University of X") via Zapier into a paid weekly/quarterly newsletter or PDF info-product (~$100/semester) instead of building a live concierge/community, to avoid the overhead of running a community.
- Caveat raised: unclear if there's proven demand — no existing competitor was identified, panel was unsure people would want live personal contact vs. just information; one host explicitly "killed" this idea for lack of proven demand ("I'd rather be copying someone that's doing this").
- Verdict: split — one host would "date" it (worth testing), the other would "kill" it.

## Panel's overall rankings (marry/date/kill)
- Host 1: marry = apparel liquidation; date = university concierge; kill = a farmers-market food idea (mentioned only briefly, not detailed).
- Host 2: marry = tuition negotiation; date = a farmers-market pizza-selling idea (not detailed); kill = university concierge idea.
