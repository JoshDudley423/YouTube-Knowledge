# Fastest Way to Copy Winning Digital Products (Picsart)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cBdJajFcSzg | Published: 2025-04-29

- Business idea: sell digital products (printables, digital wallpapers, planners, etc.) online by reverse-engineering what's already proven to sell on Etsy, then using AI tools to create your own variant fast.
- Digital products have lower friction to sell than physical products but face much more competition for that same reason — pick a product with the right supply/demand balance rather than an oversaturated category (example given: personal finance spreadsheets, "10,000 other guys" already selling them).

Process for finding what to sell ("reverse engineering" on Etsy):
1. Search a broad category (e.g., "printable"), filter to "instant download," sort by top reviews to see what's actually selling (ignore Etsy's personalized/algorithmic homepage, which is biased by your own browsing/purchase history).
2. Look for sellers with a large number of total reviews across their shop but very few reviews on any single item — these "diamonds in the rough" indicate hidden bestsellers not yet widely copied. Example: a shop with 87,000 reviews total but only 7 on one specific listing.
3. Check a shop's total item count vs. total sales/reviews — a shop with fewer SKUs but huge review counts (e.g., 54,000 reviews, 452,000 sales, only 1,600 products) indicates concentrated winners worth studying.
4. Cross-check category trend strength with Google Trends (e.g., confirmed "junk journal" search interest exploding, while "steampunk" was flat/declining despite steampunk items showing up in the same Etsy search).
5. Growth-hack shortcut: Etsy used to expose a "bestsellers" category (since removed) but the underlying data is still reachable — search with the "Star Seller" filter, then manually edit the resulting URL, replacing the word "star" with the word "best" to reveal each seller's actual best-selling items page.
6. Once you find a winning design, save/screenshot the image and rebuild a similar one in Picsart (an AI-first, Canva-like tool that supports bulk AI image editing) — steps shown: "AI enhance" to boost image quality above what's typically seen on Etsy listings, and the AI image generator to create a new image in the same style (e.g., a rabbit in grass in the same illustration style as a winning listing), then use that in your own product photos.
7. Note images/artwork work far better with current AI generation tools than text-heavy designs — AI still struggles with rendering text well.

Launching/testing:
- Etsy ads can be run on a printable for effectively $0 product cost — cost to acquire a customer is just ad spend, since digital products have no COGS.
- Alternative: buy an existing Etsy account with established feedback/history to benefit from algorithmic trust, rather than starting from zero.
- Facebook Marketplace recommended as the best low-cost place to A/B test multiple design variants: list several designs (e.g., 5 rabbit illustrations) under identical titles/prices/descriptions and see which gets the most views/clicks/messages before committing.
- Alternative test method: run small Facebook ads (~$20 budget each) with the same ad copy but different product images/designs, see which design gets the most clicks, then move only the winning design over to Etsy.
- Recommended time investment: spend 4-6 hours reverse-engineering multiple categories (printables, coloring books/pages, etc.) to find several "diamonds in the rough" before starting.

Guiding philosophy quoted from the video: start by copying an existing successful product/design as closely as possible rather than innovating up front — "everything I change increases the likelihood that I'll have suboptimal success" — only start iterating/differentiating once you have real sales data.

Tools/platforms named: Etsy (primary reverse-engineering and selling platform — "the godfather" of digital product marketplaces due to data/scale), Gumroad, Shopify, Creative Market (alternative selling platforms), Picsart (AI image editing/generation), Google Trends, Facebook Marketplace, Facebook ads, ChatGPT/Manus AI agent (mentioned as usable to scrape Etsy bestseller data automatically).

Caveats: the digital products space is described as "jam-packed with scammers and gurus selling courses" — the creator explicitly says he has no course to sell, this is free info. Don't fixate on a single category; the "printable" search shown here surfaced mostly junk-journal sellers by coincidence, but thousands of other digital-product subcategories exist (coloring pages, stickers, homework worksheets, etc.) worth the same reverse-engineering treatment.
