# This Simple Rental Business Could Change Your Life!
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yTc3M4oMdnQ | Published: 2025-05-30

Interview with Martina and Aidan, owners of Castles of Dallas — a bounce house/inflatable rental business Aidan bought from his former employer 4 years ago (business itself is ~10 years old). Now runs 40+ inflatable units plus tables, chairs, and tents, and also does repairs and in-house manufacturing.

## The business / idea
- Buy inflatable bounce houses (new or used) and rent them out for parties, mainly birthdays. Add-on revenue from tables, chairs, tents, heaters, and window AC units for events.
- Started with 2 used inflatables bought off Facebook Marketplace for $400 each, ~6 years before this interview.
- Ancillary revenue streams: repairing units (for yourself and for competitors — "the more competitors you have, the better you do, because you're selling to them"), and manufacturing new units in-house (they now make ~15 of their 40 units themselves).

## Startup / unit economics
- Entry point: buy one used bounce house for ~$1,600, rent it out at ~$400/rental. Renting it 4 times pays it off in month one; after that it generates ~$1,600/month topline (~$18,000/year revenue from one unit), minus gas and time.
- Scale math (their framing, with stated caveats about seasonality/utilization assumptions): 10 bounce houses ≈ $180,000/year revenue if kept fully rented and non-seasonal — margins are described as "really high," with a ballpark of $100,000+/year profit achievable for a rental-focused operation.
- Startup capital claimed as low as $1,600 for a first unit.
- Units last 7-8 years with regular use (every weekend) if maintained/repaired; after that the material is too worn to be worth repairing.
- New tables cost ~$80 (e.g., Sam's Club "Lifetime" 6-ft tables) and rent for $5 each, or $12 as a table+6-chairs package. Chairs cost $14 each (sold in boxes of 10) and rent for $1.50/chair regardless of duration (few hours or overnight) — the box of 10 pays for itself after 10 rentals.
- More than 75% of their rentals include a table/chair add-on.
- Average rental ticket: $400-$500 (a plain bounce house starts around $100 and scales up from there); a big multi-unit event (3 large inflatables + ~100 chairs + 10 tables) can bill $1,000-$1,500 for a few hours of active setup/takedown labor (they do overnight rentals).
- On-site staffing/attendance during an event (if required by the venue) costs the client an extra $100-$200/hour.
- Manufacturing cost comparison: a unit that costs $1,700-$2,000 to import from China (before shipping delays) can reportedly be made domestically for cheaper with the same materials/thread/weight spec, plus you avoid shipping costs (ordering a single unit rather than a full container could add ~$400 in shipping) and can offer in-house warranty/repair, which importers can't easily match.
- Insurance: $7,000-$10,000/year (Texas). Described as a real but necessary cost — the hosts frame liability/insurance fear as often just an excuse people use to avoid starting.

## Process / how to run it
- Volume: during summer they run 25-30 rental jobs per weekend.
- Sourcing units: buy used and see "what hits" (what rents most) to inform future purchases; maintain a variety of styles/colors/sizes to serve more customer requests and cross-sell/divert clients between similar units when one type is booked out.
- Recommended starter unit: a bounce house with a detachable slide (can be rented dry as a bounce house alone, or with slide, and works as wet or dry) — described as the most in-demand item; if buying a second unit, get something different (e.g., add a separate water slide) for variety rather than a duplicate.
- Recommendation for new entrants: buy NEW units when starting out rather than used — used units have unknown history/remaining lifespan, so a new unit with warranty and proper care lasts longer and avoids early replacement costs.
- Logistics: closed vans preferred for transport (protected, spacious, fewer trips needed) but trailers and box trucks also used; a minivan (seats folded) can work for a very small-scale solo operator. Dollies used to move units (bounce houses weigh 80-120 lbs, water slides/obstacle courses 200-250 lbs).
- Maintenance cadence: weekly cleaning/inspection cycle starting Mondays — check for rips/holes before and after rentals.
- Staffing: currently 2 employees plus unpaid family help (siblings, nieces/nephews, the owners' kids) for busy periods — a family-run operation, not using paid contractors.
- Marketing that works: Facebook Marketplace (still a valued lead source), a business website, Google (organic + paid Google Ads — they say paid ads work well), social media posts/photos of every setup (documents work even repetitively, builds a following), and physical location visibility — they deliberately inflate a display unit on a busy road during the week to catch drive-by attention, calling out that this "free" advertising can be worth more than the cost of a cheaper, less-visible location (potentially saving ~$2,000/month in Google ad spend).
- Business acquisition angle: Aidan was an employee of the previous owner, who was retiring and specifically sought a buyer who understood and cared about the business, then approached him directly; he financed the purchase with help from friends and family. Host notes ~10,000 baby boomers retire daily, ~10% of whom are business owners, and most such small businesses simply close rather than sell because owners don't know how/where to find a buyer or broker — an acquisition opportunity for others watching for it.

## Caveats / risks
- Seasonality: business slows outside of favorable weather/event seasons; adding categories (tables/chairs/water slides/tents/heaters/ACs) reduces seasonality by giving customers non-bounce-house reasons to rent year-round (e.g., weddings, winter heaters).
- Rental demand dropped post-COVID compared to the pandemic-era spike (when people rented for home use), and competition has increased significantly — more entrants building newer, more "sophisticated" units.
- Being late or unresponsive is called out as the single biggest reputational risk in this business — simple reliability (answering the phone, showing up on time) is what separates operators, since a bounce house that arrives after a 3pm party has started is a lost/damaged relationship.
- Insurance/liability is a genuine cost center (Texas: $7-10K/year) and a common excuse people use to avoid starting, per the host's framing — but going without insurance means losing bookings at venues that require it.
- A one-stop-shop model (renting many categories) increases sales conversion (say "yes" more often) but adds operational complexity compared to a streamlined single-category operation.
