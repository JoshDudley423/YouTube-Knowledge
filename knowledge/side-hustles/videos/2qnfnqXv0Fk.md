# How to Make $40k in 2 Months with Christmas Decorations
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2qnfnqXv0Fk | Published: 2024-10-25

Chris Koerner's solo walkthrough of how he would launch a seasonal Christmas decoration business from scratch, inspired by a viral Instagram porch-decoration setup.

## The idea
- A seasonal (roughly Nov-Dec) home-decoration service: build and install custom Christmas decor pieces (e.g., a decorative resin planter with a dowel-rod centerpiece/tower structure) on customers' porches, similar in concept to the porch-pumpkin business referenced (Heather Torres — grew hers into a $1M/year business at 40% net margins working ~4 months a year; full interview linked as further reference on Koerner's channel).
- Title claim of "$40k in 2 months" reflects the seasonal, fast-turnaround nature of this business type (Halloween pumpkins / Christmas decor), based on the referenced comparable (porch pumpkin) business, not a specific verified number for this exact idea (speaker is explicit this specific Christmas niche is untested by him personally — "I've never launched this business... you're going to be paving new ground here").

## Materials / sourcing (concrete)
- Buy resin planters at Home Depot (try a few different shapes/sizes to test).
- Use play sand (not concrete — too heavy, ruins the planter, never comes back out) to hold a wooden dowel rod upright inside the planter; sand is reusable year to year and easy to transport in 5-gallon buckets.
- Buy wooden dowel rods at Lowe's/Home Depot.
- Buy shipping/storage boxes from Uline instead of Home Depot/Lowe's — dramatically cheaper (from ~$0.40/box up to a couple dollars depending on size; large 36" boxes available); sold in bulk quantities of 10, 15, 25, sometimes 50; local will-call pickup avoids shipping cost where available.
- Off-season storage: a 10x10 storage unit costs roughly $100/month if you don't have garage space.

## Customer acquisition tactics (the core value of this video)
1. **Build samples first**: build a few different decoration styles on your own front porch (or a friend/family member's, if yours isn't presentable) purely to generate marketing media.
2. **Cheap professional photos/video**: use Thumbtack to find a photographer competing on price in your local market — look for one with 4+ star reviews but not the most popular/expensive listing; describe the project and ask for a flat-fee quote for simple before/after photos and video.
3. **Local Facebook groups**: post the before/after media in local Facebook "mom groups" and buy/sell groups, framed casually ("what do you guys think, is this cute?") rather than as a direct sales pitch — people will organically ask how it was done; that's the opening to reveal it's a paid service. Push interested people to a phone call rather than staying in DMs/text, since closing is much easier live.
4. **Facebook Marketplace**: post pictures/video (lead with video — Marketplace's algorithm favors video first). Important nuance: Facebook Marketplace only allows product listings, not service listings — title the listing like a product ("Christmas Decorations") and keep the text description vague about it being a service, while using Canva-style text overlays on the images to actually sell the service; listings that look too obviously like an interior-design/service offering risk being deleted (not a full account ban, just listing removal).
5. **Personal Instagram**: post regularly about the process regardless of existing follower count — both platforms are increasingly algorithm-driven, meaning reach depends more on content quality/uniqueness than follower count; use trending audio/filters; document the build-in-public journey rather than posting hard sales pitches; ask for reviews and referrals explicitly once you have your first ~5 customers (referrals don't come unprompted).
6. **"Piggyback" partnerships with adjacent home-service providers**: approach Christmas light installers, landscapers, pressure washers, window washers, and car detailers (many landscaping/pressure-washing operators are also seasonal Christmas light installers, but generally won't be doing this specific decoration service) and offer either: (a) a pure referral fee — $100 per customer they refer who converts, with the referring business keeping 100% of their own separate services/revenue, or (b) a white-label arrangement — the partner quotes and collects the full job price directly from the homeowner (e.g. quotes $400), then pays the decoration business a cut (e.g. $300) to actually perform the work as an unnamed subcontractor.
7. **Cold outreach via scraped lead lists**: use Outscraper (Google Maps scraper) to pull leads for adjacent service categories (there's no direct "Christmas lights" category, so search under "landscaper" etc.) filtered by zip code/city/county — exports a spreadsheet with phone numbers, emails, names, addresses, business names, and websites; described as a fast way to generate a large volume of leads for the piggyback partnership outreach above.

## Pricing approach
- Deliberately under-price at launch to land the first customers/testimonials/reviews (still ensuring profitability from the very first order), then raise prices as demand and reputation build — do not lock in end-state pricing on day one.
- Suggested starting price point: ~$300 for the customer to keep all decoration materials at season's end; if the business retrieves/reuses the materials afterward instead, offer a 10-15% discount off that price — but treat this as a hypothesis to validate via customer feedback (some customers may resist giving materials back even with a discount, others may expect it as standard).

## Caveats / risks
- Entire niche (Christmas decor specifically) is explicitly untested by the speaker — extrapolated from an adjacent, verified business model (Halloween porch pumpkins), not a proven case study itself.
- Deliberately seasonal (roughly Nov-Dec business window) with a heavy front-loaded logistics/storage burden between seasons.
- Facebook Marketplace listing rules require careful wording to avoid removal (service listings disguised as product listings).
- Speaker frames market size as effectively unsaturated: estimates a city of 50,000 people could support 10-15 businesses like this simultaneously ("law of abundance").
