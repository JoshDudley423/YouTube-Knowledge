# 8 Best ChatGPT Tools to Make Money With (Ranked)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E8ARq-4ous8 | Published: 20250808

Chris Koerner solo episode ranking ChatGPT's 8 core products/models worst-to-best by money-making potential, with a monetization angle for each. Filmed just before GPT-5 launched; an on-screen update near the end folds GPT-5 in as effectively combining all eight into one.

## The business idea (ranked worst → best, each with a monetization angle)
8. **GPT-4.1/mini/nano**: cheapest, smallest model — best for building simple "ChatGPT wrapper" apps/sites/chatbots branded as something else. Monetize via bulk content services (product descriptions at scale), small-business website chatbots, social-media caption generation for agencies, or cold-email/outreach copy at scale.
7. **GPT-4o**: the "jack of all trades" (multimodal — talk, see, hear); much better answers via text/voice-to-text input than live voice chat. Monetize via virtual assistant services ($15-30/hr), real-estate virtual tours, customer-service automation ($200-500/mo/client).
6. **Sora (video generation)**: commercial-quality video from text, but the "wow factor" is fading fast as AI video gets commoditized. Monetize real-estate listing photos converted into walkthrough videos — pictures are common, professional videos are still rare.
5. **GPT-4.5**: the "creative genius" — best for big-picture strategic thinking, weaker to price/defend since output quality is subjective. Monetize as a much-cheaper alternative to a $10-50K brand-strategy consulting engagement (charge 90-95% less, deliver via a Zoom call using AI-generated pre-planned questions).
4. **Deep Research**: combines web search + analysis + report generation — positioned as replacing $5K-$500K consulting engagements at ~80% the quality for ~80% less cost. Gets most valuable when you upload a client's own proprietary/internal data (e.g. sales performance) that the public internet doesn't have. Monetize: M&A due diligence ($50-500K/deal), market research reports ($2,500-250,000/report), competitive intelligence ($10-100K/study).
3. **o3 / o4-mini (reasoning models)**: best for complex coding, advanced math, technical analysis — a "PhD-level" reasoning engine, slower but more accurate. Monetize via code audits, technical documentation, data-science consulting, patent research, trading-algorithm development — technical work commands high, easily-justified budgets.
2. **ChatGPT Agent**: combines deep research with actual task automation (books flights, places orders, handles transactions) — a true "AI employee," not just an advisor. Framed as the single biggest labor-replacement opportunity: could let a company that planned to hire 100 people over 2 years hire only 10. Exercise suggested: log every non-complex task you do for a day, then ask ChatGPT to help automate/outsource each one. Monetize examples: a travel-booking agency where you just supervise the automated bookings; a competitor-price-monitoring service for e-commerce brands (auto-generates a pricing spreadsheet overnight).
1. **GPT-OSS (open-source, local)**: ranked #1 "by a landslide" — lets a company (or individual) run a private ChatGPT-equivalent locally with zero data leaving their own servers, solving the data-privacy objection that stops most companies from adopting AI at all. Monetize as a "private company GPT" implementation service: charge a large upfront fee (thousands to tens of thousands, potentially per GB/TB of data) plus an ongoing monthly fee to maintain/expand it — pitched as the AI era's version of the gold-rush/oil-rush opportunity, with very high client switching costs once implemented (a competitor later offering 20-40% cheaper re-implementation is unlikely to win the account).
- **Post-GPT-5 update** (filmed hours after GPT-5 launched): GPT-5 auto-picks the best underlying model per task, has a much larger context window than prior versions, hallucinates less, and is described as strong at both coding and writing simultaneously (previously assumed to be a tradeoff).

## Numbers
- Custom AI-model-training projects for companies reportedly cost clients $4M-$2M+ per project today (implying room for a much cheaper independent operator to compete).
- Data-privacy consulting search interest cited as trending "up and to the right" on Google Trends.
- If a $100K/yr employee's tasks can be replaced, the pitch framing is charging the client ~$20K/yr for the AI-driven replacement.

## Tools/platforms named
- ChatGPT products discussed: GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Sora, GPT-4.5, Deep Research, o3/o4-mini, ChatGPT Agent, GPT-OSS, GPT-5.
- Comparison mentions: Claude, Gemini, Grok (used for cross-referencing deep-research prompts).

## Caveats / risks
- As of filming, GPT-4.5 was already discontinued (per an on-screen correction) and GPT-5's real context window is 400K tokens, not the 1 million initially rumored — this space moves fast enough that specific model specs go stale within weeks.
- Only ~2% of businesses were estimated to even know ChatGPT Agent exists at time of filming, and almost none of those were using it — a narrow early window before this becomes common knowledge.
- The GPT-4.1 "wrapper" tier is explicitly called the least defensible: cheap to build, therefore heavy competition and thin margins.
