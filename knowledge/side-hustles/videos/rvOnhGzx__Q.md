# The Credit Card Side Hustle No One Talks About
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rvOnhGzx__Q | Published: 2026-05-16

Interview with Colin Stroud, 26, founder of Go Somewhere -- a credit card rewards/points consulting business for business owners.

- Idea: "credit card rewards consultant" -- help business owners (a) build a credit card strategy to maximize points earned on their existing spending, and (b) redeem/book trips with points they're already sitting on ("travel agent for people with points"). Not churning (opening many cards for sign-up bonuses) -- focused on optimizing 1-2 cards for people already spending large sums monthly.
- Origin: discovered the model by watching another person on LinkedIn doing the same thing (cohort education, 1:1 consults, trip planning) and going semi-viral; explicitly says "I essentially copied him," and considers the niche to have "plenty of market share" for multiple operators. Started as a side hustle (~15 months) while working a full-time insurance job, before knowing much about the space himself -- learned via blogs and ChatGPT/Claude over "a weekend" and describes anyone being able to reach expert-relative-to-the-public level quickly this way.
- Pricing/offers:
  - Main offer: $875 one-time consulting package -- 20-min discovery call, 45 min prep/research, 75-min consult call reviewing personal+business spend and building an optimized card strategy, a written strategy doc, email/text implementation support, plus planning one future trip using the resulting points (executed whenever the client is ready, sometimes months later).
  - Secondary offer: trip-booking/research service open to anyone (no consulting needed) -- $50 deposit to start, then a flat fee of $200-$600 depending on trip complexity (average $300-400) once a points-funded trip itinerary is found and booked.
  - Also earns credit-card affiliate commissions (~$400 per approved premium card signup) by recommending cards to clients/audience/newsletter -- described as a significant and fast-growing revenue line once discovered.
- Revenue numbers: first $1,000 month at ~6 months in (March 2024, side hustle, full-time job still held); quit job in November 2024 after a $13,000 month (mostly profit); full first year as a full-time business (2025): $231,000 gross revenue, ~$40,000 expenses (~$40K = software, some travel, and two hired contractors who now do ~90% of the trip-research work) -- roughly $190K net profit. Year-to-date (through April 2026): $89,000 revenue. Running costs before scaling: as low as ~$100/month in software subscriptions.
- Time cost per $875 client: roughly 20 min discovery + 45 min prep + 75 min call + 40 min follow-up email, plus variable follow-up trip-planning time later (can be 20 minutes or up to 4+ hours) -- some clients never actually use the trip-planning benefit, which is pure profit for the consultant, though he notes many clients are satisfied just from improving their earn rate even without ever booking a trip.
- Customer acquisition: 100% inbound from LinkedIn organic content, posting 1-2x/day, 6 days/week; also referrals; zero outbound sales experience/effort. Built a 7,000-person email list almost entirely sourced from LinkedIn traffic. Also tried X/Twitter without much traction yet.
- Bootstrapping/proof-of-concept tactic used before having real clients: research and "mock book" (fully work out, but don't actually pay for) an impressive trip deal using points, then post about the process/findings publicly ("here's what I just learned/found") as content -- one such post (finding a complex Turkish Airlines/United deal to Hawaii) went to ~100,000 impressions from only ~1,000 followers and validated market interest before the business had real traction; recommends this as a way to build credibility and a portfolio of "case studies" with zero capital and no clients required.
- Customer profile: mostly blue-collar/local business owners (e.g., construction, medical practices) who spend heavily on cards but don't optimize because they don't have time/interest -- not typically "internet business" or already-savvy travel-hacker types (who tend to already know the tricks and are less receptive/valuable as clients). Example case: his father's medical practice was spending $40K/month on a 1% cash-back local bank card; switching to a 50/50 Capital One + Chase business card strategy plus a limited-time Chase points-doubling offer earned over 1 million points in year one, funding a ~$9,000 no-cost trip (ocean-view resort suite in the Dominican Republic).
- Concrete card/points tips given in the episode:
  - Focus on earn rate (points per dollar), not card perks/benefits -- perks (lounge access, insurance) don't change based on how much you spend, so a high-spend business owner should optimize for the best multiplier on their top spend category.
  - Example: Amex Business Gold earns 4x on advertising spend but caps at $150K/year (drops to 1x after); Chase Sapphire Reserve for Business (newer product, launched roughly within the past year of this recording) earns an uncapped 3x on ad spend -- a meaningfully better fit for someone spending heavily on Facebook/Google ads.
  - "Earn and burn," not hoarding: points programs devalue over time (cites Hilton pricing at the Waldorf Astoria Maui rising ~20%+ year over year) at a rate generally worse than currency inflation -- holding large point balances long-term is a losing strategy; better to redeem regularly even at moderate (not perfect) value than sit on a devaluing asset indefinitely.
  - Card recommendations for smaller/newer business owners who don't want to pay big annual fees: Chase Ink Unlimited (free, flat 1.5x cash back convertible to points if paired with a Chase personal points card) as a no-annual-fee starting point; Amex Gold and Chase Sapphire Reserve for ad spend multipliers; Capital One Venture X for strong transfer partners at 2x flat earning -- explicitly advises against feeling pressured into $895/year premium cards (e.g., Amex Platinum) just for status.
  - Tool recommendation: seats.aero -- an award-flight search engine (with an AI assistant layered on top of its own flight-availability API) that searches ~25 airline/hotel loyalty programs simultaneously instead of manually checking each one; used daily.
- Tools used to run the business: the trip-research service's backend (CRM/dashboard managing ~35 active trip requests) was built using Lovable (AI app builder), operated by two hired contractors who do the actual points-expert research/service delivery -- AI is used to host/build internal tooling, not to replace the human-delivered advisory service itself (deliberately kept human-voice/human-call driven because clients want to talk to a person, similar to a lawyer or financial advisor).
- Scaling plan/ceiling: the $875 1:1 consulting arm doesn't scale past the founder's own time (has considered hiring under himself but isn't focused on that); the trip-research service ($50 deposit + $200-600/trip) is seen as the more scalable line -- currently ~$6,000/month from that service alone with zero paid advertising, believed capable of reaching 100+ trips/month with a more streamlined process and paid marketing (Meta/Google ads), which was not yet turned on at time of interview.
- Advice for starting this as a side hustle: spend a weekend with points/rewards blogs (The Daily Drop free course, Frequent Miler, The Points Guy, One Mile at a Time) and an AI model like Claude to reach above-average public knowledge quickly; you don't need to have personally traveled extensively or made real bookings to start -- practice by fully researching and "quote booking" hypothetical trips (find a deal, work out point transfers, stop short of paying) to build skill and case-study content without needing real clients yet.

Caveats/risks:
- Business is highly dependent on a single platform (LinkedIn) for nearly all lead generation; founder admits this "terrifies" him and has no fully developed backup channel yet (X/Twitter attempted with little traction; a 7,000-person email list is the main owned asset as a hedge).
- Revenue from the $875 consulting package is inconsistent per-hour -- some clients need minimal follow-up (great effective hourly rate), others require disproportionate ongoing trip-planning support relative to the flat fee, and some clients simply pay and never claim the trip-planning benefit, which the founder frames as "not always ideal" even though it's technically pure profit.
- The founder acknowledges general-purpose AI models (Claude/ChatGPT) already answer most credit-card points questions competently, and openly discusses that being "no different from an AI" is a real long-term competitive risk to the human consulting side of this business; his stated differentiator is that AI tends to overstate best-case redemption scenarios (e.g., a rare cheap business-class fare) as if typical, and that this client base still generally prefers a human.
- Points/rewards programs devalue over time and terms/multipliers on specific cards can change (e.g., the described Chase personal-card points-doubling promotion was no longer available at time of recording) -- specific card recommendations and caps are time-sensitive and require staying current.
- This episode discloses a sponsorship/investment relationship with Beehiiv (the newsletter platform advertised mid-episode) and sells a separate $19 paid business-plan guide for this same idea -- treat those segments as promotional.
