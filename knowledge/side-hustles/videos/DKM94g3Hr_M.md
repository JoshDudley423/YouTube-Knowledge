# He Told 5 AIs: Make Money or Get Deleted
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DKM94g3Hr_M | Published: 2026-07-16

Interview with Brandon Doyle about his personal experiment giving 5 AI models ($1,000 each real money) to autonomously manage stock/crypto portfolios in competition. This is primarily an investing-experiment/entertainment episode, not a side-hustle how-to — flagged here for the one directly relevant business idea (an AI consulting agency) and general takeaways, since it's the substantive content in an otherwise investing-focused episode.

## Not a side-hustle idea per se — the experiment itself
- Gave Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Perplexity $1,000 each (real money, via Charles Schwab brokerage, one portfolio per model) — told each it was in a competition against the other four, and its "paid $20/month subscription" would be canceled if it lost, to see if framing it as a personal-stakes competition changed its risk behavior.
- Had to explicitly prompt each model to give real (not just theoretical/hedged) trade recommendations and explicitly permit maximum risk/total loss — Grok complied immediately, ChatGPT and Claude were initially resistant/hedged more due to liability concerns.
- Weekly cadence: screenshots each model's own holdings (and, only once a month, all five models' holdings, to limit copycat behavior) back to that model and asks whether to trade.
- After ~6 months (as of filming): Gemini down to $445 (lost >50%, after a leveraged 2x Solana ETF bet went wrong then rotated to a robotics/AI ETF ticker BOTZ); Grok up to $1,451 (+45%, in a 3x-leveraged tech ETF); Perplexity $1,731 and ChatGPT $1,739 (each ~+70%, both holding SOXL, a 3x-leveraged semiconductor ETF, with ChatGPT also in TQQQ, a 3x-leveraged Nasdaq ETF); Claude at $2,400 (+140%, holding ~4 positions including SOXL — bought first, before others copied — and Intel, bought on a "US government stake in Intel" thesis, up 285% on that specific position). Total across all five: $5,000 to ~$7,800 (+56%) vs. S&P 500 up only ~5% over the same window.
- Perplexity, notably, doesn't pick stocks itself — it selects and defers to other underlying models (mostly Claude Opus and ChatGPT, occasionally an open-source Chinese model) for its own recommendations.
- All five models converged on aggressive triple/double-leveraged ETFs (SOXL, TQQQ, leveraged tech/crypto funds) rather than conservative picks, despite (or because of) the "you must win or be deleted" framing — used as an example of AI models exhibiting sunk-cost/gambler behavior when down (Gemini specifically described as "becoming a gambler" after early losses, doubling down on risk).
- Explicit caveat repeated multiple times: not financial advice; experiment ongoing (planned 1-year duration), could still lose money; small sample/short time frame; a genuinely flat/mixed market period.

## Investing takeaways discussed (not a business idea, but stated as generalizable lessons)
- Hardest part of investing is knowing when to sell (not when to buy) — selling at a peak is psychologically hard because it requires giving up further upside.
- Having a stated, falsifiable investment thesis with a rough time horizon (e.g., "this will work if X regulatory/catalyst event happens by Y") makes it easier to know when to exit versus vague/instinctive buying.
- Host's own personal rule mentioned as contrast: never sell unless forced to (e.g., to pay the mortgage).
- Host separately mentions a self-built ("vibe coded") backtesting tool called "NEFL stocks" (Network-Effects, Founder-Led) that scores public companies 1-10 on network effects and founder-led status and reportedly shows NEFL-scored companies (e.g., Robinhood, Meta) outperforming the S&P significantly since IPO — mentioned as a possible future follow-up experiment, not a completed one.

## Caveats
- Entire premise is anecdotal (n=1, 5 accounts, 6 months so far); leveraged ETFs are high-risk/volatile instruments amplifying both gains and losses; both hosts explicitly disclaim this as financial advice.
