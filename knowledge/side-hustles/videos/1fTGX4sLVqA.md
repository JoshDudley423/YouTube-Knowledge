# The Most Overlooked Way to Make Money On the Internet
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1fTGX4sLVqA | Published: 2025-05-02

Interview with Frey Chu, who builds SEO-driven directory websites (niche listing sites) as a mostly-passive income stream.

## The idea
- Build simple, single-purpose "directory" websites (a curated list of businesses/locations/resources for a specific niche + location, e.g. "dry ice near me," "vegan restaurants," "movie filming locations") and monetize with display ads / affiliate links once they rank on Google.
- Model: find an underserved search niche (more search demand than quality supply), build a barebones site (WordPress + basic theme is enough), get a small number of backlinks to rank, then it runs largely passively.
- Explicit framing: "does passive income exist? No. Is this as close as you get? Yes." Requires real upfront SEO work; payoff is low-maintenance income afterward.

## Concrete numbers
- Frey owns 6 directory sites total, but only 3 got full effort (keyword research, data organization, build, SEO, monetization); those 3 are profitable and ranking.
- Combined revenue across his 3 active directories: ~$2,300-$3,000/month, "completely passively," untouched for 6 months at time of interview.
- Traffic: directory #1 (thrifting niche) ~35,000-60,000-90,000 monthly visitors (fluctuates; Ahrefs estimate vs. actual can be off by 20-30k); directory #2 (plasma donation, B2B attempt) ~6,000-10,000 monthly visitors; directory #3 (unspecified) fills out the ~70-90k combined range.
- RPM (revenue per thousand pageviews) on his thrifting directory: ~$16-17; noted RPM scales up with more traffic, and some niches (e.g. gaming) have notoriously low RPM vs. others (e.g. finance).
- Monetization mix: Mediavine (display ad network) — specifically "Mediavine Journey" tier (his main directory didn't qualify for standard Mediavine's 50k-100k session threshold but got into Journey) — plus a $19 Canva-made PDF digital product that "sells almost every day" on one site.
- Startup cost cited: "anyone with about $100 can start a directory."
- Cost of Ahrefs subscription (main research tool): ~$150/month.
- Backlink case study: one $50 paid backlink swap got his thrifting directory to rank #1 for its main keyword, generating ~$2,300-$3,000/month for 2 years from that single ranking.
- Comparable directory examples cited with estimated traffic: Penguin Dry Ice (single-page site) ~15,000 monthly visitors (peaked ~83,000 in Oct 2024) despite being "one of the worst websites" in terms of design; Movie-locations.com ~81,000 monthly visitors, estimated ~$5,000-6,000/month passive revenue.
- Mobile traffic share across his directories: ~80-82% — design/aesthetics matter far less than mobile usability.
- Rule of thumb sweet spot for keyword search volume when hunting niches: ~15,000-60,000 monthly searches "found the most opportunity in that range" (not a hard rule).

## Process / steps described
1. **Niche discovery**: Use Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, search generic terms like "near me," sort/scroll for keywords with low keyword difficulty (KD) and high search volume — "asymmetric bets." Look for green (low KD) with high volume. A beginner should target KD 0-5; an "intermediate" niche might be KD ~20-30; broad/competitive niches (e.g. "towing near me," KD 26, 84k searches) are deceptively hard because of vague search intent and heavy existing competition — avoid as a first project.
2. **Vet the niche further**: Check Reddit for repeated unanswered questions in the space (signal that Google/AI isn't satisfying searchers — an opportunity); check TikTok comments for genuine unfiltered demand signals; consider whether the topic is "evergreen" (won't change) vs. trend-driven (e.g. skip a viral TikTok food trend keyword because it may not last).
3. **Find a "bounty"**: Locate an existing, successful-but-unsophisticated ("mom and pop," ugly) site already ranking in the niche — proof the niche is viable and beatable; use it as a link-building/content target ("SEO is a PVP game" — you don't need to be #1, ranking 2nd-5th is fine and profitable).
4. **Build the site**: WordPress + a basic theme (Elementor Pro used, despite it having a reputation for being "slow and clunky" — speed of shipping an MVP is valued over polish). For large data sets (e.g. 2,000+ business listings), use a directory-specific plugin (GeoDirectory) to publish listings programmatically from a database rather than manually; WP All Import for bulk data import; JetEngine for building custom filters (e.g. filter dog parks by "fenced area for small dogs").
5. **Enrich the data**: Use AI to enrich/verify listings (e.g. built a tool, enrich.directory, that scans business review data and answers questions like "does this crematorium accept horses?" using AI-processed reviews) — the goal is saving the searcher time versus manually digging through Google Maps reviews.
6. **Domain**: prefer an exact-match keyword domain (e.g. "petcremation.com") without "near me" in it (searches are already geo-contextualized); prefer .com, then .org; a hyphenated .com is preferred over a straight .org if forced to choose.
7. **Get backlinks** (described as the primary ranking driver):
   - Identify the "bounty" competitor's existing backlinks (via Ahrefs).
   - Contact the site currently linking to that competitor and offer to pay (e.g. $50) to swap the link to point to your directory instead of adding a new one — swapping (not just adding) both hurts the competitor and lifts you.
   - Judge backlink quality by: domain rating/authority, the referring page's own traffic (a link from a page with zero visitors is weaker than one with real traffic since user click-through signals matter), and the number of outbound links on that page ("link juice" splits like a birthday cake — fewer links on the page = more value to you).
   - Paid backlink services ($500/month for X links, Fiverr $5-for-1000-backlinks) are largely low-quality/spammy — risk of a Google penalty or de-indexing; vet any vendor carefully, or route a purchased backlink to an intermediary article that then links to your site to reduce risk.
   - Manual outreach ("would you link to us, and if not, can I pay you $50 to link to us") is called the most reliable, best-balance approach.
8. **Monetize once ranking**: multiple options once you have traffic — display ads (AdSense, Mediavine/Mediavine Journey), affiliate links, digital products, or (attempted, less successfully) B2B sponsored listings sold directly to companies in the niche (his plasma-donation directory tried this — didn't work well because there were only ~8 potential B2B buyers in that niche).

## Tools/platforms named
- Ahrefs (primary keyword/backlink research tool, ~$150/mo)
- WordPress + Elementor Pro (site builder/theme)
- GeoDirectory (WordPress directory plugin, for programmatic/bulk listing builds)
- WP All Import (bulk data import)
- JetEngine (custom filtering)
- Mediavine / Mediavine Journey (display ad network)
- Google Maps reviews, Reddit, TikTok (research/validation sources)
- enrich.directory (Frey's own AI data-enrichment tool)
- Canva (used to make a $19 PDF digital product)
- Lovable (mentioned as something Frey is now experimenting with for AI-assisted coding of directories)

## Caveats / risks
- "Passive income doesn't really exist" — significant upfront SEO/build work is required; payoff is slow (results may take months; Frey: "I don't see progress two weeks after launch" is a normal, expected complaint from beginners who give up too early).
- Picking too broad/competitive a niche (e.g. "towing near me") as a beginner is a common failure mode — people "choose the wrong niches, can't rank, and give up," concluding wrongly that "directories don't work."
- Product-based keywords (e.g. "Dubai chocolate bar near me") are risky picks — they compete against e-commerce sites, which dominate those SERPs, and may be tied to unstable viral trends.
- Informational/data-heavy directory content is described as vulnerable to Google's AI/core updates (AI Overviews potentially cannibalizing traffic) — Frey mitigates by checking whether real user demand persists on Reddit despite AI answers being available.
- Third-party traffic estimates (Ahrefs, etc.) can be inaccurate by 20-30% compared to real analytics — treat traffic estimates for competitor sites as rough guides only.
- Revenue-per-visitor varies wildly by niche (RPM) — a gaming directory might need ~1,000,000 monthly visitors to match a finance directory's revenue at 50,000 visitors.
- Buying cheap/bulk backlinks risks Google penalties up to de-indexing (site becomes unsearchable) — the worst-case outcome.
- Small/thin niches (e.g. plasma donation B2B) can hit a hard ceiling — only ~8 potential buyers existed for Frey's sponsorship pitch in that niche, killing that specific monetization plan even though the site still gets organic traffic.
