# This AI Finds the Idea, Customers, and Does the Work
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZbjmWNmIkc | Published: 2026-09-16

Interview with Alex McDonnell of Hyperagent (an AI agent platform, built by the Airtable team) about using autonomous AI agents to find business ideas, generate leads, and run outbound sales/marketing — followed by Chris live-demoing the tool to research and start scoping a "boring business."

## What the tool (Hyperagent) actually does
- Agents run with their own virtual machine/browser and can navigate the live web like a human (clicking, reading, filling forms, taking screenshots) rather than only using APIs — meaning they can operate on sites/tools that were never built for bots. Agents can spawn "sub-agents" to run parallel workstreams (e.g., one checks satellite imagery while another checks zoning applications) and report back to a coordinating agent.
- Agents can be set to "live mode" — a persistent loop that re-runs the same job on a set cadence (hourly, daily) indefinitely, effectively functioning as a 24/7 employee rather than a one-off task; they message the human via Slack, Telegram, WhatsApp, email, or Discord when they finish a run or need attention, rather than requiring the human to prompt them each time.
- Agents "learn" over time within a thread: feedback given on one run is remembered and applied in future runs and even persists across the type of task without needing to be repeated (compared to onboarding a new employee once, not repeatedly).
- Marketplace of pre-built agent/skill templates exists (free to use/clone), including a widely used one called "Signal" — an always-on outbound sales agent that sources leads matching a defined ideal customer profile (ICP), does deep research on each prospect, builds a personalized landing page (with copy, video, and product mockups specific to that lead) as the outreach artifact instead of a plain cold email, manages CRM logging, and reports learnings back (e.g., which prospect types/messaging are earning replies) to keep refining itself.

## Concrete lead-gen / agency business models demonstrated
1. **"Find businesses with no website / bad website and build them one for free or for sale" agent** (a literal free skill on Hyperagent's marketplace): agent searches local businesses (e.g., via Google Business Profile signals) for strong review counts but missing/outdated websites, scrapes contact info, studies what customers say they love about the business from reviews, and actually builds a full working website (including a booking system with plausible pricing) for each candidate — end to end in about 20-30 minutes per batch of candidates. Business model: offer the finished website as a paid implementation, or give it away for free to earn trust/a foot in the door (e.g., a Facebook/Instagram-style "free viral gift" hook), then upsell ongoing services. Explicitly framed as a way to replace the old cold-outreach agency workflow (manually finding leads, cold emailing at a ~0.5-1% response rate, waiting days-to-weeks for replies) with a "set it up, go to sleep, wake up to leads" model.
2. **Personalized sales/proposal landing pages for high-consideration B2B sales** (case example: Swiss Solar, self-cleaning solar panel technology, selling into industrial solar farm projects): agent researches the specific project and decision-maker, then builds a one-off personalized landing page for that individual prospect containing a custom hero video, screenshots of relevant software/product, and an explainer of how the tech applies specifically to their project — pitched as dramatically outperforming a plain cold email for expensive/considered purchases, and letting a shy or non-committal buyer explore at their own pace without live sales pressure.
3. **Visual "vision" proposals for home/commercial service businesses** — cited working examples:
   - A landscaping company using agents to auto-generate a visual "before/after" mockup of a customer's own yard from a submitted photo and short description, delivered fast, as a competitive edge against competitors who only respond with "we'll see when we can come out."
   - A paving company using satellite imagery of aging parking lots to generate AI-based repaving visualizations and cost estimates as outbound sales collateral.
   - A branded-apparel company using AI image generation to mock up a prospect's own logo/branding onto sample products (polos, hats) as a personalized pitch.
4. **Niche market-mapping / lead-database creation** — live demo in the episode: Chris asked the agent to find local pond-dredging leads in North Texas by combining satellite imagery, GIS/mapping data, tax records, and zoning applications to build a spreadsheet of ponds likely needing dredging (motivated by Chris's own real need — he'd learned via a separate ChatGPT query that there are ~800,000 ponds on farms/ranches in Texas with no existing directory/data broker). Positioned as a repeatable niche-data-brokering concept: whenever there's a large real-world asset category with no existing "Zoom Info"-style directory, an agent can potentially assemble that data from public/satellite sources and either use it internally for lead-gen or sell it as a dataset/service.
5. **Deployable "packaged agent" businesses for AI-implementation/automation agencies**: an agency can build one well-tuned agent for a specific niche (Hyperagent's own example: a "pond dredging project manager" agent) and then resell/redeploy that same template across every client in that niche, using the platform's "teams"/workspace structure so each client has an isolated workspace, the client pays for their own ongoing AI usage costs, but the agency retains backend edit/config control while the client only uses the agent day-to-day — pitched as solving the typically hacky/manual work agencies currently do to package and resell automations.
6. **Personal-use marketplace-scouting agents** (not a business per se, but a reusable pattern): agents set to live mode that continuously monitor Facebook Marketplace (or similar) for specific criteria (e.g., a specific make/model/price range of vehicle or equipment) and message the user when a match appears — cited real example: Hyperagent's own CEO runs an agent that watches vintage sports car listings for him; Chris discusses adapting this to watch for ranch equipment (side-by-sides, Bobcats, tractors) matching his own buying criteria.
7. **Live demo of automated "boring business" opportunity research**: Chris prompted Hyperagent with "find me 10 boring local businesses that quietly make over $500,000 a year and that almost no one talks about, with startup cost, typical margins, and why more people aren't doing it." The agent asked clarifying questions (did "$500K/year" mean owner profit/SDE specifically, and which country), then researched using business-broker listing data as a source for real seller-reported earnings (explicitly treating broker figures as unverified claims to flag, not confirmed facts), and produced a formatted page of results including: portable sanitation, parking lot striping (cites Chris's own prior deep-dive episodes on this — startup cost as low as near-zero with a basic paint roller versus $8,000-$90,000 for full equipment, ~35-40% net margin, requiring ~$1.25-1.43M in revenue to hit $500K net profit; barriers are night work, weather-dependent scheduling, and bid-accuracy skill), septic/drainage services, portable restroom rental routes, commercial janitorial contracts, pest control routes, elevator maintenance (noted as gated by licensing + labor requirements), fire protection inspection, tree care/arborist work, towing/recovery/impound, and pallet recovery/repair/resale.
   - When asked to swap one category out for laundromats, the agent produced a further deep-dive; Chris then had it build an actual interactive published web page mapping every laundromat within 100 miles of a specific zip code (using what appeared to be Google Places/Maps data and census population data) to visually identify underserved "gap" towns for a new laundromat location, complete with real business listings, star ratings, and reviews pulled into map pins — plus a section on typical startup cost, pricing, and customer-acquisition tactics, all as one shareable/publishable page. This was then saved as a recurring scheduled agent ("Deal Scout," run every morning) to keep re-scanning for new laundromat opportunities in other zip codes.

## Practical starting advice given for a beginner
- Start with one small, genuinely tedious "computer chore" task in your own work and have Hyperagent build a dedicated agent to do just that, then put it into live/recurring mode — a low-stakes way to experience what agent automation feels like before attempting anything ambitious.
- Reframing exercise recommended: imagine you could hire your "dream" employee with unlimited budget for a role (e.g., a dream salesperson) — list everything that person would do if they never had to sleep (constant prospecting, constantly refining messaging, deep research per prospect, personalized proposals, never letting a warm lead go cold, perfect CRM hygiene, creative sourcing of contact info) — then have your agent attempt as many of those behaviors as possible.
- Agents are also being used for creative/production tasks beyond lead-gen — cited example: a "motion design agent" that builds Hyperagent's own marketing demo videos end-to-end (navigating the actual product like a real user, coding a recreation, storyboarding, and rendering the final video) in about 30-40 minutes per run, typically needing 3-4 rounds of human feedback to reach final quality, with iteration cycles getting shorter over successive runs because the agent retains feedback as persistent memory/skill updates.

## Caveats / risks
- Agents surface unverified, self-reported figures (e.g., business-broker "seller's discretionary earnings" claims) that should be treated as leads to verify, not confirmed facts — the agent itself flagged this distinction in the live demo rather than presenting broker numbers as ground truth.
- Some cited startup-cost figures from the agent's own research were flagged by Chris as inaccurate/overstated based on his own prior direct reporting (e.g., the parking-lot-striping startup-cost range) — a reminder that agent research still needs a knowledgeable human to sanity-check output.
- Getting agent output to a "serviceable" state takes little effort, but getting it to match a specific personal/brand standard (visual polish, tone, timing) typically requires several rounds of iterative feedback, not a single prompt.
- This episode's specific figures (websites built in 20-30 minutes, $500K+ SDE thresholds, 100-mile laundromat maps, etc.) are live-tool demonstrations rather than a documented, completed real-world business outcome — no actual client, sale, or revenue is reported as having resulted from the on-air demo itself.
