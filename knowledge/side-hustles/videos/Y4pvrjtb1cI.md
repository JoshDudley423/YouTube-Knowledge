# The Simplest Side Hustle You Can Start Under $100
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4pvrjtb1cI | Published: 20260415

Interview with Josh (@directmailjosh), who runs "Purple Mountain Advertising," a shared direct-mail postcard business.

## The business idea
- Sell advertising space on shared postcards ("share cards") mailed to thousands of local homeowners; only one business per category per card (no direct competitors on same card).
- Uses USPS **Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM)** tool — no mailing list/names needed, just pick a zip code and mail every address in it. Cost ~25 cents/home in postage.
- Two card formats:
  - **9x12" card**: goes to 5,000–10,000 homes, ~16 ad spaces, more premium/customized, best for home-service businesses (one-time or recurring runs).
  - **6x11" "community card"**: goes to 2,500 homes, cheaper spaces, better for brick-and-mortar/lower-ticket businesses (bakeries, restaurants) wanting to offer a coupon; often sold as a 3-month recurring run.
- Revenue model: fill the front of the card to cover print+postage (breakeven), everything filling the back is profit.

## Numbers
- Started the business for under $100 (bought 2 "guides" for direction — not necessary).
- No LLC for the first two card runs.
- Unit economics per 10,000-home run: ~$2,500 postage (25¢/home) + ~$2,300 printing (14pt UV gloss cardstock, both sides) = ~$4,800 total cost.
- Sells spaces: $600 (single 3x4" space), $600, $600, $999 (double space), plus half-spaces at $350.
- Fill the card front+back → ~$9,000–$10,000 revenue per 10,000-piece mailing → **$4,000–$5,000 profit per run**.
- Pricing rule of thumb: don't charge more than 10 cents/home for an average space (16 spaces/card typical); double space ~10¢/home, single ~6¢/home. Regional variance: Southern California businesses will pay ~$800/space; small Midwest towns may max out at $450–500.
- Smaller 2,500-home community cards: charge $250–300 for a 2.7x2.5" space (10–12¢/home) — lower barrier to entry, sells faster even though per-home rate is higher.
- First-ever card: sold a $1,100 double space to a plumber in 5 text messages (no call).
- Track record after ~1 year (started April, side hustle): shared cards — $26K revenue, $14K gross profit, ~$11K net profit. Solo cards (single-business-only mailers, lower effort, lower margin) — $13K revenue, $2–3K net profit. Total: ~$40K revenue, ~$15K profit in year one, from a $90 initial outlay.
- Best month: ~$5,000–6,000 revenue in one month (took ~6 weeks to fill that card).
- No employees needed at this scale — vendor prints/ships, Josh just aggregates advertisers and invoices via Stripe.

## Process / steps
1. Pick target zip code in USPS EDDM tool; sort by higher income / single-family homes (avoid apartments/condos for home-service ads).
2. Find advertisers by posting in **local Facebook business-networking groups** (not general neighborhood groups — those have slow admin approval and don't like ads). Facebook limits posting to 10 groups at once; 2 particular groups drove most of his sales.
3. Post ~once/week (avoid spamming); best times found: **Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday, 6:45–7:15pm** (after work, people scrolling Facebook after dinner). Keep the post short (don't make people click "see more").
4. Growth-hack for social proof: like your own post immediately, then comment on it from your separate business Facebook page asking people to comment/DM if interested — creates early engagement signal so Facebook's algorithm ranks the post higher.
5. Close deals via Facebook Messenger/text — ~95% of deals close this way, no calls needed. Design ads for free (with free revisions) as part of the purchase.
6. Provide a **free dynamic QR code** per ad so advertisers can track scans (also differentiates offers by which perform better — simpler ads/offers tend to get more scans).
7. Design cards in Canva, send to a printing company (get a sample first before ordering a full run), have prints shipped to your house or directly to a USPS distribution hub; cards must be pre-bundled into 100s for USPS.
8. Standard mailing sizes are 2,500/5,000/10,000 (printers price in these increments — most cost-effective batches).
9. Retain past advertisers by offering them first right of refusal on the next card in a nearby area before opening that category to new prospects.
10. Follow up relentlessly with advertisers post-mailing to collect ROI case studies — these become the best sales tool for future prospecting (a suggestion from host Chris Koerner that Josh admits he doesn't do enough).

## Other prospecting channels mentioned
- Cold calling and emailing (email described as least effective/his least favorite).
- Driving around town looking for yard signs (indicates the business owner is already open to physical local advertising) and cold-texting/calling them.
- Suggested (not yet tried by Josh): scrape businesses in a target industry, mail them a sample card showing "your ad here" placeholders plus a case study (e.g., "Lehi, Utah plumber saw 300x ROI"), to sell ad spots directly via mail.

## Scaling / upsell ideas (discussed, not yet executed by Josh)
- Once you have a relationship/trust with a local business via the card, upsell Facebook ad management, Google SEO, or website-building services (many small businesses on the card don't have websites at all).
- Expand into other print marketing materials (business cards, etc.).
- Consider running paid ads to acquire advertisers (Josh has been 100% organic so far).

## Caveats / risks
- Cannot guarantee ROI to advertisers — you're selling **exposure**, not guaranteed leads. Results vary heavily: some advertisers get no measurable response, others convert big (e.g., a $13,000 concrete job cited as an example; a bakery got 4 walk-in redemptions same day at 2,500 homes; a carpet cleaner got a call 4 months after mailing).
- ROI is hard to attribute (phone calls instead of QR scans, delayed conversions, elderly customers who don't use QR codes).
- Some business types (B2B, e-commerce) are a poor fit for a homeowner-targeted mailer — Josh proactively turns these away.
- Direct mail's legality/lack of regulation (vs. CAN-SPAM email rules or $500/text-message-marketing fines) hinges on it costing money to send, making spam infeasible — but also means occasional public backlash from recipients who dislike getting mail.
- Setting up USPS bundling/logistics correctly the first time is a hassle ("a pain") though it becomes easy once established.
- Best-performing niches: home services (cleaners, landscapers, lawn care, junk removal, carpet/window cleaning, plumbers, permanent holiday lighting), real estate (e.g., "what's your home worth" QR offers). Actual product/service quality still matters — exposure only converts repeat customers if the underlying business delivers.
