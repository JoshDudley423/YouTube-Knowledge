# I Did Something Insane. Here Are the Businesses I Found in It
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HOosrT6vj_Y | Published: 2026-08-27

Chris Koerner solo episode (sponsored by Bizee, an LLC/compliance filing service) about buying a 231-acre cattle ranch outside Dallas, and the business ideas he surfaced along the way — most explicitly designed so they DON'T require owning land yourself.

## Deal/context numbers (background, not itself a side hustle, but useful reference)
- 231-acre ranch, ~45 minutes from Dallas Fort Worth, purchased for ~$10,300-$10,500/acre (~$2.4M range implied); negotiated ~$400,000 off list price.
- Financing: local farm-and-ranch bank (not a residential lender) financed 85% LTV, 15% down, ~6.7% interest rate, 20-year amortization with a 5-year ARM; closing took ~2 months.
- Texas "ag exemption": properties 5+ acres used for agricultural purposes (cattle, crops, hay, wildlife management) get ~97% lower property tax (example: a $2M ranch might otherwise owe $45,000-$50,000/year in property tax, vs. ~$1,500/year with the exemption) — requires active application/compliance; letting it lapse triggers retroactive clawback of back taxes, potentially hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- Used Claude (his own $20/month subscription) to analyze the property appraisal against comps he uploaded — Claude caught that the appraisal's per-acre comps were based on much smaller (60-acre) parcels, and correctly noted larger parcels are worth less per acre (analogous to price-per-square-foot dropping for bigger houses) — he credits this analysis with saving "hundreds of thousands of dollars" in the negotiation.
- Real estate license note: his wife was the buyer's agent, netting a 3% commission; they gave back 2% of that as a price concession at closing (~$50,000, ~$200/acre) — framed as a reason it can be worth getting a real estate license (~$1,000 cost, "tens of hours" of study) if you plan to buy property for yourself, since you can rebate part of the commission into the deal.
- Floodplain land valued at roughly $5,000/acre vs. non-floodplain at $15,000-$16,000/acre on this property (a ~1/3 ratio, cited as typical for large parcels).

## Business ideas discussed (most explicitly usable without owning land)

### 1. Grass-fed beef reselling — no land or ranch required
- Core mechanic: don't own cattle or land — simply buy live cattle from an existing rancher at the point they'd normally sell to auction (~500-600 lbs, ~$1,400/head at auction in his specific example), arrange a slaughterhouse date (book months in advance — slaughterhouses get very busy), have the meat processed/packaged, and resell direct to consumers/e-commerce.
- Precedent cited: large branded beef resellers (Butcher Box, Snake River Farms) don't own ranches or cattle themselves either — they buy from farmers and are not vertically integrated.
- Unit economics given: sell finished beef at $8-$20/lb depending on grade (choice, prime, wagyu, etc.); a quarter-cow (~125 lbs) sells for roughly $1,500 (~$12/lb), a whole cow around $6,000; net margin cited at roughly 40%.
- Framed as a durable, non-"winner-take-all" commodity business — nearly universal demand (only exception noted: vegans) and current context of historically high beef/ground-beef prices makes it a favorable time to start, in his view.
- His own specific plan: sign a grazing lease with the rancher he bought the land from (he keeps running his existing ~85 head of cattle on the property, paying Chris a monthly grazing fee), and Chris will later "earmark"/brand specific cattle to buy and grow to slaughter weight for his own beef brand ("888 Ranch") rather than managing the herd himself.

### 2. Statewide/regional "pond database" — a data business idea, unbuilt
- Observation: there are reportedly ~800,000 farm/ranch ponds in Texas alone, with no existing public database, despite all the necessary tools (GIS mapping, satellite/aerial imagery, Google Earth, AI agents) already existing to build one.
- Potential monetization angles floated: a marketplace charging anglers for access to fish on private ponds; selling leads to pond management/dredging/digging companies (ponds need regular maintenance — dam leaks, beaver damage, etc. are common); other unspecified data-resale use cases.
- Explicitly unbuilt/idea-stage — presented as a brainstorm, not a validated business.

### 3. Heavy equipment flipping
- Reference: acquaintance's business model is buying dozers/heavy equipment at farm, ranch, or heavy-equipment auctions and reselling them without ever taking physical delivery.
- Cost anchors given: crappy (non-street-legal, ranch-only) dump trucks $5,000-$15,000 on Facebook Marketplace; dozers $10,000-$30,000 at auction.
- Comparison point on owning vs. hiring equipment: hiring a company (with their own equipment) to dig/dredge a pond can cost $200-$500/hour (his friend's real example: paid $125,000 total for pond digging), vs. buying your own equipment, hiring an operator for $20-$50/hour, then reselling the equipment afterward — potentially saving "tens of thousands of dollars" net, especially if you can resell the machine after the job.

### 4. Renting/leasing raw or "unloved" land — without buying it
- Key insight: land itself can be leased extremely cheaply (e.g., ~20 acres of farmland leasing for "low hundreds a month") — much lower barrier to entry than purchasing.
- Concrete real-world example (a friend's business in Canada): find raw, undesirable/unloved highway-adjacent land (e.g., in a floodplain, been sitting on the market unsold), negotiate a rent-to-own or lease-with-purchase-option agreement with the landowner, bring in **portable/temporary storage units** to build a de facto self-storage facility without permanent construction, and start renting storage space immediately. If it doesn't work out, walk away at end of lease and resell the portable units elsewhere (no sunk construction cost); if it does work, exercise the purchase option once the business model is de-risked and proven.
- Framed as generalizable to other temporary/portable-asset business types on leased land — not limited to storage.
- Suggested sourcing approach for land: search Zillow for land listings that look "unloved" (floodplain-affected, highway-fronting, stale listings, discounted asking price) and cold-reach the owner about leasing/rent-to-own rather than buying outright.

### 5. Other on-ranch revenue streams mentioned more briefly (mostly for context, less emphasized as pure side-hustle plays)
- **Shop/building rental**: renting an existing structure to a local business tenant (his ranch came with a tenant already renting the shop below market rate, as part of a handshake deal to not disrupt the existing tenant relationship at purchase).
- **Hunting leases** (guided hog hunts — hogs are an invasive species in Texas so no season restriction; deer hunting; clay pigeon/rifle range fees): explicitly said to be "not super profitable" and carries meaningful liability risk — the least recommended idea in the episode.
- **Farm stays / Hipcamp / RV and campsite rentals / tiny-home communities**: cited comparable business next door — a 14-unit tiny-home community on 20 acres charging $1,450/month per unit, fully occupied, generating ~$17,000-$18,000/month (i.e., meaningful income on a much smaller footprint than his 231 acres); he's also considering a novel glass-floor "bridge Airbnb" built over a creek on his property (acknowledged added cost/complexity: needs electrical, solar, septic — septic placement near waterways is separately regulated).
- **Renting owned equipment when idle** (skid steer, RV, tractor, etc.) on Facebook Marketplace to other landowners.
- **Personal-brand YouTube channel** (Triple 8 Ranch) documenting the ranch/farm work, leveraging his existing production team/skills (thumbnails, retention, hooks) built from his main channel, aiming for it to eventually cover the ranch's mortgage payment via ad revenue/audience — framed as "your learnings compound."
- **Tax depreciation** on ranch equipment/structures as an incidental financial benefit of ownership (not itself a resellable idea).

## Caveats / risks
- Ag-exemption tax status requires ongoing compliance; a lapse can trigger a large retroactive tax clawback — a real, specific risk for anyone buying/leasing agricultural-exempt land.
- Floodplain portions of land carry building restrictions (no permanent structures without stilts) and are valued at roughly a third of non-floodplain land — a factor to price into any land deal.
- Hunting leases are explicitly flagged as low-profit relative to their liability exposure — the one revenue idea in the episode the host is not bullish on.
- The pond-database idea is unvalidated/unbuilt — presented purely as an observed market gap, not a tested business.
- The land-lease/rent-to-own storage model depends on finding a landowner willing to do a lease-with-option-to-buy structure and on the temporary/portable nature of the asset (storage units) to keep downside limited if the location doesn't perform — it is explicitly NOT the same risk profile as buying land outright first.
- Some of the ranch-specific ideas (grazing lease income, shop rental, hunting leases) depend on already owning suitable land and are less directly replicable by someone without land; the beef-reselling and land-leasing ideas are the two most explicitly framed as replicable without land ownership.
