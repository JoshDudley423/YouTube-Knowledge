# What I Learned From My Failed $50m Business Partnership
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9LaHuYqEy0A | Published: 2024-07-29

Not a how-to video — a cautionary personal story about a mobile home/RV park acquisition partnership that collapsed, with lessons for anyone structuring a business partnership.

## The business (context)
- Chris Koerner partnered with "Jason" (real estate investor with hundreds of single-family homes and access to investor capital) to buy mobile home and RV parks, since deploying investor capital into large parks is more efficient than single-family homes.
- Deal structure: Jason raises capital and brings real-estate expertise; Chris handles operations, marketing, hiring, and finding parks. No acquisition or property-management fees — equity and net operating income split 50/50 with investors, investors put in 100% of the capital.
- A third partner ("Omar") was added early as an equal 1/3 partner for his supposed commercial real estate expertise, contributing capital but from day one added almost no operational work.
- Over 18 months the partnership acquired 15–20 parks (Oklahoma, Texas, Kansas) and reached ~$80–90K/month in rent collections (~$1M/year run rate), just reaching break-even/first distributions.
- A California investor group offered to invest $50M (vs. the ~$500K raises they'd done before) — a scale-up opportunity that surfaced the unequal-contribution problem.
- Result: partner Jason (with Omar) pushed Chris out at a Starbucks meeting — reduced from 33% equity to 3.3%, no operational role, forced non-compete on future mobile home/RV park deals. The $50M deal then fell apart (attributed publicly to "due diligence," but Chris suspects investors learned of the partnership dysfunction). Parks were later sold off, mostly at a loss; the other two partners' unrelated business ventures (real estate, healthcare) also blew up and they ended up suing each other.
- Aftermath/pivot: Chris began writing/posting online about mobile home & RV park investing (as "the mobile home park guy," analogous to niche real-estate content creators). Two years later a fund with an established track record recruited him as a co-GP based on his audience/sourcing ability — he now sources deals (e.g., a Montana park), helps market parks, writes investor updates, and helps raise capital, generating income without personally raising the funds himself.

## Concrete takeaways / process notes for structuring a partnership
- Red flag to watch for: a partner who resists tracking hours/contribution to determine profit splits ("value-add isn't about hours, it's about experience") — usually means they know their real contribution is low.
- Don't add a partner "on trial" without clear, written performance/contribution terms — a trial partnership that's never formalized in the operating agreement tends to calcify into permanent inequity.
- Money contributed ≠ value added: a partner who puts in equal capital but does none of the operational work is a structural risk, especially once outside capital raises the stakes.
- Have an unbiased third party review the operating agreement, and have an ongoing unbiased third-party mediator/advisor available throughout the partnership to resolve disputes.
- Clarify each partner's specific role (fundraising vs. operations vs. expertise) explicitly at the outset — ambiguity about who is responsible for raising money became the retroactive justification for diluting Chris's equity.
- Caveat/lesson stated directly: "don't get into bed with someone right when you meet them" — the partnership was formed on a first lunch meeting without vetting the partner's history with his other close associate (Omar).
- Large incoming capital (a $50M investor) can be the trigger that surfaces and weaponizes unresolved partner-contribution imbalances — get equity/contribution issues resolved *before* a big capital event, not during it.
