# She Makes $10M/Year and Doesn't Even Exist (AI Influencers Explained)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNMNQrCbHnk | Published: 2025-10-14

Interview with Romain Torres, founder of ArcAds (AI talking-avatar/video generation tool), on building businesses around AI-generated video ads and AI ("virtual") influencers.

## The business ideas
1. **AI-generated ad agency**: sell AI-made video ads (using a tool like ArcAds) as a service to brands/companies who already run paid ads, replacing or supplementing expensive human UGC (user-generated-content) creators.
2. **AI influencers**: create a fully synthetic "person" (consistent AI-generated face/voice/personality) that posts on Instagram/TikTok, builds an audience, and earns via brand sponsorships — cited example: an AI influencer ("Mikuela/Mika") reportedly earning ~$10M/year in brand deals despite not existing; another AI-ad company scaled from $0 to $12–20M in annual recurring revenue in ~12 months purely through AI-generated Facebook/Meta ads.
3. **"Clone yourself" content**: creators use an AI avatar of their own face/voice to produce more video content (e.g., voiceover/talking-head segments) without personally being on camera each time — cited examples of clone-based video reels getting 2 million+ views.
4. **AI voice agents as a service**: package an AI phone-answering/appointment-booking agent (for hairdressers, dentists, etc., who currently pay a human secretary) and sell it as a much cheaper alternative; market it using AI-generated video ads that demonstrate the exact product ("dogfooding" — using your own AI ad tool to sell an AI voice-agent service).

## Concrete numbers / process for the "sell AI ads to brands" path
- Cited quickest path to real income: create 20 AI-generated ads/month for a client for $2,000/month (~$100/ad) — cheaper than hiring human UGC creators, and small relative to a company's ad budget if they're already spending heavily (example: a company spending ~$500K/month on Meta ads treats $2K/month as under 1% of spend).
- Suggested go-to-market: browse the public Facebook Ads Library to find companies running many active ads (a sign of ad spend and appetite for more creative), find the decision-maker's LinkedIn (prefer smaller companies where you can reach the actual owner/CEO, not a 200+ person org where you can't find a decision-maker), create one free/spec ad cloned from an ad style they're already running, and pitch replacing a $500 human-UGC ad (that took ~3 weeks to produce) with an AI-made equivalent.
- Projected funnel from that outreach: pitch ~100 companies → expect ~20-30 replies/calls → roughly a third of those convert to paying clients at $3,000–$5,000/month each → estimated ~$15,000/month in new revenue achievable within about one month, starting with zero existing network, using only LinkedIn outreach.
- To estimate a target company's ad budget (and therefore their willingness to pay): for any mobile app, look up its public monthly revenue on Sensor Tower, then assume roughly 60-70% of that revenue is being spent on paid user-acquisition ads (a common rule of thumb specific to the mobile-app industry, and possibly applicable to Shopify/ecom too).
- Scaling further ("advanced" tier described): instead of manually brainstorming 1-5 ad scripts/day, feed large sets of existing competitor ad transcripts into ChatGPT to generate new scripts, then wire the whole pipeline (script generation → AI video generation via ArcAds API → distribution) through automation tools (Zapier, Make.com, n8n, Gumloop) to produce 100-500 ad variants/day, increasing odds of finding a viral/high-performing ad without manual ideation.
- Full automation concept discussed: an agent that each morning scans the Facebook Ads Library, prioritizes ads that have been running longest (a signal they're performing well), recreates them with AI, finds the advertiser's owner on LinkedIn, messages them, and only produces the real ad once the prospect responds positively (e.g., "send me a free sample") — framed as a way to scale a service business without hiring a large team.

## Which audiences/niches respond best to AI-generated ads/influencers
- Older audiences (55+) engage more with AI-generated content — hypothesized to be because they're less attuned to spotting AI and less skeptical of new tech in general.
- International/non-native-English-speaking audiences: an AI-generated-ad-driven English-learning app scaled to $10M+ in revenue using only AI ads; theorized that international viewers are less AI-savvy and more used to seeing dubbed/non-native content, making AI-narrated ads land better.
- People with disabilities / people over 50 seeking benefits: cited example "Disability Path" running heavy AI-ad volume for lead-gen (offering to help people over 50 with a health issue get up to a stated allowance) — again a demographic hypothesized to be less likely to detect or care that content is AI-generated.
- Products/services where authenticity/trust in the messenger doesn't matter much for a purchase decision (e.g., functional utility products) perform better with AI ads than categories where personal credibility matters more (e.g., health/wellness endorsements, where a real, liked personality may convert better — discussed via a Tim Robinson vs. Matthew McConaughey comparison).
- A distinct emerging niche: audiences who already expect/accept a product to involve AI (e.g., AI voice-agent services) — an AI-generated ad for an AI voice-agent product is "on-brand" rather than a mismatch.
- Niche discovery tools mentioned: TikTok Creator Search Insights (surfaces trending keywords/hashtags with unmet content demand — shows search volume vs. existing video supply for a given topic); Facebook Ads Library (public data on any advertiser's active ad count/creative); Foreplay (a tool built on top of the Facebook Ads Library for saving/organizing competitor ad inspiration into boards).

## Why most AI influencer/ad accounts fail
- Same failure mode as ordinary content creation: lack of consistency (most people quit after a couple of weeks) and lack of creativity (a good hook, retention, and continual iteration matter far more than the AI production quality itself).
- Posting only 1-2 AI-generated videos and expecting results has, per Torres, roughly a 99% chance of failing.
- Recommends: study what's already performing well in your niche (via Ads Library, TikTok Insights, or downloading a creator's whole catalog to find their outlier post that did 10x their normal views, then analyzing why and replicating the pattern with AI) rather than only brainstorming from scratch.
- Recommended creative techniques for hook/retention: strong opening visual "hook" gestures (e.g., the actor pointing at the camera in the first second), unusual or novel wardrobe/setting for the AI actor (e.g., a costume), and situations impossible or unsafe to film for real (e.g., an actor "driving" while not watching the road, used deliberately for shock/retention since it's obviously safe when it's AI).

## ArcAds tool mechanics (as context for anyone building this)
- Workflow: write a script (with emotion/accent tags — e.g., specify an accent to be spoken), select an existing AI actor from a large library or generate a fully custom one from a text description (age, look, setting), refine appearance with consistency across edits, then generate a talking, lip-synced video of that actor delivering the script.
- Voice realism was cited as the single biggest factor in whether an AI avatar reads as believable — a robotic voice breaks the illusion regardless of visual quality.

## Caveats / risks
- Survivorship bias acknowledged directly: for every viral AI-influencer success story, there are many accounts that post AI content and get essentially no traction (example cited: accounts with thousands of videos and only ~82 followers).
- AI-ad effectiveness is heavily audience- and niche-dependent — the same generic AI ad that crushes it with an international or older audience may actively repel a skeptical, AI-literate audience (example: a 30-something audience deeply familiar with ChatGPT/AI tools reacting negatively to an obviously-AI ad for a competitive product like creatine gummies).
- The technology is moving fast — the speakers expect AI-generated ads to become effectively indistinguishable from real ones within roughly six months of this recording, which could change what's effective and reduce the current "spot the AI" advantage/disadvantage dynamics discussed.
