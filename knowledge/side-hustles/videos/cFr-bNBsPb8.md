# He Spends 1 Hour a Week on This Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cFr-bNBsPb8 | Published: 2026-05-03

Interview with Jay Meldrum (Swing Cave Golf), a single-bay membership golf simulator business, ~6 months old at time of filming.

## The business
- Single-bay (one simulator, one room) golf practice space run as a membership model, not pay-per-visit. Positioned as neither "the golf course" nor "Topgolf" (entertainment/corporate events) but a private practice space for golfers who want to improve their swing without an audience.
- Origin idea: home simulators sit unused ~23 hours/day even for owners who use them daily ~1 hour/day -- inspired a shared/membership model instead of a private home unit.
- Opened last September; by ~6 months in had 28 members, targeting a max of 40 members (one simulator can't practically support more without booking friction).
- Owner explicitly built it as "a customer of one" -- designed for what he personally wanted (private space, bring family, no strangers watching) and it worked because of that focus/passion, not because he chased a broad market.

## Numbers
- Startup cost: ~$100,000 upfront (build-out, equipment, finish-out).
- Break-even: reached in 3 months (needed ~15 members at their pricing to cover costs).
- Revenue at 28 members: ~$7,000/month (average revenue per member ~$250/month).
- Monthly profit at 28 members: $3,000-4,000/month; projected $6,000-8,000/month profit at full capacity (40 members) — targeting ~$100k/year profit at max capacity.
- Net profit margin: 50-55%.
- Monthly costs are low: rent ~$31/sq ft (~$3,300-3,400/month total), scheduling software $40/month, insurance, utilities -- no significant technology/staffing costs.
- Software subscription for door access/scheduling: $40/month runs the entire operation (24/7 automated door access tied to booked tee times).

## Membership/pricing model (three tiers)
- "Par" (entry): $175/month for 4 tee times.
- "Birdie" (mid): $275/month for 8 tee times.
- "Eagle" (unlimited): $325/month, come anytime -- economically the best value per visit, and the business intentionally didn't want to underprice this to avoid overdemand.
- Also added punch cards (5 or 10 sessions, must use within 6 months) later to capture lower-frequency users.
- Member tee time length: up to 2 hours per session for members; up to 1 hour for punch-card users.
- Unlimited guests per tee time allowed (families of 8-14 have come) -- differentiator vs. franchise competitors who cap guests at 3-4 per bay.

## Process / how he got started
- Talked to ~10+ friends before opening; 5 verbally committed, all 5 eventually became paying members (2 took longer to convert). Early friend-members gave product feedback (data display, club storage, putting space) before public launch.
- Chose location within 5 miles of home specifically to keep it low-burden; found a strip-mall space with road frontage and residential proximity (rationale: high foot/drive traffic still creates awareness, but a small membership cap (40) doesn't require the high-traffic anchor typical of retail).
- Negotiated the lease creatively: found out the existing tenant (a vape shop) was under investigation for selling to minors, contacted the landlord directly offering to take over the lease at 15% below the existing rent -- landlord agreed two weeks later.
- Negotiated a lease clause so rent didn't start until the business opened, reducing risk of paying rent during buildout delays.
- Used a personal connection (a real estate attorney friend) to secure leasehold improvement allowances that reduced buildout costs.
- Deliberately did zero marketing/grand opening in phase one to isolate and learn organic/word-of-mouth demand before spending on marketing for future locations (~15 of 28 members were personal contacts; the rest were drive-by/word-of-mouth).
- Roughly 100,000 people live within 5 miles of the location; only 40 members are needed to max out the business -- illustrates how small a target market slice is required.

## Equipment/vendor notes
- Considered budget fold-down-net home simulators but went premium once he committed to a membership (public-facing) model.
- Two dominant simulator brands named: Full Swing and TrackMan. TrackMan is popular with PGA instructors and portable, but has a 2-3 second display lag versus instantaneous feedback on Full Swing-style units indoors; some large franchises reportedly switched from TrackMan to Full Swing for this reason. He priced slightly above market on some tiers to reflect the more responsive tech.
- Simulator includes non-golf games (soccer, football, "Battleship"-style target games) which pulls in family/entertainment use beyond serious golfers.

## Scaling plan
- Next step: open a 2nd location, already has 3 sites identified and a right of first refusal on one starting that fall; this time will intentionally add real marketing/grand-opening spend to see how much faster ramp-to-40 can go compared to the word-of-mouth-only first location.
- Longer-term scaling ladder described: 1 → 2 → 5 locations within a couple of years, then reassess for efficiencies before going 5 → 10 → 20. At ~20 locations, would consider franchising vs. staying owner-operated.
- Prefers owning over franchising: believes franchisees at 2-3 locations of proof won't hit ramp benchmarks (e.g., reaching 40 members within 6 months) as reliably, causing franchisee friction.
- Considering reciprocal membership access across future multi-location members (a member near one location being able to use another).

## Caveats / risk mitigants explicitly mentioned
- "I hate the phrase passive income" — very low maintenance (24/7 automated door access, remote monitoring via cameras) but still required real active work up front to reach that state.
- Member mix matters: too many low-frequency members undersells capacity économics (leaving demand unmet), but too many high-frequency/"obsessive" members creates booking friction and lowers effective max capacity (40 could shrink toward 35 with heavier users) — needs "enthusiast, but not obsessive" customers.
- Recommends not waiting for perfect conditions (e.g., permits) before opening — learned meaningfully from early access/member feedback during buildout.
- Advises using personal network for deal-structuring advice (attorneys, lease terms) to de-risk the large ($100k) upfront capital commitment.
