# You Can Now Build Apps for Free With Google AI Studio (w/ Google Insider)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9uBW16KlwB4 | Published: 2025-10-24

Interview with Logan Kilpatrick (Google DeepMind, leads AI Studio/Gemini API developer product team) about "vibe coding" apps with Google's AI Studio and the business opportunities it opens up. Live demo of building two apps (a video-analysis tool and a voice-agent lead-gen form) in AI Studio during the call. Less a single "side hustle idea" video, more a toolset + business-opportunity discussion.

## Core business idea: AI implementation/automation agency for small businesses
- Opportunity framed explicitly: small businesses (Chris cites ~400 million small businesses worldwide) need AI but don't know how to use the tools — even Fortune 200 executives at a conference Logan attended "don't know how to use AI tools" despite knowing it's important.
- The pitch is not that the tools are hard to build with (they're now nearly free/instant) — the opportunity is in doing the implementation *for* business owners who won't do it themselves, same as people still pay for Facebook/Google Ads management or website builders despite those being technically easy to DIY.
- Concrete sales tactic described: build a working AI-powered demo of a prospect's own website/product first (e.g., Logan describes vibe-coding a mood-boarding/virtual try-on app live on a call with a clothing brand while they were still describing the idea), then use the finished demo itself as the cold-outreach pitch — "Hey, I redesigned your website using AI, here it is" — instead of a generic "I do AI stuff" pitch. Marginal cost of producing each personalized demo is near zero, enabling much richer, more effective outreach at scale.
- Because building is now nearly instant, take many small "shots on goal" rather than one big bet — reference to indie hacker John Rush (a past guest, "makes $3M/year from a dozen-ish AI tools," admittedly 80/20 where most tools don't make much) cross-selling multiple small AI tools to the same startup/indie-hacker audience via shared email lists and ad accounts to increase surface area for finding what sells.

## Tooling and workflow specifics (for building AI products/demos cheaply)
- Google AI Studio (ai.studio / ai.studio/build) lets anyone describe an app in a prompt and get a working prototype in ~60–90 seconds, at effectively $0 cost to build (cost only kicks in once you deploy and serve real users).
- Live demo built during the episode: a video-analysis app — upload any video (they tested a 270MB file, supports up to ~1GB) and Gemini (2.5 Pro model, used via AI Studio) parses it, extracts "10 key takeaways," and lets the user customize the report style with follow-up prompts. Works even on video with no audio/text (native video+image understanding, not just frame sampling).
- Also demoed a voice AI agent (using Gemini's "Live API") that acts as a website's lead-gen form: greets a visitor conversationally, collects name/email/company, then (intended to) trigger an email + CRM/database entry once done — built from one long prompt in under 90 seconds, though the CRM-trigger part didn't work zero-shot and needed further prompting ("you're 10 prompts away from the home run" — persistence/iteration is framed as the main skill, not getting it right on the first try).
- Practical use case for creators: build a personal video-search pipeline — vibe-code a tool that ingests your own back catalog of podcast/video content and lets you query across all of it for things you said but forgot ("did I ever talk about X?").

## Product/idea-generation advice
- Bias toward *more* niche, not less ("that's not niche enough" rather than "too niche") — e.g., an app that shows only different curtain styles (not full room redesign) solved the actual narrow problem a real user had.
- Best ideas often come from solving your own specific problem/domain experience (e.g., a hairstylist building "Haircut AI" with real insider insight) rather than copying a trending idea with no domain edge — but he also argues an outsider/non-expert can stumble into good ideas precisely because they aren't biased by industry conventional wisdom (cites the book "Range").
- Packaging multiple thin/niche AI tools together for one persona (e.g., "10 AI tools every student needs") can reach critical mass/search volume that a single ultra-niche tool (low individual search volume) can't get alone — an underexplored go-to-market angle per Logan.
- Voice AI agents and computer-use agents flagged as the two most underexploited opportunity areas right now (Google shipped a new computer-use/agentic-navigation model literally the day before this recording).

## Caveats
- Interview explicitly states it is not a paid/sponsored placement (Chris discloses he's a Google shareholder and DeepMind paid $0), so the enthusiasm for Google's tools should be read with that in mind.
- No concrete revenue/pricing numbers given for the "AI implementation agency" idea — Logan explicitly says "I don't know what the actual numbers look like" on market size, this is directional opinion, not validated data.
- Flagged as an overhyped/weak AI use case: AI travel-booking apps that just automate simple tasks (e.g., flight booking) that are already easy via existing airline websites — the panel argues AI should solve the *hard* orchestration problems (multi-leg logistics, local transit info) rather than trivial ones, and that naive routing logic can produce absurd results (e.g., inserting an unnecessary layover to save $3).
