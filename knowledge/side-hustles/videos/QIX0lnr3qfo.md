# How to Make $120k/Year with An Army of AI Influencers
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIX0lnr3qfo | Published: 2026-01-29

Solo screen-recorded walkthrough by Chris Koerner demonstrating how to create and monetize an AI-generated "influencer" using Higsfield.ai's AI Influencer Studio, aimed at people who don't want to show their own face on camera. (Note: despite the title's "$120k/year" claim, no such figure or its derivation actually appears in the transcript — the video doesn't substantiate that number; treat it as aspirational marketing framing, not a demonstrated result.)

## The idea
- Use an AI video-generation platform (Higsfield.ai) to design a fully synthetic "AI influencer" character, generate short-form video content of that character (optionally driven by uploading a video of yourself so the AI character mimics your own movements/actions), and post the content to social platforms — for people who want to create content and monetize it without showing their own face.
- Design philosophy for making the AI character engaging: pick unusual/distinctive visual traits (skin tone/condition, hair, eyes, scars, etc.) specifically to maximize viewer retention (people pause/stare trying to figure out if it's real) — cites an existing viral AI influencer with half-black/half-white skin as inspiration. Avoid going so obviously non-human (e.g., visibly reptilian/animal features) that viewers immediately disengage — the goal is to delay the "wait, is this AI?" realization for a few seconds, which platforms reward with retention-driven distribution.
- Higsfield's character creator has ~90 customization parameters (species/human, gender, ethnicity, skin color/condition, eye color, hairstyle, body type, facial features, surface patterns like visible veins, etc.); each character generation costs a small number of platform credits (2 credits cited for the character; 8 credits for an 8-second-ish clip).
- You can feed Higsfield a video of yourself performing an action, and it will generate a version of your AI-influencer character performing that same action/movement — described as a hybrid approach injecting personal creativity/direction into an otherwise fully synthetic character, argued to be more likely to succeed than a "fully autopilot" AI character with no human creative input behind it.
- Suggested use cases beyond a personal alter-ego: comedy pages, brand mascots, virtual models, faceless niche content pages, or topic-based pages (e.g., a Formula 1 racing content account) — the AI character doesn't have to represent a specific "person."

## Monetization process (step by step, Higsfield's affiliate/creator program)
1. Connect a social account (demonstrated with Instagram) to Higsfield by requesting a verification code, adding it to your Instagram bio, and verifying.
2. Browse "active campaigns" under Higsfield's "Earn" tab.
3. Post AI-generated video content to the connected platform (e.g., as an Instagram Reel) with a specific required caption phrase ("Made in AI Influencer Studio on higsfield.ai") to qualify for payout — Higsfield pays creators for driving traffic/awareness to their tool this way, functioning as an affiliate arrangement, notably even on Instagram Reels (which Instagram itself does not pay creators for directly).
4. Submit the post's link and a payment email (e.g., PayPal) through Higsfield's platform to register it for payout.
5. Minimum bar to qualify a brand-new account for Higsfield's program: 1,000 cumulative views across at least 3 posts (not 1,000 views per post) — described as an easily achievable threshold.

## Platform payout rates cited (RPM = revenue per 1,000 views, what actually nets to the creator, distinct from CPM)
- Facebook Reels: $0.10-$0.30 per 1,000 views (author claims his own Facebook Reels commonly earn $50-$500 per video since Facebook Reels "go viral quite easily").
- Instagram: does not pay for Reels directly, but does pay for regular posts/carousels (which Higsfield can also generate images for).
- YouTube Shorts: $0.05-$0.35 per 1,000 views; YouTube long-form video pays substantially more, $4-$25 per 1,000 views.
- TikTok: videos must be over 1 minute to qualify for monetization; pays $0.30-$3.00 per 1,000 views, but is described as inconsistent/"all over the board."
- Threads and LinkedIn: do not pay creators directly.
- Explicit caveat from the host: direct platform ad-revenue payouts alone are very unlikely to make anyone a millionaire — the bigger monetization opportunity is using AI-influencer content to drive traffic off-platform: building an email list/newsletter, selling affiliate products, or selling your own digital products/courses.

## Caveats
- The demonstrated example in the video (posting a low-effort test clip to a small niche account) was explicitly framed by the host as a low-expectation test, not a proven monetization result — no revenue figures from his own test are given.
- Lower barrier to entry (any of these can be launched with a small number of platform credits, no filming equipment or on-camera presence needed) also means increasing competition over time — framed by the host as a reason to act early rather than wait.
- The title's "$120k/year" figure is not derived or explained anywhere in the transcript; there is no case study or math shown to support it in this video.
