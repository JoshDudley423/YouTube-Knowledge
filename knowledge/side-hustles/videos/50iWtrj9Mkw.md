# Forget Big Apps. These Tiny Tools Are Making Millions
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=50iWtrj9Mkw | Published: 2026-05-19

Solo episode by Chris Koerner on "the great unbundling" / "API arbitrage": wrapping one narrow feature of a large existing platform (scraping tools, AI APIs, data marketplaces) in a simple branded website, vibe-coded with no technical background, and charging a markup. Includes a real case study and 12+ specific business ideas.

## The core concept: "the great unbundling"
- Historical framing: Craigslist bundled dozens of features (jobs, personals, housing, for-sale, forums, services/gigs) onto one ugly page; separate companies "unbundled" single categories into standalone, better-designed, mobile-friendly billion-dollar businesses — Zillow/Airbnb/Redfin (housing), Tinder (personals), Indeed/ZipRecruiter/Upwork (jobs, Indeed alone >$3B/year revenue), OfferUp/Facebook Marketplace (for-sale), Reddit (discussion forums, $7B IPO valuation), Thumbtack/TaskRabbit/Shiftgig (services/gigs).
- Current-day equivalent: today's "new Craigslists" are AI/API platforms that bundle thousands of features into one tool — named examples: OpenAI/ChatGPT, Anthropic/Claude, Apify (scraper/AI-tool marketplace, 30,000+ pre-built "actors"/scrapers), Clay (clay.com, a lead-enrichment tool with ~400 different features), Zapier (automation/API marketplace).
- The opportunity: pick ONE narrow feature from one of these bundled platforms, wrap it in a simple, clean-branded website/app (vibe-coded, no engineering background required), and sell access to that single feature at a markup to a specific niche audience — "you don't have to build software, you don't have to invent anything new, just put your own rims on it."
- Why customers pay a markup rather than using the underlying tool directly: the underlying tool (e.g. Apify) may be too technical, may carry an unwanted monthly subscription, or customers simply may not know it exists / prefer a simpler, single-purpose interface even at a higher price — the wrapper business competes on price, support, and ease of use, not on technology.

## Case study: Nico (the "API arbitrage" example)
- Background: former pasta cook at his family's restaurant in New Orleans, zero tech/coding/sales experience. Went to Mexico during COVID for a planned 4-day trip, stayed 6 months after meeting remote tech workers, decided to leave hospitality.
- Path: got a job as an SDR (sales development rep, cold-calling/cold-emailing for a software company) — learned Clay.com there for lead enrichment (finding emails/LinkedIn/company data), which exposed him to what "tech" tools could do.
- Quit his tech-sales job in late 2024/early 2025, began vibe coding (using Claude, OpenAI Codex) with no coding background, partnered with a technical friend who fixes what AI-generated code breaks and handles scaling — Nico is "the idea guy," his friend does the technical heavy lifting when needed.
- Product built: a Google Maps lead-scraper website — user types in e.g. "gyms in Texas," gets a spreadsheet of every matching business. Built on top of an existing Apify scraper (API), vibe-coded frontend, Stripe integration for payment.
- Pricing/model: $35/month, but unused subscription value converts to non-expiring credits (customers can cancel and come back months later to use remaining credits) — explicitly designed to respect subscription fatigue and reported to be well received.
- Results (~7 months in at time of recording): $3,500/month recurring revenue, near-zero costs, near-zero customer support (self-serve), growing "double digits" monthly, acquired entirely through word of mouth (told friends in the sales space, no paid ads ever run).

## Where to find "actors"/APIs to wrap
- **Apify** (apify.com): marketplace with 30,000+ pre-built "actors" (scrapers/AI tools) — e.g., Instagram follower scraper, LinkedIn scraper, Facebook group scraper, Reddit scraper, Google Maps scraper, micro-influencer scraper, Import Yeti-style import/shipping data scraper, Upwork freelancer scraper, App Store/Google Play/Trustpilot/Indeed/Crunchbase/Reddit-sentiment scrapers. Mechanism: get an API key from the actor, plug it into a vibe-coding tool (Replit, Lovable, Claude, Codex), and connect it to a simple frontend with a search bar — described as buildable "in an afternoon."
- **Zapier**: an automation/API marketplace; example built idea is a customer-service auto-responder for Shopify stores (Zapier reads FAQ/return/shipping policy content and past sent emails to auto-answer repetitive customer service emails via OpenAI) — cost is low (Zapier's $29-59/month tier covers hundreds/thousands of credits), suggested resale price ~$100/month per Shopify store, ~95% margin.
- **OpenAI API** — used as the "brain" behind report-generation tools (see Amazon review analyzer idea below); cost is "pennies per report."

## 12+ specific business ideas discussed
1. **Apify actor wrappers (generic)** — same playbook as Nico; buy a $10 domain, vibe-code a wrapper around any actor (Instagram/LinkedIn/Facebook-group/Reddit scraper), host it, done in an afternoon.
2. **Niche Google Maps scraper** — instead of "all businesses," narrow to dentists, roofers, lawyers, "Texas small businesses," etc. Buyers: B2B sales teams, cold callers/SDRs, marketing agencies needing local business lists. Suggested pricing: $35/month + non-expiring credit packs. Marketing tip: just post about it on LinkedIn even with a small (452) follower count — no influencer status needed.
3. **Amazon review scraper/analyzer for DTC brands** — customer pastes in a competitor's Amazon URL; tool scrapes ~500 reviews (via Apify), runs them through ChatGPT/OpenAI API, outputs a one-page report of top compliments/complaints/common phrases/suggested improvements. Target market: ~3-4 million Shopify brands + several million independent Amazon sellers wanting competitive intel. Suggested pricing: $50-100/month subscription or $19 one-off report; cost is "pennies" per report.
4. **Zapier-based Shopify customer service auto-responder** (described above under Zapier).
5. **Micro-influencer marketplace/scraper** — scrape Instagram/TikTok/YouTube creators in a follower-count band (e.g., 5,000-100,000, or specifically "under 50,000"), optionally niche-specific (micro-fitness, micro-food, mom influencers). Could be delivered as a $10/month newsletter of scraped leads rather than a live tool. (Host notes he personally tried this in 2019 and gave up because scraping tools were too immature at the time — implying it's more viable now.)
6. **Import/supplier intelligence tool** — Import Yeti shows which supplier/factory a company imports from and often what they pay; an Apify actor can scrape this same data, letting you build a cheaper/differently-targeted wrapper around it (example: look up "Lululemon" to see their import sourcing/containers/lead times).
7. **Shopify store list/scraper reseller** — services like BuiltWith.com sell access to lists of the 3-4 million Shopify stores (used as a customer-acquisition list for several of these other ideas); could hire an Upwork VA who already maintains such a scraped list, or build a "BuiltWith but only for Shopify stores" at a fraction of BuiltWith's ~$450/month price (suggested: $4.50-$45/month or ~$5/scrape in credits).
8. **Upwork natural-language freelancer search tool** — wrap an existing Apify Upwork-scraping actor so users can type natural-language queries (e.g., "Ukrainian mobile developers, 5+ years experience, 4.8+ rating, $50K+ earnings") instead of using Upwork's limited native search filters. Suggested price: $100/month; buyers: agencies, recruiters, startup founders.
9. **Unbundle Clay.com itself** — Clay bundles ~400 lead-enrichment features, but most users likely only use 1-2 regularly (e.g., email validation, or attaching a first name to a bare email address); build a single-purpose tool doing just that one thing very well/cheaply. Suggested testing approach: build 5 such single-feature ideas, spend $100 in ad spend testing each, keep the one with the lowest cost per acquisition.
10. **"Niche Zillow" for underserved real-estate categories** — Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com cover residential; LoopNet and Crexi cover commercial but are expensive, limited, and aggressive on sales outreach. Opportunity: build a well-designed, Zillow-quality-UX marketplace for a narrow real estate subcategory currently served only by bad-UX listing sites — examples: RV parks, mobile home parks, self-storage facilities, laundromats, car washes, duplexes/fourplexes. Suggested build approach: since building a two-sided marketplace from scratch is hard, scrape listings that already exist in adjacent broad marketplaces (Crexi, LoopNet, BizBuySell) rather than sourcing sellers directly.
11. **"Niche BizBuySell" for specific business-for-sale categories** — e.g., ATM routes, vending routes, billboards, convenience stores, yogurt shops. Suggested validation method: scrape all BizBuySell listings in one state, re-scrape a week later, diff what sold to find which business categories move fastest/have the most transaction velocity, and only build an unbundled niche marketplace around categories proven to sell well (e.g., host notes if restaurants historically sell poorly on BizBuySell, don't build "Zillow for restaurants").
12. **Weekly trend-report newsletters powered by scrapers** — e.g., a TikTok hashtag/trend scraper run weekly for a chosen niche (beauty, fitness, finance), compiled into a trend report emailed to marketing agencies for $50-100/month.
13. **Local-business data scraper/reseller (Yelp-based)** — e.g., "every roofer in Phoenix" or "every chiropractor in Atlanta" with phone/email/owner name/review count/rating, sold to local service businesses wanting to identify acquisition targets or to suppliers wanting to pitch them.
14. **Niche Indeed clone** — e.g., a job board narrowly for nurse practitioners, built by scraping/unbundling Indeed's listings for that one category.
15. **Crunchbase funding-alert scraper** and **Reddit/Twitter brand-sentiment scraper** — brands want to know what's being said about them; Twitter/X API costs have recently dropped, making this more affordable than before.

## Key caveats / timing framework
- **"The efficacy of a growth hack is inversely correlated with its lifespan"** — the better a tactic works, the faster it gets copied and the shorter its window of opportunity. Historical examples cited: cold email in 2010 (100 emails → ~15 replies) vs. today (10,000 emails needed for similar replies); Google Ads in 2005 (~$2 customer acquisition cost) vs. today (~$40); Facebook ads in 2014; TikTok in 2020. Host estimates the API-arbitrage/unbundling window is currently "year two or three of a 7-10 year span" — implying urgency to act soon rather than an indefinitely open opportunity.
- Explicit rebuttal of the old VC mantra "don't build something that could just be a feature of a bigger platform" (cited counter-example: Snapchat, dismissed by that logic in the 2010s, is now a public, thriving company) — argument is that in the AI era, since foundation-model differentiation has narrowed (e.g., DeepSeek and other open-source models reaching ~80% of frontier model quality), "a feature can be a company," and building "ChatGPT for podiatrists" is not meaningfully competing with OpenAI.
- General framing: cost to start is near-zero (domain + vibe-coding tool + API credits), tools are free/cheap because VC-funded platforms subsidize free trials, and big platforms have no incentive to compete with or shut down small niche wrapper businesses built on their APIs.
- Explicit call to action given: pick something to unbundle this week, build a minimal wrapper (search bar + one API call) in an afternoon/weekend without necessarily even launching it, purely as a forcing function to learn the skill.
