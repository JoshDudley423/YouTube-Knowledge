# My 4 Favorite AI Business Ideas for 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7RUdSNFwIw | Published: 2024-05-20

Riffing episode with Nik (Nikonomics Podcast). Substance below; the episode ends in extended comedy/personal riffing that is skipped as filler.

- Idea 1 -- "Facebook group lead-gen as a service" (productizing a known growth hack): a person named Danny took the tactic of posting in local Facebook groups as "a young guy/new business owner" asking for community support (which triggers people's supportive/"pity" instinct and generates leads) and turned it into a paid service: he gets the business owner's Facebook login, posts in local community groups on their behalf using a systemized version of the pitch, funnels responses to a Google form, and charges the (often older/less tech-savvy, "Boomer") business owner $750/month for the leads generated this way.
  - Why Facebook specifically: older demographics (Boomers+) are disproportionately active on Facebook and have more disposable income, while being less likely to know how to run this kind of lead gen themselves -- described as "shooting fish in a barrel."
  - Example of buying/using an existing Facebook group as a lead funnel for an owned business, rather than a client's, was also mentioned as a related tactic previously used by the hosts.
  - Reference case of a related visual pattern: "College Fund Landscaping" and similar branded landscaping companies with wrapped trucks apparently doing well by using relatable "help fund my education" framing at scale (Danny also pitched a plan to scale a landscaping business nationwide via college students).
  - Business partnership take: hosts preferred the pure lead-generation service model (find businesses, generate leads, no franchise/field-ops needed, centrally scalable) over the landscaping/franchise version, which requires managing many local operators/logistics -- "more heads, more logistics, more scale, more profit" vs. "less headache, less profit" tradeoff explicitly discussed; hosts leaned toward the lower-operational-complexity version.
- Idea 2 -- "Negative CAC" content-driven service businesses: model where a business generates revenue/attention from a YouTube audience for performing a visible, satisfying service for free, meaning customer acquisition cost is effectively less than zero (you're paid, via ad revenue/sponsorships, to acquire the customer). Examples cited: a YouTube channel with millions of subscribers that mows people's lawns for free and films it; Kevin Espiritu's "Epic Gardening" channel, where YouTube ad/sponsor revenue funds selling raised garden planters to the audience.
  - Key requirement identified: needs a visual, satisfying "before/after" component to work as content (mowing, power washing, gutter cleaning, tree trimming, hair salons/haircuts) -- and needs to be a service any homeowner/consumer could plausibly use (ruled out something like roofing as not fitting this pattern as well). Referenced "Primitive Builders"-style ASMR/no-dialogue build videos and Tesla-supercharger car washing (car wash while charging) as adjacent visual-content business concepts, framed half-jokingly as solvable engineering problems despite the obvious "you can't mix water and EV charging" objection.
  - Requires already having built an audience/distribution before the free-service tactic works as a customer acquisition channel -- doing the free service without an audience first doesn't generate the same negative-CAC effect.
- Idea 3 -- AI-generated/automated niche podcasts and content aggregation:
  - Backstory: hosts previously ran an experiment (~"Startup Gems," started ~Aug of the prior year) using a cloned AI voice (of the host) reading curated popular business stories into 5-minute daily episodes, for about 4 months, garnering modest but real traction (10-30 downloads/day). After transitioning that voice/library into launching an actual named podcast, they noticed ~10% of new podcast downloads were people going back and binging the old AI-generated back-catalog episodes -- used as validation that a standing content library compounds in value over time even after being "abandoned."
  - Proposed model: fully automated, disclosed-as-AI-generated niche podcasts/content that update on a recurring cadence (e.g., daily), covering a narrow topic (e.g., home health/hospice industry news, or a daily recap of a specific niche) -- framed as extremely asymmetric economically: cited cost as low as ~$18/month for podcast hosting, with automation (e.g., Zapier) handling daily uploads with near-zero ongoing time investment once set up. Key point: disclose it's AI-generated rather than trying to pass it off as human, since it's serving people content they're already actively seeking out (a summary of yesterday's news, industry-specific updates), not attempting deception.
  - Related idea: repurpose an existing back-catalog of short AI-generated content episodes as a YouTube binge-watch playlist to build audience trust/intimacy at scale.
  - Related idea: an AI-powered daily social-media digest/summary tool -- auto-summarize the prior day's tweets/notifications from people you follow (or a specific group chat/community), optionally filtered to only high-engagement posts (e.g., tweets with 50,000+ impressions), and optionally read aloud via text-to-speech in a chosen voice, so users don't have to manually scroll to catch up. Same underlying pattern applied to group chats (WhatsApp, family texts) that get too voluminous to manually catch up on.
  - Related idea (custom trained chatbot on your own content): example given of someone building a custom ChatGPT bot trained on the hosts' own podcast episode transcripts, suggesting doing something similar combined with scraped daily industry news, delivered in the hosts' own cloned voice/personality as a niche daily briefing product.
- Idea 4 -- AI-powered "80% functionality for 10% of cost" software arbitrage, applied to global talent/staffing: referencing Chamath Palihapitiya's "8090" fund concept of using AI to rebuild expensive enterprise software (e.g., Salesforce-equivalent) at a fraction of the cost/customized per business. Applied specifically to the outsourced/global-staffing space: rather than building broad global-talent placement infrastructure (sourcing pipeline, overseas team management, sales) from scratch, niche down to a single vertical placement type (e.g., only lead-generation talent, only video editing/content-creation talent, or only home-services staffing) and white-label an existing overseas staffing infrastructure provider on the back end -- letting you capture demand in a specific niche without building full operational infrastructure yourself.
  - Business-model comparison of two real companies discussed as an example of unbundling a broader marketplace (Upwork) into niche-specific plays: one company charges a one-time placement fee (~$3,000-5,000 per hire, ~80% margins), another (apparently white-labeling off the first) charges a recurring monthly fee (~$3,000-5,000/month) for what's functionally the same sourced talent -- illustrating that the same underlying labor supply can be repackaged into different pricing/business models by different downstream niche players ("unbundling never stops"; once niched enough, a business can "land and expand" into adjacent services, similar to a roll-up/agency acquiring adjacent complementary service providers).

Caveats/risks:
- The Facebook-group lead-gen tactic depends on getting a client's Facebook login/account access to post as them -- a real practical/trust dependency, and community groups or Facebook policy could restrict or penalize this kind of account-sharing/soliciting behavior over time.
- The "negative CAC" content model only works once you already have a built audience/distribution; the visual/satisfying "before-after" requirement limits which service categories are a good fit, and the hosts acknowledge they hadn't done deep research validating other industries beyond the ones named.
- AI-generated content podcasts/summaries are explicitly recommended to be labeled/disclosed as AI-generated -- presented as a quantity/utility play (delivering content people are already seeking), not a way to fake authenticity.
- The staffing/white-label niche-down idea is speculative commentary/analysis of two real companies' business models by the hosts, not something either host had built themselves at time of recording -- framed as an opportunity thesis, not a proven playbook.
