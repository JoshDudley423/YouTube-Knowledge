# He Makes $27K/Month Sending Text Messages
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=69eN9vRtWB8 | Published: 20260930

Interview with Aaron Fuks, founder of Hytros, who builds paid referral pipelines connecting home-service businesses to each other (e.g. roofers to siding companies).

## The business idea
- Broker introductions between two complementary (non-competing) home-service businesses that naturally refer work to each other along a customer's project journey — e.g. a roofing company doing an insurance job often also needs a siding repair, so connect them to a siding company; a general contractor/luxury home builder needs a painter, electrician, or landscaper.
- You are not generating consumer leads (like Angie's List/Thumbtack/HomeAdvisor, which sell the same lead to multiple competing businesses and leave a bad taste); you are introducing two business owners who then refer real paying jobs to each other repeatedly, for free, going forward.
- Revenue comes from charging the smaller/specialized business for each qualified introduction/appointment with a potential referral partner, then later for ongoing "nurturing" (keeping the relationship active) as a monthly retainer.
- Origin story: started as a part-time receptionist for a small siding-repair company, noticed it depended entirely on referrals from one larger siding company; realized the relationship itself (not just leads) was the valuable asset, and started manually building the same kind of relationships for pay.

## Numbers
- Revenue ramp: $14K one month (June) → $27K the next full month (September) → $15K in a following 30-day window, roughly doubling over about nine months after a pricing/model pivot (see below); current monthly recurring revenue (nurturing fees only, excluding one-time introduction revenue) is about $16K.
- Original pricing: $150 flat fee per qualified appointment/introduction handed to a client.
- Current pricing: $6,000 for a package of 10 introductions/appointments (a ~4x price increase from the original per-appointment rate), plus a separate monthly nurturing/relationship-management fee of $250-500/client.
- Guarantee offered: at least 2 real bids/quotes out of the first 20 appointments delivered, or non-performing partner introductions get replaced with new ones (not refunded).
- Example payoff for one painting client: a single text campaign to custom home builders and interior designers produced 4 bids worth $17,000-$64,000 within 48 hours — described as paying for roughly 3 years of that client's nurturing service in one shot.
- Another example: a landscaping client (wanting to move from residential to commercial/developer work) was introduced to an 80-home subdivision development, yielding a $4M project bid opportunity plus introductions to every homeowner in that subdivision.
- Another example: an electrician was introduced to a luxury home builder with 5 active jobs, including one $110,000 project on a 10,000 sq ft home, discovered only through this outreach.
- Cold-call response/close rates (original phone-only model): 20-40 calls/day yielded roughly 2 appointments/day (5-10% close rate on calls reaching a live person); each call-and-dial cycle took only ~20 minutes total because unanswered calls are abandoned after ~15 seconds with no voicemail left.
- Cold-text campaign response rates (current pivoted model): mass-texting a list of potential referral *partners* (offering them a free project/bid) gets a 2-5% reply rate; mass-texting a list of potential paying *clients* (leading with an already-secured lead/value, not an ask) gets a much higher 10-15% reply rate.
- Reported economics: ~90% profit margin — software (GoHighLevel) costs a few hundred dollars/month, texting/calling costs ~$100-150/month; the only "labor" cost is one person helping with calls on a straight 30%-of-appointment commission, no base pay, no other employees.

## Process / steps
- Two-sided outbound motion, built in this order: (1) first mass-text a list of potential referral *partners* in a complementary trade (e.g. roofers, if your client is a siding company) asking if they currently have a project needing your client's specialty and offering a free bid — this generates a small number of real, provable leads; (2) use those real secured leads as proof when mass-texting potential paying *clients* ("I have a roofer right now with an insurance bid that needs siding — want the introduction?") — leading with delivered value gets a far higher response rate than a cold ask.
- Call, don't just text, the moment someone replies positively to a text — text is used only to filter/qualify at scale, the actual relationship-building and closing happens on a live phone call.
- Explicitly frame every pitch and conversation as "we connect businesses" / "we're building a relationship," never as "we sell leads" — many small-business owners have been burned by lead-gen companies before, and this framing lets the business genuinely (and truthfully) say "no, we're not a lead-gen company."
- Price low to start (the original $150/appointment) specifically to de-risk the first client relationship while the close rate on your appointments is still unproven, then raise price sharply (4x in this case) once you have evidence the appointments convert to real bids.
- Qualify the right contact on the referral-partner side: a conversation with the actual business owner converts far better than one with a project manager or office gatekeeper, since the owner is directly incentivized by new referral relationships in a way an employee usually isn't.
- Track every contact, referral sent, and follow-up need inside a CRM so a client's referral network doesn't go stale once they get busy — this ongoing relationship maintenance (storm-triggered mass outreach to a client's full partner network, re-engagement automations) is what the recurring monthly "nurturing" fee is sold against.
- Expanding to new verticals: the same two-step (secure a real lead for a partner business → use it to pitch a paying client) was repeated across electricians/custom builders, painters/interior designers, and landscapers/property developers — described as a generalizable pattern applicable to almost any pair of non-competing, sequential-in-the-customer-journey home-service trades.

## Tools/platforms named
- GoHighLevel: used for the built-in power dialer (set to a 15-second ring/abandon threshold), mass SMS campaigns, custom call-disposition tracking, per-client sub-accounts, and referral/relationship-tracking automations (e.g. auto-texting a client's whole partner network when a storm hits their area). Sponsor of this video.
- hytros.com — the guest's own business site, which also sells a self-serve "referral pipeline blueprint" for business owners who want to run this playbook themselves without hiring the guest's agency, plus white-label/partnership arrangements for marketing agencies wanting to resell it.

## Caveats / risks
- The guest explicitly notes some uncertainty around TCPA/opt-in text-messaging compliance, stating he hasn't run into problems because response rates are high and opt-outs are low, and because business-to-business texting (to numbers publicly listed on Google Maps) is subject to more lenient rules than business-to-consumer texting — this is presented as his own risk assessment, not verified legal guidance.
- A real structural risk is "working yourself out of a job": if you successfully deliver enough referral partners to a client, they may stop needing new appointments — the guest's answer is to monetize the ongoing relationship-management/nurturing work instead of only one-time introductions.
- The model depends on the person making calls/texts being comfortable with heavy daily outbound outreach — the guest attributes his own success partly to an unusually high prior comfort level with cold-calling strangers from an earlier job.
- No case study yet shown scaling this beyond the guest's own single-operator business (plus one commission-only caller) — the economics described are for a very small team, not a proven larger-scale agency.
