# Everyone Overlooks This Simple Side Hustle
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5TZylaKGWUE | Published: 2026-06-18

Interview with Don, owner of Boo Boo's Lemonade (boobooslemonade.com), a fresh-squeezed lemonade business that grew from a farmers-market side project with his kids into a ~$250K/year operation across markets, catering, and cart rentals.

## Origin and product
- Started 3 years before this recording after making fresh-squeezed lemonade for his kids at home; began doing farmers markets (Frisco Fresh Market, later added Dallas Farmers Market) as a side project while running an unrelated catering business, then left that business to focus on lemonade full-time.
- Recipe: one lemon per 32oz cup, wedged then smashed/juiced (retains oils), simple syrup (homemade, 1:1 sugar-to-water by volume, made in ~60-70 gallon batches at their warehouse from 50lb bags of cane sugar bought via Restaurant Depot / Ben E. Keith), filtered water, ~1 lb of ice per cup (roughly 50% ice, 50% liquid by volume in the cup). Recipe was learned from YouTube videos, not formal training. Optional flavor add-ins (strawberry, mango purees) offered but not the default — deliberately kept the base product simple/shelf-stable to avoid complexity.
- Deliberately differentiates from "fake" fresh lemonade competitors at other markets/festivals who actually sell reheated/rebranded bottled lemonade (e.g., Country Time or Milo's from Walmart) while claiming it's fresh-squeezed — makes juicing visible/theatrical to customers (wedger → smasher → syrup → shake) to prove authenticity, and says this differentiation matters more than direct competition concerns.

## Revenue and unit economics
- Year 1: ~$25,000-$30,000 revenue (didn't track profit closely that year).
- Most recent full year (2 markets, Frisco + Dallas): ~$250,000 in sales, achieved over roughly 8 effective months (Frisco market closes 2 months/year; rain also cuts into days).
- Per-cup economics: sells a 32oz cup for $7; direct cost ~$1.25 (including packaging/cup). Breakdown: ~1 lemon (~$0.20, seasonal), ~3oz simple syrup, ~1 lb ice (~$1/lb delivered via an ice supplier — called out as one of their biggest single cost lines). At partner venues where a commission (20-40%) is owed, price is raised to $10/cup, prioritizing volume over margin at those locations.
- Per-market-day revenue: Frisco market averages ~$1,200-$1,500/day (8am-4pm); Dallas market averages ~$2,500/day, i.e., ~$5,000 for a 2-day Dallas weekend. Booth fee at Dallas market: $300 total (not per day), including electricity.
- Best single day: an eclipse-day pop-up event (a weekday, non-recurring) in Irving, TX generated ~$6,000 in 3 hours, ~$5,000 of that profit.
- Startup costs: roughly $1,500 for the manual equipment (Sunkist citrus wedger ~$350, plus either a pneumatic lemon smasher — more expensive but audibly/visually theatrical and a customer draw — or basic handheld smashers from Amazon ~$120, which work but lack the same showmanship). Additional (variable) spend went into branding/decor R&D (his wife is a graphic designer and did packaging/booth design). Explicitly notable: the whole operation can technically be run with just a muddler, knife, and cutting board if volume is low — but at their volume, that would cause repetitive-strain injury ("you'll have arthritis after a few weeks").

## Business lines beyond the farmers-market booth
- **Catering**: typical corporate/family event catering order is $1,000-$3,000 for a 2-4 hour event. Example given: a $2,900 order for 300 pre-batched cups at $5/cup, once travel, "squeezologist" (server) labor, service fee, and bar-cart rental are factored in. Customer acquisition for catering: SEO plus upselling at their farmers-market booths; graduation season was specifically and successfully targeted as a seasonal catering niche.
- **Cart/trailer rental (no staff)**: rents out their lemonade cart/converted horse-trailer bar for events (weddings, cocktail hours, corporate events, even a "low-rider car show") at $500 for a 4-hour straight rental with drop-off, no labor required from them — the renting business/venue supplies staffing (e.g., a bartending company). Normal add-on: a $250 delivery/pickup fee (waived as a current promotional rate via Facebook Marketplace). Lead source for this specifically: Facebook Marketplace (no post-boosting needed — driven by organic posting plus personal network) and their own website.
- **Frozen banana / "apple nachos" catering add-on** (inspired directly by a prior Chris Koerner video about frozen banana businesses): example cited — a single catering event serving 1,500 frozen bananas + apple nachos billed at $19,000 total, with an estimated ~$12,000 (~63%) of that as profit after heavy labor costs; bananas sourced from Ben E. Keith (restaurant supplier, specific ripeness stage requested). Practical notes: frozen bananas don't spoil/perish once frozen (unlike lemons), making inventory forgiving; toppings (chocolate warmer, nuts) are simple to execute; lemonade is easier to sell on-site than bananas ("you have to explain bananas"), so they're planning to run both together and are investing in a small mobile chest freezer (like the coolers used at Buc-ee's) to go mobile with the banana side specifically.
- Growth ambitions at time of recording: targeting ~$500,000/year within 1-2 years via concession-company partnerships, convention center placements, and a continued push into graduation-season catering; working toward opening their first physical retail storefront (targeting Frisco, TX) by September; long-term aspirational goal of getting into large venues (e.g., sports arenas like American Airlines Center, Cowboys Stadium).

## Regulatory note
- Texas has a "lemonade law" (referenced as passed ~5 years prior) exempting minors (under 18) running a lemonade stand from needing HOA permission or health permits — Don planned to use this to have his own kids run an unpermitted stand at a local park.

## Advice given for starting this type of business
- "Just do it, stop overthinking it" — explicitly discourages upfront over-planning (e.g., agonizing over whether to form an LLC before ever setting up a table/tent).
- Get your recipe right, get a table and a tent, and post up — most markets require an application/vendor approval process, but persistence (having been a repeat vendor) helps.
- Pricing approach: settled on $7 as an "anchor price" based on researching competitor pricing in the market and wanting to signal good value at that size; reports getting essentially no pushback on price, suggesting there may be room to charge more.
- Same "just do it" advice applies to replicating this in any single-ingredient novelty food/drink concept (his explicit example: frozen bananas) — watching other creators' business-idea content directly generated this specific pivot for him.
- General framing echoed by the host: overlooked, "kids' summer lemonade stand"-tier business ideas are dismissed by adults as beneath them, but can scale into six-figure-plus operations with the right execution and systemization.
