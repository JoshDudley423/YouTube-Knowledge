# RV Park Business Investing Webinar
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dYgFGQvXif0 | Published: 2024-05-13

Live investor webinar (Chris Koerner + partner "John," a commercial real estate attorney) pitching two specific RV park syndication deals to accredited investors, plus a fund vehicle. This is a real-estate investment/capital-raise presentation rather than a hands-on operating tutorial -- most substance is about deal structure and RV park investing economics generally.

## Background / track record
- Koerner has been buying/investing in mobile home and RV parks for 5+ years; bought smaller long-term parks 2019-2021 across Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, Louisiana, Georgia with two partners, then sold that portfolio in 2021.
- Now operates as a co-GP at "Blue Metric" with John (attorney) and a partner; the firm has purchased ~$50M+ worth of RV parks (recently referenced growing from $30M/~$50M book value toward $50M purchased/~$70-75M book value), concentrated within roughly an 8-hour radius of Nashville, TN, aiming for portfolio scale/consolidation in a still-fragmented asset class.
- Thesis: RV parks are an under-consolidated, recession-resistant asset class with high barriers to entry (increasingly expensive to build from scratch) and more demand for RV stays than there is supply of parks.

## Deal 1: Wills Creek (existing park acquisition)
- Location: near Fort Payne, AL area, along the interstate connecting Birmingham and Chattanooga; near summer camps that draw traffic from Atlanta, Nashville, Chattanooga, and Birmingham.
- Size: 45 acres, 64 pad sites currently, largely full occupancy; long-term-stay park (as opposed to seasonal/short-term).
- Utilities: city water and city sewer (not septic) -- notable because septic-only parks are common in this space and scare off some buyers, but are often how a buyer secures an 10-11% cap rate.
- Raise: $2.2M total raise, ~$400k remaining to fill at time of webinar (an LP backed out despite the deal being fully allocated, which is why the raise was reopened).
- Financing: 30% down / 70% LTV loan, 7.95% interest rate, 5-year term; refinance rate assumption also ~7%.
- Return structure: 8% preferred return to LPs (100% of distributions up to 8%), then LPs get 70% / GPs get 30% of returns from 8-15% IRR, then converts to 50/50 above 15% IRR -- the "bump" typically triggers once they refinance and return capital, targeted year two or three of a hold.
- Expansion plan: adding RV sites along a creek on the property plus tiny homes (called "EverRooms" in the transcript, likely "Everoom"/site-built tiny home product), site-built for roughly $25,000 each; considered "phase one" with more land available for further expansion later. CapEx budget for expansion given as ~$1.1-1.266M (some ambiguity in the numbers as transcribed).
- Deal timeline: expected to close within about a week to a week and a half of the webinar.

## Deal 2: Seven Meadows (ground-up development)
- Location: ~30 minutes south of Nashville (outside Davidson County, in a fast-growing corridor south down I-65, near Spring Hill/College Grove -- an area cited in the Wall Street Journal as one of the most expensive/fastest-growing zip codes with heavy in-migration from NY/CA).
- Key demand driver: directly adjacent to a wedding venue (Birchwood at Bear Creek Farms area) with no nearby lodging -- thesis is RV sites + tiny homes ("EverRooms") will capture overflow wedding-guest demand; the region is described as a major wedding-destination market ("Bachelorette capital of the country," reportedly surpassing Las Vegas).
- Plan: 100 total RV sites (85 pad sites to start) plus tiny home units, developed in phases so RV sites can start generating cash flow before the full project completes; brought in a local experienced developer and an "EverRoom" partner (Eric) for execution since this is ground-up (higher-risk) vs. Blue Metric's usual playbook of buying and expanding existing parks.
- Financials: 10% preferred return, 60/40 LP/GP split up to 15% IRR, then 50/50 above that; target 35% IRR (more aggressive than Wills Creek given higher development risk); target hold 2-5 years with refinance in years 2-3; targeting a 3.05x equity multiple.
- All-in cost estimate: ~$735k for land (later corrected to ~$4.2M including land with buffer up to $5M) + ~$2M in improvements, projected to result in a ~$10M+ finished park value -- justified by scarcity of buildable land for new RV parks near fast-growing metro areas like Nashville, where zoning/regulatory approval is getting increasingly difficult.
- Risk factors named explicitly: construction cost overruns, delays, and risk to bonus depreciation eligibility if assets aren't placed in service within the tax year (60% bonus depreciation this year, dropping to 40% next year, contingent on stalled legislation). Site paving (a bigger cost than gravel, but improves long-term park value and can be phased in later) also flagged as a cost variable.
- Revenue assumptions described by presenters as conservative: only 30% RV occupancy and ~40% EverRoom occupancy assumed for 2025 in the model, with ~$195k projected pre-booking-driven revenue for the year via the wedding venue relationship.
- Raise deadline: aiming to close on land in June; investors encouraged to commit by end of that month due to anticipated high interest in the deal.

## General RV park investing/operating knowledge shared
- Macro demand drivers cited: Boomers retiring and traveling more, Millennials buying RVs and traveling more, and rising unaffordability of traditional housing pushing more people toward long-term RV/park living.
- Risk-hedging lever across the portfolio: if a park (short-term/seasonal) has occupancy issues, reposition marketing toward long-term tenants; if that's still insufficient, "backfill" by buying RVs and seller-financing/renting them to tenants who don't already own one, removing that barrier to occupancy.
- Primary realistic risks in this asset class are described as CapEx/expense-ratio related, not occupancy related.
- Value-add levers repeated across their portfolio: metering individual sites for electricity and passing utility costs through to tenants directly (rather than large blanket rent increases) -- accepted tradeoff: sometimes loses a few heavy-electricity-usage tenants who weren't very profitable anyway.
- Underwriting/due diligence process: request bank statements, tax returns, and P&Ls from sellers; cross-check reported revenue against bank deposits and physical occupancy counts (site-by-site headcount vs. claimed rent roll) since "mom and pop" park bookkeeping can be unreliable, especially with cash-heavy operations. Common surprises found during inspections: concealed/hidden electrical work and cracked pools that inspectors missed (occurred on at least two parks).
- Deal sourcing: largely manual/relationship-driven -- a retired police officer works full-time sourcing deals, cold-calling (~100 calls/week yielding maybe a few "maybes"), inbound deal flow from Koerner's audience, and occasional broker listings (often overpriced, but not always).
- Regulatory trend: zoning and development requirements for new RV parks are tightening, especially near growing metro areas (cited stricter septic/sanitary regulations passed in Eastern Tennessee); existing larger parks were grandfathered in, but new development is getting harder, which the presenters frame as a moat/opportunity for existing operators.
- Management structure: parks run by resident husband-wife on-site manager teams overseeing a small cluster of nearby parks each, reporting up to a "Director of Ops" who sets KPIs/bonuses; they emphasize efficiency gained through repetition (tiny home construction, sub-metering, consistent booking software) across their growing portfolio.
- Likely exit path once the portfolio scales (mentioned above $100M AUM): sale to a private equity buyer -- cited some inbound interest already from PE, a family office, and UBS's investment banking division, comparing the roll-up opportunity to Marina operator consolidation.
- Fund structure alternative offered to investors: a blind-pool fund vehicle (minimum $25,000, vs. $50,000 minimum per individual park deal) that invests across all current and future deals until it closes at $10M raised.

## Caveats explicitly raised
- Higher-interest-rate/harder-refinancing environment is flagged as a real risk factor across all real estate, with the fallback plan being to simply hold and continue distributing cash flow rather than force a refinance at unfavorable rates.
- Presenters explicitly avoided over-promising the refinance timeline or bonus depreciation eligibility, citing uncertainty in both interest rate trajectory and pending tax legislation.
- Not every market fits this thesis -- e.g., a good deal in a hard-to-manage remote location (e.g., a hypothetical Montana park) is deprioritized in favor of maintaining geographic clustering for management efficiency.
