# Deep Dive on the Sauna & Cold Plunge Business (+2 others)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFIzgoI5WLE | Published: 2024-10-29

Interview with Sam Unkart (Fort Worth, TX), covering three sequential businesses he built: fencing/pergolas, stock tank pools, and a sauna/cold-plunge contrast therapy studio inside a gym.

## 1. Fencing → Pergola/Deck business

- Quit a corporate job at Alcon to learn construction from the ground up; joined a fencing crew at **$15/hour** for ~6 months to learn the trade before starting his own business, being upfront with his employer about his intent (employer wasn't threatened — plenty of market demand, and the employer did higher-end custom fencing/gates).
- Framework offered for picking a home-service niche: look for **"low barrier to entry" + "genuinely unpleasant manual labor"** — this combination filters out casual competitors even though technically anyone could learn it (fencing can be learned well enough to build 3 fence types comfortably within about a week; a comparable example given: pond cleaning, where the entire "barrier to entry" is a $20 rake, but almost nobody wants to wade into a pond and haul out waterlogged weeds).
- Turned down subcontracted subdivision fencing work from a home-builder friend because it required scaling to 5 crews immediately with tight multi-day turnaround windows across 40-100 homes at a time, and builder rates were "nickel and dime" — concluded the tight time-and-crew-scaling demands of production-builder subdivision work outweighed the volume benefit; he preferred smaller private/custom residential jobs.
- Pivoted to pergolas/decks (~6 months): required more skill (basic carpentry, structural safety, sometimes wiring in ceiling fans) and city permitting, but appealed to his personal interest in woodworking.
- Marketing: ran Yelp Ads, Angie's List listing, Google Ads, and self-produced "TikTok-style" progress videos of builds, posted to personal and business social plus paid ad promotion — reused the same job's footage from multiple angles to build a portfolio while still early with few completed jobs.
- Numbers: pergola jobs cost **$3,000-$4,000 in materials** (mostly cedar lumber — a single 6x6 cedar post alone can run ~$400, plus ~$400-500 in hardware), charged **$8,000-$10,000 per job**, roughly 8-10 jobs completed over ~6 months. Initially self-built (with occasional help from an off-duty friend), later subcontracted labor to a crew he met at a Lowe's parking lot for about **$2,000 per job in labor** — described this handoff as transformative for actually running the business rather than just doing the labor himself in Texas heat.
- Tangential add-on: began installing above-ground pool kits (assembly-only, no special skills beyond being "handy," 2-3 person crew) for around **$500/job** after a customer asked; filmed and posted these too, which directly generated more pool-installation leads via Angie's List and social/paid ads.

## 2. Stock Tank Pool business (main deep dive)

- Origin: wanted a low-maintenance backyard pool for himself; discovered "stock tank pools" (galvanized steel cattle-trough tanks converted into small plunge/lounge pools), inspired partly by an existing Austin company, **Cowboy Pools**, known for strong marketing/higher-end setups. Posted a video of his own backyard install; it went viral (with plenty of skeptical/negative comments, which he says only helped ad engagement) and turned into a business.
- Target customer: framed as ideal for **Millennials without $100K for an inground pool** (Dallas–Fort Worth summers reach 105°F+ for ~5 months/year), but also drew older/retired couples wanting a low-maintenance cool-off spot, and families with young children.
- Sourcing: tanks bought from **Tractor Supply** and regional farm/feed stores (e.g., "Rustlers/Russell Feed & Supply" locally in Texas); pool equipment (pumps etc.) from Amazon.
- Unit economics (per pool, cost only, excluding labor):
  - 10-ft diameter tank: **$850-$900** from Tractor Supply (price varies by location).
  - 8-ft diameter tank: **$550-$600**.
  - Pool equipment/plumbing fittings/pump: **$300-$400** (rounded to "$350").
  - All-in cost for a fully equipped 10-ft pool: **~$1,200** (materials only).
  - Labor: about **1 hour or less** per install for a level site (drill holes, connect PVC, position, fill with hose, run pump, check for leaks).
  - Depth: 2 feet — explicitly a lounge/soak pool, not a swimming pool.
  - Charges **$2,200 for an 8-ft pool** and **$2,400-$2,600 for a 10-ft pool** (raised to $2,600 the prior season "and was fine").
  - Upcharges for ground leveling on sites with more than ~4 inches of incline: typically **+$300**, up to **+$500-$600** for extreme inclines; he proactively asks homeowners about their site grade/soil type beforehand (many competitors reportedly skip leveling service entirely).
  - Chemical maintenance is minimal — small chlorine tabs only; avoid harsh chemicals since the galvanized steel tank can rust.
  - Disposal is trivial vs. other pool types: drain it, flip it, list on Facebook Marketplace or haul to the dump.
- Customer acquisition: primarily **Facebook paid ads** — spent about **$3,500 over one summer**, which drove roughly **80% of jobs**; the remainder came from organic social sharing/referrals (customers tagging the business after installs). Confirmed the channel is scalable and he intended to increase spend, but was constrained by having only himself plus one helper — at points had an 8-9 day backlog and had to throttle ad spend to avoid overcommitting.
- Seasonal/product extension: floated adding heaters and better insulation (a poly liner inside the steel tank, optionally spray-foamed) to market it as a year-round "cool tub"/hot tub in mild-winter climates like DFW — viable there but explicitly not recommended for colder climates (insulation is too poor for a genuine winter hot tub; electricity/propane costs would be high).
- Scaling bottleneck identified: buying tanks reactively from local retailers (which stock only 5-6 units at a time) meant frequent cross-metro driving to source tanks, wrong sizes, or damaged units. Fix in progress: buying **direct from the manufacturer** — minimum order ~**$35,000** for roughly 70-80 tanks — plus a dedicated storage unit, projected to save **$300-$400 per job** versus retail sourcing and remove the sourcing bottleneck entirely.
- Comparable/competitor data point: Cowboy Pools (Austin) reportedly ships tanks nationwide via box truck and does experiential marketing at music festivals (e.g., near Austin City Limits) — cited as evidence of the category's scalability and marketing potential, though exact logistics/revenue are unknown to the guest.
- Stated growth target: 100+ pool installs in the next full season, projected at six figures in profit.

## 3. Sauna & Cold Plunge business ("Drip," inside a World Gym in Cleburne/"Curn," TX)

- Origin: 5 business partners on a bachelor trip tried a contrast-therapy (sauna + cold plunge) facility in Louisiana to cure hangovers, loved it, and spent ~2-3 years trying to find a commercial location before Sam (a member at World Gym) spotted an underused office space roughly "2x the size of a bedroom" inside his own gym and pitched using it — became a partner in exchange for being the on-site operator/manager, avoiding the need to hire out.
- Location strategy: piggybacking inside an existing gym gave built-in foot traffic (compared to Planet Fitness, this gym was positioned as nicer/cleaner/busier) without needing to run ads to reach a cold audience, and avoided competing with the gym's own offering (gym had no sauna/cold plunge, so no internal competition). Cross-promotion: gym provided its member email list and in-gym signage (including strategically near water coolers); the studio pays the gym **$2,000/month rent** with no other formal revenue-share — described as an informal "symbiotic," trust-based relationship rather than a commission split (they explored including it directly in gym membership but found that "gets hairy").
- Explicit advice on space-hunting: this kind of small-footprint concept is hard to place because most commercial retail buildouts start around 1,200+ sq ft, while the business only needs a few hundred; searching directly for gyms/other venues with small leftover office space (as opposed to standard retail listings) was key — he personally toured ~10 DFW gyms looking for similar unused space before finding this one. A packed independent barbershop next to the gym, also thriving via gym foot traffic, was cited as validating the "piggyback" location strategy (compared elsewhere in the episode to a GameStop opening next to a new Walmart, or a GNC/Jamba Juice opening next to a new LA Fitness).
- Equipment: 4 infrared saunas (kit-based, ~**$7,000-$8,000 each**, include red light thertherapy — described as more expensive than a basic traditional sauna, which can be DIY-built in a closet with cedar planks and a ~$500 electric heater) and 2 cold plunges (**~$10,000 each**, continuously circulating and chlorinated, chilled to ~35-36°F — colder and more physiologically demanding than a static ice bath because circulation prevents the body from forming a warming barrier layer). Total equipment investment **~$50,000** ($30K saunas + $20K plunges), financed via a one-year note that was fully paid off within the first year.
- Facility layout: one private cold plunge room (for privacy) plus one public/visible cold plunge near a lit sign for social visibility/shareability (deliberately designed for the "dare"/shock-value social media angle); saunas fit 3 people each (one private, three shared) for up to ~10 people in saunas plus 2 in plunges at a time (12-person practical capacity, though not "capacity" in a formal sense).
- Membership pricing:
  - Unlimited membership: **$75/month** for 45-minute sessions, includes booking-ahead access via a white-labeled booking app (licensed third-party software, gym-branded).
  - Lower/non-paying-ahead tier: walk-in access without ability to reserve time slots in advance.
  - Single walk-in visit: **$30**.
  - First visit free (used explicitly as a hook — "get them hooked," staff report high conversion from free trial to paid membership).
  - Competitive pricing context: comparable Dallas facility using identical sauna/cold-plunge equipment charges **$150-$175/month** — Drip prices at roughly half that.
- Traction (within first ~10-11 months, opened Dec 1 of the prior year): ~**130 monthly recurring members**; described as "close to break-even" once equipment financing, insurance, staff pay, rent, and Facebook ad spend are accounted for. High insurance cost flagged as a real cost driver (liability risk from wet floors/slip hazards near the plunge). Stated capacity/growth goal: **200 members** (not true physical capacity, just their target).
- Operational note/regret: they didn't install a floor drain for the cold plunge, so it must be manually pumped out through a hose every 2 days (called his least favorite recurring task); planned first equipment upgrade once profitable. Saunas need only occasional resurfacing/sealant maintenance (sanded and clear-coated around the 6-month mark).
- Advice given: reframe front-desk staff explicitly as "membership sales associates," not just facility attendants — their real job is selling memberships, not merely keeping the space clean/presentable.
- Growth plan: prove profitability at this single location, then open 1-2 more company-owned locations, then franchise; one partner has finance/fundraising background specifically to support that scaling path. Business model explicitly compared to a Float Spa concept — high one-time upfront equipment cost, low ongoing fixed costs (mainly one front-desk staffer), and once fixed costs are covered by MRR, incremental members are high-margin.
- Additional customer acquisition idea discussed (not yet implemented by Sam, mentioned as working well in an unnamed "different industry" with similar demographics/price point): **directly DMing local Instagram users** who check in at nearby locations (indicating they're local Millennials) with a discount offer to join — described as looking unscalable but actually working well at scale in that other business.
- Marketing channel: leveraging a gym-based fitness influencer with ~200,000 followers for video content, generating up to ~100,000 views per video (though most of his followers are non-local, so this is more useful for eventual multi-location expansion than immediate local acquisition).

**Closing meta-point (Chris Koerner's framing, not Sam's business per se):** contrasts a hyper-focused single-niche operator (a trash-can-cleaning business owner mentioned as a contrasting example) against Sam's "chase rabbits"/pivot-often style (fencing → pergolas → pools → sauna business) as two equally valid paths, arguing that switching ideas doesn't "reset" compounding as long as you stay in business generally — experience and momentum carry over between ventures.
