# How a Mom of 3 Makes $50K a Month With AI (No Experience)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lG5U30gKCaY | Published: 2026-08-02

Interview with Sandy Lee (YouTube: @sandyleeai, sleeautomation.com/resources), who built multiple income streams to $50K+/month within 9 months using ChatGPT/Claude Code, starting with zero AI or SEO experience while working a full-time job and raising three kids under 8.

## The business idea(s)
- **Freelance AI/automation consulting via Upwork**: pitched and sold AI-automation/SEO services she had zero prior experience in, using ChatGPT to both build the actual deliverable (an automation/workflow system) and to self-educate enough to speak credibly to clients.
- **Content creation / personal brand (YouTube: Sandy Lee AI)**: teaching others how to use AI tools, which became her largest inbound lead-generation channel.
- **Consulting/fractional roles from inbound leads**: after building an audience, received unsolicited offers to be a "senior marketing officer," AI strategist, and consultant for various companies/groups.
- **A SaaS app for content creators**: an all-in-one tool that generates video scripts, thumbnails, and teleprompter text, modeled on studying what already works (an "outlier video" analysis approach, compared explicitly to how MrBeast studies outlier videos) — built by observing her own video ideas going viral organically and productizing the pattern.
- **Paid community ("school")**: a membership community tied to her content, using the app above as a lead magnet.
- Additional income streams stacked together: client retainer work, her day job (funeral-home marketing/sales, regional manager), brand sponsorships in her videos, the community, the app, and YouTube ad revenue.

## Numbers
- First paying client: $3,000/month retainer for SEO automation work, landed via Upwork after only 7 applications (in November of the prior year), despite having zero SEO experience — done using ChatGPT to build the system and inform herself.
- Later increased that same client's retainer to $5,500/month after proving results, by expanding the automation from English/Japanese blog content to also cover Spanish and Korean (her own languages) — an internal cost of ~$3,000 to duplicate the work herself, framed to the client as a "discount" versus a theoretical $6,000 full price.
- That client relationship ended after about a year (last month at time of recording).
- Total current revenue run rate: $50,000+/month across combined streams (client retainer income, day job salary, sponsorships, community, app, YouTube AdSense) — exact per-stream breakdown discussed on-screen via Stripe dashboards but not fully read out in the transcript.
- Time investment: 1-2 hours/day maximum on business work total, including client work (~1 hour/day max); YouTube is the most time-consuming stream at 3-4 hours/week; the app requires ~zero personal time now after hiring an engineer 2 weeks prior; the paid community takes ~10 minutes/day of comment engagement plus one weekly group call (~3 hours/week total); sponsorships take ~2 hours per sponsored video, all inbound (no outbound sales effort for any of these).
- Backstory numbers for context: grew up across 7 countries without her parents from age 11; in college was down to $2/day (living on discount vending-machine sandwiches) and was evicted once, sleeping on a beach until police moved her along.
- A prior, unrelated content channel (teaching Korean to Spanish speakers) grew to 550,000 followers across three platforms but generated no meaningful income because the audience (mostly teenagers) had no ability/willingness to pay — cited as a cautionary tale about optimizing content only around passion/skill and ignoring monetizability and market need.

## Process / steps
- Applied to Upwork jobs slightly outside the most saturated categories: instead of tagging herself under the highly competitive "AI" or "AI automation" category, she applied under the less crowded "SEO" tag for a job that happened to want AI-driven automation — a deliberate "tilt the niche slightly" tactic to reduce competition.
- Built credibility with zero testimonials/portfolio by: (1) recording a short (2-3 minute), unedited Loom video explaining exactly how she'd solve the client's problem, practiced/re-recorded until it sounded good, and (2) having ChatGPT translate her unrelated past work history (even entry-level service jobs) into automation-relevant "portfolio" framing.
- Explicit sequencing rule: "sell the idea before you build the product" — pitch the specific solution/workflow to the prospective client first, and only build it after securing the deal, rather than building speculatively and then trying to sell it.
- Learned any needed subject matter (e.g., SEO fundamentals, backlinks, internal links) immediately before client calls, treating time pressure as functional motivation ("learn the thing right before you have to do the thing").
- For content strategy, deliberately built her current channel around all four quadrants of the "Ikigai" framework — what you love, what you're good at, what you can get paid for, and what the world needs — contrasted against her earlier, unprofitable channel that only optimized for passion/skill. Host added a refinement: aim for what you can *most easily* get paid for and what *your specific corner* of the market needs, since not all monetization paths are equally accessible.
- Product ideas for her app were validated by publishing content first (e.g., a video about building an AI website using Claude Code that went viral, prompting her to actually build a companion app afterward) — same "sell/validate before you build" pattern applied to product development, not just services.
- Uses Claude Code as her primary AI development tool of choice (has also tried ChatGPT, Manus, and Codex) despite having no engineering background — stated explicitly "you don't have to know anything technical because AI is doing it for you."
- Recently hired an engineer to take over ongoing app development/maintenance so she could redirect her own time toward YouTube content creation.
- Referenced her authority-building tactic even during outbound (pre-inbound) outreach: linking to her YouTube channel in cold pitches even when it had only ~200 subscribers, since a channel with any content at all gives prospects a way to vet her credibility/fit before hiring.

## Tools / platforms named
- ChatGPT (paid, $20/month) — used for building automation/AI systems, learning subject matter on demand, and drafting outreach materials.
- Claude Code — her stated preferred coding/build tool, used to build her app and website with no engineering background.
- Upwork — freelance marketplace where she landed her first (and highest-value) client relationships.
- Loom — used to record short pitch videos for prospective clients.
- Her own SaaS app (unnamed publicly beyond "GEO audit app" / content-creation all-in-one tool) — offers script generation, thumbnails, and teleprompter text; also used as a lead magnet for her paid community.
- YouTube (channel: Sandy Lee AI) — primary content/personal-brand and inbound lead channel; monetized via AdSense, sponsorships, and a paid community tier.

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly frames the "fake it till you make it" approach as viable specifically because AI tools now let a non-expert credibly deliver expert-level output — this is presented as a real strategy, not merely confidence-building rhetoric, but it does carry real risk of underdelivering to clients who are paying for genuine expertise.
- Both guest and host acknowledge the risk head-on: worst case, a client asks for a refund after a month if the work doesn't hold up — treated as an acceptable, low-stakes downside worth risking.
- Success depended on stacking several distinct income streams (not one silver bullet) built up gradually over 9 months, not an overnight result.
- Guest notes Upwork/freelance AI-services competition has increased since she started (Nov of the prior year) — her specific niche-tilting tactic (applying under adjacent, less crowded categories) may need to be re-applied to whatever is currently under-competed, since competitive dynamics shift over time.
- Time-efficiency numbers (1-2 hrs/day) depend on her already having each pipeline/workflow built (e.g., the app requiring near-zero time only after hiring an engineer) — early build-out of each stream likely required more upfront time investment than the steady-state numbers suggest.
