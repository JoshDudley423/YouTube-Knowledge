# Brilliant Idea: The Liquid Death of Candles
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mb1DTzNZUHs | Published: 2024-11-13

Conversation with returning guest Connor Gross (whose father owns a candle manufacturing business in New Jersey). Two main topics: (1) detecting/managing "over-employed" workers with secret second jobs, and (2) brainstorming a disruptive candle brand ("the Liquid Death of candles"). Part 1 of a two-part episode.

**Topic 1 — employee productivity / detecting a second job (not really monetizable as a standalone side hustle, but includes one concrete product idea):**
- Trigger: multiple entrepreneur friends suspected employees were secretly working a second job (slow to respond, deleted LinkedIn, unusually large house relative to salary).
- On Upwork specifically, you can see an employee's total open jobs and cumulative hours logged per client — a real (if imperfect, since old unclosed jobs can inflate the count) way to estimate if a contractor is spreading hours across multiple clients.
- A vetted anonymized "Glassdoor for employees" (letting employers rate/flag problematic former employees) was floated but dismissed as suffering fatal selection bias (bad actors simply wouldn't opt in, so ratings would trend artificially positive) — not a viable business as discussed.
- **Idea with legs (framed as "one of the best ideas" in the episode): a standardized pre-hire "work speed" test** — a personality-test-style product (like Myers-Briggs/DISC) but for measuring how fast a candidate actually works, not just whether they're "productive." Concept: give applicants a simulated task environment (fake inbox to clear, a spreadsheet with basic formulas to solve) during the interview process and score their completion speed.
  - Rationale: Parkinson's Law (work expands to fill the time given) means slow workers often look fine on paper; being able to identify genuinely fast workers lets an employer hire fewer people for more roles.
  - Real-world data point cited: in hiring for a fast-growing e-commerce company's US-based customer support roles, one guest's own recruiting business required a minimum 60 words-per-minute typing test; among 5 finalist applicants, 3-4 scored around only 35 wpm — showing real, meaningful variance most employers don't screen for.
  - Suggested go-to-market: make the basic test free/widely distributed to make the "fast worker" concept as culturally standard as "introvert/extrovert," then monetize via upsells (paid/deeper versions), similar to how paid personality-test products like Predictive Index already monetize.
  - Suggested employer-side framing trick: administer it disguised as a "paid trial task for feedback" rather than an explicit speed test, since employees might resist being told they're being timed.
  - Mentioned an existing standalone tool: a Mac app called **Word Counter** (a solo-founder product, ~$20 lifetime license) that tracks how many words a user types per day across Mail, Excel, Slack, Chrome, etc. — floated as a lower-friction way to approximate productivity/output without needing a formal test.

**Topic 2 — reinventing the candle industry ("Liquid Death of candles"):**
- Premise: candle brands (including the guest's father's business, ~30 years combined industry experience under current + previous ownership) are largely undifferentiated commodity products; opportunity to build a distinctive, headline-grabbing candle brand the way Liquid Death disrupted bottled water packaging/branding.
- Industry insider detail: major manufacturers (e.g., Yankee Candle) pour candles in two layers — a base layer, then let it set/straighten the wick, then a second top layer where most of the fragrance oil is concentrated. This is why store-tested candles smell strong (you're smelling the fragrance-heavy top layer) but may smell weaker once burned down — framed as a potential consumer-education/marketing angle ("educate customers on this difference, create an enemy/villain out of the practice").
- Guest's father's business revenue mix: roughly ~30% Amazon, ~15% direct-to-consumer, ~55% wholesale; originally had a Christian/Catholic brand angle when purchased but has drifted from it over time; considered doubling back into that niche (e.g., a scripture verse revealed at the bottom of the candle after burning) as one differentiation option.
- Brainstormed product/branding concepts (unvalidated ideas, not existing businesses):
  - "Mystery" themed candles with a surprise scent tied to a theme (e.g., a "woodshop" mystery candle smelling like cedar/pine/sawdust, or a "man cave" themed candle smelling like motor oil) — riffing on niche scent preferences (e.g., people who like the smell of skunk).
  - Redesigning the physical vessel/packaging as real home decor (e.g., bathroom-specific white porcelain candle vessels, fireplace-specific vessels) instead of standard glass jars — comparing this to Liquid Death's shift from plastic bottles to aluminum cans as its core packaging disruption.
  - A **refillable candle** concept: sell a permanent decorative vessel (potentially reverse-engineered from an already-bestselling HomeGoods/Hobby Lobby item — using the "add a URL suffix to sort any Shopify store by best-sellers" trick mentioned elsewhere in the channel) with a wax subscription for melting/refilling over time, possibly using a wax-melter format instead of a wick.
  - Story/character-driven candle branding, comparable to wine brand "19 Crimes" (labels depict a convicted criminal's story, with an AR app animating the label) — proposed crossing candle buyers with fiction/romance readers via narrative branding.
  - Pet-themed candles: e.g., a "fresh groomed dog" scent candle personalized with a customer's uploaded pet photo printed on the label.
  - Hyper-local/programmatic SEO angle: create a distinct candle "for every niche town in America," paired with geo-targeted Google ads to current or former residents of that town.
  - Scent-sampling via direct mail: literal scratch-and-sniff postcards with up to a dozen scent samples and a QR code for frictionless mobile ordering (Apple Pay checkout) — most viable as a subscription model to offset the higher cost of direct mail plus scent application, and especially suited to Q4/holiday gifting mailers when purchase intent is highest.
  - **Co-marketing/cost-sharing direct mail idea**: partner with a company that already sends huge volumes of direct mail (e.g., a national real estate brand like Century 21) and pay them a few cents to use excess space/weight allowance on their existing mailers (e.g., paying the marginal cost difference between a cheaper postage tier and the next one up) to include scent samples — cuts direct mail costs for the candle brand while giving the mail-sender extra margin.
- Referenced existing "irreverent branding" comps that validate the strategy in unlikely commodity categories:
  - **Titan Caskets** — called "the Liquid Death of caskets": aggressive/funny marketing (e.g., a "buy now, die later" Valentine's Day promo) in a traditionally somber industry, high AOV (~$8,000-$10,000 caskets), including a novelty Yeti/Liquid Death branded casket that was raffled for $70,000-$80,000.
  - **Homesick** (homesick.com) and a competitor "hometown candles" concept — candles marketed around nostalgia/place, with strong pop-culture licensing partnerships (e.g., a "Jerry's apartment"/Seinfeld-themed fragrance, a Barbie Dreamhouse fragrance).
  - **Birth date candles** — a candle brand targeting horoscope/astrology audiences, personalizing candles by birth date/zodiac sign with matching scent and printed astrological description; also sells a "birthday pendant" product (~$85, according to guests browsing the live site).
  - **Drive Coffee** — cross-branding coffee with classic-car/adventure aesthetic packaging (selling beans in motor-oil-can-style packaging) as an example of intersectional audience branding.
- Caveats/risks discussed: candles are genuinely hard to sell online because the core value proposition (scent) can't be conveyed digitally — sampling (direct mail scratch-and-sniff, subscription sample packs) is offered as a partial workaround, not a solved problem. Both guests explicitly frame most of this segment as unvalidated brainstorming, not a proven business.
