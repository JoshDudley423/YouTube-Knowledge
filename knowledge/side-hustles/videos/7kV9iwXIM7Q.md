# Greg Isenberg on AI Tools That Will Replace You (or Make You Rich)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7kV9iwXIM7Q | Published: 2025-04-02

Interview with Greg Isenberg (host of the Startup Ideas podcast) covering current AI tool stacks and several concrete AI-era business/side-hustle ideas: programmatic SEO lead-gen sites, an "accreditation/plaque" racket business, and an AI agent-driven reverse-engineering newsletter.

## AI tools mentioned (workflow / productivity, not businesses themselves)
- **Deep research tools** (Grok 3 Deep Research, ChatGPT Pro Deep Research, Perplexity): Isenberg uses Grok 3 deep research for ~80% of research queries (fast, free at the time); ChatGPT Pro ($200/month) for deeper 5-10 minute research; Perplexity for quick/simple queries with a cleaner UI. Argues "deep research" branding undersold the product relative to OpenAI's "Operator," even though deep research is functionally a more useful agent.
- **Grok 3**: viewed as a surprisingly strong LLM despite Twitter/X having a smaller but highly relevant, timestamped real-time dataset (~20 years of tweets).
- **Whisper Flow**: voice-to-text app that transcribes speech into clean, properly punctuated text (removes filler words like "like"/"literally") for texts, Slack messages, etc. Both hosts describe it as a major personal productivity unlock, replacing native tools like Siri dictation.
- **Limitless pendant**: always-on wearable audio recorder/transcriber Isenberg wears daily (2-day battery, must stay within ~2 feet of mouth); auto-summarizes conversations per-day, identifies speaker voices, and lets you query it (e.g., "highlights of my conversation with X yesterday"). Framed as a personal-life-logging tool, not a business, but relevant as an example of consumer AI hardware adoption curve.
- **Note-taking AI (Granola, Fathom)**: both hosts admit they rarely re-read AI meeting notes — treated as "insurance"/psychological comfort rather than a genuinely used tool.
- **Gumloop** (gumloop.com): a no-code automation/scraping tool compared to Zapier, Make, and Lindy AI, praised for a nicer UI that increases the odds a non-technical builder actually finishes an automation instead of abandoning it half-built. Isenberg's team uses it for scraping data into Google Sheets/Docs, then applying an LLM to filter/rank/edit that data (e.g., cross-checking SEO-ranked "best X" lists against actual quality), then routing results to Slack bots/team notifications. Framed insight: when businesspeople say they want "AI," they usually mean automation — AI is just the sexier word.
- **No-code / prompt-to-app builders**: Bolt and Lovable recommended for non-technical builders; Cursor preferred by more technical users. Both agree the "last mile" (turning a working prototype into a fully functional, polished product) still typically requires hiring human developers — a prototype from Replit/Bolt is good enough to validate demand, then hire 1-3 developers to finish it properly.

## Business idea 1: Programmatic SEO lead-gen sites (case study: pet cremation)
- Isenberg's team built a programmatic SEO site ("Animal Aftercare," animal-aftercare.com) a year prior, purely as a test: thesis was that pet cremation demand is rising (more pets than kids in many households) but supply (actual cremation facilities) is sparse in most cities, so search demand exists with little content/competition.
- Built 5,000 landing pages: 5 keywords (pet cremation, dog cremation, pet euthanasia, dog euthanasia, horse cremation) × top 10,000 US cities by population, plus a parallel set for the top 100 Canadian cities.
- Result: ranks organically for many long-tail queries and receives inbound phone calls "every hour" from people wanting cremation services, entirely passively — they never touch the site, and they don't even monetize the leads (don't sell them or route them to providers) despite the flowing demand, illustrating pure "low risk, high probability of success" arbitrage.
- Tooling suggested for scaling this to other niches: Ahrefs (or similar) to find high-search-volume/low-competition keyword clusters; a tool like Repl(it) to build and template the same design once, then copy/paste across many programmatic-SEO sites at scale (thousands of landing pages per site) for other high-ticket niches (example given: orthopedic surgery, or generically "braces near me"). End monetization options: sell leads directly, or sell the entire site/business to the largest player in the market (roll-up buyer) or a private equity firm.
- Historical parallel cited: Demand Media (founded 2006), a "content farm" pioneer (e.g., eHow.com) that used search/ad-auction data to identify high-value topics and had ~$500M/year revenue at its peak — the argument is that current AI + programmatic tooling makes this strategy far easier to execute than it was in the mid-2000s.
- Domain strategy note: believes short, dictionary-word two-word domains will become more valuable in an AI-search world — an arbitrage opportunity in domain acquisition. Isenberg's team has also invested in a dedicated programmatic SEO agency, Boring Marketing (boringmarketing.com).
- Debate/caveat between host and guest on timing: both agree the space will get more crowded (a "first mover gets ~80% share" dynamic, similar to Uber), but Isenberg argues the pie of possible niches/keyword combinations is so large that meaningful opportunity persists for 5-10+ years, and that "we are always early" relative to a technology's full lifespan — the real risk isn't being late, it's not acting at all (his stated bias: "the longer you wait before acting, the more likely you are to never act").

## Business idea 2: Paid "accreditation" / awards plaque business
- Origin insight: Isenberg walked into a dental office in Miami after seeing a "Best Dentist in America 2020" plaque in the window — became a loyal, high-spend customer and referred ~25 people, despite the accreditation being from an obscure, likely-paid award source (a confirmation-bias-driven trust signal).
- Proposed business: a directory site (like a "Product Hunt" for a chosen niche, e.g., AI startups) that cold-emails new product/company owners saying they've been "nominated" for an award (e.g., "Product of the Year"), then runs a **silent auction** among nominees in a category — whoever bids the most is ranked #1, next-highest is #2, etc. Winners get a physical plaque mailed to them and a digital badge to embed on their website (cited example price point: ~$200) that plausibly increases their own conversion rate by signaling third-party validation.
- Framed as "a total racket" but legally/ethically framed as legitimate (the fine print discloses it's just one obscure website's opinion) — Isenberg says this is a very achievable path to a $2-3M/year business: high margin, doesn't require a large team, and can expand into many verticals/categories.
- Cited proof-of-concept: YouTube's own "100,000 subscribers" physical plaque and Shopify's own milestone plaques as evidence people value and display these accreditation objects.
- Framed within a broader "barbell strategy" for idea generation: alongside AI-forward ideas, deliberately also list "anti-AI" ideas (a human stamp-of-approval product is the anti-AI complement to an AI-saturated market).

## Business idea 3: AI agent-driven "reverse engineer this business" newsletter
- Extension of idea 2: build an agent that monitors Twitter/X for people posting about their new accreditation plaque/milestone (e.g., a Shopify or YouTube plaque), then automatically does a deep-dive investigation of that business or channel (web archive history, when the store/channel launched, growth trajectory) using existing scraping/research tools (e.g., Gumloop), and outputs a clean "how to copy this business" report.
- Monetization: package these auto-generated deep-dives as a paid newsletter — subscribers get an email breakdown within minutes of someone posting about their milestone, showing exactly how that business/channel grew.

## Caveats / cautionary framing
- Coined term "Vibe Revenue" (parallel to "vibe coding"): warns that many AI startups reporting $1-10M ARR are actually riding "curiosity revenue" — free-trial signups and short-term experimentation spikes from people trying novel AI products, not real durable product-market fit. "That which is easily gained is easily lost."
- General caution against chasing AI hype/FOMO into building unsustainable businesses funded by other people's money; argues there's no shortage of durable, non-hype business ideas available (e.g., via ChatGPT brainstorming) for anyone motivated enough to act.
- On competitive timing for programmatic SEO/AI-tooling businesses: acknowledges the field will get progressively more saturated as more people copy the playbook (needing no funding or team = fast follower dynamics), but argues AI is simultaneously expanding the total opportunity space (many more addressable niches/business types), roughly offsetting the increased competition.
