# The Simplest $10K+/M Business You've Never Heard Of
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=McQucrnkSt0 | Published: 2026-08-22

Interview with Rachel Smith (Community Cash Playbook / Big Sky Automation Services), whose business gives restaurants a free specials sandwich board, sells ad space on it to other local businesses, and uses it as a foot-in-the-door to upsell higher-ticket digital/AI marketing services.

## Business #1 — Community specials board advertising

**The idea:** noticed restaurants already have specials boards near the host stand that customers naturally look at while deciding what to order. Gave restaurants/coffee shops/gyms a **free** dry-erase (not chalkboard) sandwich board with the venue's own specials in the main area, plus **6 small paid ad slots at the bottom** for other local businesses.

**Product details:**
- Aluminum sandwich board, dry-erase coating, indoor/outdoor rated (used on brewery patios too). Cost to have made: **~$350 each** (a local Missoula printer/fabricator, no minimum order — will make just one at a time), no shipping needed since sourced locally.
- Ads are printed/pressed directly onto the board (permanent for the ad portion), the specials area stays dry-erase for the host venue to update themselves.
- Standard layout: 6 ad slots, though flexible — a business could buy multiple slots, and they cap around ~10 slots max before ads get too small.
- Startup capital required: **effectively $0** — board, stand, and markers aren't purchased until all 6 ad slots are pre-sold; ad designs made free in Canva or ChatGPT image generation (no paid subscription needed until slots sell).

**Two-sided sales process:**
1. Find a **host location** first (restaurant/coffee shop/gym — anywhere with high foot traffic and where people linger; explicitly avoid low-traffic spots like dentist/physical therapy waiting rooms).
2. Cold-DM the host business on Instagram (their first success came on their **10th-15th DM**), offering the board completely free with a photo attached, then get an in-person meeting to show the physical board — closing rate in person with a set appointment is close to 100%; unscheduled cold walk-ins are much less effective.
3. Host signs a simple agreement to keep the board up and visible for one year, transferring to a new owner if the business is sold (no pushback experienced).
4. Once the host is secured, sell the 6 ad slots to other local businesses — pitch them specifically on "this board is going up inside of [Host Restaurant], which gets ~7,000-10,000 customers/month."
5. **Key growth unlock — the referral chain:** ask the host business owner for referrals of other local businesses (their insurance agent, attorney, HVAC company, etc.) rather than asking an open-ended "who do you know" question — direct, specific prompts ("who does your insurance?") work far better than vague ones. This dramatically sped up filling each board (first board took ~2 weeks without asking for referrals; later boards filled in a couple of days once they started asking).
6. From ~12-20 referral names collected per host, they typically close about **6 advertisers**.

**Pricing & unit economics:**
- Single-location board: **$600/ad slot/year** ($3,600/board/year total for 6 slots), paid entirely upfront (framed to advertisers as "~$1.50/day").
- Two-board package (same advertiser on boards in 2 different locations): **$900/slot** (effective ~50% volume discount vs. buying two boards separately).
- Cost per board: **~$400** (board + stand + markers) — profit per board is **~$3,200** (one board) or **~$4,600** (two boards), roughly **85-90% margin**.
- Advertiser retention/reuse is high: many advertisers from earlier boards buy slots on each subsequent new board launched.
- No CPM-style guarantee, but for sales purposes they translate exposure into rough terms: a 7,000-10,000 customer/month restaurant works out to roughly **$30 CPM equivalent** — competitive vs. billboards/Facebook ads, though harder to directly attribute sales to it (advertisers are told to think of it as brand-awareness/ubiquity, not trackable ROI — "the only issue is you don't know which 50% of your marketing is working").
- Anecdotal proof of ROI: one painter got a referral job directly from being seen on a board at his favorite brunch spot.

**Traction (6 months in, on top of an 18-month-old separate digital agency):**
- **~$50,000 in board-only revenue** in 6 months, **~$45,000 of that profit** (~90% margin) — on pace for $100K+ in year one.
- **63 unique host/advertiser relationships** across boards to date, run in both Missoula, Montana and Knoxville, Tennessee (proving it's not a one-market idea).
- Most common/easiest-to-close advertiser categories: **tax preparers, insurance agents, and realtors** (already used to buying local ad placement like yard signs/bus ads) — easier to close than home services (HVAC, painting) despite realtors having a much higher deal value ($15K commission on a $500K home vs. a painter's ~$3K job), partly because home-service trades are already busy/understaffed (cited: a 3-million-worker blue-collar labor shortage) and don't feel pressure to advertise, while there are ~2 million realtors nationally, making that market far more competitive and eager for exposure.
- Downside acknowledged: **the board revenue itself isn't truly recurring** — each board's ad sales are a one-time push (though ads run a full year); to keep growing you must continually place new boards in new host locations, and geography is a real constraint (a market like Missoula has a limited number of restaurants). Framed as "you're only as good as your last deal" until you build up enough boards.
- Idea floated (not yet implemented) by the interviewer: give hosts a board with unsold generic QR-code ad slots ("your ad here") as a way to test/pre-place boards before fully pre-selling all 6 spots, since ~10% of adult customers are themselves business owners.
- Vertical-specific niche variant in progress: a Knoxville rollout placing boards inside a **humane society** location, targeting vets/pet groomers/pet supply stores as advertisers, with proceeds partly donated back to the humane society — illustrates adapting the host-venue/advertiser match to any foot-traffic location + relevant local business category (not limited to restaurants).

## Business #2 — Upsell into a digital marketing/AI agency (Big Sky Automation Services)

- The board business predates but was intentionally built as a **lead-gen/trust-building front door** into their pre-existing marketing agency (websites, reputation management, AI receptionist/chat widget, automation).
- Sequencing: get paid for the board first, deliver it, THEN pitch digital services — deliberately delaying the AI/automation pitch so as not to overwhelm or trigger skepticism ("micro-commitments" — free board, then $50 service, then $500, then $5,000, rather than pitching high-ticket immediately).
- Uses a **Go High Level (GHL) automated prospecting/audit report** — enter a business's info, get an automated scorecard of gaps (citations, review response rate, etc.) plus an estimated likelihood-to-close score; used as a conversation tool once trust is established, not as the first pitch.
- Conversion from board customers to digital customers: **about half of their 63 board customers (~30) have bought a digital service.**
- Revenue mix within the digital side: **~60% brand/reputation management**, **~30% websites/hosting**, **~10% higher-ticket AI services** (AI receptionist, automations, appointment booking).
- Pricing: websites **$1,000-$6,000** setup (custom-coded, not built in GHL — hosting from **$50/month** basic up to **$1,000+/month** with automation/AI); reputation management around **$300-$500/month** flat regardless of the client's own customer volume (GHL "snapshot" cloning makes onboarding a new client take only ~10 minutes plus a 20-30 minute onboarding call).
- Reputation management mechanics: automated review-request system triggered off the client's invoicing/payment system, sent 3x via email + 3x via text (text gets better response), plus automated reply-to-reviews (improves Google ranking) and auto-posting 5-star reviews to social media.
- Which client types upsell best into digital services: **home services (HVAC, painting, contractors)** — opposite of who's easiest to close on the board itself — because they understand the value of Google reviews but lack any system to generate them.
- Estimated average annual revenue per upsold digital client: **~$7,500/year** (mix of one-time setup fees and recurring), projecting the agency side toward **~$200,000-$225,000/year** from ~30 active/past digital clients.
- Additional inbound channel: the husband (who builds the sites) posts educational content (Google Business Profile tips, etc.) on Facebook/YouTube (800 YouTube subs, 1,300 Facebook followers at time of filming) — low view counts but highly targeted, generating inbound DMs (6 new customers in the 3 weeks before filming) purely from consistent content, no outbound chasing.
- Combined business numbers at time of filming: **$10,000-$15,000/month total revenue across both business lines, ~85% profit margin.**

**DM/outreach mechanics and caveats:**
- Cold Instagram DM response rate to local businesses: roughly **10-20%**; of every ~10 DMs sent, expect 1-3 responses, and (since they only need 1-2 host slots per push) that's usually enough.
- Local framing matters a lot: a DM referencing the same city/area gets weighted far more trust than a generic pitch — explicitly compared to how the podcast host himself is more likely to respond to a DM naming his own city than a generic sales pitch.
- Platform caveat: **Facebook aggressively rate-limits/blocks cold outbound DMs** (can get blocked after just 5-10 messages sent close together); Instagram enforces no such limit ("the wild west").
- Tools used: Stripe (payments), Go High Level (CRM/automation/hosting), Canva and ChatGPT/Claude (ad and content creation — noted Claude was considered weak for image generation specifically at time of filming, but strong for other tasks), Instagram/Facebook/YouTube for outreach and content.

**Explicit advice given:**
- The board business alone is pitched as a viable **low-capital side hustle**: "one board placed and you can make $3,000/month," requiring effectively $0 to start and minimal ongoing time once running.
- Encourages treating any such simple front-door offer as a stepping stone — have a clear answer ready for "what else do you do?" since satisfied clients will ask, and that upsell path is what turns a small side hustle into a much larger business.
- General market-saturation pushback: argues most "saturated" markets aren't actually saturated among people who show up consistently — using an anecdote about 8 ice cream shops within 3 miles in a small Utah town still all doing well, arguing more supply can grow (not just split) total demand, and that consistency of outreach/marketing effort is the real differentiator, not the number of competitors.
