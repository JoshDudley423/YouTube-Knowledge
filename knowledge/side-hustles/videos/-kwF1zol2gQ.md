# The 11 Best Side Hustles for 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-kwF1zol2gQ | Published: 2026-02-06

Countdown of 11 beginner-friendly, low-startup-cost business ideas (whittled down from ~150), counted from #11 (least favorite) to #1 (most favorite).

## 11. Wall printing (mural printing service)
- Uses a relatively new commercial wall-printer machine (3-5 years old tech); machines can be financed and come with a warranty.
- Charge by the square foot (or square inch); costs are predictable (paint + machine financing), so margin = markup on paint + time.
- Market: residential + commercial (restaurants, offices, warehouses/break rooms). ~1.8 million retail businesses in the US alone as a rough addressable market signal.
- Presenter says he's had 6-10 people DM him after starting wall-printing businesses successfully.
- Good fit for short-form video marketing because the printing process (time-lapse) is visually compelling and "sells itself."

## 10. Riding lawn mower flipping (two variants)
- **Export arbitrage**: buy used John Deere riding mowers on Facebook Marketplace (~$1,000-2,200 each), crate them, ship via Lubbock, TX through Mexico/Central America to Bolivia, where John Deere is seen as a premium/gold-standard brand (unlike in the US). Shipping cost ~$1-2/lb (~$500). Example: bought for $2,200, ~$3,000 all-in, resold for $6,000-7,000 in Bolivia.
- **Domestic fix-and-flip**: buy broken/cheap riding mowers on Facebook Marketplace, repair (parts can be learned via ChatGPT even with no mechanical background), resell. Cited examples: a 16-17-year-old YouTuber (channel "Noah") made ~$1,000 on one mower flip; another operator had 30-40 active mower listings in Dallas-Fort Worth; a YouTuber "Matt Haul" claims $51,000 in one month flipping mowers on Facebook Marketplace.
- Costs: buy for under $1,000; repair parts $50-500; time investment high initially but drops with experience.
- Best suited to a metro area with 500,000+ people to have enough deal flow; need a vehicle hitch + trailer (can rent from U-Haul to start).

## 9. Driftwood/branch LED wall art
- German maker takes driftwood/branches, routs notches, inlays $15 Amazon LED light strips, sands/stains/mounts as backlit wall art; sells for $300-1,200.
- Started on Etsy, now has own website, doing six figures/year.
- Idea: replicate the exact product/process in a market he's not serving (i.e., outside Germany).
- Customer acquisition: organic vertical short-form video (showing the build process).

## 8. AI avatar video service for local professionals (using HeyGen)
- Example: a mortgage broker (Christie Olson) uploaded a single headshot to HeyGen (~$50/month tool) and generated 60-second "instructional" videos (e.g., about HELOCs) that look like she's talking in an office.
- Result: only 480 Instagram followers, posted ~10 videos getting 200-500 views each (3,000-5,000 total views), but generated 10 new mortgage loan originations — each worth "thousands of dollars, or at least $10,000" net to her.
- Business-ize it: offer this as a done-for-you service to local professionals/small business owners — collect a headshot, upload to the tool, generate videos for them. Suggested pricing: ~$1,500/month for ~10 videos.
- Caveat acknowledged: the AI video quality is imperfect (mouth movement looks "weird" to anyone paying attention) but it still converts/works regardless.

## 7. Car seat cleaning service
- Example operator in Derby, KS (population 26,000), found customers purely via Instagram (2,600 followers) posting videos of herself working.
- Process: picks up car seats in bulk, cleans per manufacturer instructions, drops off in bulk, gets paid in advance, leaves on doorstep to minimize interaction time and maximize throughput.
- Equipment: just a vacuum + cleaning supplies (e.g., from Dollar General) — no specialized gear.
- Pricing: she charges $50/seat; commenters say they'd pay up to $200; competitors charge $75-125 — presenter argues she's underpricing and should charge $150.
- Economics: ~1 hour per seat; at $80-120/hour profit, that's roughly a $200,000/year equivalent full-time wage.
- Framed as a low-competition niche because most people won't do this kind of unglamorous work.

## 6. Trash/garbage bin cleaning service
- Guerrilla marketing tactic: find neighborhood trash pickup day; the day of/before, put flyers with QR code on bins; that evening, drive through the neighborhood blasting a jingle with a magnetic sign on your vehicle (not a full wrap — too expensive at ~thousands of dollars — just to test the concept cheaply).
- Pricing mentioned in the video: $25/bin as an anchor, though presenter revises down, saying based on a prior podcast interview the realistic price is closer to $15/bin.
- Process: pressure-wash each bin, ~5 minutes each; cited math: 20 bins/day = $500/day (at the $25 rate).
- Startup cost: under $1,000 (a solid, non-industrial pressure washer costing "hundreds of dollars").
- Referenced a prior guest who quit a six-figure job to do bin cleaning full-time and is doing well (episode from Oct 2024, linked in show notes).
- Caveats: dirty/unglamorous work, hard to scale, relatively new/low-competition niche.

## 5. Novelty food ideas (farmers market plays)
- **Chocolate-covered chips**: buy a chocolate fountain (a few hundred dollars on Amazon), set up at a farmers market, melt chocolate over Lay's potato chips in front of customers (the visible-production effect lets you charge more), cool with a fan, bag after ~5 minutes, sell for ~$20. Startup cost: ~$500.
- **Upgraded hot chocolate presentation** (referencing a trend seen in India): rim the cup with marshmallow fluff, torch it (kitchen torch from Amazon), add Oreo crumbles; ~2 extra minutes of labor turns a $6 hot chocolate into a $9 one. Setup: a folding table (e.g., from Costco) at a farmers market or similar foot-traffic spot.

## 4. Kids' artwork consolidation/canvas service
- A business where parents mail in their children's artwork; the operator arranges pieces on a large canvas (bought from a craft store like Hobby Lobby), adds resin, and sells the finished piece back to the parent for "hundreds or thousands of dollars."
- Framed as selling nostalgia/parental sentiment rather than the physical product; cited as having very high social-media view counts (tens of millions), indicating demand.
- Low-cost/approachable to start; can outsource the "eye for design" step by photographing the laid-out artwork and asking ChatGPT how to arrange it.

- Framed as unsexy but not requiring sales skill, just willingness to talk to business owners directly (selling jerky, candy, chips, sodas, etc. into small retail locations without needing a vending machine).

## 2. "Free service + video it" content/lead-gen model (a la SB Mowing)
- Model: knock on doors offering a home service completely free (mowing, pressure washing, roofing, fencing, painting, etc.) in exchange for permission to film and post the transformation online.
- Reference case: Spencer/"SB Mowing" channel has grown to an estimated 50-70+ million subscribers across platforms doing this with lawn care/pressure washing.
- Another operator (Cole Simpson, Columbia SC) independently built the same model, is making six figures/year on YouTube after only a few months, with millions of views per video; presenter estimates he could hit seven figures the following year from YouTube revenue alone (not counting sponsorships/merch/memberships).
- Monetization: primarily platform ad revenue/monetization at scale (cited estimate: could be $10M+/year net profit with a small team — tripods, GoPros/iPhones, editors e.g. hired from the Philippines).
- Advice: don't feel you need a novel service — "siphon" attention from an already-proven format/niche (mowing, pressure washing) or make small variations; test multiple service concepts (soft washing, fence restoration/painting, house painting, roof washing). More visually dramatic/expensive-seeming services (e.g., roofing) can convert better on video because perceived effort/cost drives retention and reach.

## 1. Imported "jet boat" (cheap jet-ski alternative) arbitrage/rental
- Product: a ~30 mph "jet boat" from Chinese manufacturers (Alibaba/AliExpress/DHGate/Temu), costing high hundreds of dollars, positioned as an ~80% cheaper alternative to a real jet ski (which costs many thousands).
- Monetization path 1 (resale): import units, list on Facebook Marketplace including the word "jet ski" in the description for search visibility; presenter estimates ~$1,000 profit per unit.
- Monetization path 2 (rental): run a rental business analogous to jet-ski rentals — charge ~20% less than jet-ski rental rates (since it's slower) while paying ~80% less for the unit, netting a better margin.
- Process for vetting import products generally (presenter has personally imported "a dozen different things" from China, ranging $5-$5,000 items): DM manufacturers on Alibaba/AliExpress, request samples, negotiate over WhatsApp (mind the ~13-hour time difference with China), use Alibaba's trade-assurance/insurance option to avoid being scammed, and check seller reviews.
- Validate demand *before* importing: list the product (using stock photos from Alibaba) on Facebook Marketplace at various price points, track clicks/messages/impressions in a Google Sheet, and even pre-sell with a deposit ($50-500) before placing the actual import order.
- Rationale/caveat: many people are unwilling to wire money directly to unknown Chinese manufacturers and will pay a markup to a domestic middleman to take on that risk — presenter argues this "middleman" role is legitimate and profitable (his own 15-year background was in China import/export).

## General caveats across the episode
- These are pitched as "beginner-friendly, approachable, affordable, and scalable," but several ideas are explicitly called unglamorous/hard to scale (bin cleaning, car seat cleaning) or require a learning curve with real time investment before becoming efficient (mower repair).
- Several ideas depend on short-form/video content for customer acquisition — presenter repeatedly stresses that ideas suited to visually compelling video (time-lapses, dramatic before/afters) grow faster organically.
