# How To Make $100K Building Websites for Realtors
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WjtcP2K7XLU | Published: 2024-02-19

- Business idea: build/sell websites to real estate agents. This video is a coaching-call critique of a founder's early approach and a pivot to a better version of the same idea.
- Original (rejected) approach: scrape realtor.com for agents who have NO website, cold email them offering a custom website starting at $2,000, using a mockup screenshot (agent's face/name/service area) as the pitch.
  - Problem raised: agents with no website skew toward two bad segments — (a) inactive/stale agents (scraped data may be 6 months–6 years old) who don't want a website at all, or (b) small-town agents who are already successful without one and won't be easily convinced they need one. Pitching "you need a website" to people who visibly don't think so is a hard, low-conversion sell.
- Recommended pivot: target realtors who ALREADY have a website (so they're pre-sold on the value of having one) but whose site is outdated/low-converting — much easier sell than convincing someone they need a website in the first place.
- Concrete sourcing method:
  1. Identify a WordPress plugin that supports IDX (the API that syncs an MLS feed into a realtor's site, e.g. linking listings the way Zillow does).
  2. Use BuiltWith.com to scrape/export the 100,000+ websites using that specific IDX plugin.
  3. This list is now a pre-qualified lead list: WordPress-based real estate sites, most of which are outdated/low-converting/poorly designed.
- Pitch mechanics:
  - Don't say "your website is ugly." Instead lead with a business outcome, e.g. "a website with X/Y/Z features results in 36% more leads for real estate agents" (pull a specific stat like this from a real estate website SaaS platform's own marketing — e.g. platforms like CarrotBoards/Grasshopper-style templated real estate site builders).
  - Show examples of OTHER realtor sites already built on that same SaaS platform as implied "case studies" — the prospect infers they're past clients, but you never claim that outright; they're just examples of the platform's output.
  - You don't need a completed customer or portfolio to start pitching — point to other people's sites on the platform.
  - You can build your own demo site on that same platform (even without being a realtor) to have something to show.
- Delivery/cost approach to keep margins high: use existing SaaS real estate website platforms/templates (mentioned by name-ish: "Carrot," "Grasshopper," or similar) rather than building custom sites from scratch.
  - Setup time per site cited as low as ~30 minutes using templates.
  - You can still charge a premium price even though the underlying site is templated — cited example: people charging $2,000 per site built this way; the idea's own initial pricing target was ~$2,000/site (title implies scaling to $100K, i.e. ~50 sites/year at that price point).
  - Just swap out city name and personal details across template deployments — highly repeatable.
- Outreach channel notes: cold email plus cold calling/texting realtors directly.
  - Realtor contact info is easy to obtain (public listings, MLS-linked sites, brokerage directories).
  - Downside: realtors are heavily prospected already by other cold outreach (website sellers, lead-gen services, etc.), so inbox/phone fatigue is real.
  - Upside cited: realtors are unusually likely to actually answer their phone (professional habit — every call could be a potential listing), making cold calling more viable for this niche than most.
- Caveat: the guest had no validated data yet (had only just sent one cold email) — the whole conversation is advice/critique on an unvalidated idea, not a proven case study with real revenue numbers.
