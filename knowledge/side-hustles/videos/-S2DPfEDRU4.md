# The Most Overlooked $1M Business Every Town Needs
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-S2DPfEDRU4 | Published: 20260923

Behind-the-scenes tour of Laundry Spot's flagship laundromat in Hamilton, OH with co-founder Tyler Purcell.

## The business idea
- Laundry Spot: a laundromat concept built around oversized, tech-managed washers (up to 130 lb capacity) and a "million-dollar store" ambition, in contrast to the typical "buy an old mat, it's passive income" pitch.
- Revenue mix per location: self-service washing/drying (the core), commercial/wholesale laundry (hotels, fire/police departments), and dry-cleaning wholesale (added purely for customer retention, not margin).
- Now franchising (launched Jan 2026): 36 territories sold in 8 months across FL, OH, KY, AL, with more markets (e.g. Indianapolis) in discussion.

## Numbers
- Flagship location (built during COVID, ~$2M all-in, opened ~2022, now in its 4th year): $1.25M revenue / $420K net profit last year; ~mid-$700Ks profit in its first year.
- Biggest washer (130 lb): ~$130/day revenue at 6-7 turns/day, base wash $22 rising to $24-25 with add-ons (extra rinse/pre-wash); utilities run under 10% of revenue on this equipment (weighs the load, doses water/soap precisely) vs. 20-30% on a laundromat's 20-year-old machines.
- Commercial laundry at the flagship: ~21% of that location's revenue; grew from ~30,000 lbs/month toward a projected 50,000 lbs/month; billed $1-3/lb depending on the account — approaching ~$100K/month from commercial alone at one store.
- Dry cleaning: charge $7/shirt, pay ~$3.50 (50%) to the wholesale processor — a loyalty add-on, not a real profit center.
- Fairfield location (their first, leased 2019, $1.1M to open): $100K self-serve revenue in the first quarter post-grand-opening (~$400K annualized), ramped to $155K in Q4 of year one (~$620K annualized); cash-flows "high $300Ks" net/year at maturity; could sell today for ~$2M (5-6x cash flow).
- Franchise startup cost (per FDD): $1.2M-$2.2M all-in depending on store size, not including real estate; ~$1.1M of that is equipment alone (bonus depreciation lets a buyer write off much of that in year one against other income).
- Costs: labor ~20% of revenue (the single biggest line) — attendants start ~$15/hr, attendant/managers up to $22/hr, typically 1 attendant on weekdays and 2 on weekends (50% of self-serve revenue happens Saturday/Sunday); utilities ~9.5% of revenue; insurance and property taxes both described as "negligible."
- 28% of customers still pay by quarter/coin despite full card, app, tap-to-pay, and loyalty-card options being available.
- Track record cited: their 4 franchise locations have put 11 competing local laundromats out of business — framed as a net local job gain (each new store brings 40-50 part-time jobs vs. ~10 lost per closed competitor).
- Market sizing: official US laundromat count ~30,000, but likely closer to 40,000 once unlisted mats are counted — a market they think can support "well over a thousand" Laundry Spot units long-term.

## Process / steps
- Grand-opening playbook: ~3 months out, start "coming soon" direct mail + chamber-of-commerce outreach + local press; ~1 month out, host a VIP red-carpet grand opening (live music, open bar, catered food, door prizes, a few thousand dollars total) inviting other local business owners to build referral relationships; run **two consecutive** free-laundry weekends (not one) — giving a full week between events lets word-of-mouth compound before the second wave; push hard for 5-star Google/Facebook reviews during the free weekends since customers aren't being charged, making it the easiest time to collect reviews.
- Equipment as differentiation: proprietary direct-soap-injection system offering up to 8 selectable detergent/softener/bleach combinations (reportedly fewer than 15 laundromats nationwide have this at all); an ozone sanitization cycle marketed for odor removal (sports gear); cloud-based remote cycle customization so staff can adjust wash settings without being on-site.
- Site selection: look for areas that already have 3-5 competing laundromats (proof of real demand) rather than avoiding competition entirely; do hands-on competitive research — visit competitor stores, do your own laundry there, read their utility meter to estimate their revenue; favor older (1990s-era) apartment complexes with neglected shared laundry over brand-new apartment developments; a ~60,000-population city can typically support only about 2 laundromats.
- Explicitly rejected buy-and-renovate: considered buying a pack of 4 existing laundromats and upgrading them, but concluded it made more sense to build new competing stores and put the old ones out of business instead.
- Commercial account acquisition currently runs through the GM's local networking/chamber involvement and inbound referrals, not a dedicated outbound sales process.

## Tools/platforms named
- Laundry Spot's own cloud dashboard for remote machine cycle control and per-cycle usage/revenue data.
- tkowners.com (host's paid community, standard channel plug).

## Caveats / risks
- Explicit warning against buying an old "passive income" laundromat: verifying true income/expenses on a poorly-documented old mat is "almost impossible," most buyers overpay, and aging equipment breaks constantly — turning the "passive" pitch into an unpaid full-time low-wage job.
- Nothing about running a laundromat is actually passive, even with a GM/AGM in place — active oversight is still required.
- Self-service revenue matures around year 2-3 per location as the surrounding radius saturates; growth after that comes mainly from price increases and expanding commercial accounts, not new organic self-serve customers.
- Capital requirement is large ($1.1M-$2.2M, excluding real estate) — this is not a low-capital side hustle.
