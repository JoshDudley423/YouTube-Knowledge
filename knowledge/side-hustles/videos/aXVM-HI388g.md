# We Copied His $10K+/Month Business From Scratch in a New City
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aXVM-HI388g | Published: 2025-11-05

- Business idea (follow-up to an earlier viral episode): buying/acquiring cheap used washers and dryers and renting them out on Facebook Marketplace, demonstrated by relaunching the business from zero in a new, supposedly "oversaturated" market (Dallas-Fort Worth, DFW) to prove it still works despite increased competition since the original video went viral (60+ competing DFW listings found vs. an estimated 10 before the original video).
- Guest: Kyler (22 at time of original video), zero employees, ~5 hours/week, originally built to $10,000/month in Utah; this episode is a live re-launch/stress test in a new city.
- Sourcing units: check Facebook Marketplace "garage sale" listings (bundled multi-item sales) rather than individual appliance listings — sellers doing a garage sale often haven't bothered to list the washer/dryer separately, so you can negotiate a bundle price. Example in video: negotiated a washer+dryer set down from $175 to $125 for both.
- Unit economics on that $125 set: renting the dryer alone for $40/month and the washer alone for $40/month ($80/month combined) means profitable by month 2; alternatively, the set could be flipped/sold outright (e.g., listed at $400) for a quick profit.
- Rental pricing and customer lifetime value (LTV) math used in the episode:
  - $60/month typical listing price for a washer+dryer set with free delivery/install/repairs (some competitors listed $65–85/month; they intentionally priced at the lower end, $60, to stand out).
  - Example real deal: a customer ("Shanae") signed for 12 months at $60/month = $720/year; average customer retention assumed at 2+ years based on Kyler's experience, so profit after buying a $125 unit nets roughly $600 profit year one, ~$720 profit year two — cumulative estimated lifetime value per customer of roughly $1,300 over ~2 years.
  - A single $20 Facebook "boost" (paid ad spend) on a listing reportedly generated the $700–1,400 LTV Shanae signup within the first hour, illustrating a customer acquisition cost (CAC) of ~$20 against $1,300+ LTV — used to argue that a "saturated" market is still highly profitable if CAC stays low relative to LTV.
- Logistics/equipment tips:
  - For occasional/early-stage moves, renting a truck from Hertz/Avis/National (~$30/day, 5-day minimum, no mileage charge in the example, ~$161 total for 5 days) is cheaper than Home Depot/U-Haul trucks that charge ~$0.75/mile — recommended only to validate demand before committing to buying/leasing a dedicated truck.
  - Always replace old water supply hoses/gaskets with new ones on every install (a few dollars in parts) to avoid leak callbacks — this is standard practice on every job.
  - Get a customer's phone number before delivering, not just a Facebook Messenger handle — protects against no-shows/ghosting once you're already on-site.
  - When only one of the two units (washer or dryer) is wanted, price the single-unit rental at more than half the set price (since per-unit demand/economics justify it), because customers renting one unit often end up renting the second when it eventually breaks too, extending the relationship.
- Volume/scaling math presented on-camera: in one ~4-hour session (with only ~10 minutes actual phone/response time), two rental deals were closed worth $70/month combined new recurring revenue — extrapolated (very optimistically) to $17.50/hour cash-flow-positive labor, and further extrapolated (assuming this pace sustained 40 hrs/week) to a theoretical $440,000/year net profit figure — explicitly a rough, best-case extrapolation, not an actual annual result. More grounded projections given: ~$70/day x 5 days/week ≈ $350/week ≈ $1,200–1,500/month in new recurring revenue in month one, growing to an estimated ~$18,000/month in monthly recurring revenue after a year if sustained daily; a lower-effort weekend-only pace (3–4 deals/weekend) was estimated to reach ~$26,000/month in monthly recurring revenue after a year.
- Apartment complex angle: identified a complex with ~256 units that have washer/dryer hookups but no included machines — pitched as an opportunity to reach a property manager directly and distribute door hangers; back-of-envelope math: 256 units x $60/month if fully converted = ~$15,000/month recurring revenue from a single complex.
- Additional appliance-sourcing loop: on-site delivery visits often lead the customer to offer their old washer/dryer for free (wanting it hauled away), which can be refurbished for ~$30 in parts and re-rented — described as even better economics than buying replacement units outright.
- Loss/theft rate cited: Kyler reports only 1 unit stolen out of his total rented inventory (~1%); compared to an industry reference point from "Rent-A-Center" of a 1–2% average loss rate — used to argue theft risk is a minor, budgetable cost (e.g., ~$0.70/month theft-shrinkage reserve per $70/month rental).
- "Market saturation" framing/caveat: host explicitly argues rising competition (more Facebook listings for the same service) is not necessarily bad — it raises overall market awareness of "you can rent a washer/dryer" as a third option (vs. buying or laundromat), growing total demand ("rising tide") faster than it dilutes any one seller's opportunity, provided you're responsive and price competitively; success in a crowded market comes down to speed of response and being first to deliver/communicate, since most competitors' pricing is similar.
- No LLC, business name, logo, website, or insurance was set up before launching in the new city — explicitly skipped/deferred, illustrating the low administrative-startup-cost nature of getting the first customers.
