# I Tried OpenClaw for a Month and I'm Never Going Back
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vQ3SxSy8J0g | Published: 2026-02-28

Podcast-style episode (Chris Koerner + Nik/"Nikonomics") — Nik spent 4 months full-time learning AI agents, then took a job at a large public company specifically to test whether AI tooling actually works inside a real corporation. Focus: where the real monetizable AI consulting/service opportunities are right now, based on direct observation of corporate AI adoption gaps.

## Core thesis: corporate America is far behind on AI, and that gap is the business opportunity
- Nik's first-hand observation working inside a large publicly traded company: executives and staff "know nothing" — many can barely Google effectively, let alone use AI tools. A casual 15-minute conversation with the executive leadership team turned into a 5-hour session because they had so many basic questions; the CEO reportedly said it was the first time he "actually realized it's happening."
- Cited data point (a viral chart): of the world's ~8.1 billion people, roughly 80% have never used AI at all; ~10% have tried a free chatbot; only ~24 million people (~7 dots at 3.2M/dot) pay for an AI subscription; and a vanishingly small sliver use actual "coding scaffolding" tools (OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, Replit, Lovable, Cursor). Takeaway: people embedded in tech/Twitter circles wildly overestimate how "ubiquitous" AI adoption actually is — the market opportunity to be the person who bridges this gap is still wide open.
- Explicit advice: **"go get a job"** at an established company (even for just a year) if you want to understand real, monetizable AI use cases — you gain access to real corporate pain points, real budgets, and real urgency that you can't replicate by only building things for yourself. Working inside a company also lets someone with only moderate AI fluency appear as "a magician" relative to non-technical coworkers — a low bar with real leverage, similar to how having a Facebook account made you the go-to "social media person" in ~2010.

## Rapid capability growth (context/evidence cited)
- Referenced the "ARC-AGI"-style benchmark tracking how many hours of human work an AI model can complete in a single output at ~50% accuracy: GPT-5 (Dec, cited period) ~2 hours; Claude Opus 4.5 (same period) ~5 hours, on a trend of roughly doubling every 4 months. Claude Opus 4.6 (released ~1.5 months later) jumped to ~15 hours per output at the same ~50% accuracy threshold — tripling in 6 weeks instead of the expected doubling, suggesting the capability curve may be accelerating into exponential territory rather than steady linear growth.
- Analogy used to push back on "what have you actually built?" skepticism about AI hype: early electricity adoption had a long lag between novelty (lighting a house) and transformative use cases; the same lag is expected with AI, so dismissing current experimentation as unproductive misses that transformative applications typically emerge only after a period of "playing around."

## Business idea 1: build a personal AI agent/"second brain" using OpenClaw, then productize the same capability
- **OpenClaw** ("Gary," Nik's personal instance) is an open-source-style AI agent framework that can be given persistent access to email, texts (via an MCP connection to iMessage), Google accounts, and other personal data sources, and runs autonomously with scheduled "heartbeat" jobs.
- Deployment options: run it locally on your own computer (data stays local, no hosting cost beyond your own hardware, but you still pay per-token "inference" costs to whichever model you connect it to — cited pricing: ~$5/million tokens for Gemini, ~$10/million for OpenAI's newest model, ~$25/million for Claude Opus 4.6), or run fully open-source/open-weight models (example cited: "Minimax," benchmarked competitively against OpenAI/Gemini/Claude) locally for zero inference cost, at some capability tradeoff. Rule of thumb given: match the model/tool to the actual task ("you might just need a golf cart, not a Ferrari" — e.g., managing a calendar or replying to routine emails doesn't need frontier-model-level power).
- OpenClaw's structure: builds and maintains a small set of persistent files (roughly 7) covering identity/personality ("soul"), user profile info, agent-run instructions, long-term memory, tools/skills, recurring scheduled jobs ("heartbeat"), and identity — these accumulate automatically the more it's used, without needing all context reloaded every session.
- Nik's build process: exported all of his ChatGPT and Claude conversation history plus texts, then had OpenClaw ingest, standardize, and structure that data into an organized, queryable personal knowledge base (a "people framework" for relationship management, decision frameworks, commitments tracking, etc.). Result: the agent proactively reminds him of outstanding commitments, drafts prep documents/spreadsheets ahead of meetings based on what he previously said he'd do, and answers natural-language queries like "what did Chris and I talk about last time?"
- Technical explainer on why this requires special handling: LLM context windows are finite (recently reaching ~1 million tokens) but a company's/person's accumulated documents/conversations can total billions of tokens — far too much to load directly. Solution is retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)-style tagging/chunking/embedding of data (OpenClaw specifically uses a lightweight "Quick Markdown" semantic-search approach rather than classic vector-database RAG) so relevant pieces get pulled into context only when relevant to a given query, rather than needing everything loaded at once.
- **The single highest-leverage, easiest-to-implement tactic given ("the 80/20")**: just record your meetings, transcribe them, generate a summary, and build a simple accountability/follow-up mechanism on top of that (a reminder system) — described as ahead of 95% of people/companies, who mostly rely on manual notes, memory, or tools like Copilot (described dismissively as inadequate) and currently have no reliable way to archive, query, or follow up on what was discussed and committed to in meetings.

## Business idea 2: "clean the company's data" consulting — described as a multi-million-dollar opportunity
- Core problem: AI tools can only produce good output from clean, well-organized data. Real companies commonly have severe data hygiene problems: the same category of document (example given: durable medical equipment/DME contracts) scattered across multiple inconsistently-named folders ("DME contracts," "durable medical equipment," "hospital equipment," "skilled nursing facility equipment"), mixed file formats (PDF, Word, HTML, JPEG) that complicate automated extraction, and inconsistent/typo'd naming conventions.
- Proposed service: come in and build a clear taxonomy (a consistent hierarchical categorization/naming system), standardize formats, and structure the data so AI tools can actually query and act on it. Framed explicitly as a real, sellable consulting offer, not a hypothetical.
- Caveat on automation limits: AI-assisted categorization can typically get you to ~80% confidence/completion automatically, but the remaining ~20% of edge cases requires a human with real domain experience to manually resolve — meaning this isn't a fully "set and forget" automatable service; it still requires skilled human judgment for the long tail.
- Analogy used: this kind of data unlock is like fracking for oil — extracting previously inaccessible value (data trapped in messy, siloed company records) using a fundamentally cheaper, more accessible extraction method than was possible before (previously required expensive custom SQL/PowerBI/database engineering; now an "AI UI" can sit on top of unstructured data and answer natural-language queries directly, e.g. "show me where we have the largest labor cost deficiency right now").

## Business idea 3: worked example — an "earnings call prep pipeline" tool for public companies
- Origin: Nik got curious about competitors in his company's industry (home health, hospice, senior living), scraped publicly available SEC filings (10-Ks, 10-Qs) and earnings call transcripts (found via an API marketplace, "API Ninjas," ~$20/month) using OpenClaw, and built a dashboard tracking competitor reporting dates, quarterly performance, revenue trends, and recurring analyst questions/themes.
- When shown internally, colleagues revealed how much executive time is burned every quarter on preparing earnings call scripts/language (executives spending days debating word choice like "great" vs. "amazing" vs. "exceptional").
- Built a full pipeline: scrape a company's past earnings call transcripts -> build a "voice"/style profile for each executive -> ingest current-quarter data -> generate a first-draft script -> AI-assisted strategy/workshop session -> refinement -> export a finished script. Reported spending ~12 hours building this to a genuinely sellable/finished state.
- Value proposition framed concretely: saving "the four highest-paid people in the company" roughly 10 hours/year each of preparation time — explicitly high-leverage, high-perceived-value work.
- Important caveat stressed twice: AI does not "just do everything" — the workflow still requires human workshopping/refinement of messaging at each stage; the tool produces a strong first draft, not a finished, unsupervised output.

## Business idea 4: AI literacy / advisory services sold to executives and companies
- **Cold outreach to executives**: since public company executive contact info and company data (filings, etc.) are publicly available, use an AI agent to compile a target list of public companies and their executives, then cold-email a simple pitch: "I've been deep in AI for the last year and a half. I know what's coming, and most of your competitors don't. I'd love 5 minutes to update you on what's coming down the pike." Expect a high rejection rate but argues even landing a few meetings can convert quickly once the executive sees a live demo, because most have simply never been shown what's actually possible.
- Monetizable service formats suggested: a weekly/recurring AI briefing or "fractional AI officer" newsletter-style service for an executive team; a paid multi-week executive AI bootcamp/course (explicitly mentioned: a company asked Nik to run a 12-week, 1-hour/week course for their executive team); one-off paid strategy/briefing sessions (cited example: charging a couple thousand dollars for a single session that gives an executive team a "taste" of what's coming, similar to informal sessions Nik has done with a billionaire who paid to pick his brain).
- Framed as directly analogous to the ~2010 social media consulting wave, where simply having used Facebook made someone a credible "run our social media" hire/consultant — the bar for perceived AI expertise inside most companies right now is similarly low.
- **Time-sensitive arbitrage warning**: this cold-outreach/AI-literacy channel is currently under-saturated because most people aren't yet fatigued by AI-flavored outreach, but this window is expected to close within roughly 6 months as more people adopt the same playbook (cold email campaigns run via agents at scale) and the channel becomes flooded/less effective — advises moving decisively now rather than waiting, while acknowledging the next arbitrage opportunity after that isn't yet known.

## Tools/platforms named
- OpenClaw — open agent framework used as a personal AI assistant/"second brain," connectable via MCP to email, iMessage, Google accounts, etc.
- Claude (Opus 4.6) — Nik's primary model for deep analysis once data/context is prepared; also used for writing PRDs (product requirement documents) when building software.
- ChatGPT — used for large-scale, reliable data-extraction tasks ("the Honda Accord" of models — consistent, less flashy, gets the routine job done).
- Gemini (3.1) and OpenAI Codex — used in Nik's workflow specifically to run "adversarial audits" against a plan/spec Claude produced, iterating through several rounds before finalizing an implementation plan.
- Minimax — cited example of an open-weight model that can be run/customized locally without per-token inference costs to a closed-model provider.
- API Ninjas — API marketplace (~$20/month) used to pull public company 10-K/10-Q financial filing data.

## Caveats
- Nik explicitly frames this episode's ideas as directional/exploratory rather than turnkey ("you're not going to make a million dollars tomorrow") — the value is in early positioning and skill-building, not a guaranteed immediate payout.
- The "record meetings + follow-up" and "clean the data" ideas are presented as currently underexploited precisely because most organizations are unaware of them — this is explicitly a time-sensitive, first-mover-style opportunity rather than a stable, durable moat.
- No concrete pricing, contracts, or verified revenue figures are given for the consulting/advisory ideas (bootcamp, briefings, cold outreach) — these are proposed plays based on Nik's direct experience and reactions from one company's leadership team, not demonstrated business results with third-party clients.
