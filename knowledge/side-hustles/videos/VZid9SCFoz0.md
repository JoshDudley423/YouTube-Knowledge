# This One AI Prompt Could Land You a $15K Client
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VZid9SCFoz0 | Published: 2025-04-24

Freeform conversation with Sam Thompson (repeat guest) about ChatGPT memory features and, more substantively, using AI image generation (ChatGPT/Sora) to build home-services marketing/lead-gen businesses. Part 1 of a 2-part episode.

## Core business idea: AI-generated "after" photo marketing for home service companies
- The pitch: use AI image generation to show a homeowner a realistic mockup of their *own* specific house/yard "improved" (repainted, landscaped, pool added, garage converted, kitchen remodeled, tree trimmed), then market/sell the actual service (or sell the mockup-generation service to the home-service company that will do the work) using that personalized image as the marketing creative.
- Framed as a B2B service business: sell home service companies (landscaping, painting, pool installers, tree trimming, custom home builders, kitchen remodelers) a subscription for generating unlimited personalized mockups for their prospects — pitched price point: "$500/month" per client business, sign up "100 landscaping companies."
- Rationale for why this beats generic marketing: "friction" theory — homeowners want a nicer yard/home but don't know what it could look like or whether it's worth the cost; removing that friction by showing them a photorealistic personalized mockup makes the purchase decision concrete and emotionally compelling (compared to a text-only flyer).

## Concrete workflow demonstrated live in the episode
1. Pull a real listing photo of a house (e.g., via Zillow or Google Earth API) for a specific address.
2. Feed the photo into ChatGPT/Sora with a prompt like "show this home with white paint and black trim" or "show a well-designed landscaped front yard using plants native to North Texas" — produces a photorealistic mockup.
3. Layer in a second prompt to identify specific named plants/materials used (e.g., "list the shrubs in this photo") so the salesperson has real-sounding specifics for the pitch ("dwarf yaupon, Mexican feather grass, purple skullcap").
4. Ask ChatGPT/an LLM to estimate project cost and resulting home value increase (e.g., "this home is listed at $800K before, what's it worth after landscaping?" → estimated +$80,000 appreciation for a $30K job) to put a concrete ROI number directly on the marketing piece.
5. Send the personalized mockup + cost/value numbers via direct mail, text, or door-to-door sales, closing with "sign here."
- Extended variant: scale this by scraping many addresses in a target area (Zillow API for listing photos, Google Earth API for exterior images), batch-generating "after" images for filtered subsets (e.g., only homes lacking trimmed trees, or with outdated kitchens), and mailing only the highest-relevance subset — turns a claimed ~0.5% direct-mail response rate into a projected ~5-6% by pre-qualifying and personalizing.
- Suggested cold-outreach tactic for landing a first client: identify local businesses already paying for unaddressed direct mail (e.g., via Valpak.com, which lets you look up what ads run by zip code) — call the owner directly and show them a personalized AI mockup of their own service applied to a nearby home, framed as proof of a dramatically higher response rate than their current mailer.
- Suggested even more aggressive/attention-grabbing tactic (flagged explicitly as ethically gray/"creepy," used as a growth-hack idea, not necessarily endorsed as best practice): generate a realistic image of a tree that has fallen onto the homeowner's own house/roof and mail it with a tree-trimming company's offer and a QR code — plays on fear/vanity to guarantee the postcard gets attention (people will study a photo that appears to be their own house in distress).
- Suggested test-before-you-sell approach: run this as a service without a real underlying business first — generate leads, then approach a real home-remodeling/landscaping company afterward and either sell them the leads or sell them the ongoing mockup-generation service, citing your own test data (e.g., cost "~$0.80 per postcard" via a tool like Banner Bear for auto image generation) as proof.

## Adjacent AI-image ideas discussed
- Custom coloring books from personal photos (uploaded engagement/family photos → "turn this into a coloring book") — Sam's viral tweet (1M+ views) on this; noted that Etsy sellers were already doing this commercially with hundreds of reviews before the tweet went viral. Consensus: standalone consumer AI-image products like this are viewed as a *short-lived* opportunity (roughly a 3-4 year window before market saturation/awareness catches up), since barrier to entry is extremely low and it becomes a "best marketer wins" game (whoever finds the right ad channel/pricing first).
- Hyper-personalized wall art/home decor: turning a child's photo into a "Pixar princess," or generating custom art styles (e.g., "Leroy Neman painting" style) from personal photos for canvas prints — compared explicitly to the existing pet-portrait-commission niche (send a photo, get a hand-drawn portrait) and to Chatbooks (a real, ~$30M/year business built on turning synced Instagram/Google Photos into physical photo books, including a "subscription" model that auto-mails a new photo book of that month's posts).
- Niche variations suggested: youth sports team photos turned into "2K video game" or "GTA" style animated art for kids' walls; broad "print on demand" fulfillment is described as fully solved/outsourceable via services like Printful, Printify, Pod Partners, Pixieset — the entrepreneur only needs to handle marketing/image generation, not printing or shipping.
- Suggested go-to-market pattern for consumer image-gen products: launch broad (one Shopify store, many product/niche tests), run ads across several audiences, then double down on whichever niche's conversion data is best (e.g., discovering weddings converts best, then going all-in on wedding-specific offers).
- For the existing-business (not new-product) opportunity: AI image gen is seen as more durably valuable when integrated into existing ad/creative workflows (e.g., merging a proven ad-creative template with a client's product photo automatically) — cited examples of Facebook ad agencies becoming "10x more effective" and an email/landing-page agency using ChatGPT to mock up sample emails from a client's asset library as an early-stage research/pitch accelerator, not a full designer replacement.

## Numbers and specifics cited
- Illustrative example: a landscaping "after" image job pitched as worth "$15,000" for the full install, or offered "DIY for five grand" — used to illustrate a wide price range depending on how the service is packaged/sold.
- Suggested SaaS-style pricing for selling the mockup-generation tool itself to a home-service business: ~$500/month for "unlimited custom mockups, graphics, and quotes."
- Estimated timeline for meaningful industry adoption: guessed roughly 10 years before even 10% of landscaping companies use this kind of tool in marketing — framed as generational adoption lag, not a near-term crowded market.
- Postcard cost cited: roughly $0.80-$5 per personalized direct-mail piece depending on execution.

## Caveats / risks raised
- Explicitly framed the standalone consumer AI-image-product opportunity (coloring books, wall art, etc.) as likely short-lived (a few years) due to low barrier to entry and fast market saturation once more people become aware of what tools like ChatGPT/Sora can do.
- The "creepy" fear/vanity-based direct mail tactic (fake storm damage photos) is ethically questionable and was discussed candidly as attention-grabbing rather than recommended as best practice — worth treating as a risk/reputation consideration, not a clean recommendation.
- Cost/value estimates generated by an LLM (e.g., project cost, home value increase) are approximations, not verified appraisals — the speakers note you can improve accuracy by feeding the model real local labor-cost data (e.g., actual subcontractor hourly rates) rather than relying on generic estimates.
- This is a brainstorming/ideation conversation between two operators, not a case study of a proven, operating business — no revenue/track record is given for the home-services AI-mockup concept itself (unlike other TKO episodes profiling live operators).
