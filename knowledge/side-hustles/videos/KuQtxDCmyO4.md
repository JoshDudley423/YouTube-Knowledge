# She Makes $1k/Day Sending Letters
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KuQtxDCmyO4 | Published: 2026-02-14

Interview with Hannah, founder of "The Tiny Project" (Austin, TX) -- a paid snail-mail subscription club ("The Tiny Post") plus a related in-person recurring market event ("The Tiny Farmers Market"). Business is ~6 months old at time of filming.

**The Tiny Post (core business): paid physical letter subscription**
- Product: a monthly (mailed once a month) 1-oz envelope containing an original art print/postcard, a recipe card, 2-3 stickers, a surprise paper item (varies monthly -- e.g. a collage pack, hand-made tarot cards from a collaborator, pen-pal matching), and a long personal/vulnerable handwritten-style letter from Hannah (functions like a public journal entry). Price: **$11/month**, no long-term contract ("no turnover" commitment).
- **Origin/inspiration**: directly modeled on an existing creator's "Poem Club" (Brittany Wilder, ~$8-9/month, sends a monthly poem + sticker) discovered on TikTok; Hannah deliberately avoided becoming a customer of that product to prevent creative influence bleed-through, launching her own version about a week after conceiving the idea.
- **Subscriber growth trajectory** (explicit month-by-month): Month 1 (Aug): 50 subscribers. Month 2: 107. Month 3 (Oct): 1,200 (driven by one viral TikTok video reaching ~1.1M views). Month 4: 2,500. Month 5 (Nov, another viral video): 3,200. Month 6 (Dec): 3,700. Month 7 (Jan): prepping 4,100, still growing.
- **The viral video mechanic**: Hannah had secretly filmed herself telling her (confused, MBA-holding) husband about the idea months earlier, then posted it later alongside footage of the business's growth in a "Hope Core" style (an aspirational/wholesome content genre she notes is currently trending well on TikTok). The video also sparked comment-section confusion about "what even is snail mail," which she used as an opportunity to explain/build authority around the concept -- effectively free educational marketing.
- **Waitlist as a growth/scarcity mechanism**: paused new signups at 1,200 subscribers (had planned to cap at 400 but let it run) and moved to a waitlist that grew past 3,000 people; fewer than 1,000 of those ultimately converted to paying subscribers, in part because an early sign-up form didn't clearly state the subscription was paid (some people signed up expecting it was free) -- lesson explicitly flagged: be unambiguous about pricing on lead-capture forms. The waitlist itself is credited with creating pent-up demand/scarcity that boosted signups once reopened.
- **Financials at ~4,100 subscribers**: ~$45,000-50,000/month recurring revenue, with an estimated ~$25,000-30,000/month in profit (Hannah's own estimate, roughly 50-60% margin).
- **Unit economics**: hard cost per letter ~$3 (a "forever stamp" at $0.78 + stationery/stickers/inserts), giving roughly 70% gross margin before labor. The 1-oz weight limit (to stay at one stamp) is the binding constraint on what can be included.
- **Labor**: initially fulfilled by family (her brothers, brother-in-law) assembling envelopes in one concentrated week per month; six people can produce about 1,000 letters in ~2.5 hours (about 18 person-hours per 1,000 letters, ~1-3 minutes/envelope). As of filming, just starting to bring in additional outside labor/standardize the process because family-only labor was becoming unsustainable at scale. No viable automation/machinery exists for the full process (some machines fold letters but can't pack them), which she frames as a feature -- customers value that a human personally touches/packs every letter, reinforcing why nothing similar exists at scale in the market.
- **Churn**: budgeted for ~10% monthly cancellation rate but actual churn has run closer to ~5%/month; reports zero negative reviews.
- **Customer acquisition**: entirely organic/free -- TikTok and Instagram content (telling the founding story repeatedly resonates), plus Pinterest added later (started after noticing other people re-posting her content there without attribution -- decided to claim that channel herself). No paid advertising used.
- **Monetization boundaries deliberately avoided**: explicitly turned down an advertiser who wanted to pay to include ads/inserts in the mailers, judging it would undermine the mission and erode customer trust; the host explicitly praises this restraint as core to the brand's success ("consumers are smart, they know where your intentions are").
- **B2B extension being explored**: local businesses (a realtor and a run club have reached out) asking for a white-labeled/co-branded version of the concept as a client-touchpoint/retention tool -- Hannah is exploring building "Tiny Post"-style mailers for professionals like realtors/financial advisors/bankers as a recurring, personal-feeling client-relationship product, while keeping the consumer-facing product ad-free.
- **Ideas for expansion mentioned**: an archive/back-catalog shop letting subscribers buy past months' letters (inspired by a similar feature the Poem Club offers); a second/higher price tier (e.g., ~$16 for twice-monthly delivery including one new + one archived letter) to raise average order value without added creative burden; connecting subscribers to each other as pen pals to increase stickiness/reduce churn; adapting the concept for other niches/demographics (kids' craft/educational versions likened to Kiwi Crate/Crunchlabs shrunk into an envelope; niche versions for men around hobbies like classic cars, golf, hunting, or survival skills; photography-based versions from a contributor with excess unsold prints; even a shared-fictional-world/pen-pal "Animal Crossing"-style concept from another operator she's talked to).
- **Referenced comparable business (industry benchmark, not this channel's video)**: "Flower Letters," a Utah-based couple selling serialized historical-fiction novels delivered in letter format, reportedly doing over $10 million/year with very low churn (each series has a fixed ending, so subscribers stay through the full arc). Host suggests the same "novel delivered as letters" format could be replicated for other genres (mystery, thriller, memoir) or male-skewing content.
- **Market/trend context cited**: host pulls up Google Trends showing "junk journaling" and "letter subscription" as search terms both trending up over the past 5-22 years, framed as part of a broader "anti-AI" / analog-nostalgia consumer trend; junk journaling (a scrapbooking-adjacent hobby of collecting/pasting ephemera like receipts and ticket stubs into a journal) is specifically noted as an enthusiast community that shares/unboxes snail-mail-club contents online, functioning as unpaid word-of-mouth marketing and product feedback.
- **Content/growth tactics discussed**: her most successful video was one being fully transparent about a production disaster (spilled red wine on a batch of letters, requiring every letter to be re-stamped due to added weight) -- audience responded very positively to the vulnerability/authenticity rather than a polished narrative. She learns what resonates by watching her own video performance rather than studying other creators' techniques. A "golden ticket" random-winner mechanic (a lucky subscriber's letter includes a free year of the subscription for them and a pen pal) was just introduced; host suggests testing unrelated prizes (e.g., a $100 Amazon gift card) to see if that changes response rates further.

**The Tiny Farmers Market (secondary/adjacent business, community-driven)**
- Started as a personal front-yard "lemonade stand"-style pop-up selling homemade jams to meet neighbors; grew via monthly consistency and flyering into a recurring event now hosting 33 vendors in her yard, with free drinks, live music, and face painting, benefiting a different local charity each time.
- Traffic/economics: most recent event drew ~800 attendees; Hannah's own booth (selling jams, art, salts, vanilla extract -- rotating creative products) nets her "a couple thousand dollars" in the ~3-hour event; vendor fees charged are largely reinvested into event costs (e.g., heaters). Not treated as a primary income driver, more a community/brand-building project alongside the letter subscription.
- Her husband has since quit his job (previously an AI consultant) to work full-time on The Tiny Project alongside her.

**General transferable takeaways (host's framing)**
- This is presented as a highly replicable business model: pick any niche/passion (not necessarily poetry or art), package a small physical/tangible item into a sub-1oz envelope, charge roughly $8-16/month, and grow via organic short-form video storytelling rather than paid ads.
- Explicit "hypocritical" advice given by the host: rather than diversifying into every monetization idea suggested (ads, affiliate/referral programs, viral loops), keep doing the single core thing slightly better each month -- citing that even staying niche (current market penetration: ~7,000 people out of "180 million female Americans") could scale to tens or hundreds of millions of dollars in lifetime revenue without ever expanding scope.
- Host separately floats a related but distinct direct-mail concept of his own called "chunky mailers" -- marketing envelopes with a small physical object inside (thin enough to stay machine-mailable at regular postage, roughly under a quarter-inch) to guarantee opens, on the psychological principle that people always open mail containing something they can feel; notes a former business partner ran a version of this for a few years with modest success, and that direct mail is not "dead" when targeting/offer/creative are right.

Caveats/risks raised: Hannah's own stated fear is that the "snail mail" trend could be a moment-in-time (aided by hitting virality) rather than a durable category; churn management and manual labor scaling (found to be more time-intensive than expected once volume grew) are cited as the operational stress points; deliberately declining brand-advertiser revenue is framed as a trade-off that protects trust/retention over short-term monetization.
