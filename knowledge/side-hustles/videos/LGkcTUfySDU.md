# The Most Profitable Side Hustle No One Is Doing Yet
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LGkcTUfySDU | Published: 2026-06-11

Interview with Derek, founder of Backyard Movie Theater (backyardmovietheater.com), on turning a Christmas gift into an events business (outdoor/indoor inflatable movie theater rentals + LED dance floors).

**Origin & growth timeline:**
- Started 2 years before filming as a Christmas gift for his own family: a 16-ft inflatable movie screen bought on Amazon for **$250-$300**.
- After doing a free movie night for his son's friends, word of mouth from neighborhood parents led him to list the service on Facebook Marketplace, "not trying to make it a business."
- First paid booking was an HOA event — he was unprepared (his consumer-grade inflatable screen flapped badly in ~10mph wind, had to move the event indoors to the amenity center, comped the client). Learned from this to invest in commercial-grade equipment.
- Upgraded to a **20-ft industrial movie screen from Alibaba**: ~$3,500 for the unit + ~$900-$1,100 shipping (~$4,400-$4,600 total), sourced from overseas.
- First real paid booking (post-upgrade): $290, priced by researching competitors ("way rock bottom" in hindsight).

**Revenue/numbers (as of filming, ~2 years in):**
- Did **$10,000 in sales/bookings in a single week** shortly before filming (includes new LED dance floor add-on).
- Prior full year revenue: **~$40,000**.
- Projected current-year revenue: **~$90,000-$100,000**, at **70-80% profit margin** ("it's just my time" — equipment is a sunk cost).
- All customer acquisition is informal: Facebook Marketplace listings, referrals, word of mouth — no formal marketing funnel or CRM/lead system in place (he explicitly says he wants to build a repeatable lead-gen system, referencing an SEO episode of the podcast).
- Not his only business — he also runs a drinkware business with his wife (supplying Macy's, American Eagle) and previously did commercial drone flying.

**Pricing / packages (outdoor movie theater):**
- Beginner package (screen + speakers only): **$375**.
- + 6 chairs: **~$450**.
- Packages scale up through 10-15 chairs; a 20-chair LED-chair custom package: **$850**.
- Top-tier package: **$1,500** (sold ~4 of these).
- Equipment cost breakdown for the outdoor rig: ~$4,200-$4,600 screen, **$1,500 each** for top-of-line JBL speakers (2 speakers = $3,000), **~$1,100** projector — roughly **$10,000 all-in** for hardware/equipment. Uses wired (not Bluetooth) speaker connections for reliability/quality.

**Indoor inflatable theater (differentiator/niche):**
- Built to solve a specific problem: in Texas summers it doesn't get dark until 9:30pm, so outdoor movies can't start until then — too late for young kids, and a 90-minute movie wouldn't end until 11pm.
- Custom-designed (took ~2 months to spec, ~3-4 months to ship) fully inflatable, air-conditioned/heated indoor theater room with full carpeting and LED lighting for a real "theater experience"; his original screen manufacturer couldn't build it, so he found a different manufacturer.
- Cost breakdown: inflatable structure ~$2,000, carpeting ~$400, 4 portable A/C units (bought secondhand off Marketplace) ~$150-$200 each (~$600-$800 total; 2 units weren't cold enough, needed 4) — total incremental build cost **~$3,500** (reuses the same projector/speakers from the outdoor rig).
- Charges **$1,700** for the indoor "fully decked out" (carpeted) setup; had ~10 bookings for it at time of filming.
- States he's not aware of anyone else offering this specific indoor inflatable AC theater concept ("I'm the only one... I looked").
- Client profile noted: indoor-theater clients tend to be homeowners on multiple acres of land who don't blink at the price; price-sensitive inquiries are a signal the client isn't a fit.

**LED dance floor add-on (newest offering):**
- Idea originated organically: kids kept dancing to music/videos he played after movies ended, prompting him to add a literal dance floor.
- Spent about a year sourcing/negotiating with an overseas manufacturer (language barrier made it hard to even know what questions to ask) to get the right price and figure out logistics for running multiple simultaneous bookings (needs extra power/equipment per unit to book more than one job per day).
- Sizes: 8x8 (small), up to 16x16 (fits ~70-80 people), 20x20 to 24x24 for full wedding receptions (fits ~100-130 people). Floors come in interlocking 2-ft panels.
- Equipment cost for a 24x24 floor: **~$20,000** to buy.
- Charges **$3,000** for a 24x24 rental — implies breakeven after roughly 7-8 rentals.
- Only completed one paid LED dance floor booking as of filming ("went great"); primary customer segments emerging: weddings, milestone anniversaries (25th/30th/50th), and school year-end celebrations.
- Tech limitation noted: floor color-sync to music works but isn't very responsive — best effect is just revolving colors; speaker has to be placed close to the floor's control equipment for any sync effect; no pressure/step sensors.

**Cross-sell dynamic:** movie nights naturally generate post-movie dancing (music/videos played as guests leave), which became the organic discovery/upsell path into the LED dance floor product — same client base, same events, three revenue lines from one setup.

**Advice given:**
- Do thorough research before picking a manufacturer — "lowest price is not always the best... down the road."
- To learn the ropes without competitive risk, reach out to owners of similar businesses **out of state** (rather than local competitors) for advice, being transparent about what you're building.
- Business grew from an unplanned personal project into a company — didn't originally set out to build a business, so early execution (e.g., the disastrous first HOA event) was rough; treats that as a normal, expected learning cost rather than a reason to quit.

**Caveats/risks raised:** consumer-grade inflatable equipment (sub-$300 Amazon screens) is not reliable for real commercial bookings, especially in wind — a bad first professional event (wind ruining the inflatable, having to move indoors, comping the client) is presented as a predictable early failure mode if you under-invest in gear before taking paid bookings.
