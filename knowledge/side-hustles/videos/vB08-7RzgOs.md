# The Easiest Online Business to Start in 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vB08-7RzgOs | Published: 2026-05-28

## Core idea: white-labeling GoHighLevel (GHL) into boring, underserved niches
- "White labeling" = putting your own brand/logo on someone else's backend software (here, GoHighLevel, an all-in-one CRM/funnel/marketing platform) and reselling it to a specific niche audience as if it's your own product.
- Thesis: boring/overlooked industries (notaries, gyms, insurance agents, tutors, estheticians, bail bondsmen, dog groomers, wedding photographers) have weak software competition but real, underserved demand — "the more boring, the better."
- Businesses referenced were identified via GoHighLevel's own public award tiers (e.g., "Diamond" level = 1,000+ paying customers verified) — a repeatable way to find proof-backed case studies to reverse-engineer.

## Seven case studies broken down (each: niche, price, estimated revenue, founder pattern)
1. **Loan Signing System** (Mark Willis) — sells to mobile notaries; $97/month or $1,000/year software plus a separate ~$400 training course; Diamond-level award (1,000+ customers) => at least ~$100,000/month, likely more; founded ~2024 (only ~2 years old at time of filming); website design is unremarkable, illustrating that a polished site isn't required to convert.
2. **PushPress** — gym management software; $229/month plus a $700 setup fee, with higher tiers at $550-600/month; assuming a $200/month average across 1,000+ customers => $200,000+/month, likely $5M+/year with setup fees and upsells included. Started as simple gym check-in software (over 1 million check-ins processed) before layering on full CRM functionality as an upsell — illustrates the "unbundle one narrow feature, sell it hard, then upsell more software" strategy.
3. **ScaleTutor** (formerly TutorBoss; founder Elliot Phillips, UK) — helps tutors generate more student leads; reported 20,000 students enrolled, $10M in income, 250,000 leads generated, 1,000+ educators served => $100K+/month. Founder was not a software/tech person — built trust working directly with online educators first, then built the software for that same audience's known pain points.
4. **IUL Insiders** — trains and tools up insurance agents selling Indexed Universal Life (IUL) policies; white-labels both the software (GoHighLevel-based CRM/community/training) and the insurance product itself (they're not the actual insurance provider). Pricing: $500 for a 90-day intro then $180/month ("Ignite" tier); $8,400/12 months mid-tier; top tier is "apply now" (implying higher, undisclosed pricing). Insurance agents selling this product reportedly earn $20,000-$100,000 per case. Extremely sticky/low-churn because the agent's entire commission pipeline, training relationships, and peer community live inside the same software.
5. **ZappyChat** — breaks the white-label pattern: instead of white-labeling GoHighLevel for an end-industry, it sells an AI chatbot add-on/plugin *to other GoHighLevel agencies* (the ~70,000+ existing HighLevel agency operators). Pricing: $200/month per sub-account or $500/month for unlimited sub-accounts; at 1,000 customers on the unlimited tier => $500,000/month ($6M/year). Framed as "Path B": selling tools to people already reselling GoHighLevel (Path A), which has far less competition and reaches customers who are easy to find (already active in HighLevel's own Facebook/Slack communities) and are less price-sensitive because they're already running businesses on speed/efficiency.
6. **Techmatics** — an Australian, female-founded direct GoHighLevel clone/reseller (websites, funnels, CRM, email marketing, courses/memberships) priced in AUD ($97-149/month); estimated $2-4M/year in revenue. Cited as proof that a GHL-clone-for-a-specific-country/currency doesn't require living in that country — used to launch the "localize GoHighLevel for an underserved country/language" idea below.
7. **ClickSlow (ClickFunnels' white-label competitor referenced by name as an example of what NOT to do)** — sold a supposedly "lifetime" plan for $97-$497 one-time, then allegedly bait-and-switched customers into ongoing monthly billing; ended up with a 2.4-star Trustpilot rating (complaints about fake reviews, poor support) and reportedly went bankrupt despite reaching the top of the award leaderboard.

## Key strategic lessons extracted
- **Go with what you know**: prior experience in *any* industry (even as an employee, cashier, or gig worker) is a real competitive advantage — you already know the pain points, what operators Google late at night, who they trust, and what they hate. Host's own example: he sold iPhone repair parts into iPhone repair stores after having owned repair stores himself and knowing the supplier gaps firsthand.
- **You don't need your own audience**: partner with a micro-influencer (5,000-50,000 followers) in the target niche who lacks technical skills, and offer to build the software/white-label product for them, splitting revenue 50/50. Suggested cold-outreach hack: message them on Twitter/Strava/Facebook, or send a small amount of money (e.g. one cent) to their PayPal/Venmo email with a partnership pitch in the memo — PayPal will keep emailing that person to claim the money even if they don't yet have an account there, effectively guaranteeing message delivery.
- **Find your product wedge via competitor review mining**: search G2.com for reviews of niche-specific software (dental, salon, dog grooming, etc.), feed the reviews into ChatGPT and ask what features users say are missing — build that gap as your differentiator.
- **Finding underserved niches with high willingness to pay**: search YouTube for "[niche] + training academy/certification" — any niche where someone is charging $10,000+ for a certification/training program with 100+ students is a strong candidate for the IUL-Insiders-style "software + training ecosystem" play.
- **International/language arbitrage**: GoHighLevel-clone businesses exist as the default localized CRM/funnel tool in various countries (examples cited: Funnel.ai and other Diamond-award winners serving Spanish-, Italian-, German-speaking markets). Most non-English-speaking countries/regions (Latin America, Brazil/Portugal, Quebec/France, West Africa) have growing entrepreneur populations but no strong local CRM competitor — framed as "the easiest, most underexploited play in this entire video": buy a localized domain, translate a GoHighLevel snapshot and support docs using Claude/ChatGPT/Grok/Gemini, launch. You don't need to live in or be from the target country.
- **Pricing model matters enormously**: GoHighLevel itself charges the white-labeler a flat monthly fee (roughly $300-500/month) regardless of how many end customers they have (vs. just $97/month if you use GoHighLevel purely as your own internal CRM, not white-labeled/resold). Therefore recurring monthly pricing to your own end customers is essential — a one-time-fee model (like ClickSlow's alleged "lifetime" pricing) creates an unsustainable structure where GoHighLevel's bill keeps recurring monthly forever while customer revenue is front-loaded and finite (support/service costs continue after the one-time payment is exhausted, typically 6-8 months in), effectively requiring an ever-growing pipeline of new customers just to cover fixed costs — described as functionally similar to a Ponzi/pyramid dynamic. Rule of thumb before copying any of these models: confirm your pricing covers the GoHighLevel bill + your time + ad spend + ongoing support, every month, indefinitely.
- General advice: don't try to innovate on the business model — copy what's already proven to work (monthly recurring pricing, niche focus, creator partnerships) rather than trying to be a "first mover," since the seven case studies that succeeded all use monthly billing and the one that broke that pattern (ClickSlow) went bankrupt.

## Reverse-engineering tools named (6 new tools, in addition to 17 from an earlier video)
1. **SpyFu** — shows every keyword a competitor has bid on in paid search over multiple years; maps their entire ad strategy.
2. **Wappalyzer** — free Chrome extension; instantly shows what tech stack/platform any website runs on, including whether it's secretly built on GoHighLevel.
3. **PublicWWW** — searches the web for specific code snippets (e.g., a Facebook Pixel ID) to find every site using it, useful for uncovering multiple brands owned by the same operator.
4. **PredictLeads** — tracks company hiring signals and product launches (e.g., a sudden wave of sales-rep hiring signals a company is scaling).
5. **Apollo.io** — LinkedIn-style contact database with scraped emails for founders/executives; useful for direct outreach to prospects or acquisition targets.
6. **TheirStack** — cheaper alternative to BuiltWith; tracks tech stacks used across millions of companies/apps at scale, filterable by niche + specific tool combinations.
- Suggested research process for whichever business you pick to emulate: study pricing/website, check Meta Ads Library for their running ads, check Wayback Machine/Internet Archive for how their site/offer evolved over time, read G2/Trustpilot reviews (feed them to ChatGPT for synthesis), and look up the founder on Apollo to see if they're building in public.
