# AI Lets You Start This Amazon Side Hustle in Minutes
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDtJaQynQkA | Published: 2025-11-08

Solo episode walking through publishing AI-assisted "low content" books (journals, coloring books, planners) on Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) using BookBolt for research/design.

- Idea: publish low-content/no-content books (journals with blank pages + a funny title, coloring books, planners, calendars, guest books, activity books) to Amazon KDP. No inventory, no shipping, no customer support -- Amazon prints/ships print-on-demand and pays royalties. Content is mostly AI-generated (or trivial to produce, e.g. blank ruled pages) but wrapped in a well-researched, well-designed package targeting an underserved keyword niche.
- Tool used: BookBolt.io (paid tool, has a free trial referenced) -- provides keyword research, product/competitor search with sales estimates, and "Studio" templates to design/format book interiors correctly for KDP (trim size, margins, etc.) so you don't have to learn Amazon's formatting requirements manually.
- Core research method -- exploit the "long tail": most people search generic head-terms (self-help, fiction) which are saturated; the money is in specific long-tail keyword niches with real search demand but low supply/competition. Process demonstrated:
  1. Start with a topic keyword you personally know well (needed so you can properly edit/verify AI-generated content) -- example used: "Texas barbecue" (91,000 Google searches/month, $0.96 CPC, low competition) but Amazon-side sales volume was tiny (~1/month) — too niche for a book despite Google demand (people were searching for restaurants, not books).
  2. Broaden slightly if a niche is too thin: "barbecue" alone had ~25x the search volume (149 monthly Amazon sales volume) but competition score "F" (highly competitive) -- illustrates the tradeoff between demand and competition; look for a fair middle ground.
  3. Use BookBolt's "Product Search" tool to see comparable published books' BSR (Book Sales Rank, indicates relative sales velocity within a category) and estimated monthly sales/revenue. Examples cited: a coloring book ranked ~30th in its category with ~865 estimated sales (~$10,000 made); a "Dad, I want to hear your story" journal and a stress-relief coloring book each also cited around $10,000 made, both AI-generated.
  4. Filter product search by realistic targets (e.g., BSR rank 100-1,000, price $10-30, monthly sales 100-5,000) to find winnable, non-oversaturated niches rather than chasing outlier mega-sellers.
  5. Use the keyword tool's "movers and shakers" / "evergreens" views to spot currently trending or steadily selling low-content categories (e.g., 2026 calendars, graduation guest books, word search puzzle books).
- Concrete niche examples found with strong demand/low competition: "tea tasting journal" (~10,000 searches/month), "fibromyalgia symptom tracking journal" (~1,500 searches/month), "RV journal hardcover spiral journal" (~10,000 searches/month, competition score A) -- used to illustrate that seemingly ultra-niche topics can still have solid demand.
- Case studies cited (via ChatGPT deep-research prompt, sourced from YouTube/Reddit/Indie Hackers/etc., unverified by the host beyond restating them):
  - Chris Ray Dog: $16,000 from a single coloring book.
  - A gag/notebook journal analyzed by publisher Kristen Walters: sold 28,000 copies in one month.
  - A therapeutic-prompts journal priced at $12: over 1 million copies sold, ~$12 million total revenue.
  - A word search puzzle book with only 22 reviews: pulling in $11,000+/month at $9.99.
  - Some coloring-book self-publishers scaled to $100,000/year across multiple titles.
  - "Akara Sarah" (content creator): turned two books into a $500,000/month business.
  - Spanish-language-learning low content books ("Spanish in 60 Days," "Spanish for Real") still selling despite competition from Duolingo/Rosetta Stone/AI translation tools -- used to argue that book-format demand persists even against seemingly superior digital alternatives.
- Practical build workflow shown: pick a working title (e.g., "Conversations I Rehearse in the Shower" -- a blank journal, differentiated only by a funny/relatable title), use BookBolt Studio to set trim size (6x9), interior color (black & white), paper color, and page count (100 pages), and export properly formatted for KDP upload. Use ChatGPT/AI to brainstorm batches of similar funny/relatable title ideas (e.g., "I had a thought once, better write it down," "Stuff I swear I told someone already," "Password hints that will make zero sense later") to generate multiple test titles from one winning concept.
- Testing/scaling cadence recommended: publish and test multiple titles (the host suggests you may need to test 20-30 low-content books to find ~10 real winners), since your first book is "mostly for learning" and likely won't be great. Once a title shows organic traction, add Amazon Ads spend behind it to accelerate.
- Rough unit economics illustrated: a book selling 10 copies/day at ~$3 royalty/copy = ~$30/day = ~$1,000/month; ten such winning titles = ~$10,000/month. Framed as an "asymmetric bet" -- total software cost cited as roughly $20/month for BookBolt plus another ~$20/month for AI tools, versus the (uncapped, if you find a hit) revenue upside; even a "failed" book teaches transferable research/publishing skills for the next attempt.

Caveats/risks:
- Explicitly not a get-rich-quick scheme -- host stresses most first attempts will underperform, requires consistent testing over 3-12+ months to build a real income, and results are "not guaranteed."
- Oversaturated niches, generic/un-customized templates, and skipping keyword research are named as the most common reasons people fail at this -- don't just publish whatever the software's default template outputs; customize designs to stand out.
- The host acknowledges some content will be visibly AI-generated but argues buyers don't care as long as it solves their actual search need/problem -- this is a stated opinion/rationalization, not something independently verified against Amazon's KDP content policies (which have specific and evolving rules around AI-generated content disclosure).
- All the named case-study earnings figures come from a single ChatGPT "deep research" query results and secondhand online claims/testimonials the host did not independently verify -- treat as illustrative, not confirmed data.
- This episode is sponsored by/promotes BookBolt with an affiliate link and free-trial offer -- the enthusiastic tool endorsement should be read with that in mind.
