# How to Make Over $10m Per Year with Rural Youth Baseball Fields
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KvsuNNwotaw | Published: 2024-05-23

Interview with Lance Bird, owner/founder of Rocker B Ranch, a youth-travel-baseball tournament complex an hour east of Fort Worth, TX (Palo Pinto County, population ~27,000). Started as a family ranch, evolved organically over ~19 years into a four-field baseball resort with lodging, pools, food service, and merchandise -- described by the host as "the Buc-ee's of baseball fields."

**Origin and organic growth timeline**
- 2005: bought the ~320-acre property (then ~$1,500/acre, funded from cash flow of his oil & gas business, Cero Energy -- not built with outside investors/partners) intending it as a family ranch/horse facility, not a business.
- ~2009: converted an old cutting-horse arena/round pen into a casual whiffle-ball field for his young son; a local country club groundskeeper was paid informally to teach them proper turf care, upgrading it to a real grass field.
- Growth was fully demand-driven and incremental, never planned as a business: one field led to informal local tournaments, which led to word-of-mouth demand, which led to building a second field, then a third (high school-sized, ~2015-17), then a fourth (2023, built reactively -- see below).
- 2010: added first lodging (10 hotel rooms + 4 cabins), timed just before Airbnb existed; has since expanded to 50 rooms.
- Recent (~2023) major expansion, done in ~90 days during their December-February off-season: took food & beverage service back in-house, built a new commercial kitchen, added 28 more hotel rooms, built a 115,000-gallon resort-style pool (capacity ~400 people) with cabanas, an open-air covered pavilion, and the fourth baseball field -- all funded from reinvested cash flow, no debt (notes it's hard to borrow money for this kind of build in the current environment, so growth is self-funded).

**The "accidental" 4th field / evidence of latent demand**
- On Jan 3rd, a single announcement (via one Facebook post) for the year's first 14 tournaments caused a software bug in their signup tool (TeamSideline/"Turning Machine") that let team caps blow past their per-division limit (meant to cap at ~24-29 teams per weekend, ended up admitting up to 35). This oversold ~10 weekends by more teams than 3 fields could physically host.
- Forced choice: refund/anger hundreds of teams, or build a 4th field fast. Built the field in ~60 days using on-hand materials/leftover pipe, at a cost of ~$300,000 for a no-frills field (no lights yet -- lights alone would add another ~$100,000). This let them retroactively accommodate the demand.
- Illustrates a "let real demand force capacity decisions rather than pre-building speculatively" growth pattern that recurs throughout the interview (e.g., pool/kitchen also expanded reactively once existing facilities were overwhelmed).

**Customer acquisition: zero paid marketing, pure word-of-mouth**
- States they have "never really done any marketing" beyond occasional boosted Facebook posts; virtually all growth comes from organic sharing.
- Built-in virality mechanic specific to youth travel sports: teams must share/tag to get their whole extended support network (both parents, siblings + a friend, grandparents, uncles) to attend -- average headcount per team attending grew from ~35 people to 65-75 people per team as this word-of-mouth compounded.
- Demand outstrips capacity dramatically: 12 of 14 tournaments for the year sold out within 2 minutes of a single announcement post; the other 2 took "a couple more days."
- COVID-19 was a major inflection point: because the facility was outdoors and rural, Texas's governor granted an exception letting them run 9 tournaments during the pandemic when most competitors were shut down -- this generated a surge of social media attention and word-of-mouth that permanently accelerated their growth trajectory afterward.
- Corporate event / team-retreat side of the business is explicitly under-marketed by comparison (relies on inbound calls) and identified by Lance as a growth opportunity that needs dedicated marketing effort.

**Revenue streams / unit economics**
- Core tournament fee: $799/team for a 3-day tournament, ~$1,200/team for 4-day (12 kids/team, so roughly $65-100/kid) -- explicitly priced below premium/exclusive competitors nationally as a deliberate affordability choice given it's "the kid business, the family business."
- Lodging: hotel booking opens the day *after* tournament signup (not simultaneously) to ensure only confirmed teams book rooms; requires a 2-3 night minimum. All 50 rooms typically sell out alongside tournament slots.
- Food & beverage: in-house-made, scratch-quality products positioned as a premium differentiator, not typical "sports tournament food": wood-fired Neapolitan-style pizza (dough made from scratch, proofed 2 days, cooked in a 750°F dome oven purchased secondhand from a defunct pizza chain; ~350-400 pizzas made by hand per weekend; $19.99 for a cheese pizza plus toppings), house-made gelato (owner personally still makes it some weeks), house-smoked barbecue, and "$16 margaritas" made with two shots of premium tequila (Casamigos), Cointreau, and fresh lime juice (no pre-made mix). Owner learned pizza-making via Vito Iacopelli's YouTube channel/paid masterclass, watched multiple times, then scaled the recipe from home-batch to a 150-dough commercial mixer.
- Merchandise: a "surprise" breakout success -- sells 400-600 Rocker B branded hats per weekend; started as owner personally screen-printing shirts in a garage, now a meaningful line item; brand visibility reported anecdotally at Texas Rangers games and in random locations nationwide (e.g., Cabo, airports).
- General admission/parking: a flat $20/car parking fee grants access to the pool, fishing lakes, and grounds regardless of whether the family is there to play in a tournament -- explicitly not run as a gated ticketed attraction (unlike, e.g., Six Flags); parking fee revenue partly funds lifeguards and support staff.
- Corporate events/retreats: rent out the entire facility exclusively (no other groups on-site) with customized catering menus (chefs work with the client's budget/dietary needs); historically priced around $13,000/night before the room expansion, now priced per number of rooms needed (up to all 50); described as high-repeat-business ("almost every company that's ever come out there has come back") but currently under-marketed. Also runs off-season (Dec-Feb) team-building/practice retreats for high school and college teams during the winter down period.
- Cancellation policy: no refunds once paid (rain-outs get a transferable, resellable/giftable credit instead) -- necessary because ~75-100 staff (bartenders, lifeguards, umpires, parking attendants, cooks) must be paid regardless, and cannot be if reservations were freely cancelled.

**Regulatory/infrastructure surprises (relevant for anyone building large rural hospitality/recreation facilities)**
- Because the property is unincorporated (no municipal water/sewer), once a business hosts more than 25 people on more than 60 days/year, it automatically becomes a legally regulated "public water supply" under the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ, the state's EPA equivalent) -- required significant capital investment (new tanks, filters, compliance work with a contracted engineer) to convert their existing reverse-osmosis well system into a compliant public water system. Being unincorporated also meant faster permitting (state-level only, no city permitting), which enabled their ~90-day expansion sprint.
- Also had to build a large-scale septic/sewer system themselves (no city sewer access), upgraded during the recent expansion.
- Fire insurance/coverage cited as more expensive than general liability due to regional wildfire risk (cedar brush, canyon terrain); the ranch hosted an annual charity concert ("Buckets and Boots") for ~5 years that raised about $1 million total for local volunteer fire departments.
- Local economic impact: the facility now brings ~60,000 visitors/year to a county of ~27,000 residents; is one of the county's largest employers (75-100 staff on a typical weekend, largely local high schoolers filling weekend-only labor needs), and drives significant local hotel-occupancy tax revenue in nearby Mineral Wells.

**Growth/positioning philosophy**
- Never took on business partners or outside capital -- funded entirely by reinvesting cash flow from tournament/lodging/F&B revenue ("constantly compounding" is the stated motto); this let him retain full control over pace and direction but also limits how fast he can build (self-imposed capital discipline; explicitly says he's cautious about which capex projects to prioritize with "so many other needs").
- Deliberately differentiates from other travel-baseball facility models (e.g., Cooperstown Dreams Park charging ~$1,200/kid with families staying off-site at hotels; Ripken Baseball facilities in Myrtle Beach/Pigeon Forge) by making the property itself a resort-style destination people don't want to leave (on-site stocked fishing lakes, pool, disc golf, shooting range for corporate events, general store) -- versus the typical "play your game, then sit in a random hotel" travel-tournament experience.
- Considered adjacent expansions: dedicated softball fields (current fields have pitcher's mounds/grass infields unsuitable for softball, which prefers dirt or turf infields), and an enclosed gymnasium/indoor court for basketball or wrestling tournaments during winter (host's suggestion) -- Lance is lukewarm on the gym idea specifically due to uncertain demand and HVAC/build costs, prioritizing more fields and lodging instead since those have proven, immediate demand.
- On franchising/replicating the model elsewhere (e.g., Ozarks, Smoky Mountains -- similar scenic topography): open to the idea long-term (has been approached by former pro baseball players interested in opening similar facilities elsewhere) but says he wouldn't personally operate a second location without partners, and it's not currently on his roadmap -- he's still "drinking from a fire hose" scaling the original site.
- Target demographic is narrower than "all baseball": focuses on youth ages ~8-14 (peaks around 11-13), deliberately not high-school-recruiting-age travel ball, because high schoolers travel solo for recruiting-focused tournaments and don't bring the large family entourage that drives the resort/lodging economics.
- Land utilization: currently uses only ~35-40 of the property's 320 acres, with the majority still open/flat and available for future fields or lodging -- infrastructure (water, sewer, some utilities) was intentionally overbuilt during the recent expansion specifically to make future incremental builds cheaper and faster.

No churn/retention or total annual revenue figure was explicitly disclosed by Lance beyond directional descriptions ("going the right direction," declining to quantify overall scale precisely); the video's title figure ($10M+/year) is the host's framing/estimate, not a number Lance confirms on-record in the transcript.
