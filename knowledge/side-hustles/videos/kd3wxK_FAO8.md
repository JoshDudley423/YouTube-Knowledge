# Year in Review. Net Worth Breakdown, Investments & More
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kd3wxK_FAO8 | Published: 2025-01-04

Chris Koerner's personal 2024 year-in-review: net worth composition, investment philosophy, RV park investing, and content-creation business lessons. Less a "side hustle idea" episode and more his own operating playbook — includes concrete numbers and repeatable tactics.

## Business/investment activities covered
- **Tree service business bootcamp** (Jan 2024): hosted ~20 people (half virtual, half in-person in Dallas) for ~60 hours over a few days in a rented WeWork conference room, teaching home-services business fundamentals. Several attendees went on to start tree businesses from it. Explicitly noted: stressful to run in-person, didn't make much money on the event itself, but valuable/may repeat in a different format.
- **Podcast/content launch** (Feb 2024): started this podcast + YouTube + Instagram + TikTok as an explicit "10-episode test," modeled as "Dave Ramsey for entrepreneurs." Iterated format multiple times (live call-ins, pre-recorded questions), scaled from 2 to 3 episodes/week.
- **RV park investing via "Blue Metric" partnership** (Jan 2024 onward): partnered with two operators (Jeff and John) who had already acquired ~8 RV parks; connection made via Twitter after they saw his content. Did due diligence on the deal and the partners, then began raising capital.
  - Raised **~$4 million almost exclusively from Twitter**, tweeting about investment opportunities roughly once a month (not aggressive promotion).
  - Portfolio grew from ~8 parks to **~26 parks** (worth ~$50M) over the year; he personally holds stakes in roughly 10 of them.
  - Explicit plan to *slow down* further RV park acquisitions going into 2025 because "operations are hard" and things had "gotten a little hairy" — a caution against over-scaling operationally intensive real estate.
- **Side vending**: mentions a short-form video experiment that turned into a business selling perfume vending machines (mentioned only in passing, no numbers given here).

## Net worth / portfolio breakdown (percentages, not dollar figures — he declines to share total net worth)
- Stocks: only ~1.5% of net worth. Rarely buys, does no research, buys impulsively when something he already believes in dips (Tesla, Coinbase are his largest and best-performing holdings; also holds Zoom, Peloton, Facebook, Chipotle).
- Crypto: ~15% of net worth (down from as high as ~80% historically after some bad decisions — margin-called on borrowed-against crypto, lost money on ICOs). Composition: ~50% Bitcoin, 40% Ethereum, 10% "moonshot" bets. First bought Bitcoin in 2016 at $680/coin. Also owns a Mutant Ape NFT bought for $60k, now worth an estimated $7-10k (holding rather than selling at a loss).
- Investment philosophy/lesson: sold his entire crypto and stock portfolio in a panic in March 2020 during the COVID crash (had been up big on early Tesla, Nvidia, Shopify, Facebook positions) — explicitly calls this "the dumbest thing," would have been up ~21x on Tesla/Nvidia had he held. Now follows a strict "never sell" philosophy for both stocks bought since 2020 and all remaining crypto, treating early panic-selling as the single costliest mistake to avoid repeating.
- Real estate: personal home (built 2016, refinanced once, pulled six figures of equity, still appreciating) plus an adjacent 1-acre lot with a 2,100 sq ft shop rented to a landscaping crew (owned outright, no loan) — combined "seven figures" of equity. Long-term plan to convert his own home into a wedding venue (learned there was market demand after temporarily Airbnb-ing the home while relocating for a family medical need, and several renters used it for small weddings); would require rezoning to commercial and moving out first.
- RV parks: roughly another "seven figures" of net worth, concentrated in the Blue Metric partnership described above.
- Angel investing in small tech deals: under 1% of net worth.
- The rest sits in his own operating companies — he says he deliberately assigns conservative valuations to his own equity since paper valuations have previously evaporated or been overstated.
- Overall net worth grew "double digits... call it 30-40%" in 2024 (his own rough estimate; states he only checks a net-worth spreadsheet "once every couple years" and doesn't track it closely).

## Content/audience growth numbers and tactics (relevant to anyone building an audience-driven side hustle)
- Grew from ~60,000 to 1.2 million followers across platforms in 2024; content viewed over 500 million times for the year; in November 2024 alone: ~77 million people reached, +270,000 followers, and averaging 8,000-10,000 new followers/day combined across platforms.
- Explicitly did NOT bootstrap new platforms off an existing audience — started from scratch on short-form video and published 40-50 videos that got almost no views before finding traction.
- States a specific content funnel strategy: use short-form video (TikTok/Instagram Reels) purely as top-of-funnel discovery, then funnel engaged viewers into long-form content (podcast/YouTube/newsletter) where deeper trust and "execution credibility" (not just ideas) gets built — this is why he thinks short-form creators who have "nothing interesting to say after second 61" fail to convert.
- Believes short-form video is one of the most underpriced ("asymmetric") opportunities available right now for building a following cheaply.
- Personal goal for 2025: reduce his own internet/screen usage by ~80% by age 40, framing constant online availability as the real "downside" of a content-driven business, contrasted with the historical convenience of desktop-bound internet access.
- Mentions plans to begin monetizing content more directly going forward (had started a bit a month prior to this recording) and a longer-term interest in possibly pivoting into nonprofit/faith-based content in a few years.

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly states "none of this is investment advice" and that his own stock-picking approach (impulsive, no research) is not something he's recommending others copy.
- RV park investing at scale is explicitly flagged as harder than expected on the operations side, prompting an intentional slowdown in acquisitions — a caution against assuming real estate roll-ups scale painlessly.
- Converting a personal residence into a commercial wedding venue requires rezoning and moving out — a multi-year, uncertain regulatory path, not a quick pivot.
- The tree-business bootcamp, while valuable to some attendees, was "super stressful" to run in-person and didn't generate much direct revenue for him — a caution about the operational cost of hosting live cohort-based education products.
