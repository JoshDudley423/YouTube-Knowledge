# This AI Strategy Will Change How You Work Forever
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9-cVF9QHwo | Published: 2025-01-15

Idea-brainstorm episode with recurring guest "Sam" (part 2 of a series). Despite the title, most of the substance is about home-service maintenance/concierge subscription models, viral-content arbitrage, and AI/GPT-wrapper app ideas — not a single coherent "AI strategy."

## Idea 1: Cheap recurring "priority/insurance" plan for home service businesses (tree trimming example, generalizable)
- Concept: charge homeowners a small annual fee (e.g., **$50/year**) that guarantees priority service if something happens (a storm-damaged branch, etc.) — even if the plan itself is barely profitable or loses money.
- Real value isn't the plan revenue — it's the **retention/top-of-mind effect**: paying customers become far more likely to recall and recommend you when a relevant need comes up, vs. non-payers who forget you exist among all the vendors competing for their attention.
- Layer 2 (upsell mechanism): offer **quarterly in-person inspection visits** included in or added to the plan. This isn't really about inspection value — it's a recurring, non-awkward excuse to be on-site and pitch additional paid work (e.g., "your mulch looks old, we can redo it for $400 tomorrow").
- Real-world caveat/anecdote (host's own experience with a septic company): host was pitched a $200/year maintenance plan, declined for 8 years, then had a $1,600 break — breakeven math didn't favor the plan, but he was pressured into buying afterward, and the "quarterly visit" turned into a recurring quote/upsell mechanism worth roughly ~$1,000/year in extra revenue per customer, which the host frames as "genius" business design but also feels is a bit of a "scam" from the buyer's point of view.
- Key execution point: the first 1-2 visits should NOT include any upsell pitch — build goodwill first; upselling too early breaks the illusion and customers see through it ("oh, I see what this is").
- Framing: this is the physical-world equivalent of paying for a Facebook/Instagram ad — you're paying (or charging) to be higher on the customer's mental priority list, competing against every other vendor for top-of-mind space.

## Idea 2: "Home concierge" / maintenance middleman subscription (not tied to any specific trade)
- Concept: a subscription service ($0/year or a paid tier, e.g., ~$5/year mentioned as one variant) where a company/person visits a home quarterly, does a basic walkthrough (check for pests, tree/roof issues, general condition), and leaves a note proving the visit happened.
- You don't need to be a tradesperson yourself — you act purely as a **referral/middleman**, farming every actual job out to a network of vetted vendor partners (plumbers, HVAC, landscapers, etc.).
- Monetization options discussed: (a) charge homeowners a nominal membership fee (e.g., $5/year) that unlocks a 20% discount on any service booked through your vetted partner network; (b) mark up vendor pricing when you broker the job (example given: vendor quotes $250 for a job, you quote the customer $350 and keep the spread); (c) put the homeowner's credit card on file and control billing/AR, capturing card-processing float/points.
- Avoid "home warranty" framing — it has a scammy connotation; "concierge" is suggested as better positioning.
- Naming idea mentioned in passing: a broader "family concierge" service (~$99/month) that handles any personal-assistant-style task via text (scheduling dry cleaning, restaurant reservations, finding a gardener, etc.) by farming tasks out to a vendor network — same underlying middleman model applied beyond home maintenance.
- Automation/scaling idea: standardize the quarterly visit into a structured multi-page checklist/report (like an apartment move-in/move-out condition report), potentially AI-assisted (e.g., auto-generate lawn-care tips via ChatGPT based on observed conditions) to make visits fast, repeatable, and low-cost to deliver at scale.
- Comparable existing model referenced: host has done a similar "quarterback" consulting model digitally — auditing a client's marketing channels and then referring them to vetted specialist vendors (e.g., PPC agencies) rather than doing the execution himself, monetizing on trusted-filter expertise rather than labor.

## Idea 3: Turning viral DIY/recipe content into sellable digital/physical products (a content arbitrage play)
- Observation: DIY craft videos (example: a woman making a realistic papier-mâché "moon" lamp from household scraps) rack up tens of millions of views but the creator often isn't monetizing beyond small (~2%) Amazon affiliate links.
- Proposed play: identify a viral how-to video with clear product-market signal (high view/like count = proven demand), use ChatGPT to convert the demonstrated technique into a detailed step-by-step written guide/plan (iteratively prompt it to elaborate section by section until it's a full multi-page PDF), then package and sell that guide as a digital product on Etsy (mentioned price point: **$19.95**).
- Suggested creator partnership approach: reach out to the original creator (via any platform where they'll respond, since viral creators are flooded with DMs and often ignore repeat/copy-paste messages, but do respond on some platform) and offer a revenue share (mentioned: **$5 per sale**, or 25% of sales) in exchange for them re-promoting the content with a link in bio or a ManyChat automation directing to your Etsy listing.
- Tactical note: creators can safely repost old content (a "safe" full repost, not a boosted/paid repost) without penalty — most creators don't realize this, so proposing a free repost with a monetized link attached is low-friction for them.
- Tooling named: **ManyChat** for automating "comment X and I'll DM you the link" funnels on Instagram/social content (host mentions having an affiliate link/discount for ManyChat).
- Suggested "reset the algorithm" tactic: use Instagram's algorithm-reset feature and deliberately train a fresh account (via searches, bookmarks, likes) to surface a specific content niche (e.g., DIY crafts) to make sourcing viral source-videos systematic. Host describes running 3 separate Instagram accounts each trained toward different content niches for idea-sourcing purposes.
- Etsy execution tips: seed initial reviews (e.g., have friends/family purchase and review) before running paid ads to the new listing; use the original viral video prominently in the Etsy listing.
- Applies the same framework to viral cooking/recipe videos (e.g., air-fryer recipes) — proposed building a compiled digital cookbook, niche content site, or simple iOS/software app around a specific viral recipe niche, again without needing the original creator's active participation, just credit and optional revenue share.
- General thesis: "the market is already telling you what it wants" via view/like counts — no separate demand research needed, just execution speed.

## Idea 4: Simple, narrow "ChatGPT wrapper" apps targeting non-technical audiences
- Core insight: the average consumer doesn't distinguish between "an app built on GPT" and "a custom AI app" — most people don't know or care that a niche app (e.g., plant identification) is just a GPT wrapper; competitor apps of this type reportedly continue doing well ("doing just fine") without adding real technical differentiation.
- Anti-pattern named: dismissing GPT-wrapper apps as too simple to be a real business is "selection bias" from being in tech-savvy circles (Twitter/early-adopter bubble) — the mass market (the "crossing the chasm" majority) hasn't heard of or doesn't use tools like ChatGPT/Perplexity, so a narrow branded wrapper still has a large addressable, underserved audience.
- Concrete example: host used a free 7-day-trial plant-ID app, identified a tree, then deleted it rather than pay — yet notes these paid plant-ID apps are still commercially successful because most users never think to just ask ChatGPT for free.
- Suggested untapped idea (host says "steal my idea"): a Bible-study-companion GPT wrapper app (working name "PSTR"/"Pastor," vowels removed) — conversational AI "pastor" you can discuss scripture with, that remembers notes/questions you've logged and resurfaces related past notes months later (e.g., "you took a similar note on John chapter 7"), with add-on features like daily accountability check-ins ("did you pray today?").
- Revenue framing: target is NOT big-ticket sales — model is a low, mass-market annual subscription (mentioned: **$60-70/year**) at scale (e.g., "1,000 customers at $69.99/year = ~$70,000 recurring revenue") rather than expensive B2B contracts.
- Related host asset mentioned: an Instagram account ("Send Prayers") with 13,000 followers built around posting Christian music content, used as a warm audience for this kind of niche religious-app idea.
- Suggested cheap validation tactic: compare Google Trends search volume for "ChatGPT" vs. "Perplexity" to illustrate that most consumers have never heard of tools tech insiders consider ubiquitous — meaning a "Perplexity wrapper" targeted at the mass market is still a largely unexploited niche, since ordinary consumers ("the guy bagging your groceries") have zero awareness of the underlying tech and just want the specific narrow function solved (e.g., identifying tomatoes in a garden).

## Idea 5 (mostly ethically-flagged, less actionable): "Placebo" branded products
- Springboard fact cited: studies reportedly show placebo effects hold even in orthopedic knee surgery (fake surgery, real recovery) and even in "open-label" placebo trials where patients are told outright they're receiving a placebo and still improve.
- Host floats (explicitly labeling it ethically questionable, not something he's done) the idea of giving one of his kids a sugar pill framed as "medication that helps with focus," inspired by the placebo research, and separately floats a commercial "Placebo Adderall" product: a sugar pill marketed and sold explicitly disclosed as a placebo, but leaning on cited placebo research to imply it still "works." Estimated economics: COGS near-zero (a few dollars, mostly packaging/shipping if shipped flat in an envelope under 1/4 inch to qualify for stamp-rate shipping), suggested retail price ~$28.
- Comparable existing product referenced as a real-world precedent for "marketing over efficacy": Tabs (a chocolate marketed with a suggestive/aphrodisiac framing) — host frames this as good marketing even if the product's actual effect is placebo-driven.
- Legal caution flagged in the discussion: using a trademarked name like "Adderall" directly would create trademark/legal exposure; must be clearly disclosed as a placebo product to avoid deceptive-marketing liability.
- No concrete go-to-market plan given for this one — it's presented as a "thinking out loud" idea, and the hosts explicitly debate whether pursuing it (especially aimed at children) would be unethical.
