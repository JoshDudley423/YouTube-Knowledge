# 4 Business Ideas You Can Start with $0 in 2026
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_Xs6bcP1JY | Published: 2024-10-19

"HoldCo Bros" brainstorming episode (Chris Koerner + Nik) covering four distinct ideas: Instagram Close Friends monetization, follower-scraping as a service, negative-COGS product acquisition, a real lake-weed-removal business case study, and a "top 10 home service businesses for viral time-lapse marketing" list.

## Idea 1: Selling "Close Friends" setup as a service to Instagram influencers/business accounts
- Instagram's "Close Friends" feature lets an account prioritize story visibility to a curated list — stories to Close Friends show at the front of followers' story queue and reportedly increase story view engagement 3-6x (per host's research on forums/message boards; some large accounts like Rihanna/Kim Kardashian allegedly paid to have this set up at scale).
- Business idea: build/hire someone to build the custom code needed to add an account's entire follower list (tens of thousands to millions) to Close Friends in bulk (this isn't natively supported by Instagram at scale), then sell this as a one-time flat-fee service to Instagram accounts that monetize (influencers, personal brands, even a local restaurant with 30,000 followers running story promotions).
- Cost to build the automation was quoted to the host at $30-50 (one-time, via Upwork); resale price suggested: $100-$1,000 flat fee per client, "build it once, sell it a thousand times."
- Host's own data point: a story with a lead-magnet call-to-action (a scraped list he gave away) reached ~23,000 views (~10% of his follower base) and converted to 1,500-2,000 email subscribers; extrapolated that having all followers in Close Friends could have yielded 3-6x more views and subscribers.
- Target customer: any Instagram account with a monetizable audience/product — framed as a large and underexploited market given how many "micro-businesses" now exist purely via social audiences (a distribution mechanism that didn't exist pre-social-media).

## Idea 2: Follower-scraping / audience-profiling as a service
- Problem: Instagram's API restrictions make it hard to get a full list of your own followers with contact details; consumer Chrome extensions throttle/cut off scraping after ~1,000-2,000 profiles.
- Host paid a freelancer on Upwork $100 to manually scrape all ~230,000 of his followers into a spreadsheet over 2 days — captured first name, last name, username, profile URL, follow status, verification status, phone number (if listed), email (if listed), location (if listed), and website (if listed).
- Value: roughly 10% of followers list a phone number or email in their public profile — for the host, that's ~20,000 usable contacts. These aren't opted into a newsletter, but can be cold-emailed with a low-pressure opt-in pitch ("you follow me on Instagram, I send a free weekly business idea, want to subscribe?"), plausibly converting 10-20% into confirmed subscribers.
- Additional use case: identifying high-follower-count accounts (100K+) within your own follower base for potential collaboration outreach — "it's not who you know, it's who knows you."
- Service pitch to sell to influencers/brands: scrape their followers for a fee (e.g., $500 bundled with the Close Friends setup, or sold separately), and use an AI-in-spreadsheet tool (e.g., an OpenAI Google Sheets extension) to auto-flag which followers look interesting/relevant for collaboration based on the client's business description — since most clients won't otherwise know what to do with a raw list.
- Adjacent framing/expansion ideas raised (not fully fleshed out): building an automated "audience profile kit" (like a media kit) for influencers to hand to brands seeking ad placements, showing follower demographics/pricing per post; or helping brands qualify which influencer's follower base best matches their target customer before signing a sponsorship deal.

## Idea 3: Negative-COGS businesses built on waste/byproduct streams
- Concept (referenced from a prior episode): building a business where you get paid to acquire your raw "product" (waste material) rather than paying for inventory — i.e., negative cost of goods sold.
- Trigger for this discussion: a listener in the pallet-recycling/waste industry cold-messaged the host, revealing that one of his customers spends roughly $30,000/week (~$120,000/month) on pallets alone to ship out vegetables that are edible but unsellable (excess/near-expiry produce, thousands of tons headed to landfill).
- Reference case study: EcoScraps (Provo, UT) — got paid by grocery stores to haul away old/unsellable produce, mixed it with sawdust in a warehouse, shredded it, composted it for about 3 months, bagged it, and sold it to Home Depot at $5/bag. Was eventually acquired by a large company (Central Garden & Pet, via its "TruGreen"/"Kellogg Garden" brand referenced in the video as "Kemlon"/TruGreen). Their pitch to grocery stores undercut existing trash-hauling costs (e.g., offered to do it for $1,500/month vs. the store's existing $2,000/month trash bill) while still being profitable because they were also being paid indirectly via the compost sales.
- Similar historical example mentioned: a company that collected discarded pulp from cold-press juice shops for free and turned it into fertilizer.
- Operational reality check/caveat: this model requires significant capital equipment — an industrial-grade food shredder (expensive), a truck, and a warehouse; without shredding, waste must sit and decompose for ~3 years instead of ~3 months, making shredding essential infrastructure, not optional.
- No idea was executed on-air; framed purely as a live brainstorm based on an inbound tip, with an open invitation to listeners to pursue it.

## Idea 4: Case study — lake/pond weed removal business (real example, not a brainstorm)
- Two 19-year-old college friends (Chicago-area, attending University of Nebraska and University of Missouri) started by doing paid dock installation for lakefront neighbors (~$25/hour) during a summer home from school.
- Pivoted into removing lake weeds/algae by hand after a neighbor asked for help — used simple leaf rakes, standing waist-deep in water, physically pulling waterlogged seaweed onto the dock/yard; extremely labor-intensive (hours of manual work), no chemicals or machinery.
- Pricing power: charged around $500-$700 for a morning's work; demand grew purely through neighbors seeing the visible before/after result and asking for the same service — "no one says no."
- Differentiation vs. existing competitors: existing lake-cleaning companies use chemicals that kill weeds but don't remove them (lake still looks ugly, weeds just stop spreading) — this chemical-free, fully manual removal was a distinct, more effective offering.
- Growth via content: posted time-lapse-style before/after videos to Instagram/TikTok, building 10,000-30,000+ followers, with some individual videos reaching 1-2 million views due to the highly visual nature of the transformation.
- Investor/equipment break: a wealthy business owner (~63, runs a large forklift/equipment business) discovered them via TikTok, related to their hustle, and offered to personally buy and insure a specialized ~$100,000 lake-weed-removal boat, then rent it to them at a fair rate — dramatically increasing their operating capacity/efficiency without them needing to raise the capital themselves.
- Result at time of filming: more business than they can handle.
- Host's structural observation: extremely labor-intensive, unglamorous, manual "sweaty" businesses tend to be durable moats precisely because few competitors want to do them (references the 1-800-GOT-JUNK founder's line: "as long as pianos weigh 800 lbs, there will always be a moving business").
- Suggested next steps for the operators (unexecuted, host's advice): franchise or license the business model using their existing social media audience as a distribution/sales channel for franchisees, or sell an SOP-based course, rather than only running it locally themselves. Business is somewhat seasonal and geography/weed-species dependent but reportedly works in most regions to varying degrees.

## Idea 5: "Top 10" list of home-service businesses best suited to viral time-lapse/before-after video marketing
- Core thesis: if you want to start a "sweaty" local service business with no marketing budget, choose one from this list specifically because the before/after transformation format has proven high retention and shareability on social video (working backward from what the algorithm/audience already rewards, to pick the business).
- The ten (in the order discussed): pressure washing (especially dirty rugs/carpets), pool cleaning (especially draining/pressure-washing a pool), home/hoarder-house cleaning, tree/hedge trimming (hedge trimming for visual satisfaction; tree removal via chainsaw specifically for suspense/retention as viewers wait to see where a cut limb lands), painting (including a specific viral sub-niche: painting brick houses white/black, which the host notes is highly polarizing online because many older homeowners believe painting brick causes structural/moisture problems — that controversy itself drives engagement), landscaping/lawn mowing (especially removing dirt overgrowth onto sidewalks), window washing, gutter cleaning, and carpet cleaning (host's personal favorite format).
- Distribution logic: post this content not just to build a national following but to your business's Google Business Profile — eye-catching video content there both draws attention on Google Maps searches and builds local trust/legitimacy ("I've seen his work, I'll hire him"), which is often more valuable than viral reach for actually converting local customers.
- Tactical content-acquisition idea: offer to film a small local business's work for free (backyard cleanup, painting, etc.) in exchange for using the footage on your own channel — "let me put a tripod here, I'll give you a copy" — a way to source content without waiting to build your own service business first.
- Alternate monetization path suggested (beyond running the service business yourself): build a YouTube/social channel purely around one of these visually engaging niches, capture massive traffic/leads, and instead of only running your own local service business, sell the resulting leads to an existing national franchisor in that space (e.g., a pressure-washing franchise brand) — a franchisee lead was estimated to be worth $10,000-$50,000, potentially more lucrative than selling an info-course off the same audience.

## Caveats
- The Close Friends/follower-scraping automation methods rely on custom code/workarounds against Instagram's API restrictions and platform limitations — described as difficult to find providers for and not officially supported functionality.
- The negative-COGS pallet/produce idea is a live inbound brainstorm, not a validated or executed business — no confirmed numbers beyond the pallet spend figure, and the tipster's identity/company was not disclosed.
- The lake-weed-removal case study is real but success depended partly on a lucky, unsolicited $100K equipment investment from a stranger — not a guaranteed or typical outcome.
