# How to Launch and Grow a $4m/Year Stump Grinding Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gr5fozH3l3g | Published: 2024-07-10

"HoldCo Bros" episode: Chris Koerner and Nik discuss testing a B2B stump-grinding service as an add-on to their existing tree-trimming business (marketing-heavy, operationally-light model: they own a truck, use an operating partner ("James") to do actual work, no warehouse/employees/chainsaws). Note (per video update mentioned in description): this idea was later actually launched and did $32K in its first two months — see other episodes in the "Stump Grinding Business" playlist.

## The business idea
- **B2B stump grinding as a service to other tree/landscaping companies**, not to consumers. Most tree-removal companies don't own a stump grinder (renting one is inefficient/a hassle), so they treat stump removal as a break-even "loss leader" baked into their tree-removal quote. The pitch: be the reliable specialist sub that tree companies call for grinding, so they don't have to rent equipment themselves.
- Modeled loosely on Patch Boys/Patch Brothers, a drywall-patching franchise built by Aaron Harper (CEO of Rolling Suds) that grew via B2B referrals (handymen/contractors referring drywall work) rather than direct-to-consumer marketing — cited as proof that a narrow, single-service niche business can scale via B2B referral relationships.
- Considered variant: turn it into a pure marketing/lead-gen agency for existing stump-grinding-capable companies (agency doesn't buy equipment or take on capital risk, just supplies leads and takes a cut/fee) — described as lower risk, lower upside, more scalable than owning the equipment.
- Adjacent idea discussed at length: a **market-research/site-selection service** for small business owners considering a second location (e.g., laundromats, restaurants, addiction-recovery centers) — see "steps" section below.

## Concrete numbers
- Market size estimate (DFW, pop. 9M): ~1,000 tree-trimming businesses + ~2,000-3,000 landscaping businesses ≈ 3,000 total addressable B2B customers for stump grinding.
- Equipment cost: stump grinders range $4,000-$150,000; a solid working unit ~$40,000-$60,000. Add a $5,000-$10,000 trailer + truck. Total buy-in to own one crew's equipment ≈ $75,000. Renting a $50,000 grinder costs about $3,000/month (option to test before buying).
- Test/validation: cold-texted 200 tree-business owners with a direct pitch ("Do you own your own stump grinder or rent/sub it out? I only do stump grinding for tree businesses in DFW — any need for reliable subs?"). Got only a 15% response rate (~30 people) — lower than expected because many companies already own a grinder or rent. A more strategic (two-step) text approach was suggested in hindsight: first ask "Do you do stump grinding?" (expected 40-60% response), then follow up with the pitch separately.
- Pricing for stump grinding jobs: ~$350 average per stump (an ~18-inch stump); price scales roughly $10/inch of stump diameter; multiple stumps at one site are cheaper per-stump (e.g., 2 stumps ~$500 total) since travel cost is already sunk; add-on pricing for removing wood chips, and further add-on for filling the hole with soil.
- Financial model at 5% share of the 3,000-business DFW market (150 companies referring work): 150 companies x ~7 stump jobs/month x $350/job = $367,500/month revenue ≈ $4.4M/year.
  - That implies ~40 grinding jobs/working day; each job takes as little as ~20-30 minutes to grind once on site.
  - Staffing: 8 one-person crews (each with truck + grinder + trailer) + 1 manager = 9 people. Generous pay assumption: crew ~$80K/year each, manager ~$200K/year → ~$1.2M/year total labor (incl. payroll costs).
  - Other costs: ~$200K/year gas, ~$200K/year repairs/insurance/central storage for backup equipment. Total costs ≈ $1.8M/year.
  - Implied profit at 5% market share ≈ $2.6M/year — but requires ~$600,000 of upfront capex just to buy 8 stump grinders (crews built up over time, not day one).
- Reference laundromat economics (tangent from a separate podcast guest, Tyler Facel): average laundromat revenue ~$785K/year (his own locations; can reach ~$1.2M after 5-year stabilization), with ~50% net operating margin — cited as surprisingly high for what looks like simple real estate/equipment.

## Process / steps described
- **Validating a home-service niche idea**: don't guess — do direct outreach (cold text/call) to a meaningful sample of the target business type and ask a qualifying question before pitching. Referenced a rule of thumb from a market-research contact: ~387 respondents is the statistically meaningful minimum sample size to be confident in a signal (confidence interval gains taper sharply after that point); 400 was treated as a good practical target for a genuinely validating test.
- **On-site sales workaround for "must quote in person" services**: because tree companies already have a rep/sales person on-site quoting the tree removal, that existing salesperson can take stump measurements (tape measure or a photo/app) and quote the grinding job on your behalf using your set pricing — so you never have to visit the site yourself before doing the work. This halves logistics/driving.
- **Culturally-matched cold outreach idea**: for a target list skewing Hispanic-surnamed business owners, hire a cheap Spanish-speaking VA/cold caller (sourced via Upwork, from a Latin American country) who calls in Spanish or English depending on the prospect, pitches as a "co-owner," and is compensated with a small equity/commission stake (e.g., 1% of the business) plus fair market rate — theory being this increases both credibility and pricing flexibility with either audience segment.
- **Adjacent "site-selection / competitive market analysis" service** (a distinct sellable idea raised in the same conversation): use tools like Outscraper to pull a list of local competitor businesses (e.g., all addiction-treatment centers, restaurants of a given type) in a market, map them, and identify underserved geographic gaps ("holes in the market") for a business considering a second location. One-off real example: did this analysis for a friend running a single-location addiction-recovery center in ~18 minutes using Outscraper + a Loom walkthrough, found an underserved area (Allen, TX) — delivered free but noted it "could have been worth $1,000." Proposed pricing: $500-$1,500+ per report depending on scope (location analysis alone vs. + keyword/demand analysis). Target customer: businesses with exactly 1-2 existing locations (proven concept, actively considering expansion) — not first-timers, not big chains/franchises (who do site selection in-house). Suggested selling this analysis "white-labeled" through commercial real estate brokers who need site-selection data for their restaurant/retail clients, charging ~$5,000/report, broker marks it up to their client.

## Tools/platforms named
- Outscraper (scraping local business/competitor data)
- Google Maps (visually identifying business clusters, e.g. "zombie laundromats")
- Loom (recording walkthrough deliverables for clients)
- Texas DOT website (public traffic-count data, useful for site-selection: e.g. preferring a 30,000-count street over 10,000)
- Upwork (sourcing cheap international cold-callers/VAs)

## Caveats / risks
- The self-described test result (15% response rate on 200 texts) was "not as good as I thought" — the direct-pitch-first approach (rather than a qualifying question first) likely suppressed response rate.
- No stump grinding or tree-removal work can be quoted purely virtually/remotely — in-person (or an on-site partner) is required because size/scope is hard to judge from photos/FaceTime alone; the workaround is piggybacking on an already-present salesperson rather than skipping in-person quoting altogether.
- Classic "why hasn't anyone done this already" risk flagged explicitly: answer given is that stump-grinding businesses do exist, but they mostly sell direct-to-consumer (Facebook Marketplace) rather than running a dedicated B2B-referral model targeting tree/landscaping companies — the B2B angle is the perceived unexploited niche, not the service itself.
- The market-research/site-selection service is explicitly called "not recurring" — it's project-based/consulting-like unless the client keeps growing and reordering reports, which is a downside versus a recurring-revenue model.
- Handing a business owner their most important decision (where to locate/expand) to a third party carries real risk if the analysis is wrong; better suited to being an internal service, a private-equity/family-office roll-up tool, or embedded in another business's process (e.g., broker) than sold as a stand-alone advisory to a single independent operator making a one-time bet.
