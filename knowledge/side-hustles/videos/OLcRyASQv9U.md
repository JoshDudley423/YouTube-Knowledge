# You'll Never Guess How Profitable this Seasonal Business Is...
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OLcRyASQv9U | Published: 2024-10-02

Interview with Heather Torres, founder of Porch Pumpkins (Dallas-Fort Worth) — a seasonal service that delivers, arranges, and later removes decorative pumpkin displays on customers' porches for Halloween/fall.

## The idea
- Service: pre-set package options (not fully custom) where customers pick a package based on price point/porch size; team delivers, artfully arranges ("stacks") pumpkins on the porch, and later (Nov 1 - Dec 3) offers pickup/removal.
- Started as one Instagram post in 2020 offering friends 50% off in exchange for using their porches as portfolio photos; grew via word of mouth and one share from an ~80K-follower Instagram account, which brought in strangers as customers.
- Origin insight: pumpkins rot faster in landscaping/soil than sitting on a porch — informed both the branding ("Porch Pumpkins") and the value proposition.
- No prior competitor existed doing exactly this; founder considers herself the inventor of the category. By 2022, copycat businesses began appearing in the same DFW market and elsewhere; she treats this as validation/flattery rather than a threat, comparing it to lawn care or Christmas light install businesses that also have many local operators.

## Numbers (year-over-year order volume)
- 2020 (season 1): originally expected ~35 houses, ended up doing ~250.
- Year 2: 500. Year 3: 650. Year 4 (per public reporting): 750. Year 5: 850. Year 6 (current, as of Oct 2024): 1,034 orders.
- Revenue not stated directly, but implied via package pricing (below) and volume.
- Profit margins: ~40-45% net (not gross) depending on season. Season 1 was ~50% net margin because she and her husband personally did ~90% of the labor. Two years prior to this interview, margin dipped to ~35% during a period of rapid headcount/scale growth; efficiencies (sourcing, routing) since then improved it.
- Example single-day design-fee earnings: she personally designs multiple houses/day at $75/design fee each; one viral-video day she personally designed 21 of 50 total houses done that day, netting $1,500 in design fees alone for herself; team-wide "hard $10,000 day" referenced as a peak revenue day (in design fees).

## Pricing
- Four package tiers. Package 4 (the "cash cow," lowest price point) = $325 base + $75 optional design add-on + $5 optional end-of-season pickup. This is the best seller (~420 of 1,033 orders that season) — appeals to budget-conscious customers and smaller porches/narrower walkways that can't fit a bigger package.
- Package 1 (highest tier) priced up to ~$1,350; sold most heavily in wealthy zip codes (Highland Park, Preston Hollow, Dallas — homes often $1M-$10M+). These 5-6 zip codes account for ~40% of total business.
- Average customer overall is estimated to live in a $650K-$800K home, not the ultra-high-end segment.
- Pricing has risen over time (original package price was $250, now $325) due to inflation on pumpkin costs and labor as the business scaled and needed paid staff instead of just founder/spouse labor.
- Key pricing lesson (from own experience and cited from a hairstylist friend): raising prices when fully booked filters out the bottom ~85-90% price-sensitive tier while retaining the top 10%, effectively managing capacity via price rather than turning away business outright. Underpricing out of fear/insecurity is called a common, correctable mistake — "don't cut yourself short."

## Process / staffing model
- Deliberately built as a flexible-schedule job model designed for stay-at-home moms: designers submit their available hours/days (e.g., can't work during kids' games, or only mornings before school pickup), and the schedule is built around them.
- Season 1 ended with 5 paid staff + 2 unpaid (founder + husband); by the time of this interview the team had grown to 31 people, including dedicated warehouse staff (3 in Dallas, 2 in Houston).
- Creative/artistic hires ("moms with creative backgrounds") pick up the design method almost immediately (as fast as 30 minutes of training); non-creative but motivated hires take longer (~3 hours across 5-6 practice porches).
- Operational turning point for scaling: moving from manual order-taking (phone calls, texts, a physical notebook, manual invoicing until midnight) to a proper website in year 2, enabling pre-orders, zip-code/week capacity tracking, and route planning.
- Delivery logistics: initially delivered/designed personally out of her own SUV; added a trailer (driven by husband after his day job) once volume exceeded personal capacity.
- End-of-season pickup: ~65-70% of customers opt into paid pickup service (starts Nov 1, typically wrapped by Dec 1-3). Picked-up pumpkins are donated to a charity farm that feeds them to livestock (pigs raised for bacon) and uses straw for animal bedding — a partnership, not a landfill outcome.

## Market context / demand drivers
- Halloween/fall spending is a large and growing retail category, comparable in scale to Christmas for some retailers — appeals to a broader demographic than religious holidays and is amplified by parents wanting to decorate for kids.
- Recession sensitivity: growth slowed somewhat in a softer economy — sold out on Aug 15 the prior year but had to keep taking orders until Sept 7 in the year of this interview to hit volume targets; average order value held steady because roughly equal numbers of customers "packaged up" and "packaged down." Business is viewed as resilient to recession because the core, high-value customer segment (top earners) is less price-sensitive, similar to luxury retail (analogized to Neiman Marcus).
- Considered risk: IP theft — competitors have screenshotted and reused her copyrighted photos/logo as if it were their own work; she has trademarked/copyrighted her branding and photos and has had to send legal letters/involve a lawyer.
- Exploring a Christmas-season revenue extension via partnering with an existing product/service provider rather than building her own Christmas decorating service (she doesn't want to overextend given the pumpkin season already runs through Dec 1-3, leaving little recovery time before a new holiday push).
- Suggested (by interviewer) but not yet implemented: using AI tools (e.g., ComfyUI) to let customers upload a photo of their own porch and preview a design before ordering.

## Caveats
- Very labor-intensive, physically demanding work (lifting 30-70 lb pumpkins repeatedly for long hours) — not a passive/low-effort business despite appearing simple.
- Requires local density/warehouse space and enough population of higher-income customers to be viable at scale; her densest zip codes are affluent Dallas suburbs.
- Margins were much higher when done solo (50%) vs. at scale with a 31-person team (40-45%), illustrating a real scale/margin tradeoff.
- Growth rate decelerated after the initial post-launch surge (roughly +20% orders per year in recent years rather than continued doubling).
