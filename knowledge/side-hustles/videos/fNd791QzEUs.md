# How to Make Millions with a Remote Cleaning Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNd791QzEUs | Published: 2024-09-23

Interview with Johnny Robinson (founder of Orange Window Cleaning; now runs Home Service Academy, teaching remote house-cleaning business startup).

## Business ideas discussed
- **Remote house cleaning business** run with independent contractor cleaners (not employees), managed remotely rather than in the field — the core model Home Service Academy teaches.
- Contrast case: **window cleaning business** (his first business) — he argues this model is *harder* to replicate/teach broadly because it requires more specialized equipment (water-fed poles, deionized water tanks, trucks, ladders) and a smaller pool of available contractors than house cleaning, which only needs basic supplies anyone can get at Home Depot — so contractor recruiting via Indeed works far better for cleaning than window washing.
- **Tangential/adjacent opportunity flagged as hot right now**: epoxy garage-floor flooring — cited as a booming home-service niche (high ticket, ~$4K jobs), though he flags it lacks recurring revenue and could be recession-vulnerable since it's not a "must-have" purchase.
- **AI answering-service idea** (from a landscaping franchise owner, Mike Andes/Augusta Lawn Care, not yet publicly released at time of recording): using an AI phone system to guarantee 24/7 call answering and instant job booking, with a customer guarantee of free service if the business ever fails to answer the phone — pitched as broadly applicable across all home-service verticals.
- **"Backend/Ascension" consulting business idea** (brainstormed live with host): most high-ticket course/coaching sellers ($2K+) can generate front-end sales but fail to build a backend (a year-long mastermind/extended coaching tier) that extends customer LTV — an opportunity to be a specialized consultant/CSM-for-hire who builds these backend "ascension" systems and journeys for course creators, analogous to how Gym Launch (Alex Hormozi) achieved a ~$47K customer LTV vs. ~$4K for typical gym-coaching competitors.

## Concrete numbers
- Window cleaning business: grew to ~9 full-time employees before shifting to contractors; still ranks #1 in Orange County's Google map pack for window cleaning years after selling. Sold for "a little over $100K" — buyer put ~$50K down with the rest seller-financed over 12 months. The buyer (a former employee-turned-contractor) has since grown it to ~$65K/month average revenue (best month $70K), over $1M/year.
- Home Service Academy (the coaching/course product): pre-sold 15 programs at $4,200 each = $50K seed money. They sat on that $50K for 3 months to prove out results with early cleaning-business customers before selling more. From that ~$50K seed (April) to December of the same year, scaled to **$2.8M revenue / $1.5M profit**.
- Test-clean "swindle" rate: roughly **50% of contractor applicants**, when secret-shopper tested, will try to poach the customer directly (offer to do the job cheaper off the books) rather than refer them back to the company — used as a hard screening filter.
- Cold-call/cold-contact response-rate benchmark cited twice in the conversation: roughly **80% of small home-service businesses (e.g., cleaners, tree trimmers) don't even answer their phone** when called — cited as evidence the competitive bar in home services is very low.
- One cautionary case: an aspiring operator scaled to ~$100K/month largely on a single large Airbnb/short-term-rental portfolio client, then was fired by that one whale client (grew too fast / service quality dropped) and cratered to ~$10K/month — illustrates customer concentration risk.
- Course-creator/mastermind cost context: host mentions paying $54K/year for a high-ticket mastermind (Cole Gordon's "8-Figure Boardroom" via Closers.io) — valued mainly for networking/relationships, not the content itself.

## Process / steps described
- **Core thesis**: a house-cleaning business is fundamentally a staffing/recruiting business, not a cleaning business — solve hiring well and most other problems (quality, reviews, map-pack ranking, referrals, recurring clients) resolve themselves.
- **Hiring funnel**: post on Indeed (templated job post language provided by Home Service Academy) to generate a flood of applicants; expect to reject ~80% and only 20% pass; require candidates to have 1+ year cleaning experience, their own transportation, and their own cleaning equipment; run a background check.
- **"Test clean" screening technique**: send a finalist candidate to clean a friend/family member's house for free, presenting it to the cleaner as a real paying job. The tester is instructed to ask "how can I book again?" at the end — if the cleaner refers them back to the company, that's a strong pass signal; if the cleaner offers to do it privately/cheaper, that's an automatic reject (they'll try to steal customers). Roughly half of candidates fail this test. Also serves as a quality/professionalism check (one cited failure: a hired pair showed up without a vacuum and tried to sweep carpet with a broom rather than tell the customer; one of them also turned out to have an undisclosed criminal record).
- Ongoing hiring cadence framed as "hire three, fire two" — continuous churn/filtering to keep only the best cleaners.
- **Customer acquisition — early/organic, near-zero CAC method**: join local Facebook groups (mom groups, buy/sell groups, Nextdo0r/community groups); post a warm, personal self-introduction (photo, hometown details, mention of the business) rather than a hard sell; DM the 10-20 people who engage with the post; get the first customer, do an excellent job, then ask for a review posted back in that same group — triggers a "keeping up with the Joneses" referral effect as neighbors see neighbors using the service. Repeat across every local group available.
- **Scaling beyond organic (Year 2+) acquisition channel stack, ranked by Johnny's ROI preference**: (1) Google Local Service Ads (LSA) — paired with organic to reduce blended cost per lead; (2) Yelp (strong for California specifically, high commercial intent); (3) cold outreach/relationship building with real estate agents/brokers, property managers, apartment complexes, schools, and especially custom home builders (source of recurring high-ticket $2-5K construction-cleanup jobs) and owners of multiple short-term rentals (Airbnb portfolios — but see customer-concentration caveat above); (4) direct mail, once you have 1-2 years of customer data to heat-map your best-paying customer geography and target that radius specifically.
- General framing repeated twice: most home-service (and adjacent) industries have a very low competence bar — simply answering the phone and doing a "good enough" job differentiates you from a majority of competitors who don't.

## Tools/platforms named
Indeed (hiring), Facebook groups / Nextdoor (organic customer acquisition), Google Local Service Ads, Yelp, Direct Mail, Booking Koala/"Booking Quality" software (mentioned as the scheduling software their client base uses to run cleaning companies).

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly warns against calling this "passive" — states plainly that anyone marketing home-service ownership as passive/automated is misrepresenting it; it requires active management, especially of contractor relationships.
- Biggest recurring headache: contractor leverage/pickiness once you've built a team — cleaners can effectively hold the business "hostage" by refusing jobs or demanding more money; requires continuous recruiting pipeline to avoid dependency on any one cleaner.
- Host's personal experience: he tried a remote cleaning business himself and abandoned it — attributes the failure to running too many businesses simultaneously and to the accumulation of many small, "ticky-tack" customer complaints (a hair in a sink, lateness, etc.) that feel more draining than a single "pass/fail" service business, making it easy to quit when distracted by other ventures.
- Cited a serious personnel incident: two employees (not contractors) were caught doing drugs inside a customer's home (in the client's child's bathroom) — despite already having doubts about these employees, the business kept them on during a busy season, illustrating the guest's warning that "keeping an employee you already have doubts about will always screw you."
- Recommends against believing organic acquisition alone scales indefinitely — must pair with paid channels (LSA) to keep growing past the initial local-group phase.
- Epoxy flooring (the flagged adjacent opportunity) has no recurring revenue model — a "sales mode forever" business, and is viewed as more exposed to a recession since it's a discretionary/non-essential purchase.
