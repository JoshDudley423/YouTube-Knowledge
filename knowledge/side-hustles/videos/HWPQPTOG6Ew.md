# The Easiest Way to Start an Online Business (SaaS)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HWPQPTOG6Ew | Published: 2025-06-28

Chris Koerner solo episode (contains an affiliate link for HighLevel, disclosed). Core idea: white-label the HighLevel CRM/marketing platform (~$300/month wholesale) and resell it as your own branded, niche-specific SaaS product — using free/cheap tools to reverse-engineer businesses already doing this successfully.

## The business idea
- HighLevel is a white-label-friendly CRM/marketing-automation platform. Businesses license it, rebrand it entirely as their own software, and resell "sub-accounts" to end customers as if it were their own proprietary SaaS product — end customers never know HighLevel is the underlying engine.
- The play is **positioning/niching**, not building technology: instead of selling a generic CRM (competing with HubSpot/Salesforce), package the same underlying software as "CRM built specifically for [X profession]" — e.g., insurance agents, real estate agents, lawyers, veterinarians, dentists, orthodontists, podiatrists, gyms, medical clinics. A niched, positioned product beats a generic one for a specific buyer, even though the underlying tech is identical.
- Source of the idea: HighLevel publishes an awards page recognizing companies built on top of their platform (Diamond/Gold/Platinum tiers based on number of paying "sub-account" customers — 100/500/1,000+ respectively). Chris treated this awards list as a list of provable, scaled businesses to reverse-engineer.

## Real examples cited (with numbers)
- Agent CRM (insurance-agent-focused CRM): charges $97/month, reportedly 1,000+ customers (≈ $97K+/month revenue potential at that count); estimated via SimilarWeb to get 12,000-16,000 website visitors/week; back-of-envelope funnel estimate: ~60,000 visits/month → 3% to free trial (~1,800 trials/month) → 20% trial-to-paid conversion (~360 new paying customers/month) → at $100/month, ~$36,000 in new MRR added per month (a rough estimate, not confirmed revenue). Traffic sources: 82% US, 72% direct traffic (high intent — people going there on purpose, not stumbling on it), only ~1.5% paid, ~19% organic, ~6% referral — interpreted as a business driven mainly by outbound (calls, texts, emails, voice agents, ringless voicemail drops) rather than paid ads or SEO.
- Push Press Grow: gym management software, identified as a HighLevel white-label built for the gym/fitness niche.
- 7Figure Docs: serves 3,000+ medical clinics; pricing not public, but estimated via AI-assisted research (prompted to dig through Reddit/X/Facebook) at ~$1,500/month — implying a hypothetical ~$4.5M/month if fully realized at that customer count and price (company founded 2017, so this is a multi-year build, not an overnight result).
- DearDoc: reportedly serves 4,500+ doctor's offices.
- Jim Lead Machine: cited as one of America's 5,000 fastest-growing companies (per Inc. 5000-style list reference), healthcare-focused, scaling rapidly via partnerships (one HighLevel-based competitor mentioned partnering directly with a franchisor to instantly access 1,000 franchisee customers paying $1,000/month each).
- Overall framing: 67 businesses were reportedly identified this way generating between $50,000 and $500,000/month; one cited example grew from $0 to $1.8M in 11 months.
- HighLevel's own actual pricing (as of recording): roughly $97/month base; ~$300/month for white-labeling with some AI features; $500/month for unlimited white-labeled sub-accounts (i.e., unlimited resold customers) — you don't need to commit to the $500/month tier until you already have enough paying customers to justify it.

## The "reverse-engineering toolkit" (free/cheap tools + process)
1. **Meta (Facebook) Ads Library** — free. Search by company name, industry, or geography to see every ad a business is currently running. Filter to ads that have been running for months (not days/weeks) — a long-running ad is a strong signal it's actually converting/profitable ("banger ad"), since companies normally kill underperforming ads quickly. Multiple long-running ads from one advertiser signals real scale. The UK version of the Ads Library additionally discloses ad spend (EU disclosure law), unlike the US version — useful for estimating a competitor's real ad budget. Screenshot found ads and feed them to an AI agent (Manus, ChatGPT with "Operator"/agent mode, etc.) to identify what software/platform (e.g., HighLevel) the advertiser is built on, plus their pricing, website design, and — most importantly — their core offer.
2. **Wayback Machine** — free. Look up a target company's historical website snapshots to see how long they've operated, how their pricing/positioning has evolved over time (e.g., Agent CRM started at $47/month with a wordier headline, later doubled pricing and simplified messaging), and which parts of their offer have stayed constant since launch (e.g., a persistent 14-day free trial signals that offer element is core to their success, not an experiment).
3. **SimilarWeb** — free account required. Estimate a target site's weekly/monthly traffic volume and traffic-source breakdown (direct/organic/paid/referral %) to reverse-engineer their acquisition channel mix and roughly model their funnel/revenue (see Agent CRM example above).
4. **BuiltWith** — free via its Chrome extension (shows what any single website is built on: platform, CRM, payment processor pixels like Stripe/PayPal/Authorize.net, ad pixels like Facebook Pixel, indicating whether they run Facebook ads). The full paid plan (~$450/month) allows bulk-scraping thousands of sites' tech stacks + contact info to a spreadsheet — but a cheaper workaround is to post a small ($20-50) job on Upwork asking a freelancer/agency who ALREADY has a BuiltWith subscription to run and export the scrape for you (near-zero marginal cost to them, ~95% savings versus paying for a subscription yourself).
5. **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — technically paid, but offers a 30-day free trial (cancel immediately after signing up so you don't forget and get charged). Used to see what industries/company sizes a target competitor's customer base actually consists of (via mutual connections/customer lists visible through Sales Navigator's targeting data), informing your own niche messaging.

## Step-by-step launch playbook
1. Sign up for a HighLevel white-label account (via an extended 30-day free trial, vs. the standard 14-day trial elsewhere), pick an AI-generated logo and color scheme — produces a fully white-labeled product that looks like an expensive proprietary SaaS, free during the trial period.
2. Pick a niche based on data, not just guesswork: ideally something you already have some passion or experience in; alternatively, scrape the entire HighLevel awards page list of company names and ask an AI (e.g., ChatGPT) which represented industries have the least existing competition, and start there.
3. Copy (don't reinvent) a proven competitor's ad angles, pricing, offer structure, and website structure — treat this as a "good artists copy, great artists steal" exercise, since these elements have already been tested over years by the businesses you're reverse-engineering.
4. Copy their onboarding system too — welcome emails, setup calls, training materials — using AI to help draft these and the Wayback Machine to see historical versions of a competitor's onboarding/messaging.
5. Move fast because the free trial clock is running — the real research/building work should happen mostly during the 30-day trial window, not endlessly beforehand.
6. After building the offer, actively call/talk to potential customers rather than continuing to research indefinitely ("analysis paralysis") — explicitly framed as needing real sales and marketing effort, not a passive/automated income stream.

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly NOT passive income — Chris directly says "this isn't your passive income, make-money-while-you-sleep" pitch; it requires real, ongoing sales and marketing work, handling difficult clients, and things breaking, same as any business.
- The core technology is fully commoditized/replicable by design (anyone can white-label the same underlying software) — the entire competitive edge is in positioning, niche selection, offer design, and customer acquisition execution, not any technical moat.
- Revenue estimates for competitor businesses (e.g., the $36,000 MRR estimate for Agent CRM, the $4.5M/month figure for 7Figure Docs) are explicitly acknowledged as back-of-envelope estimates from public traffic/pricing data, not confirmed actual financials — treat as directional, not precise.
- Success stories cited took real time to build (e.g., 7Figure Docs since 2017, i.e., 8 years to reach its estimated scale) — the "0 to $1.8M in 11 months" example is presented as an outlier, not a guaranteed timeline.
