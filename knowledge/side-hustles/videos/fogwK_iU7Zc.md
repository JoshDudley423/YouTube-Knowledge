# How Often Would You Play the Lottery If It Were Free?
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fogwK_iU7Zc | Published: 2025-07-25

Chris Koerner (solo, workshop/webinar-style) explains his short-form-video-to-newsletter growth funnel — this is a content/audience-building playbook rather than a specific business idea, but it's the exact mechanism he uses to generate leads for his own businesses (newsletter, community, business-idea content), and is presented as replicable for any niche/business.

## Core mechanism ("business idea" here is audience-as-a-business-asset)
- **Thesis**: short-form video is a near-free "lottery ticket" — cost is trivial (~$6 in editing labor + a few minutes of filming) but potential payoff (viral follower/subscriber growth) is very high, so publish constantly and treat each post as a cheap bet, not a precious asset to over-optimize before publishing.
- **Funnel**: short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels, Snapchat Spotlight) → drive viewers to follow → convert a small percentage of followers into a newsletter subscriber list (uses Beehiiv) — an "owned audience" that can't be taken away by a platform algorithm change.
- Explicitly disputes the common claim that short-form viewers don't convert to long-form readers/subscribers — says with the right funnel design they do.

## Concrete numbers
- His own growth history: started newsletter ~1.5 years before this recording; had 2,300 subscribers as of December 2023 (built mostly via ~50,000 Twitter followers). Started a podcast February 2024, then pivoted to short-form video.
- First front-facing-camera video that "worked": went viral, 2 million views, 45,000 new followers overnight (from ~50 followers) — he calls this his best-ever view-to-follow conversion, not typical/repeatable at that rate.
- Ongoing pace (at time of recording): adding roughly **15,000-30,000 organic newsletter subscribers per month**; of ~173,000+ total subscribers, only 1,000-3,000 came from paid (Facebook ad) acquisition — the rest organic.
- Conversion benchmarks he tracks: **0.02% of short-form viewers become newsletter subscribers** (i.e., ~200 subscribers per 1 million views, or 1 subscriber per 5,000 views) as a conservative baseline; some videos convert as high as 0.1% (5x better).
- Follower conversion rate from views: roughly **1 follower per 300-800 views** when using a strong call-to-action, versus **1 follower per 1,000-5,000 views** with no call-to-action — he explicitly disagrees with the "no CTA" advice popularized by Alex Hormozi.
- Newsletter open rate ~55%, click-through rate ~4%.
- Publishing cadence: 3-7 short-form video "reels" per day, each one tied to a distinct business-idea case study with an auto-generated business plan (built via a custom long-standing ChatGPT/Claude prompt) published to his website and linked from the newsletter.
- Estimated newsletter subscriber value: ~$10/subscriber, conservatively (derived from blending potential monetization paths — paid newsletter tier ~$12/month at 70% margin, ad CPM revenue, community/course upsells — assuming only ~1% conversion on any single offer).
- Example single-video case study: one viral video (~20 million views across platforms) at 0.02% conversion → ~4,000 newsletter subscribers from one video; production cost ~$6 (freelance editor) + ~10 minutes of his own filming time; he frames this as "~$40,000 of value for 10 minutes of work."
- Average weekly reach across platforms: ~30 million views/week (100-150 million/month) with a lean, mostly overseas team.
- He personally edited/published ~200-300 videos by hand before outsourcing editing.
- Ran roughly 20-30 low-performing videos (hundreds of views each) before his first viral breakout.

## Process / steps described
- **Platform-first testing order**: post to TikTok first (because he cares least about that audience — most international, youngest, least loyal followers) and watch the retention chart for 1-2 hours before deciding whether to push the same video to the other four platforms (Instagram, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels, Snapchat). If retention is flat/strong, publish everywhere unchanged; if there's a drop-off point, they analyze and cut/re-edit that section before wider release.
- Publish to **all 5 platforms manually** (not simple cross-posting) — explicitly says Instagram-to-Facebook-Reels auto-crossposting underperforms versus a separate manual upload to Facebook Reels.
- **Every video needs two calls-to-action**: an early CTA (e.g., asking for a follow within the first few seconds) and a second CTA later (e.g., directing to the newsletter link) — he uses this exact two-CTA structure in nearly all his videos.
- **Hook design principle**: the opening seconds must "open a loop"/pose an unanswered question (visual, verbal, or on-screen text) that isn't resolved until later in the video or until the viewer completes the CTA — cites a video where an unusual visual (a moving shipping-container party bus) held retention through an unusually long 2.5-second CTA because viewers needed the "what is this?" question answered.
- **Answer the video's open question in the newsletter**, not in the video itself — this is what converts a curious viewer into an actual subscriber (they need somewhere to go to get the payoff).
- **Content recycling**: a proven "banger" video gets reshot and re-edited (not just reposted) every ~3 months so the algorithm doesn't treat it as duplicate content.
- **Niche selection heuristic**: pick something you understand deeply, could talk about indefinitely without running out of material, and (ideally) are personally passionate about — but if you can't afford to wait for a passion-based niche to become sustainable, prioritize whatever will make money fastest instead.
- **Idea/inspiration sourcing**: actively retrain the Instagram "For You" algorithm by liking/bookmarking/commenting on videos in your target niche (or with hooks worth emulating) — claims the algorithm recalibrates within about 30 minutes of consistent signal.
- **Funnel-matching for CTAs**: never CTA straight to a bottom-of-funnel paid offer (course, paid community) from cold short-form traffic — it underperforms. Instead, CTA to whatever is the next reasonable step down the funnel (follow → newsletter → paid offer), building trust progressively.
- **AI-assisted production workflow**: use scraping tools (mentions "Ampify"/similar API tools) to pull transcripts of competitor/niche videos, feed them into ChatGPT to rewrite scripts with a stronger hook/payoff structure, or to generate large batches of new content ideas from a handful of reference examples — costs roughly $20/month in AI tooling.
- Video production is deliberately low-fi: an iPhone 16, a clip-on mic (audio quality flagged as important), no teleprompter or script — described as largely "freestyled."

## Tools/platforms named
Beehiiv (newsletter platform — his sponsor plug, discount code "chris30"), TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels, Snapchat Spotlight, CapCut (formerly used for editing, now avoided due to a terms-of-service change), Premiere and "Edits" app (current editing tools), Upwork (source of his $6/video freelance editor), Ahrefs (keyword research for the auto-generated business-plan blog posts), ChatGPT/Claude (auto-generating a business plan for every video's featured idea via a fixed long-standing prompt).

## Caveats / risks
- Explicitly warns that lazily reposting auto-generated podcast clips to short-form platforms does not work well ("very very very very hard to go viral") — direct-to-camera, purpose-shot content performed far better for him.
- Follower count still matters for algorithmic reach (more data points = better targeting) even though many creators claim it doesn't — a video that would get 100,000 views at his current follower count would only have gotten ~5,000-10,000 views when he had 10,000 followers.
- Cross-platform audience transfer does not work — his ~50,000 Twitter followers did not meaningfully convert into short-form video followers when he started that channel from zero; he had to rebuild an audience from scratch on the new format.
- The 0.02%-view-to-subscriber conversion and $10/subscriber valuation are his own estimates/assumptions (conservative, self-reported), not independently audited figures — should be treated as illustrative rather than a guaranteed benchmark for any other creator/niche.
- This is fundamentally a marketing/audience-building method, not a packaged business itself — it requires an existing (or concurrently built) underlying business/offer (newsletter, community, course, product) to monetize the audience it generates.
