# $23k/Month by Month 2: Exact Playbook (Not Clickbait)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pgn3fRvWp6I | Published: 2025-03-11

Follow-up interview with Tyler, a listener who launched a B2B stump-grinding business (subbing to tree-trimming companies) after hearing Koerner/Nik's original podcast pitch, word-for-word implementing the strategy.

## The business
- Model: stump grinding sold B2B to tree-trimming companies (not directly to homeowners) — tree companies remove trees but don't want to own/maintain a stump grinder, so they sub the stump work out.
- ~95% of revenue comes from the same handful of repeat tree-trimming company relationships (not one-off residential jobs), so marketing spend is near zero once relationships are built.
- Origin: heard the idea on the podcast (July 11), sent his first batch of outreach texts July 12, first paid job August 3.

## Concrete numbers
- Stump grinder cost: $55,000 (Bandit 2550 model); financed with a 5-year equipment loan, 20% down (~$12,000 down), ~$990/month payment.
- Monthly overhead: ~$2,000-$2,500 total (loan payment $990, trailer $175/mo, truck payment $450/mo, storage $150/mo, insurance $450/mo).
- Revenue by month: August $9,500; September $23,000 (his best month, coincides with fall being the busiest season); Aug-Dec total $69,000 (~$112k avg pace for that stretch, per host's back-of-envelope math from the full range); January ~$7,000; February (at recording) ~$11,000.
- Pricing: charges $7/inch of stump diameter to the tree-trimming company (they typically charge the homeowner $10-12/inch and keep the spread); when working directly with a homeowner he charges ~$10/inch. Minimum job charge: $150. Doubles the price for jobs requiring stump/mulch removal and hauling (vs. just grinding and leaving mulch on-site, which is ~70-80% of jobs).
- Average job size: ~$390 (skewed up by a few $4,000-$5,000 outlier jobs); average job is 1-2 stumps.
- Had $30,000 in revenue before ever building a website or having a domain (didn't get a website until ~October, several months into the business).
- Quit his W-2 B2B sales job on September 1 (negotiated a month of paid severance/transition first).
- Runs the whole business on a Google Sheet — no CRM, invoicing software, or job-tracking tool.

## Process / how he got customers
- Used Outscraper to pull lists of tree-trimming companies; sent cold texts, followed up the next day with a short "just following up on this ⬆️" message (many people are disorganized with texts and call back immediately when reminded).
- Validated demand in a different city first: hired a virtual assistant (from Upwork, referenced from an earlier co-founders episode) to cold-call tree-trimming companies in a comparable market before committing to his home market; found ~10% of tree companies were willing to sub out stump grinding.
- Tested his home market of Provo by first texting the *out-of-market* city of Boise (a "comparable market" test) to gauge demand and pricing before ever running ads/tests in Provo — deliberately probed a market where he had zero intention of working, purely to learn pricing and demand elasticity; also called around as an "out-of-market" caller to get honest per-inch pricing benchmarks.
- Rented the stump grinder for the first month from a dealership before buying (dealer rolled the rental fee into the purchase price if he decided to buy).
- Built relationships with tree companies that either had no grinder, an unreliable one, or were unhappy with their current sub; landing the first 2-3 tree-company relationships was the hardest part — after that, growth became more organic.
- Gets reviews without any marketing spend by leaving a small card with a QR code linking to his review page after every job (currently 55 five-star reviews) — positions himself to the homeowner as working "with [tree company name]," reinforcing the tree company's relationship rather than competing with it.
- Got insurance immediately from day one (paid out of pocket), unlike some other service businesses he felt were lower-risk.
- Recently started light investment in SEO (via a friend's small SEO company) and is experimenting with Facebook group-based direct marketing.
- Sells an $49 84-page PDF playbook to others wanting to copy the business (distributed via Gumroad: creator keeps ~89%, Gumroad takes ~5%, leaving ~4.5% elsewhere — numbers as stated, though the split doesn't fully reconcile in the transcript).

## Market sizing / scaling framework discussed
- Rule of thumb floated on the show: roughly 1 tree-trimming business per 10,000 people in a market.
- Estimated Provo/Salt Lake combined addressable market ~1.7M people (~170-340 tree-trimming companies by that ratio).
- Tyler estimates he could close 20-30% of all tree-trimming companies in his market if given a "magic wand" outreach opportunity, generating an estimated $5,000-$25,000/year topline per relationship depending on account size.
- Host's conservative back-of-envelope model: a market of 100,000 people (~10 tree-trimming businesses) at a 20% close rate and $10,000/account could generate ~$20,000/year topline; scaled to Dallas-Fort Worth (~10x Provo's population), this implies a $3-4M/year ceiling if replicated with a team, consistent with earlier back-of-envelope estimates from the original episode.
- Tyler's own ceiling estimate for a solo operator: realistically a $300,000-$700,000/year business without hiring; scaling further (e.g., hiring operators, expanding to a market like Dallas) would require adding people.

## Equipment/practical notes
- Bandit 2550 recommended specifically — needs to be high enough horsepower that competitors can't just rent an equivalent machine themselves; too-low horsepower machines are easily substituted by DIY renters.
- Recommends checking utility locates before grinding to avoid hitting sprinkler lines/electrical lines (two costly on-the-job mistakes described: hit a sprinkler line flooding a townhome, had to eat the cost; hit a private electrical line near a home office, had to pay for damage).
- Recommends buyers scout for dealership support in their area (parts/service) since these machines take a beating and need repairs.
- Mulch/waste disposal: dumps ground material for free at local landscape yards rather than paying dump fees (hasn't found a way yet to sell the resulting mulch/dirt mix — differs from pure wood chips, which services like "Chip Drop" handle).

## Caveats
- The stump-grinding niche itself likely caps out around $3-4M in a large metro (per host's estimate) — not viewed as a business with unlimited scale; some large tree companies own multiple grinders and don't need a sub.
- Some early customer conversations felt "dishonest" (implying he was calling as an out-of-market operator solely to extract pricing info) — described as an uncomfortable but effective tactic.
- Winter/snow reduces volume (does grind through light snow, not blizzards); revenue is seasonal, peaking spring/summer/fall.
- Business works because it's low-risk/high-cash-flow while he learns entrepreneurship broadly; he explicitly frames it as a stepping stone he may eventually sell, hand off to a hired operator, or use as a base to pursue something with a bigger TAM.
