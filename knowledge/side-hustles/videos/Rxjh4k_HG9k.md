# How to Start a Profitable Coffee and Restaurant Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rxjh4k_HG9k | Published: 2024-08-13

Interview with Mark Janasik, founder of Southern Grounds (chef-driven coffee house + restaurant hybrid, Jacksonville, FL, started 2015, bootstrapped past $10M). Focus: coffee/restaurant hybrid economics, franchising, and tangential business ideas for the restaurant/coffee industry.

## The Southern Grounds business/concept
- Hybrid model: full chef-driven food menu + serious specialty coffee program (direct trade, single-origin, educated early by Intelligentsia in Chicago) -- deliberately rejecting the conventional wisdom that a business should pick either coffee or food, not both.
- Differentiator: hospitality-first positioning, non-transactional community gathering space, sustainability angle (reduced plastic consumption by 50% early on, sustainable/compostable cups).
- Real estate strategy: built locations with 80-90% outdoor patio space in Florida to minimize capital costs (furniture, utilities, interior build-out) -- this also became an accidental COVID advantage since outdoor dining wasn't restricted the same way indoor dining was.
- First location cost just north of $350,000 to open (2015), with a $350,000 budget ("no plan B"). No bank loan, no debt.
- Original investors pulled out before opening the first location; founders self-funded rather than replace them.

## Financials / margins
- Revenue mix: ~40% espresso-based drinks/coffee, ~50-60% food program.
- Target gross profit (GP) margin (revenue minus food cost, beverage cost, direct labor): 40% company-wide goal, described as "incredibly high for the market."
- Target net margin: 15-20% (varies by location due to rent, utilities, insurance).
- Cost of goods: food program COGS ~30-35%; coffee program COGS ~12-15% (using premium single-origin/direct-trade beans).
- Average unit volume (AUV) for franchise locations: ~$2.3M.
- No formal/paid advertising ever used -- growth has been entirely word-of-mouth plus strong location selection; framed as slower but stickier/higher-quality customer acquisition than paid promos (which attract one-time deal-seekers, not loyal repeat customers).

## Supply chain tactic
- Negotiated directly with farmers for pricing, then had the food distributor (Cisco/a "broadliner") handle logistics/distribution for products the distributor didn't normally carry -- reversing the typical small-restaurant power dynamic with distributors. Requires distribution-industry experience/relationships to pull off (Mark had a procurement background from a prior multi-unit hospitality role).
- Standard non-national-chain distributor rebate/margin context given: normal "street accounts" at broadliners run ~15% vs. ~3.5% for national chains -- implying scale matters a lot for margin.

## Franchising model
- Corporate growth target: ~4 new corporate stores/year in selected regional markets to protect quality/consistency.
- Franchise growth target: ~250 stores over 15 years via selective multi-unit franchise partners.
- Minimum franchise package: 3 units.
- Franchisee profile sought: proven multi-unit operator track record, understands kitchen/hospitality operations, sufficient capital to open multiple units; will sell single/small packages in smaller markets, larger multi-unit packages (e.g., a 9-unit deal in Tampa) in bigger markets.

## Tangential business opportunities Mark identified (ideas for other entrepreneurs to build, not run by Southern Grounds itself)
- **Cheaper merchant services / payment processing** for small restaurants/coffee shops under ~$3M revenue -- current all-in-one POS providers bundle expensive credit card processing; an alternative with lower fees would be valuable.
- **Contract negotiation training/consulting** for new restaurant/coffee operators covering supply chain negotiation (with distributors like US Foods, PFG, Cisco), lease negotiation, and understanding contract clauses like CPI escalators -- many new operators take on bad terms/debt from not understanding this.
- **Coffee grounds waste recycling**: Southern Grounds goes through ~1,000 lbs of coffee grounds/week across locations, currently thrown in the trash (some picked up informally by home gardeners). No structured resale/repurposing program exists. Potential uses cited: beauty products, gardening supplies, fertilizer, or (from a separate anecdote) lawn-leveling infill mixture. Mark says he'd give the grounds away for free to anyone who wanted to build this.
  - Related example cited: "EOS Scraps" (Utah) got PAID by grocery stores to haul away spoiling produce, turned it into compost, sold it at a 300% markup, eventually sold the company to a multinational.
  - Related example: a company took discarded juice pulp from juice bars for free and made compost.
  - Related example: a decking company made composite decking material from shredded Walmart plastic bags mixed with sawdust.
- **Off-peak space monetization**: most coffee shops operate ~6am-4pm but still pay rent/electric/insurance through the evening (until ~10pm). Idea: run event-marketing/space-rental business using that idle evening capacity, low startup cost, good for a hustler good with the public.
  - Extension of that idea (from host Chris): a startup that sells/installs a turnkey access-control kit (keypad + cameras) so a coffee shop/restaurant can rent itself out unstaffed as a co-working space in off hours -- members pay a monthly fee, swipe in, camera monitors for theft, near-zero incremental staffing cost, "almost pure profit."
- **Small-batch co-packing / CPG production for niche food brands**: Southern Grounds struggled for 6-7 years to launch its own bottled nitro cold brew because third-party co-packers require minimum orders of 2-4 pallets/15+ barrels -- a co-packer willing to do runs as small as ~100 cans would unlock a "global industry" for small CPG brands. (Related business model mentioned: "Send Eats," a 3PL that specifically picked/packed/shipped for small CPG food brands that big fulfillment companies wouldn't take, charging a premium for that willingness.)

## Advice for aspiring restaurant/coffee entrepreneurs
- Hire outside expertise (legal, real estate, contract law) on a consulting basis early rather than learning the hard way -- cites his own personal-guarantee/5-year-lease mistake as a young entrepreneur, which a quick call to a knowledgeable friend could have avoided.
- Restaurant failure rate is high and margins are generally thin, but there are "a zillion business opportunities" selling into the restaurant industry given there are reportedly 400,000-500,000 restaurants in the US (vs. ~200,000 gas stations, per Chris's estimate).
- Would not change his own bootstrapped path in hindsight -- credits the struggle for making him a better operator -- but would tell a well-capitalized founder to hire talent faster rather than carry every burden personally.

## Caveats
- Figures are Mark's own recollections/estimates in an interview, not audited financials.
- The "restaurant vs. gas station count" comparison and some supply-chain economics are approximate/anecdotal.
