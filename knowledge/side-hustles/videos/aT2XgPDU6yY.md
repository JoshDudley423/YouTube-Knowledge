# You Only Need a Laptop to Start This Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT2XgPDU6yY | Published: 2026-04-12

Guest: Michael Girdley (serial entrepreneur). Voted/crowdsourced idea list (~30 ideas emailed to a 6,000-person focus group; top 7-8 discussed). Multiple distinct laptop-only business ideas.

**1. "Claude/AI-persona exchange" (Open Claw / persona marketplace)**
- Thesis: agentic AI frameworks (referred to as "Open Claw" in the episode) will become "the next operating system" (compared to DOS → cloud → mobile paradigm shifts); people will buy pre-built, fully deployable AI agent "personas" (e.g. a marketing manager persona, a sales rep persona) created by individual experts/creators, similar to hiring an employee.
- Proposed business model: build a marketplace/exchange (the "eBay" or "Upwork" for these agent personas) where creators list personas (e.g., "$30/month, uses Instantly + Mailchimp, approximate token cost shown") and the platform takes a transaction cut as middleman — the marketplace itself, not any single persona, is framed as the bigger opportunity.
- Monetization models discussed for individual persona sellers: SaaS-style recurring subscription, one-time license fee plus pass-through token/usage costs, or open-source (free) vs. closed-source (paid) tiers, mirroring general software licensing patterns.
- No dedicated domain was found available during the recording (openclawexchange.com, "Open Claw Marketplace," "Open Claw Bay," "Open Closet" all taken) — no real launch happened in the episode.

**2. AI replicas of famous experts/mentors ("talk to Steve Jobs" subscription)**
- Concept: a Masterclass-style subscription ($59-99/month) where instead of watching a pre-recorded course, subscribers converse directly with an LLM trained on a specific expert's writings/content (e.g., "have a conversation with Steve Jobs about your product idea," get coached on physics "by Einstein," or get driving coaching "from Mario Andretti").
- Extension idea: train the persona on both the expert's content AND the individual user's own data/history for deeper personalization (e.g., export all your ChatGPT/Claude conversation history and feed it in) — flagged as currently limited by LLM context-window size, expected to improve.
- Speculative extension: combine with hologram technology for an "in-person" feeling conversation.
- Niche variant discussed: a persona trained on a specific athlete (e.g., Tom Brady) reviewing amateur technique via user-submitted video (e.g., a high-school quarterback's throwing motion) with personalized coaching feedback — framed as having very broad demand ("there's no end to that").

**3. Hyperlocal AI-powered journalism / "metro section" revival**
- Problem identified: local newspaper "metro section" investigative/beat reporting (city hall coverage, local business features) has largely disappeared as papers cut staff and consolidated printing.
- Proposed solution: use AI/deep-research tools to multiply a single journalist's coverage capacity (from ~2 stories/week manually to a claimed ~20 stories/week with AI assistance) to make hyperlocal investigative journalism economically viable again, addressing a market that shrank mainly due to the internet's effect on print economics rather than lost reader demand.
- Monetization: subscription model, cited comparison points — $20/month positioned as reasonable vs. $50/month for Bloomberg-style publications; doesn't need to reach mass scale to become profitable once ad revenue is layered in.
- Caveat raised by the guest: some real attempts at this already exist but fail because they merely republish press releases rather than doing genuine reporting/relationship-building (the "gumshoe" reporter work — building sources, being physically present at city hall) which is harder to replicate with AI alone.

**4. Curated "best of" digest / personal newsletter-as-filter business**
- Model example cited: a creator (Sean Perry mentioned) sends a weekly email curating the ~5 most valuable/interesting tweets from a given niche (e.g., "SMB Twitter," "financial Twitter"), explicitly telling readers which viral things to ignore.
- Also cited: an Instagram creator mailing a physical monthly magazine curating "cool internet finds" to subscribers (host ordered one, then ordered again a week later just to gauge order volume — reported "hundreds" of orders in roughly a one-week span).
- Framed as a solid side-hustle-scale opportunity: creators/curators reposting/quote-tweeting curated content can already earn "a couple thousand dollars a month" via existing creator monetization/payout programs, without needing to go viral themselves.
- Related consumer-product tangent (not monetized in the video, just noted as an interesting idea): a parent built a physical binder of pre-approved YouTube video thumbnails (with QR codes to scan) so kids could choose videos without exposure to algorithmic "brain rot" thumbnails — floated as a potential productizable business ("YouTubeBook.com").

**5. Distraction-blocking / accountability AI agent ("Distraction Bot")**
- Concept: a background app (Mac and/or phone) where the user sets a task-specific prompt (e.g., "I'm answering emails for the next 30 minutes" or "don't unlock my computer until I've sent 100 cold emails") and an AI agent actively monitors screen activity, locking out distracting apps/windows until the stated task is complete.
- Proposed pricing/hook: $30/month personal SaaS, with a full refund if the user completes the entire month without getting "busted" off-task — an incentive-aligned pricing gimmick.
- Feasibility note: framed as newly possible because Apple only recently (within the last year or two) opened up the device permissions needed for an app to enforce this kind of lockout behavior; combined with AI's ability to understand context/intent, described as a "no-brainer" opportunity nobody has built yet.

**6. Personalized AI skincare subscription ("Hims/Hers for skincare")**
- Concept: user uploads a selfie; an AI model analyzes skin type/condition and prescribes/recommends a specific skincare routine and product formulation, sold as a recurring subscription (positioned similarly to Hims/Hers or a Sahil-style skincare line, cited as running at ~98% margin as a recurring-revenue business).
- Live demo in the episode: host uploaded a selfie to an AI model and got a real skincare routine recommendation (cleanser type, vitamin C product, etc.) in seconds.
- Two build paths discussed: (a) simple version — AI recommends off-the-shelf third-party products; (b) more complex/higher-margin version — contract with a co-packing company to produce a custom, one-to-one personalized lotion/formulation per customer based on the photo analysis.

**7. College tuition negotiation consulting ("admissions agent" for parents)**
- Observed market shift: with declining domestic birth rates and fewer international (especially Chinese) students enrolling, colleges are reportedly far more willing to negotiate tuition/financial aid directly with parents than in the past — but most parents don't know this leverage exists or how to use it.
- Proposed business: act as a negotiation agent/consultant for parents, similar to how sports agents negotiate for athletes — leveraging case studies/precedents of other families' successful negotiated discounts (suggested AI could help generate these talking points).
- Pricing structure proposed: charge a percentage of the tuition savings achieved, and/or a refundable deposit (~$2,000-3,000) upfront to filter for serious clients who will actually follow through, avoiding wasted effort on non-committed leads.
- Revenue framing: pitched as realistic at "$100,000-250,000/year" from roughly 20 clients at ~$10,000 each, done nights/weekends alongside a regular job.
- Low-cost customer acquisition tactic suggested: park a truck with a tuition-savings billboard/ad across from the highest-income high schools in your area (noted as unregulated, at least in Texas).

**8. AI audits for small businesses (named as the strongest single recommendation in the episode)**
- Definition: NOT a financial audit — an assessment of how a small business could adopt AI across its operations (a "roadmap" for near/medium/long-term AI use), aimed at small business owners who are overwhelmed by AI hype but don't know where to start.
- Why it's a strong starting business per the guest: very little existing competition, low business-owner AI literacy/high demand for guidance, and — critically — it functions as a "gateway drug": performing the audit surfaces the business's real, often-unrecognized problems, which then opens the door to higher-value follow-on work (consulting engagements, building custom tools, or ongoing monthly retainers/products).
- Real example cited: the guest's own "entrepreneur in residence" runs this exact service; the guest's company "Fixed Labs" (fixedlabs.ai) offers small business AI audits.
- Pricing/monetization stance from the guest: charge for the audit itself (don't give it away for free) — the audit-as-loss-leader-to-fix-it-for-free model was explicitly rejected ("I can't give anything away for free... that's rule number one, if you're doing work, you should get paid for it"); a freemium/cheap vibe-coded self-serve audit tool was floated only as a later-stage tactic once the space gets more competitive, converting a portion of free-audit users to paid retainers.
- AI can be used to help write/generate the audit reports themselves, reducing delivery cost per client.

**General/meta point from the episode**: the guest explicitly frames a repeatable content/idea-generation formula: crowdsource many raw ideas, poll an audience to identify the most in-demand ones, then go deep only on the validated subset — used as the format for this episode itself (not a business idea per se, but a stated working method).

No hard revenue numbers were given for ideas #1-6; the tuition-negotiation and AI-audit ideas are the two with concrete monetization math discussed on-air.
