# If People Pay for This, Your Idea Will Work Too
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JgLTWSzGgc | Published: 2025-05-26

Part 2 of the two-part episode with Brandon Doyle (8-figure marketing agency owner; part 1 is video 47UgXbHp6nM). Focus: viral/novelty event marketing, weird-niche competitions, and physical-media revenue plays. Grab-bag of ideas, not a single deep-dive business.

## Niche competition events as marketing/revenue plays
- **Horsehair competition case study**: a barber-organized event in Clovis, CA (small Central Valley city) had a viral Instagram video of intricately shaved/patterned horsehair (looks like a tattoo) promoting a horse-hair competition. Pricing: $30 general admission, $110 to enter/have your horse judged, $320 for a sponsored vendor booth. Drew thousands of signups. Framed as both a standalone revenue event and a client-acquisition funnel for the organizing barber's shop (and other participating barbers).
- **Excel/spreadsheet esports**: two real competing events — "Financial Modeling World Cup" and "Excel Esports" — where competitors livestream themselves solving Excel problems for an audience. Entry fees up to $100; sponsors include Microsoft, Notion, Airtable; one event's championship aired on ESPN2. Cited as proof that "people will pay money for anything they're passionate about."
- General framework: "no such thing as a boring business, only boring content" — any service business can create a fun/free competitive event to generate attention and leads, e.g.: a window-cleaning company hosting a "dirtiest car" speed-cleaning contest (free, for content/leads), or an accounting firm running a "fastest tax return" race between accountants.

## Proposed "vibe-coding esports" competition (not yet executed, brainstormed live)
- Concept: gather ~9-10 non-professional "vibe coders" (intentionally not expert developers — described as "average stay-at-home marketing bro" skill level) on a shared video call, all screen-sharing simultaneously (3x3 grid), give them a build prompt (e.g. "build a golf-score-tracking app"), judge on categories like UI/UX, database design, ease of use; first to ship a working app wins a cash prize (example figure: $5,000). A live commentator narrates in real time (e.g. reacting to which no-code tool — Replit, Bolt, Lovable, Windsurf, FlutterFlow — each competitor picks).
- Suggested low-friction monetization to avoid needing sponsor buy-in: charge competitors a small entry fee (~$20-40) to ensure engagement, charge spectators a small fee to watch (~$5), and award the winner roughly half the pooled door/entry fees — this also incentivizes competitors to bring their own audience since a bigger crowd means a bigger prize pot for the winner.
- Alternate format floated: "expert coder vs. no-code tool" head-to-head speed build ("Paul Bunyan vs. the chainsaw").
- Related personal anecdote (not a business, but same underlying mechanic — pooled entry fees, prize split): the host ran a live-action "Most Dangerous Game"-style event in high school — $20/head entry for both racers and paintball "shooters," racers who reach a hidden endpoint unshot split half the pot, shooters split the pot of whoever they tag, organizer keeps the difference.

## Extreme/survivalist competition business idea ("SurvivalBet")
- Inspired by DietBet.com (a stake-based weight-loss challenge site, ~5,000 monthly visitors at time of discussion) and the TV show "Alone": proposed a paid wilderness-survival competition/experience for average people (not TV contestants), priced around $3,000/person.
- Requires: access to 50-500 acres of land, liability waivers, a ~24-hour standardized crash course in fire-starting/survival basics before contestants are sent out to survive alone and be judged/scored, prize pool possibly split among participants.
- Framed as tapping into a "do hard things" cultural trend (compared to Tough Mudder, David Goggins-style extreme-challenge culture); personal anecdote cited of a father completing a real 30-day solo wilderness survival program in southern Utah as a formative life experience.

## Physical-media / print-from-digital-content plays
- **"That'sGreatNews.com" case study**: a company (website live since 2003) that monitors alumni magazines/local publications for people who get mentioned, then cold-emails them offering a printed plaque (article page + plexiglass) commemorating the feature, with a disclaimer that they're unaffiliated with the publication. Host received this pitch after being mentioned in his university's alumni magazine; estimates their close rate could be ~50% given the personal/nostalgic appeal and the business's apparent longevity/scale.
- **YouTube-tutorials-to-print-magazine case study**: a guest referenced from a prior episode ("Al Don") runs a $7M/year business converting his own YouTube tutorial content into a physical, mailed magazine — production cost ~$0.80/issue, sold at ~$7 per bi-monthly issue, described as a seven-figure *profit* business.
- Idea extension: turn this into a **repeatable service for other YouTube creators** — feed in a YouTube channel's video URLs, output a branded print magazine/newsletter; suggested as something simple enough to prototype as a Replit/AI app ("insert YouTube URL, spit out magazine"). Proposed specific pitch structure for approaching a large existing YouTuber (example used: "Epic Gardening," ~4M YouTube subscribers, ~2M Instagram): offer a quarterly print magazine to their subscribers at ~$12/quarter with ~$2 cost to produce, creator promotes it via a set cadence (e.g., one email + one mid-roll mention per week) and the magazine service keeps ~20% of topline revenue while the creator keeps the rest — pitched as a way to monetize an audience without spending one of the creator's own mid-roll ad slots.
- Same "printed proof of feature" idea extended to any online press mention (e.g., a TechCrunch article) — a business that turns any online article into a mailed physical plaque.

## Viral/attention marketing stunts (values: "get people talking," "no such thing as bad ideas that get attention")
- **"Ghost-Free Home Guarantee"** (inspired by Nathan Fielder's marketing stunts on his show): pitch for realtors — publicly and repeatedly claim/certify that a listed home has "zero ghosts" (a self-invented "Ghost Free Guarantee" badge/certification), used as a tongue-in-cheek differentiator to stand out from other realtors in a local market and generate PR/virality. Cited stat: ~45% of people believe in ghosts, framed as the target market size.
- Suggested extension: a low-cost ($10-20) third-party "certifying body" that issues the ghost-free seal/stamp to any realtor who wants it.
- Suggested grassroots/bottoms-up demand-generation tactic (referencing the 1990s JORD watch brand's strategy of having friends/family repeatedly ask JCPenney stores if they carried the brand until the retailer took notice): run geographically confined, high-spend ads targeting active home shoppers in one city pushing the "ghost-free guarantee" concept so that demand appears to organically bubble up and realtors in that market pick up on the trend themselves.
- General framing/quote used to justify weird marketing stunts: "pride and ego prevents us from making more money" — the idea doesn't need to be objectively good, it just needs to generate conversation (cited Nathan Fielder's poop-flavored frozen yogurt stunt and a "pizza in 10 minutes or it's free" promotion, both of which reportedly drove real business results despite sounding absurd).
- General "behind-the-scenes" content idea for boring/serious professions (lawyers, accountants, home-based businesses): show real (anonymized where needed) internal decision-making — a lawyer debating case strategy with colleagues, an accountant walking through discovering a client's tax savings on-screen — instead of generic before/after content, as a way to generate more interesting, differentiated marketing content.

## Caveats
- All ideas in this episode are brainstormed live and largely unexecuted by the hosts themselves — the horsehair competition, Excel esports, That'sGreatNews.com, and the YouTube-magazine business are real/existing businesses cited as inspiration, but the vibe-coding esports concept, SurvivalBet, and Ghost-Free Guarantee are speculative ideas, not case studies with real numbers.
