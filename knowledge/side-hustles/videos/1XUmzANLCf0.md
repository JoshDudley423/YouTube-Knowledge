# Here Is My Favorite AI Business Idea. Steal It!
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1XUmzANLCf0 | Published: 2025-01-23

"HoldCo Bros" brainstorm episode (Chris Koerner + Nik of Nikonomics), triggered by seeing Andrew Wilkinson's viral tweet of an AI voice agent making a restaurant reservation. Covers several AI-voice-agent business concepts plus an AI-literacy-education idea.

## Idea 1: AI voice-agent wedding-quote/negotiation concierge ("Never Call Again")

- Core insight: nobody enjoys making phone calls to get quotes (venues, catering, photography, flowers, etc.), yet getting more quotes generally means saving more money — most people stop after 2-3 calls because the process is tedious.
- Concept: an AI voice agent that autonomously calls vendors on behalf of a specific named client (e.g., "I'm calling on behalf of Mallerie Smith, planning her wedding for March 31st in Plano, Texas") to request itemized quotes against specific, well-defined parameters (e.g., "50 heads, budget $15-20/head, two appetizers, one side, one meat").
- Key tactical detail for handling AI voice latency/awkward pauses (a known weakness of voice agents circa 2024-2025): front-load a long, natural preamble before the first pause occurs, explicitly disclosing "I know this is weird, but I am an AI agent — you'll notice a pause in this conversation, but ask me anything you want, and I'll report everything back to [client], who will call you herself if your quote is competitive." This normalizes the pause instead of the vendor hanging up confused, and the direct honesty is framed as building trust rather than deception.
- Monetization/offer framing: pitched as a no-brainer guarantee offer — "we'll save you $5,000 on your wedding, full money-back guarantee if we don't."
- Considered productizing as a chat-based ("text a bot like a contact in your phone") interface rather than an app — described as more natural since people are already comfortable asking ChatGPT/Claude-style tools follow-up questions in a conversational back-and-forth, rather than a single query-response search.
- Two pricing tiers discussed: individual consumers planning one wedding (lower price) vs. professional wedding planners as high-volume users (higher price/usage-based).
- Generalization beyond weddings: the same "AI calls X vendors on your behalf and reports back" mechanic could extend to any recurring-need service call — cited example use: "our AC just broke, get me 10 quotes" via a $20/month membership model, framed as a household emergency-quotes utility.
- Domain acquired live on air: **NeverCallAgain.com** (bought by Chris during the recording) — chosen deliberately as a broad, brandable name (rather than a narrow wedding-only name) so the business could start niche (weddings) and expand category by category, explicitly modeled on **DoNotPay.com** (started ultra-niche as a San Francisco parking-ticket dispute bot, grew into a "$100M+ company" backed by Sequoia, now positioned broadly as an "AI consumer champion").
- Underlying business-model insight for the AI-agent-as-planner angle: because most consumers have never priced these categories before (e.g., a first-time bride doesn't know if a catering quote is "good"), an AI agent trained specifically on that vertical can ask better questions than the consumer would think to ask themselves, and — critically — as more users run quotes through the system, the aggregated pricing data becomes a proprietary network-effect asset (a real-time, crowd-sourced "what should this actually cost" benchmark) that competitors without that data volume can't replicate.

## Idea 2: AI-agent market-research/business-idea-validation caller (evolution of an earlier concept)

- References an earlier tactic the hosts had used: hiring a human virtual assistant to cold-call every stump-grinding company in a city (Houston) to survey pricing and outsourcing willingness, to validate a business idea before launching.
- Proposed AI-agent evolution: the same validation-calling workflow, but run by an AI voice agent instead of a human VA, at near-zero marginal cost, for aspiring entrepreneurs to test/validate a business idea (e.g., "what's the average price of a toilet replacement across 20 plumbing companies in Dallas?").
- Explicitly deprioritized by the hosts as too niche/high-friction to be a mass-market product — aspiring business owners are a small addressable market and most people won't proactively do market research even when told it's valuable — but folded into the broader "proprietary information/data aggregation" idea below.

## Idea 3: AI-agent-driven proprietary sentiment/data aggregation business (the episode's favorite, "billion dollar idea")

- Inspired by a conversation with Craig Fuller, founder of FreightWaves/SONAR — described as "the Bloomberg terminal for logistics and shipping": aggregates proprietary real-time freight/shipping order-flow data (what's shipping from China, what Walmart is ordering) that let Fuller reportedly predict the last four recessions before mainstream indicators did; a fully bootstrapped company reportedly valued at $100M+.
- Core thesis: the value is in owning genuinely proprietary/aggregated information nobody else has, then either selling access to it (subscription/data product) or using it yourself to make better-informed decisions ahead of the market.
- Chris's own prior "one-spreadsheet businesses" as proof of concept for this thesis:
  - **LCD Cycle** (his iPhone-parts business): started by scraping every iPhone repair store in the US into a spreadsheet of ~20,000 stores, then had 3 virtual assistants call all of them — built into a $10 million business off that single spreadsheet.
  - **"No BS Crypto"** (a token project with John McAfee): built by crawling Reddit/Instagram/Facebook/Twitter for early sentiment signals on specific cryptocurrencies from places nobody else was monitoring, which proved accurate enough at predicting which coins would pop that it got McAfee to partner on the venture.
- Applying AI voice agents to scale this: instead of manually scraping text/social data, have an AI agent autonomously phone-survey business owners in a specific niche/industry (e.g., "click a button and call a thousand lawyers in the next 5 minutes") asking a handful of standardized sentiment questions (e.g., recent sales velocity), then aggregate the responses into a report.
- **The pitch/hook that gets business owners to answer**: offer to send them, in exchange for ~30 seconds of their time answering questions, a free report showing what everyone else in their industry said — framed as an irresistible trade (minimal time cost for competitive-intelligence value).
- **Choosing which industry/niche to target — a stated tradeoff matrix**:
  - Low-friction-to-reach, low-ticket example: convenience stores (~250,000 in the US, second-largest retail category after restaurants) — owner is easy to reach directly (no gatekeeper/receptionist), but what they sell/what you can charge them is lower-value.
  - High-friction-to-reach, high-ticket example: dentists — hard to get past the receptionist, but high-value audience once reached.
  - **Sweet spot identified: lawyers** — tens of thousands+ in the US, cell phone numbers are relatively easy to scrape/obtain (low gatekeeper friction), and it's a high-value ("high-ticket") audience for advertisers.
- **Newsletter flywheel design**: build a newsletter where the *entry price* of subscribing is answering the survey questions (i.e., "email me and I'll send you everyone else's aggregated answers") — this simultaneously builds the proprietary dataset AND an opted-in subscriber list of a high-value professional audience (e.g., lawyers), which commands very high advertising CPMs precisely because the audience is so professionally valuable and pre-qualified.
- **Cost economics discussed for AI voice-agent calling at scale**: roughly $0.15/minute for AI voice-agent calls at time of recording (some providers round up per-minute billing, some don't) — costs can add up across large call volumes, so suggested optimizations: keep calls very short (~30 seconds), have the agent simply pitch the value exchange and collect an email address rather than conducting the full survey live on the phone, then send survey questions via a cheaper follow-up email/form instead. Tradeoff: email-based follow-up is cheaper per contact but has lower completion/click-through rates and introduces transcription/matching error risk; live-phone surveys cost more per contact but yield more complete, higher-quality data. Suggested tiered pricing to the end customer based on desired data quality (e.g., a lower-cost "email-collected" data tier vs. a "phone-verified" premium tier at ~10x price).
- **Cited economic logic for why this only recently became viable**: for very niche/small industries, it was never economically justified to pay even an offshore human caller (e.g., in the Philippines) to gather this kind of sentiment data — but at an effective cost of roughly $0.10/call via an AI agent, the economics flip, creating what's described as an arbitrage opportunity on an information/knowledge gap that previously had no viable collection method.
- Additional application floated: hyperlocal political/civic sentiment polling (e.g., gauging public opinion on a pending city council ordinance) — acknowledged as a much smaller addressable market (rough estimate: maybe 30-50 relevant municipal-sized markets in the US willing to pay for this).
- Caveat acknowledged: phone-survey data has inherent selection bias (only people willing to answer are represented), though more responses generally still improve model accuracy despite that bias.

## Idea 4 (tangential, discussed at length): AI-literacy education business for adults/Boomers

- Observation: most non-technical adults (including some teenagers, who mostly use ChatGPT for novelty/jokes) don't know how to ask effective follow-up questions or use AI models beyond a single query — "knowing the right question to ask is as important as the answer," and most people never learn to ask a second/deeper question.
- Cites his own use case as an example of proper LLM usage: instead of searching Google for generic pre-written articles on a specific task (e.g., configuring parental-control settings on a new Apple Watch for his kids), he wrote one detailed prompt into ChatGPT specifying his exact requirements and got a precise step-by-step answer on the first try — framed as qualitatively different from search-then-interpret behavior.
- **Proposed business**: teach in-person or virtual classes for adults (not necessarily just retirees) on how to use ChatGPT effectively — modeled on the "Saturday morning iPad classes" historically run at physical Apple Stores for older customers.
- **Suggested low-risk way to launch/validate, especially for someone with no existing audience or platform**: offer a free class through an existing community you're already part of (explicitly suggested: a local church/congregation) to test reception and interest at zero financial risk; alternatively, offer a free class at independent living facilities (specifically NOT assisted living, where residents may be less cognitively able to engage) — predicted that after a free class, several attendees would likely offer to pay (~$50 for a half-hour of 1:1 help) for further personalized instruction.
- Revenue model suggested to start: simple hourly/per-session paid tutoring once initial free classes prove demand, with potential to layer on higher-ticket offerings over time (a subscription "wrapper" app, or recorded/interactive video content) as the audience and format are better understood.
- Alternative/competing framing floated by the other host: instead of live classes, build a paid ($9.99) mobile app "wrapper" specifically aimed (via targeted paid ads) at older users that simplifies/guides prompting for a narrow use case (e.g., cooking help) — reasoning that large AI labs (OpenAI, etc.) have no commercial incentive to build or market specifically to underserved segments like Boomers, leaving room for a smaller, targeted product. Chris expressed preference for the in-person/community-class version specifically because it has near-zero startup cost/risk and is more achievable without existing technical or software-building skills, while acknowledging the app-wrapper version might reach more people faster if the operator already has some technical capability (cited tools: Cursor for AI-assisted iPhone app building).
- Framed as best suited to someone with 6+ months of runway to experiment (e.g., a college student), rather than someone needing to replace full-time income immediately, since the exact monetizable format is expected to take time to discover through iteration.

## Caveats across the episode

- The AI voice-agent-calling ideas depend on the current, still-imperfect state of voice-agent latency ("the pause") — the hosts explicitly note this technology was materially unchanged in perceived quality between when they first tested it (mid-2022) and Wilkinson's 2025 viral demo, so the "pause" workaround (proactive disclosure) is treated as a necessary, not optional, design element for now.
- The proprietary-data/sentiment-aggregation idea is explicitly called "the hard part" of the business — collecting information at meaningful scale/accuracy is the bottleneck, not conceiving the idea.
- No concrete revenue numbers exist yet for any of these ideas — all are framed as live brainstorm concepts (with one domain purchased on the spot) rather than case studies with real financial results.
