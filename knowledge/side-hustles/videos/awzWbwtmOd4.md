# He Watched This Channel and Made $32K in His First 2 Months
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=awzWbwtmOd4 | Published: 2025-10-26

Guest: Tyler Mumford, founder of a stump grinding business (Grind Time Stump Grinding) in Provo, Utah/Utah County, launched after hearing about the idea on this podcast (referenced episode #37 originally, revisited in episode #140). This episode is an on-site follow-up filmed with Tyler and co-host Nik (Nikonomics).

Business idea: standalone stump grinding service, launched as a B2B "sub" business (doing stump removal for tree companies that don't want to handle stumps themselves) before shifting toward direct B2C/homeowner demand.

Key numbers and timeline:
- Heard the podcast episode on stump grinding on July 11; tested his own market by July 12; had all equipment/insurance in place and did his first paid job August 3.
- First month (August 2024) working full-time at his prior sales job simultaneously: $9,000 in revenue, done by getting up at 5:45am to do jobs before work, hiding the trailer from coworkers (felt embarrassed), then doing more jobs after work until 8:30pm.
- Quit his job at the start of month 2 because his job schedule conflicted with a backlog of booked stump jobs (would have been fired otherwise) — described the moment of quitting as "electric," not scary.
- Month 2 (September 2024): $23,000 in revenue. Combined first two months: $32,000 in revenue, roughly $22,000-26,000 of that in profit.
- Had ~$20,000 in personal savings before quitting, calculated he could survive on zero income through the following March if needed; his wife (also employed) gave him informally "a year" to make it work.
- Prior W-2 sales job paid roughly $175,000/year (~$15,000/month); by his second month in the business he was already netting comparably or more (estimated $15-25K/month depending on season).
- Fixed monthly overhead early on: roughly $2,000-3,000/month, later grew to about $4,000-5,000/month at time of filming — covering a leased truck (~$490/month), leased trailer (~$170/month), a financed stump grinder ($900/month, purchased outright once he quit his job), insurance (~$400/month), a storage lot ($100/month), gas (his single largest recurring cost), and Google/LSA-style ads (~$500-600/month total). No CDL required to drive the equipment.
- Average revenue per job: roughly $300. Can complete 5-6 jobs of that size in a single day if routed efficiently.
- Pricing structure: $200 minimum callout fee for a single stump regardless of size; beyond that, price-per-inch of stump diameter measured at the widest point — he charges about $7/inch locally (some operators in regions with more trees/bigger machines/year-round work charge as low as ~$3/inch). Example: an 18-inch stump priced at $7/inch = $126, but the $200 minimum would apply if it's the only job at that stop. A job with three ~3-foot-diameter stumps was estimated around $800. About 20-30% of customers pay extra to have wood chip debris hauled away; otherwise chips are pushed back into the hole for free, or given free to a local landscaping yard that turns it into mulch (unmonetized side opportunity flagged but not pursued).
- Currently getting 2-3 inbound calls/day from Google (Google Guaranteed/LSA-listed), plus 3-5/day in busy season from contractor referrals; closes roughly 60% of inbound calls, attributing this partly to simply answering the phone every time (a stated differentiator, since many competitors are side-hustle operators who don't).
- Estimated potential passive profit if he stepped back and hired someone to do the physical grinding while keeping the business running as-is: north of $115,000-120,000/year.

Startup/customer acquisition method — his self-described "PRA framework":
1. Piggyback: identify adjacent trades that have this exact pain point and don't want to do stumps themselves — primarily tree companies, but also landscape installers, fence installers (when a stump sits in the fence line), and concrete contractors (landscapers who only do mowing were explicitly not useful). Found these businesses in bulk via Outscraper (a lead-scraping tool), then cold-texted lists of 20-30 contacts at a time with a simple message including a photo of himself with his business card in front of his truck's logo (to visually signal legitimacy/being an active tradesperson) plus a short script: "Hey, my name's Tyler. I run a company called Grind Time Stump Grinding... what do you do with your stumps? Do you do them yourself? Sub it out? Or own a machine?" Reported roughly 30-40% response rate to this cold outreach.
2. Reviews ("backdooring" reviews): even though the tree company was technically the customer, since he was the last person on-site after finishing the stump job, he would introduce himself directly to the homeowner and politely ask for a Google review of his own company — netted his first ~70-75 reviews this way before he even had a Google Business Profile set up (didn't create one until month 4-5). When he finally created his Google Business Profile, he text-blasted his full backlog of past customers (tracked manually in a spreadsheet) and got about 15 reviews on day one — theorized this signals to Google's algorithm that the business is established, boosting local ranking quickly.
3. Answer every call: treats answering the business phone as non-negotiable — a stated key differentiator versus part-time/side-hustle competitors who are slower to respond, since contractors especially need speed (only get paid once the stump is ground, so quick turnaround wins their business).
- Revenue mix shifted over time: 100% from contractor referrals for roughly the first 6 months; at time of filming, roughly 60-70% still from contractors, 30-40% from his own direct/homeowner customers via Google.

Risk-reduction approach emphasized throughout: didn't quit his job until the side income proved itself; started by renting a truck/trailer/stump grinder (all rentable by the day, e.g., from Sunbelt) rather than buying, accepting weaker unit economics short-term in exchange for lower risk while learning; learned the machine primarily by watching ~7 YouTube videos (not fully applicable, since most showed remote-control machines) and then doing his actual first paying job with essentially no hands-on practice; considered buying an existing business (using the "Buy Then Build" framework/book by Walker Deibel) but had insufficient capital, and considered franchising but found the upfront commitment too risky by comparison — framed stump grinding as an accessible, low-downside alternative (equipment retains resale value; worst case, he'd have a usable truck).

Deliberately did not diversify into tree trimming or land clearing despite adjacent skills, specifically to avoid competing with/cannibalizing the tree companies that were his main referral/lead source; he does still use a chainsaw to cut down anything under 6-8 feet tall himself rather than involve another business, and occasionally subs out full tree-removal jobs for a referral cut.

Risks/caveats explicitly raised:
- Not passive income — described as real work requiring daily physical labor and phone responsiveness.
- Genuine on-the-job hazards: hit a pressurized sprinkler line near an occupied townhouse window (had to intervene directly, no insurance claim needed); hit an unmarked private electrical line and got sparks flying near him (paid out of pocket for the repair; also no formal insurance claim). Insurance costs ~$400/month and was "not hard to find."
- Highly seasonal business (winter slowdown), though he found sufficient winter work locally since snow doesn't stay on the ground long in his area; he built in a cash buffer specifically to survive an assumed slow winter.
- Utah described as a highly competitive market for this specific service, including direct copycats who saw his public posts about the business model — he says this doesn't concern him because he outcompetes them on execution (mainly responsiveness).
- He explicitly discourages skipping practice reps entirely for people with less risk tolerance, though his own and the host's stated bias is toward launching immediately and learning through real jobs rather than practicing first.

Community/resources: Tyler created and sells a paid playbook/community teaching this exact model (cited: 100+ buyers, a dozen-plus people who've launched their own stump grinding businesses in other markets, e.g., Virginia, Minnesota, South Carolina); reportedly enforces informal market exclusivity so it doesn't sell the course to a second person in the same city. The Koerner Office also runs a paid community, TK Owners (tkowners.com), referenced as a place these types of operators compare notes.
