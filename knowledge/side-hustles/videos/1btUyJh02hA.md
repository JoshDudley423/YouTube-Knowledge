# Perfume Vending Machine Webinar Recording
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1btUyJh02hA | Published: 2024-07-26

## The idea
- Import and place perfume/cologne vending machines (made by Spanish company Perfuma) in high-foot-traffic venues; charge per spray.
- Speaker (Chris Koerner, via his company Co-Founders) is the authorized North America distributor, partnered with Perfuma; not the machine manufacturer.

## Numbers
- Machine price: ~$3,000 USD for 1-5 units (drops at 6+, 10+, 100+ unit quantities); price excludes shipping, taxes, customs, and a credit-card terminal add-on.
- Payment terms: 70% deposit up front, 30% before delivery; can pay by credit card or wire.
- Net profit potential: $700-$2,500/month typical; range spans $700-$900 to as much as $7,000/month in an extreme case (best-performing location saw 7,000 spray-sales/month at ~$1/spray).
- Payback period: typically 1-4 months; as fast as a few weeks in a great location, 5-8+ months in a bad one.
- Average charge per spray: $1 (up to $2.50 at high-end nightclubs).
- Electricity cost: $2-$10/month (machine is hardwired, needs an electrician; works on any voltage worldwide).
- Perfume/cologne product cost: ~4-8% of what you charge per spray (so at $1/spray, cost is single-digit cents).
- Credit card processing fee: ~3%.
- Revenue share offered to venue/location owners: typically 10-20% of revenue, or a flat fee (~$100/month); Koerner reports ~60% of hosts take a rev-share, ~40% take flat monthly fee.
- Machine holds 5 fragrance bottles, 30ml retail/display size each (~250-300 sprays per bottle) plus a hidden wholesale reservoir; total machine capacity ~1,500 sprays before refill.
- Refill time: 5-7 minutes.
- Warranty: 2 years or 100,000 sprays, whichever first.
- Dimensions: 700mm x 400mm x 185mm; ~24kg (52 lb) empty.
- Manufacturing lead time: 60 days regardless of order size (1-100 units); shipping (Spain to US) adds ~1 week via air.

## Process / how to do it
1. Order machine(s) from Perfuma via Co-Founders (authorized NA distributor); pay 70% deposit.
2. Wait ~60 days for manufacturing + ~1 week shipping.
3. Get it wall-mounted (standard) or on an optional pedestal (larger LED screen option available) by an electrician (hardwired power).
4. Scout and pitch a location: nightclubs, gyms, university campuses, nicer gas stations, movie theaters performed best in various markets; culture affects which types of venues work (e.g., universities do well in Scandinavia, nightclubs in Western Europe, gas stations/nightclubs in Croatia).
5. Location research method suggested: physically sit and count foot traffic (like scouting a retail franchise site), or informally survey passersby ("would you pay $1 for a perfume/cologne spray here?") to estimate conversion before committing.
6. Negotiate placement with venue owner: either a flat monthly fee or a revenue share (10-30%); framing matters — pitch it as bringing them more customers/value, not asking a favor (Tesla supercharger analogy: high-leverage framing gets you a better deal, e.g., paying the owner nothing while still landing the placement).
7. Include a 30-day no-penalty exit clause in host contracts since even experienced vending operators sometimes pick a location that flops despite prior data.
8. Monitor sales, spray counts, and inventory remotely via iOS/Android/web app; refill as needed.
9. Optional secondary revenue: sell ad space on the machine's built-in 10.1" LED screen; or drive bottle sales via an affiliate link/QR code to an existing perfume retailer (e.g., Amazon affiliate, ~4-5% commission) rather than building your own e-commerce/shipping operation for bottle sales (volume of people wanting a full bottle is described as too small to justify it).

## Tools/platforms/resources named
- Perfuma (Spain-based manufacturer, patent holder on the machine design).
- Co-Founders (Koerner's company, exclusive NA/Mexico/Canada distributor; contact: matt@cofounders.com for sales calls, chris@cofounders.com for international/partnership inquiries).
- NIX (payment processor integration; supports card/coin/cash and Apple Pay/Google Pay).
- Machines have GSM/CDMA SIM cards for connectivity/reporting.
- A wholesale perfume/cologne supplier contact in Switzerland is available at a discount to machine buyers for refills.
- Amazon Affiliate program suggested for a passive bottle-upsell revenue stream.

## Caveats / risks
- Machine has a GSM/CDMA SIM card, which in some jurisdictions can classify it as a "telecommunications device" rather than a simple vending machine — different customs duties, VAT, and import regulations apply; import can be "tricky" without experience.
- Perfuma holds the patent; buying a knockoff/similar machine risks patent/legal issues.
- Profit is highly location-dependent — a bad location could mean a 5-8+ month payback or a loss; even experienced vending operators (cited: a contact with hundreds of vending machines) sometimes place machines that simply don't perform despite matching data from "proven" locations.
- No official operator experience by the speaker in this niche himself — he has zero perfume industry background, general vending/import/business experience only.
- Speaker had no hard data yet on: specific fragrance performance, exact insurance solutions/costs, precise shipping costs to various countries, or long-term (5+ year) durability data (product line is only ~5 years old).
- Repairs are described as uncommon but not shipped internationally for service in most cases — buyer relies on a generic repair manual or phone support from the manufacturer for anything beyond basic fixes.
- Market saturation: at time of recording, Dallas-Fort Worth (large US metro) reportedly had zero machines — suggests early/unproven US market with upside but also unknown demand patterns.
