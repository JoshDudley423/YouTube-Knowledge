# How to Start a Fully Automated AI Travel Assistant Business
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14sqrADUseg | Published: 2024-09-09

Interview/brainstorm with Sam Thompson (returning guest, "idea guy"). Covers three ideas: an "idea conference" concept, an AI-automated travel-planning business (the core idea), and an underused Facebook cross-posting/comment-pinning growth tactic. Part 1 of a two-part episode.

## Idea 1: "Idea Fair" — a pay-to-pitch business-idea conference (concept, not launched)

- Format: one-day event at a hotel; two ticket types — presenters (~$500) and listeners (~$100). Not a Shark Tank/fundraising event — pure idea-stage pitching for feedback, not capital.
- Structure discussed: ~15-20 minute slots per presenter (or alternative: a 20-second pitch + 10 minutes of Q&A, to force clarity), live audience up/down-voting on screen (Reddit-style upvoted questions surfaced for the presenter to answer), lunch included, second half of the day as an open "science fair" style floor with a table per presenter for 1:1 networking/investor/co-founder conversations.
- Considered tying prize money to 90-day post-event execution/progress rather than same-day audience voting, to discourage people who pitch for fun/ego with no intent to build (anecdote: Chris won a college pitch competition and never built anything). Alternative floated: skip structured prize money entirely and just raffle something like a laptop to a random attendee, since the real value for presenters is feedback/networking, not a cash prize.
- Considered two presenter tiers: passive idea-havers vs. people seriously about to quit their job and execute, each wanting different depth of structured feedback.
- No cost/revenue estimates given beyond ticket pricing; explicitly framed as a first, deliberately low-frills ("no booths, no sponsors") version 1 to test, with an "anti-NDA" ethos (openly sharing ideas rather than guarding them).

## Idea 2: AI-automated, personalized travel planning business (the core idea of the episode)

- Starting observation: people already use ChatGPT directly (unassisted) to generate full day-by-day vacation itineraries (hotels, restaurants, activities) for free, given a budget and preferences — proof the underlying capability already works well.
- Reference point on market size/model: cruise lines and traditional travel agencies are essentially just "conduits" between travelers and travel providers (hotels, tours, etc.) and are a large existing market despite being viewed as old-fashioned/manual ("boomer stuff").
- **Core productized version**: build a typeform/intake (number of travelers, preferences, allergies, budget, destinations, dates) that feeds into an LLM (ChatGPT/similar), which outputs a full itinerary delivered as a Google Doc/Slides with affiliate-linked hotels/activities/restaurants embedded (monetized via travel affiliate commissions). Estimated per-output cost: ~$0.05 (five cents) in AI compute, i.e., near-zero marginal cost/COGS.
- Pricing tested in conversation: charge the end customer somewhere around $30-50 (a middle ground between free/DIY and a $300-3,000 traditional travel agent package — roughly "80% as good for a fraction of the price"). Refund policy suggested as simple customer service: if unhappy with a $30 plan, just refund the $30 rather than build complex revision workflows.
- **Distribution/whitelabel angle for travel influencers**: many travel micro-influencers on Instagram already sell $200-300 "I'll plan your vacation" offers manually via their link-in-bio. Pitch to them: keep selling the exact same offer under your own brand, but stop doing any of the actual planning work yourself — swap your current link for one pointing to this AI-automated backend, charge the influencer's existing price (or the operator takes a cut, e.g. ~$50/plan) plus keep affiliate revenue from the recommended hotels/activities, and the influencer does zero manual work going forward.
  - To avoid the output sounding like generic AI text, suggested training/prompting the LLM on the specific influencer's persona/voice (feeding it transcripts of their video content to extract tone, catchphrases, etc.) so outputs feel authentically written by that specific creator.
  - Suggested a "human-in-the-loop" review option (an email link the influencer can click to review/edit before it's sent to their customer) — though the hosts speculate influencers likely wouldn't even bother reviewing given the low price point/stakes.
  - Suggested paid-ad distribution: find each influencer's best-performing past viral videos/reels and run paid ad boosts behind them (whitelisted from the influencer's own ad account) driving to the $29.99-ish offer.
- **Programmatic SEO / content flywheel**: every AI-generated itinerary produced for a paying customer can be repurposed (stripped of personally identifying info) into a public SEO article (e.g., "Best Family-of-Four Itinerary: Greece"), creating what's described as an "infinite content loop" — the paid product generates the marketing content as a byproduct, at effectively zero incremental cost. Suggested making the raw underlying planning tool free-to-use in order to maximize volume of content generated this way, while capturing revenue via affiliate links embedded throughout.
- **Revenue/scale expectations given (explicitly NOT framed as a billion-dollar startup)**: someone putting in ~10 hours/day for 6 months could plausibly reach $30,000-50,000/month; ballpark estimate of "half a million dollars a year" achievable with solid margins. Referenced as analogous to how indie hacker Peter Levels (Nomad List, Interior AI, etc.) runs several niche "micro-SaaS" products, none individually huge (allegedly none over ~$500K/year individually at the time), assumed ~75-80% margins, but collectively adding up — the philosophy cited approvingly: prioritize high-margin, reasonably liquid cash flow over chasing a single big exit.
- **Generalization beyond travel** (same "personalized AI planning" pattern applied to other verticals, floated as copy-paste variants of the same idea):
  - Relocation planning: "tell me where to move," matched to house size/budget/family needs.
  - AI-assisted real estate: paste in a Zillow listing link and have AI assess livability against stated family preferences (e.g., flagging school district quality for a family with kids) — noted as adjacent to a broader "AI real estate agent" concept.
  - Grocery-shopping recommendations based on stated preferences.
- Reference tool named for connecting AI outputs to automation/data pipelines: **Replicate** (API for plugging various AI models into a workflow) combined with **Zapier** for automation — mentioned in the context of Peter Levels' broader micro-SaaS approach (e.g., an "Interior AI" tool that lets users upload a room photo and generate redesign variations, or generate paint-color variations for a car photo) as a category of small, narrowly-scoped, monetizable AI micro-tools.

## Idea 3: Facebook cross-posting + pinned-comment growth hack (side idea, not a full business but spawns one)

- Observation: content cross-posted from Instagram to Facebook (via the built-in IG-to-FB cross-post toggle) sometimes massively outperforms the same content on Instagram — cited real example: a personal reel got ~400,000 views on Instagram vs. ~1.2 million views on Facebook.
- Key growth mechanic: on Facebook (unlike Instagram), a link posted in the FIRST comment on your own video by the poster is automatically clickable/pinned — Instagram comments never render links as clickable. Posting trackable links (e.g., via TinyURL) as the first comment on a viral Facebook cross-post can generate very fast, high click-through (cited: 100+ clicks within the first 45 seconds of posting links in the comments of one viral video).
- **Business idea spun off this observation**: most content creators only think about Instagram and ignore that they're also (often passively/automatically) cross-posting to Facebook without exploiting it. Proposed service: identify creators (e.g., e-commerce/dropshipping gurus, or literally any local business — a dentist, a retail store, a lawn-chair seller) who are NOT cross-posting to Facebook or not pinning links in comments there, and offer to manage Facebook cross-posting and comment-pinning for them in exchange for being allowed to insert an affiliate link as the pinned first comment — essentially a zero-cost-to-the-creator arrangement (they lose nothing) where the operator monetizes via affiliate revenue on the pinned links.
- Broader framing: Facebook (including Groups and Marketplace, not just the main feed) is characterized as "the most slept-on marketing channel right now" among younger/online-native audiences who've mentally written it off, despite ~3 billion daily active users — an "asymmetric bet" precisely because competitors/peers aren't paying attention to it. (Cross-reference: buying/starting niche Facebook Groups for lead capture is covered in more depth in a separate episode, "Secrets to Earning from Facebook Communities.")

## Caveats

- The AI travel-planning idea is explicitly framed by both speakers as not a venture-scale ("billion dollar") business — a realistic, high-margin solo/small-team income business, not a category-defining startup.
- No hard data is given validating actual demand/conversion for the influencer-whitelabel travel angle — it's discussed as a promising, low-cost-to-test hypothesis rather than a proven case study.
- The Facebook comment-pinning growth tactic is a personal, recent (same-day) discovery at time of recording, not a tested business model — presented as an opportunity worth exploiting quickly before it becomes widely known.
