# How Can You Create a Serial Entrepreneur?
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CiKtDpyIWUE | Published: 2024-03-21

Long-form biographical interview (Chris Koerner on the Millennial Investing podcast, republished here) covering his background and current holding company. Less a single business tutorial, more a mix of formative lessons and several concrete business case studies with real numbers.

## Reselling Buc-ee's merchandise online (Texas Snacks / "Beaver Snacks")
- Opportunity spotted: Buc-ee's (a large Texas-based gas station/convenience chain, ~60,000 sq ft stores, 100+ fuel pumps, each location estimated at $20-30M/year revenue at the time, company valued at "half a billion dollars plus") sold no merchandise online at all despite having a full retail line (snacks, jerky wall, candy wall, apparel).
- First approach: pitched Buc-ee's directly to handle their e-commerce fulfillment via his existing 3PL (third-party logistics) company, Send Beyond — no response.
- Pivot: legally resold Buc-ee's products himself under the "first sale doctrine" (anyone can resell a legitimately purchased branded product; you just can't impersonate the brand). Launched a site initially called "Beaver Snacks," bought roughly $2,000-3,000 worth of one-of-everything from a physical store, photographed it, and listed it online with a prominent disclaimer that the site was not affiliated with Buc-ee's.
- PR/legal risk-management tactic: proactively emailed reporters about the launch to generate visibility and force Buc-ee's hand — reasoning that worst case they'd be told to shut down (acceptable), best case Buc-ee's would either partner or simply tolerate it while he already had a profitable business running.
- Outcome: Buc-ee's in-house counsel called within a day; they had no objection to the resale itself but required changes — drop "Beaver" from the name (trademark/confusion concerns) and make the non-affiliation disclaimer more prominent. Buc-ee's declined to run e-commerce themselves, hypothesized to be because they wanted to preserve the in-store experience as exclusive/novel and worried an online option would reduce that novelty or cannibalize in-store visits.
- Ongoing operations: built relationships with general managers at 2 stores (with weaker ties to 2 more), pre-placed orders that Buc-ee's staff would palletize, then physically picked up the order at the register, paying tens of thousands of dollars per trip via card, and transported it by box truck to the company's warehouse — done on a recurring (weekly-ish) basis.
- Result: grew into what's described as "a three or four million dollar a year" business (Texas Snacks).
- Caveat/lesson: the company also ran general e-commerce fulfillment (3PL) for other food brands (ice cream, candy) alongside Buc-ee's reselling, but after ~2 years reviewing financials they concluded the general 3PL business "does not make any money" — high margin for error, low profitability — and dropped all other 3PL clients to focus solely on the Buc-ee's reselling operation.

## Holding company structure: "Co-Founders"
- Portfolio at time of recording:
  - **Mining Syndicate** — sells Bitcoin mining hardware plus hosting/data-center services to crypto miners; most revenue comes from hardware sales, not hosting.
  - **Texas Snacks** — the Buc-ee's resale business described above.
  - **Fast Tree Care** — a tree-trimming business launched with an outside operator; domain (fasttreecare.com) was bought and held ~6 years before launching, after seeing a friend succeed with a similar business.
  - **Voltera** — AI software helping skilled nursing facilities find additional medical billing codes; pre-revenue at time of recording, about to onboard its first customer cohort.
  - An RV park in Waco, TX.
  - Several (3-5) other projects in active incubation, some of which may never launch.
- Recruiting model for young/student operators: recruits via job postings at hundreds of universities nationwide; explicitly does not screen by school, GPA, or major — only requires demonstrated past entrepreneurial initiative of any scale ("even a lemonade stand"), plus three traits: humility (coachable), gratitude, and high energy.
- Deal structure with recruited operators: given a choice of 3 pre-vetted business ideas, a salary, a timeline, and company-provided funding; the holding company takes majority equity (cited range: 51-70%) and provides hands-on guidance on exactly what to do.
- General 80/20 observation: acknowledges (referencing Andrew Wilkinson/Tiny) that ~20% of portfolio companies tend to generate ~80% of revenue — at time of recording that was Mining Syndicate, attributed partly to high per-unit price point (a $100k sales day only requires selling ~20 units).

## Bitcoin mining hardware business (Mining Syndicate) origin and model
- Founded opportunistically in 2021 after China banned domestic Bitcoin mining overnight, crashing mining difficulty and making mining extremely profitable for a window.
- Found and directly purchased (via a lease, not a formal acquisition — no LLC, no employees at time of purchase) a facility 30 minutes from home that already had heavy power infrastructure/transformers on-site (the scarce, hard-to-find asset) and a handful of already-operating miners netting ~$15,000/month at the time — those existing miners were later sold off since the plan was to be a hardware/hosting business, not a mining operator itself.
- Business model: sell mining hardware (example: a ~$5,000 machine capable of mining ~$12/day profit in Bitcoin) plus separately sell hosting/electricity services (~$200/month) — the hardware sale is the primary profit center; hosting is run roughly at breakeven.
- Founder had almost no prior mining experience — had only bought two used miners from eBay to learn hands-on before scaling into the business.

## Real estate / mobile home park investing
- Entry point: bought his first home in Tuscaloosa, AL for $90k in 2008 (7 months into marriage) using the $8,000 Obama-era first-time homebuyer tax credit, then used that credit to buy a nearby rental house — early evidence the seller likely would have negotiated down on price after sitting on market a year, a lesson in not assuming a broker's "they won't budge" claim.
- That grew into a portfolio: partnered with others in Dallas to buy a small RV park in Oklahoma, then 24 more mobile home and/or RV parks over the next two years; the portfolio was sold in 2021, then he resumed buying more parks with his Co-Founders partner Nick afterward; also occasionally acts as a GP on other partners' deals.
- Investment thesis for mobile home parks: buying the underlying land/infrastructure, not the homes themselves — avoids most maintenance liability. After acquiring a park, the company sells or gives the individual mobile homes back to tenants who own them, or gives non-owning tenants time to relocate; strategy trades some top-line rental revenue for a much lower management/maintenance burden (pure land-lease model going forward).

## Founder's daily operating system / productivity approach
- Uses "Parkinson's Law" (work expands to fill the time available) explicitly as a management philosophy — deliberately keeps a mostly empty calendar and lets important tasks "bubble to the surface" rather than over-scheduling or over-structuring priorities; treats dropped/forgotten tasks as a signal they weren't actually important.
- Tooling: intentionally low-tech — uses open Chrome tabs as a to-do list, a handful of pinned Google Sheets, the iPhone Reminders app, and unread email/text counts as his task tracking system; keeps his calendar nearly empty (one or two entries most days).
- Typical day: wakes ~6am, goes straight to the office, achieves inbox zero early, writes long-form content/newsletter, works ad hoc with operators, stops by 6pm for family dinner (rarely misses it), works from the car during kids' sports practice, and treats work as enjoyable ("feels like play") rather than draining.
- Idea generation described as unstructured/constant pattern-matching from daily life rather than a formal ideation process — analogized to how a songwriter like Taylor Swift generates material simply because that's the lens they see the world through; the "Fast Tree Care" business, for example, sat as a bought-and-renewed domain for 6 years before conditions aligned to launch it.

## Personal background context (included for founder credibility/backstory, less directly actionable)
- Early entrepreneurial exposure: worked as a teenager for a New Jersey-transplant "hustler" named Danny who ran pest control and restaurant businesses; cites Danny's memorable, non-standard billboard advertising (a giant cartoon toothbrush and cockroach reading "Got Bugs," no phone number) as a formative branding lesson.
- Earliest childhood business: collecting golf balls that landed in his yard/ditch from an adjacent golf course, cleaning them, and reselling them in the clubhouse parking lot — driven by financial necessity (no money for hobbies/toys growing up).
- Writing background: developed persuasive/copywriting skill informally, by studying and adapting ads that worked rather than formal training; has been posting consistently and intentionally on Twitter/X for about 15 months at time of recording; believes writing ability functions as a "superpower" combined with actually doing notable things worth writing about.
- 2017-2019 altcoin trading venture with John McAfee: built an algorithm scoring altcoins by social-media "hype" relative to market cap (implying overvalued tokens to short, undervalued/high-hype-low-cap tokens to buy); partnered with McAfee purely as a revenue-share/promotional relationship (McAfee tweeted about the project twice weekly in exchange for a quarter of profits — no equity, no investment from McAfee) to build a paid Discord community; the associated token reached a market cap of "$30-something million" and was listed on roughly a dozen exchanges before the 2018-2019 bear market caused it to decline and the community to wind down.
- Runs a live weekly Twitter Spaces call-in show ("like Dave Ramsey for entrepreneurs") at noon on Fridays, answering business questions live; was planning to do 10 episodes as an experiment at time of recording.
- Family/education notes on entrepreneurship: has his kids earn money via chore charts, run small resale/competition exercises (e.g., turning a $5 candy budget into the most profit reselling at school), and has done informal trading competitions.
