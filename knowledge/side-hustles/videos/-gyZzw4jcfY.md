# How to Scrape Millions of Leads for Almost $0 (Without Coding)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-gyZzw4jcfY | Published: 2024-10-08

Tutorial on no-code data/lead scraping tools, framed around using scraped data to validate business ideas, find customers, and do market research/competitor research.

## Tools covered (in order of use)

1. **BuiltWith** (builtwith.com) — scrapes tens of millions of websites and lets you search/filter/export them without any scraping skill.
   - Search by: product/industry category (e.g. Tools > Product Lists > Animals and Pet Supplies for 5,000 pet-supply e-commerce sites), by underlying tech stack (e.g. "global Magento stores" = 125,000 sites — useful because most competitors only target Shopify and ignore older/larger Magento stores), or by keyword (e.g. "lawyer" = 95,000 lawyer websites).
   - Exported data includes: approximate tech spend, URL, business name, social followers, estimated sales revenue (flagged as often inaccurate), page rank/traffic estimate (flagged as fairly accurate, pulled from Similarweb), phone number (~25% coverage), email (~50% coverage), social URLs, city/state/zip, last-scraped date.
   - Business-idea use case named: e.g., sell tree-trimming subcontractor leads, sell email marketing services targeted specifically at Magento (underserved) stores, sell Google/Facebook ad leads to lawyers.
   - Cost: ~$500/month. Loophole/hack: pay for one month, cancel immediately after paying, and export as much as possible during the 30 days before cutoff (no throttling mentioned).
   - Cheaper alternative hack: post a small gig on Upwork/Fiverr asking for someone who already has a paid BuiltWith account to run your export for ~$20 flat, regardless of whether it's 1,000 or 1,000,000 records (no extra cost to them).
2. **Instant Data Scraper** (Chrome extension, free, no affiliate) — for one-off scraping jobs on non-standard websites that dedicated scrapers (Amazon/Google-focused tools) don't support. You "train" it on a sample of the page (e.g., only headings, only products) then it runs and exports to a spreadsheet.
   - Example: built a database of all U.S. horse cremation facilities by scraping a humane-society website that listed them (not a standard Google category), then pasted results into ChatGPT to reformat into a clean spreadsheet.
3. **Outscraper.com** — described as personal favorite; supports Amazon, Apple, Airbnb, Facebook, Shopify, Trustpilot, Twitter, but ~99% of the presenter's use is Google Maps business-profile scraping.
   - Process demo: search a business category + city (e.g. "tree service" + Dallas, TX), cap max results (charged per result, ~$0.30-0.40 for phone+name only on 50 results), optionally scrape reviews (including filtering to only negative/1-2 star reviews).
   - Use case: find subcontractors who are also Google Maps-listed competitors (e.g., for a tree-trimming business).
   - Use case for review data: scrape businesses with consistent negative reviews, feed review text into ChatGPT to find complaint patterns, then use those specific patterns in cold outreach (e.g., "I noticed people complain your salon doesn't have open slots — want help fixing that?").
   - Built-in phone/email validation features flagged as inaccurate — presenter instead validates phone numbers via **livebroadcast** and validates emails via **bulkchecker.com**, both described as reliable.
   - Google Sheets formatting tip: strip symbols/dashes from scraped phone numbers before pasting into Sheets, since raw formatting pastes messily.

## Overall framing / caveats

- No coding/technical knowledge required for any of these.
- Applications named: validating a business idea, generating cold-outreach lead lists, competitor/market research, pricing research.
- Presenter explicitly deprioritizes breadth: prefers deeply using one tool (Outscraper for Google Maps) well over spreading across every feature of every platform.
- No revenue/margin numbers are given for a resulting business — this video is purely about the lead-generation/data-scraping mechanics that feed into other side-hustle ideas (e.g., cold outreach, lead-selling, subcontractor sourcing) covered elsewhere in the channel.
