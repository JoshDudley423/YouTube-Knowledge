# John McAfee: From $0 to $30M After Knocking on His Door
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mD9fKSAapFQ | Published: 2024-03-29

Chris Koerner's personal story (2017-2019) of building an altcoin-prediction service and later a crypto token project by partnering with John McAfee. More a narrative/case study than a repeatable how-to, but contains concrete tactics.

## The original idea: altcoin hype/market-cap predictor
- Thesis (late 2017): altcoins (any crypto besides Bitcoin/Ethereum) have no fundamentals, so price direction can be predicted from social hype vs. market cap mismatch.
  - High market cap + low social chatter (Twitter/Reddit) about a coin -> predicted to fall.
  - Low market cap + high social chatter -> predicted to rise (price hasn't caught up to hype yet).
- Build process: used a "hype score" API from CryptoCompare, pulled market cap data for 1,000+ altcoins, combined both in a Google Sheet, sorted into cohorts of 100 by market cap rank. A technical friend (Brian) built the actual API/data-scoring logic; Koerner handled the pitching/business side.
- The thesis reportedly "worked" and he personally invested based on it.
- Problem: no audience/following (only ~200 Twitter followers) and crypto ads were banned on Google/Facebook at the time, so paid distribution wasn't an option -- needed a partner with an existing crypto audience.

## Landing the partnership (cold outreach tactic)
- Target: John McAfee, then the biggest crypto influencer on Twitter (800-900K followers).
- Could not find McAfee's email; guessed many address patterns and used email-verification/open-tracking software until one didn't bounce and showed as opened.
- Persistent follow-up on that same email eventually got a skeptical reply from McAfee ("I don't believe you... even if God himself told me this works I would not believe him").
- Key tactic: when skepticism blocks a deal over email, ask to present in person -- "if you ever have a big partnership or a big deal, fly across the country and get in front of that person... it's harder to say no to someone's face than a call, email, or even a Zoom."
- McAfee agreed to a specific walk-in pitch appointment at his home in Lexington, TN, with a hard-line condition (be on time or get kicked out).
- In-person pitch: walked McAfee's bodyguard/aide through the Google Sheet first informally, then formally presented to McAfee for about 30 minutes. McAfee's reaction flipped from dismissive to enthusiastic once he understood the mechanism ("Chris, this is brilliant... of course this works").
- Deal struck on a handshake, no negotiation on Koerner's side: McAfee would tweet regularly promoting the picks/service in exchange for 25% of the profits.

## Business model v1: free Discord + paid premium picks
- Structure: free Discord community giving away some predictions, paid tier (people were paying $400-500/month for similar "secret edge" crypto communities at the time) for full access to predictions.
- Distribution: McAfee's tweets drove traffic straight into the Discord.
- Launch result: first tweet drove explosive growth (500 -> 2,500 -> 7,000 -> 18,000+ members in a short window), timed at the very start of the 2018 crypto bear market before anyone recognized it as a crash (helped fuel demand for "picks").
- Failure mode: Discord's default permissions at the time let any new joiner self-promote to admin. The group was rapidly overrun by bots/spammers/scammers, and Koerner himself lost admin status and had to abandon the original invite link.
- Recovery: McAfee refused on principle to delete the original tweet ("I never delete tweets... it's public free speech"), so Koerner had to create a new locked-down Discord and have McAfee tweet a follow-up "hackers took over the channel, join this new link" -- got thousands more, but not the original momentum since a launch is a "one shot."
- Ongoing operations: roughly twice a week, Koerner would text McAfee exact tweet copy (new picks released, high-conviction altcoin calls); each tweet pulled a few thousand more members. Group eventually reached 600,000-800,000 members.

## Pivot: launching a community token via free airdrop (no ICO)
- As the bear market deepened, they pivoted from launching the paid tier immediately to launching a token instead.
- Deliberately avoided an ICO/token sale to sidestep SEC involvement/paperwork/lawsuit risk -- did a pure free airdrop instead, explicitly as a legal risk-avoidance move.
- Token's stated purpose: a "Yelp for crypto projects" -- community members could vet/review ICOs, crypto software, and paid groups on a marketplace, flagging scams vs. quality projects. Backed by an 18-page white paper.
- Growth-hacking mechanic for the airdrop (built by a programmer brother-in-law): tiered rewards for engagement -- e.g., give email for 100 tokens, retweet the announcement for 500 tokens, comment/engage for more. This "viral referral engine" drove the announcement tweet to an estimated 15,000-50,000+ retweets and millions of impressions.
- Result: tens of thousands of airdrop claims (estimated 60,000-100,000+), though a large share were bots/scammers -- IP tracking showed most fraudulent claims traced to Nigeria and Vietnam.
- Got the token listed on smaller/mid-tier exchanges (e.g., CoinEx) -- never reached top-tier exchanges like Binance, Kraken, or Coinbase.
- Peak cumulative token market cap reportedly hit ~$30 million -- explicitly caveated as a "clickbaity" number: this reflected the theoretical value of all circulating tokens, not cash Koerner personally received.
- Operational scale at peak: a distributed team (family members, a hired collaborator, several others) running multiple Telegram groups managing the project; nearly launched an in-person crypto conference in Nigeria. Ran for roughly 12-15 months.
- Costly mistake: a team member accidentally executed an 80% token burn instead of the intended 20% by clicking the wrong button in the burn process -- irreversible.

## Eventual paid-group launch and its failure
- After ~a year+, finally launched the originally-planned paid subscription tiers ($79/month, and higher tiers around $279 and $500).
- Team invested heavily (multiple family members plus outside collaborators working "day in, day out").
- Launch-day result: expected $10,000-20,000 in day-one revenue, actually got about $800 -- described by Koerner as one of only four times in his life he's cried over something business-related.
- Some slow trickle of paying customers came in afterward, softening the blow somewhat, but it never hit projections.
- Exit rationale: rather than "wait out" a multi-year crypto bear market with no income from the project, Koerner wound the effort down since he had bills to pay and could not sustain 24 months of unpaid work on a bet that the market would recover.
- Financial outcome: made some money overall (from the eventual paid group revenue and from selling some of their own tokens to fund operations), but nowhere near the flashy $30M figure suggested -- net "not the worst startup" he's run, but not a big financial win either.

## Takeaways/caveats explicitly framed in the video
- In-person pitching after cold email outreach can convert a skeptical high-value partner where email/calls fail.
- Free-token/airdrop models avoid ICO/SEC regulatory risk but the token's speculative "market cap" figure is not equivalent to real revenue or cash in hand.
- Viral growth mechanics (tiered referral rewards) can generate huge reach quickly but also invite heavy bot/scam participation -- track by IP/geography to spot fraud.
- Platform default settings (e.g., Discord's old auto-admin permission) can destroy a fast-growing community; lock down permissions before a public growth push.
- A charismatic influencer partnership can produce explosive early traffic, but sustained monetization (the actual paid product) can still fail badly even after a huge free audience is built -- audience size does not guarantee conversion.
