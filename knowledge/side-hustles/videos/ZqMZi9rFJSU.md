## The question
- A listener started a **gutter cleaning side hustle** in late February and asks for the best way to find jobs and grow in the early stages. The advice generalizes to any local home-service business (pressure washing, home detailing, lawn mowing, etc.).

## The advice
- The real obstacle to getting first customers isn't lack of demand — it's **pride/ego**. Services like gutter cleaning, pressure washing, detailing, and lawn mowing are things friends and family already pay someone to do; the hard part is being willing to ask them directly.
- Concrete script: call friends and family individually (not text) and frame it as testing/building a portfolio — "I just started this business, I'm testing right now, I want to see if I'm any good, I need some testimonials — would you let me clean your gutters at 20-30% off (or half off)?" Get those first few discounted jobs to build proof and testimonials.
- Gutter cleaning specifically is described as a low-risk, "binary outcome" type of service from the customer's perspective — they can't inspect the inside of the gutter themselves, so as long as visible results look fine, they'll consider the job a success and be a source of referrals.
- After completing a job, explicitly and unashamedly ask for referrals.
- Numbers example (repeated from the fuller episode): an Australian entrepreneur built an **$8 million window-washing company** starting purely from friends-and-family referrals and organic word of mouth — logic: each person knows roughly 300-400 other people; if just 1% of those give referrals, 20 initial friends can cascade to 60, then 180, then more, compounding outward.
- Bottom line: before assuming you need paid marketing or lead-gen tools, most beginners haven't actually exhausted their own personal network — do that first, then systematically ask every satisfied customer for referrals.
