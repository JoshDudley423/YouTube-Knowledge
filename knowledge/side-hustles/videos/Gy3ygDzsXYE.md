# How to Start a Niche Job Board With 80% Margins
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gy3ygDzsXYE | Published: 2024-10-26

"HoldCo Bros" episode: Chris Koerner and Nik riff on multiple business ideas — niche job boards, seasonal home-service businesses, AI-generated family photos, and a Zapier→Make.com conversion agency.

## Idea 1: Niche/personality-based job board
- A job board is normally a hard business (two-sided marketplace — needs both job listings and applicants) but becomes a great, passive business once you find a specific niche/angle. Companies pay $2-$500 to list a job; applicants apply for free. Very low customer support once running.
- Example cited: a niche physician job board reportedly does ~$80M/year purely by being hyper-targeted to one profession (not high-tech, just niche).
- The specific idea floated: a job board segmented by **personality type** rather than job type/industry — e.g. "remote jobs for introverts" (inspired by a viral tweet with 2.2M views curating introvert-friendly remote jobs like Tesla/Apple roles). Other segment ideas: extroverts, Myers-Briggs types, ex-collegiate/ex-professional athletes (pitch: disciplined, hard workers — could charge employers a premium for this targeting), or hyper-niche subsets like D2 track-and-field athletes, or LDS return missionaries.
- Rationale: parallels the "unbundling" trend (cable → Netflix/Hulu/ESPN+; large social platforms → niche local communities) now happening in hiring.

### Steps to launch a niche job board
1. Use off-the-shelf, no-code job board software — JobBoard.io ("JB board") and NicheBoard-style plug-and-play platforms were named as ready-made solutions that handle private communications, payment processing, and posting infrastructure without needing to build custom tech.
2. Solve the "chicken and egg" marketplace problem by filling the supply side first: scrape/aggregate job listings from other job boards that fit your niche (many job board platforms have API integrations, e.g. with "Indeed"-type sources, that let you bulk-import external listings with one click) so the board looks active even before you have paying employers.
3. Build the demand side by finding the niche community and marketing directly to it — e.g., subreddits and Facebook groups for introverts — to get applicant traffic flowing.
4. Once there's applicant traction, go sell paid job listings to employers (flat fee per post, paid by the employer, not the applicant).
5. Suggested profile to run this: someone with no coding skill needed, but must go recruit a receptive niche community around a shared identity/cause.

## Idea 2: Seasonal home-service/pop-up businesses — piggyback + location strategy
- General framing: seasonal businesses (Christmas tree lots, Christmas lights installation, fireworks stands, snow cone/ice cream stands, seasonal car washes) are easy to miss because "by the time you notice one, it's too late" — the move is to plan a year ahead.
- Reference data point: the Halloween costume/decor industry generates ~$12B/year; Spirit Halloween alone (a pure seasonal pop-up retailer) does an estimated ~$1.2B/year (~10% of the total market) despite operating only part of the year.
- **Piggyback strategy** for launching a new seasonal service (e.g. Christmas lights): find an already-established, mid-sized service business with the right customer base and existing crew (e.g. a landscaping company with trucks) and offer to (a) have them refer customers for a cut, or (b) let them white-label/collect payment and pay you ~70% while you do the actual installation work — you tap into demand they already have access to rather than building it from scratch.
- **Site-selection strategy** for a location-dependent seasonal pop-up (e.g. Christmas tree lot): since there's no brand loyalty in this category (people just buy from whichever tree lot they drive past), the winning move is picking the best physical location, not out-competing on branding. Concrete steps discussed:
  1. Pull public traffic-count data from the Department of Transportation website for intersections in your target area.
  2. Cross-reference with Google Earth/Maps to find which high-traffic intersections actually have physical space available for a pop-up.
  3. Look up the property owner via tax records and negotiate a short-term lease (e.g., ~2 months/year) for that lot.
  4. Optional idea (untested, flagged as risky/unproven — "I've never seen anyone doing it"): pre-sell/reserve Christmas trees via Facebook ads before the impulse-buy window, sourcing trees wholesale from a commercial tree farm, to capture demand digitally rather than relying purely on walk-up traffic.
  5. To estimate potential unit volume: ask an existing tree-lot operator how many trees they sell per season at their traffic-count location, then scale proportionally by the traffic count of your own target intersection (e.g., "20,000 cars → 2,000 trees, so a 75,000-car intersection could support ~7,500 trees").

## Idea 3: AI-generated family photos / "unbundling" of family photography
- Pain point identified: traditional family photo sessions are stressful/expensive and infrequent (once a year); people would pay for a way to get "everyone smiling" without the hassle of a live shoot.
- Idea A: sell an "AI photo touch-up" package to existing family photographers/videographers — an AI-savvy operator builds/licenses image-generation tooling and sells it to photographers as an add-on service that fixes group-shot issues (everyone smiling, eyes open) using AI editing rather than dozens of retakes.
- Idea B: cut out the photographer entirely — a standalone website/service (e.g. $19-$50 flat fee) where a customer links their Google Photos/Apple Photos album (or a parent curates a batch of casual photos) and the service auto-generates polished/stylized "family portraits" (e.g., placed at a hayride, Christmas setting, Mount Everest, Western saloon) using AI.
- Caveat raised from personal experience: consumer AI photo-generation tools (tested via typical "upload 10 selfies" AI headshot generators) often produced poor likeness/quality when source photos were inconsistent in angle/lighting/resolution — quality is very source-photo-dependent; Midjourney/ComfyUI were mentioned as having improved options versus older tools.
- Alternative concept discussed: bring back "mall photo studio" style sessions (controlled studio lighting, no golden-hour scheduling stress) combined with AI post-processing, versus doing it fully outdoors/DIY.
- Subscription angle: modeled on Chatbooks (a real company, founded by Nate Quigley, that syncs with your photo library and mails a printed photo book periodically) — pitch is an "AI Chatbooks" that could generate and send themed family photo content year-round (back-to-school card, birthday card, summer recap card) instead of one static annual photo.

## Idea 4: Zapier-to-Make.com automation-cost-savings agency
- Zapier and Make.com (formerly Integromat) do functionally the same thing (connect APIs/automations between tools/platforms), but Make.com is described as roughly 85% cheaper than Zapier for equivalent functionality — though its UI/UX is clunkier and has a steeper learning curve than Zapier's.
- Zapier charges by "Zaps" (individual integrations) and then by "tasks" (each time a Zap executes an action) on a credit system; a single Zap with multiple steps can consume several credits per run.
- Business idea: use BuiltWith.com to scrape/identify websites that have Zapier integrations installed (e.g., detects Stripe or Facebook Pixel integration signals that imply Zapier is likely in use, since Zapier reportedly has ~80% market share for this kind of automation), then cold-outreach those companies with the pitch: "You're overspending on Zapier — I'll convert your automations to Make.com and charge you 30% of your monthly savings." Real example cited: a $300/month Zapier bill (or as high as $3,000-$10,000/month for larger users) could often be replaced by a Make.com bill roughly one-tenth the cost.
- This conversion is framed as a low/no-cost "foot in the door" — the real recurring revenue is becoming the client's ongoing "automation guy" for a flat monthly retainer: building new automations, fixing broken ones, maintaining the system.
- BuiltWith limitation flagged: it only detects certain named API integrations (e.g., a Stripe or Facebook Pixel connection), not raw usage volume or exact automation platform — the operator has to infer Zapier usage and spend level (e.g., inferring high Shopify Plus-tier stores are more likely paying for higher Zapier usage, or using BuiltWith's traffic estimates as a rough proxy for automation volume). No way to know exact volume/spend from the tool alone.
- Ideal operator profile: someone with hands-on experience using both Zapier and Make (or e-commerce/automation background) — enough domain knowledge to walk into a business cold and diagnose what should be automated, since you need to understand their existing payment/CRM/email/checkout workflows, not just the tooling. Fulfillment (building the actual Make.com automations) can be self-taught via AI/YouTube tutorials or outsourced to Upwork/Fiverr freelancers experienced with Make — effectively building a small agency as it scales.

## Tangent: AI and the trades
- Personal anecdote: used ChatGPT (photo of old vs. new lightbulb) to self-diagnose a tripped breaker issue rather than calling an electrician, saving an estimated $250.
- Framing/caveat: AI is viewed as unlikely to replace hands-on trade work (plumbing, electrical repair, roofing, power washing) but is expected to reduce demand for diagnostic-only visits, potentially narrowing (not eliminating) the existing trades labor shortage (cited stat: for every 5 tradesmen retiring, only ~2 are entering the trades to replace them).

## Tools/platforms named
- JobBoard.io and a "NicheBoard"-style plug-and-play job board platform
- BuiltWith.com (tech-stack/API-integration detection for prospecting)
- Zapier and Make.com/Integromat (automation platforms)
- Chatbooks (referenced as an analogy/business-model comparison, not a tool to use directly)
- Midjourney, ComfyUI (AI image generation, mentioned re: photo quality)

## Caveats / risks
- Marketplace (job board) businesses are inherently hard to bootstrap because of the two-sided cold-start problem — the niche/personality angle is proposed specifically as a way to make the supply side fake-able (scraped listings) while you build organic demand.
- The pre-sell-before-impulse-buy idea for Christmas trees is explicitly flagged as unvalidated ("I've never seen anyone doing it," "it would just make me nervous").
- AI-generated family photo quality was found to be inconsistent/poor in the hosts' own tests depending on source photo quality — not a guaranteed drop-in replacement for real photography yet (as of recording).
- The Zapier-arbitrage idea requires either genuine automation expertise or a credible path to acquiring/outsourcing it before selling to a business "cold" — you can't just pitch it as an AI-fueled promise without being able to actually implement the automations.
