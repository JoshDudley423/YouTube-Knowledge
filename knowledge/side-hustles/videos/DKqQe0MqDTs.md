# The Simplest AI Side Hustle for Beginners
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DKqQe0MqDTs | Published: 2026-06-04

Solo how-to episode: build and sell a no-code AI service business to home-service businesses (demonstrated live, end-to-end, including finding a real interested customer on camera). Niche chosen: garage door repair companies.

## The core idea / pitch
- Core insight: 78% of customers hire the first company that gives them a quote — home service businesses lose leads simply by being slow to respond, especially after hours.
- Three sellable products built live: (1) an instant quote generator web app/widget for a business's website, (2) a job profit tracker, (3) an AI voice agent that answers after-hours calls, collects caller info (name, address), and can forward urgent calls to the owner.
- Pricing models proposed: per-lead fee ($20-$200 per after-hours call that converts), a flat monthly fee (hundreds of dollars/month), or a no-risk hybrid used in the live pitch: "no contracts, no money upfront, free setup, pay $50 only when you close a lead."

## Tools and process used
- **ChatGPT**: used only to generate a detailed build-prompt for Lovable (told it the intended buyer — garage door repair owners — was not the person prompting it, that output must go to Lovable specifically, and that the app should be a mobile-friendly web app).
- **Lovable.dev** (no-code AI app builder, ~40M projects built on platform; used by Apple/Microsoft/Uber/HubSpot per platform's own claims): built both apps (quote generator, profit tracker) end-to-end from the ChatGPT-generated prompt, including frontend, backend, database, auth, and payments (recommended Paddle as a pre-integrated payment processor) — each app took roughly 8-12 minutes to build to a live, published, sellable state, including an auto-generated marketing website for the app itself.
- **GoHighLevel "AI Employee" feature**: built the AI voice agent (custom agent, named voice "Jessica," a scripted welcome message, name+address capture, 24/7 inbound-call handling) in a few minutes using a 30-day free trial; tested live with a real inbound call and it correctly captured caller name/address and sent an email transcript/summary. Advises importing a ChatGPT-generated "knowledge base"/script (pricing info, escalation rules) and setting up conditional call-forwarding to the actual owner (with backup numbers) only for urgent-sounding callers.
- **Outscraper**: scraped Google Maps business listings (search term "garage door," excluding "supplier") across 4-5 DFW-area suburb cities, max 1,000 results, phone-number-required filter. Cost: $111 for 542 raw results (host separately quotes an initial estimate of $928 for 1,000 results, actual charge was lower).
- **Google Sheets cleanup workflow** (stated as reusable for any industry, not garage-door-specific): delete unneeded columns down to business name, website, address, phone; standardize phone formats (strip "+1", strip dashes); use conditional formatting to flag names containing "repair" (144 of 542 matched); remove duplicate phone numbers (only ~10 found); filter to cell-phone-only numbers (landline/unknown excluded) — final usable list: 153 mobile numbers from an initial 542 scraped records (~28% yield after cleaning).
- **StraightText**: bulk SMS/cold-texting tool (works cross-platform, not iPhone-dependent); sends texts as blue iMessage-style bubbles, claimed to get higher response/open rates than green (Android) texts since ~2/3 of US phones are iPhones and users trust blue texts more. Used `=RAND()` in Sheets to randomize/shuffle the list before selecting the first 50 to text. Personalized each text by inserting a nearby city name (via the "first name" merge field, since real first names weren't scraped) to boost perceived personalization/deliverability. Recommends texts stay under 160 characters (avoids being split into 2 SMS segments, especially costly/ugly on Android) and that shorter first-contact texts get higher response rates.

## Concrete results from the live test
- 50 SMS sent (opening validation text: "Hey, I'm here in Dallas. Do you fix garage doors? I found you on Google Maps"), no pitch yet — over 30% response rate within the first 10 minutes, trending toward an eventual 60-70% total response rate.
- After sending the actual pitch to 18-20 of the responders, got 1 explicit "yes, interested" from a real, verified local garage door repair business owner in under 1 hour of total outreach time.
- Extrapolated math (explicitly labeled as rough/optimistic): estimated ~20,000-50,000 garage-door-repair companies exist nationally; if this ~2% "yes" rate held and half of those yeses close (1% overall close rate on total contacts), texting 20,000 companies could produce ~200 paying customers; at an assumed $500/month per customer that would be ~$100,000/month ($1.2M/year) in theoretical recurring revenue — presented as a back-of-envelope extrapolation, not a validated outcome.
- Total cash outlay to build and validate before any sale closed: under $100 (scrape cost + texting tool cost); the Lovable and GoHighLevel builds used free trials.

## Caveats
- Chose garage doors specifically because it's high-ticket ($400-$2,500 per repair) and gets after-hours emergency calls, but explicitly avoided plumbing/HVAC/electrical ("the big three") because private equity has already consolidated those niches, implying more competition/sophistication there.
- Google Maps has no dedicated "garage door repair" category, so scrapes are contaminated with garage door suppliers (~10-15% of matches, per host's estimate) requiring manual filtering.
- The single "yes" obtained on camera is only interest, not a signed/paying customer — host explicitly notes "most deals never close" and the extrapolated revenue math is optimistic/illustrative, not proven.
- Pricing was deliberately kept low-risk/low-commitment ("killer offer") for this initial validation phase; host states he'd likely add contracts, upfront fees, or a monthly retainer once/if demand is validated.
- Recommends personalizing texts with a real first name and city where possible (not fully done in this demo due to time) for better response/deliverability, and warns that sending identical/undifferentiated mass texts risks iMessage spam-filtering.
