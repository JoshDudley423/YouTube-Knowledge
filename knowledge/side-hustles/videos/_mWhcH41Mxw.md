# Google's New AI Tool Is Crazy Powerful (Here's How to Profit)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_mWhcH41Mxw | Published: 2025-09-03

- Business idea: use Google Gemini's "Nano Banana" AI image-editing model to virtually stage/upgrade real estate listing photos, then cold-outreach real estate agents to sell it as a recurring service.
- Problem/market stat cited: ~2 million real estate agents in the US; ~70% list homes with bad or unstaged photos. Staged homes sell faster and for 6–10% more (tens/hundreds of thousands of dollars of added value cited as the pain point).
- Process/steps demonstrated live:
  1. Vibe-code a simple web app (using **Lindy**, an AI automation platform with a new "vibe coding" feature) that calls the Gemini Nano Banana API: upload up to 30 photos + a prompt, get edited photos back.
  2. Get a Gemini API key free at studio.google.com/apikey (needs billing connected to actually run).
  3. Scrape real estate agent leads with **Outscraper** (Google Maps data scraper) — pulled ~1,000 max results for "real estate agent" in Oklahoma City; kept only name, phone, city, state, and mobile-number contacts. After removing duplicates and non-mobile numbers: ended with 542 real estate agents with cell numbers.
  4. Clean/dedupe the list in Google Sheets, export CSV, import into **HighLevel (GoHighLevel)** CRM, tag contacts (e.g. "real estate staging"), and auto-enroll into a pipeline/workflow.
  5. Pull an unstaged listing photo from Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com, run it through the Nano Banana app with a prompt like "add furniture" or "stage this room to look fully furnished and decorated," download result.
  6. Upload the edited photo to that agent's contact record in HighLevel; a custom-field trigger automatically fires an SMS/text to the agent with a link to the staged photo and a pitch ("I saw your listing... mocked up a demo... interested?").
  7. HighLevel automation moves the contact through a Kanban pipeline (new lead → text sent → no response/follow-up → responded → called → customer) with task reminders and appointment booking built in.
- Costs/economics: editing one photo costs ~$3.90–$4 (prompt-dependent); a 30-photo listing with ~10 interior photos costs ~$39 total to edit. Proposed pricing to the realtor: $1/photo, meaning ~96% profit margin on the edit-your-own-listing offer (charging $1 vs. paying ~$0.04–$0.39 to generate). Pitch framing: "what do they have to lose other than $20?" Long-term potential pitched as scaling into ~$500/month retainers for ongoing virtual staging and revision work.
- Tools named: Google Gemini "Nano Banana" API, Lindy (AI app builder/vibe coding), Outscraper (Google Maps scraper), Google Sheets (lead cleanup), HighLevel/GoHighLevel (CRM, automation, SMS workflows, Kanban pipeline, custom fields, triggers).
- Caveats/risks raised: this is a "bait and switch" in the sense that staged photos shown to buyers don't reflect the home's real, unstaged condition — host argues this is acceptable because it only gets more buyers to tour, doesn't create false expectations about anything other than staging; he explicitly draws the line at NOT making a messy/ugly room look clean/renovated (that would be a step too far and could upset buyers on viewing). Texting agents without permission using scraped numbers is presented matter-of-factly with no discussion of texting-consent/TCPA compliance risk. First outreach attempts shown in the video did not produce a sale by the end of filming — host acknowledges no response yet.
- Upsell path mentioned: once an agent is a customer, cross-sell HighLevel-powered services — AI voice receptionist, paid ads, content creation/posting, booking systems, lead gen, email marketing — described as "where the real money will be made."
