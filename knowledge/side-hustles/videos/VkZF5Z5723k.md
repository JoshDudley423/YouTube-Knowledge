## The business idea: perfume/cologne vending machines
- Physical unit: glass display case (about the size of a large mailbox) holding 5 bottles of perfume/cologne, each connected via tubing to a nozzle; a credit-card reader plus 5 buttons let a customer pay $1 for one spray (aimed at wrist/neck), mounted on a pedestal or wall.
- Manufacturers currently based in Europe (mentioned Latvia/Estonia); as of filming, deployed mainly in Europe, Dubai, and Saudi Arabia/Middle East — not yet established in the US.
- Unit cost: $2,000–$2,500 per machine (per the on-screen description; the video's own description/metadata separately states machines run from ~$3,250 plus tax/shipping, with volume discounts at 5+ units — treat the $2,000–$2,500 figure from the dialogue as the more optimistic/rounded number).
- Margins: described as 90%+ gross margin per spray, since each bottle holds ~300 sprays at $1/spray (~$300 revenue per ~$10-30 bottle of perfume, though exact wholesale bottle cost isn't stated).
- Add-on revenue ideas: play ads on the machine's screen; place a QR code under each bottle linking to an affiliate program for full-size bottle purchases (~$10 affiliate commission per bottle sold) — no e-commerce/website build required, purely affiliate-link based.
- Per the video description (not the dialogue): net profit reported at $700–$3,000/month per machine depending on placement/factors, with a payback period of roughly 1–4 months per machine at that profit rate against the ~$3,250 unit cost.

## Placement/location ideas discussed
- Gyms (a guest, Sam, reportedly keeps cologne in his gym bag to mask sweat without showering — suggested placing a unit in a gym bathroom or at the exit).
- High-foot-traffic public spaces: mall common areas (pedestal placement), movie theater lobby/concession lines (e.g., next to a Cinemark ticket line).

## Market viability debate (a live, informal gut-check using ChatGPT queries)
- Initial skepticism: perfume vending felt more suited to markets/cultures where deodorant use is less common (framed crudely as "places where they don't wear deodorant").
- Chris queried ChatGPT for supporting data: France, Spain listed among top cologne/perfume exporting countries.
- Follow-up ChatGPT query on consumer purchasing (2023 figures cited in dialogue): US is described as a major fragrance market, cited as $8.7 billion in fragrance sales in 2023; Brazil ~$4 billion; France ~$2.6 billion; UK ~$2.3 billion; Germany ~$2 billion. Turkey noted as having the highest per-capita perfume usage globally (cited as ~85% of the population wearing perfume regularly).
- Conclusion drawn: despite initial doubt, the US market data suggested real demand exists, undercutting the "only works in specific cultures" concern.
- Suggested de-risking approach: place units in 10–20 different location types/venues and observe performance data by location before scaling, rather than assuming one location type will work.

## Related concept mentioned (not pursued as a real idea, told as an anecdote)
- A urinal-mounted device (seen at a bar in Lansing, Michigan) that measures urine stream strength/volume, ranks users on a bar leaderboard, requires payment to use, and displays ads during use — cited only as an example of an unusual/viral vending concept, not something the hosts plan to build.

## Caveats / risks raised
- Cultural/geographic fit for the specific vending format is unproven in the US — the hosts' confidence is based on general fragrance-spending data, not a validated US pilot of this exact vending machine format at the time of the conversation.
- No unit economics were given for customer acquisition cost, foot-traffic conversion rate, or realistic spray-purchases-per-day at any specific venue — the profitability discussion is largely theoretical/back-of-envelope in this clip (see the video description's numbers above for the closest thing to real figures provided).
- No discussion of maintenance, restocking logistics, spoilage/evaporation of open perfume bottles, or vandalism/theft risk for a public vending unit.
