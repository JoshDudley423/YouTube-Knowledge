# The 10 Most Genius Business Ideas This Week
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IVn8JJcfCRM | Published: 2025-03-01

Rapid-fire reaction compilation of viral product/business reels with brief commentary on each. Short (~4 min), light on hard numbers/process detail; mostly idea sparks plus two reusable frameworks.

## Business ideas discussed

- **Backyard-invented gadget → productized/manufactured version**: reacting to a viral homemade contraption (unspecified device built in someone's backyard), recommends: use Thomasnet to find contract manufacturers, get a prototype made, file a patent pending, and start collecting pre-orders before mass-producing — turn a viral "look what this guy built" clip into an actual sellable product.
- **Dog-walking bike ("walked by your dog")**: a bike from Thailand that lets the rider be pulled/pedaled by their own dog for exercise (used in informal races there); not sold in Europe or the US. Framed as an underexploited import/licensing opportunity playing to "dogs get exercise, owner gets to be lazy."
- **Vacuum-packed/rolled furniture shipping (Casper-style compression packaging)**: generalizes the mattress-in-a-box model (Casper: roll up, vacuum-seal, box, ship anywhere) to other bulky goods — cites an example of someone applying the same compression-shipping trick to couches. Framed as a repeatable "what if we did this with X" framework for shrinking any bulky product's shipping footprint.
- **"Rent-a-chicken" backyard egg farming service**: real cited business reportedly generating **$17 million/year**. Leases a chicken coop, feed, and live chickens to households (no long-term ownership commitment) so people can raise their own eggs; priced around **$700** for a starter setup. Rationale: rising egg prices + desire for food control/self-sufficiency, despite the service working out more expensive per egg than store-bought. Explicitly called out as an easily copyable model ("anyone could copy this business").
- **Tractor + inner-tube towing rides**: pay-per-ride recreational activity — buy/use a tractor, attach inner tubes, charge **$10/ride**.
- **Underground storm shelters / "underground fridge" novelty**: references a real friend's business in Alabama selling underground storm shelters generating **~$4.5 million/year**; suggests reselling from an existing storm-shelter manufacturer rather than manufacturing yourself, or pivoting the same underground-structure concept into a novelty "underground fridge" angle (marketed on uniqueness/environmental appeal rather than practical need) — same core reseller could offer both framings/products to the same buyer pool.
- **Engine-block coffee/fire table**: take a scrap engine block, add a glass top, sell as a design/novelty coffee table or fire table; cites people already selling these online for "thousands of dollars" — presented as a low-cost-input, high-margin niche product for anyone willing to source cheap junk engine blocks.
- (Also briefly mentioned without much elaboration: RV parks as a category the creator and partners are personally invested in — cited $150M worth of RV park holdings — framing outdoor/travel demand post-COVID as a durable tailwind, not a specific side-hustle instructions.)

## Frameworks emphasized

- **"Do the hardest task first" (Parkinson's-Law-adjacent lesson)**: illustrated via a viral clip of two kids racing to move cups, one starting with the farthest/hardest cups first — advises tackling the 2-3 most difficult/dreaded tasks of the day first thing, since work expands to fill the time available regardless.
- **Cheap demand-validation before building/importing anything**: for a product seen going viral in Asia but not sold in the US/Europe, calculate rough cost to make or import, post photos/videos of it on Facebook Marketplace *before you own or have built one*, and track inquiries/clicks/views in a spreadsheet to gauge real demand before committing capital. "You either win or you learn."
- **Packaging/framing the same underlying product to different buyer motivations** (storm shelter vs. "underground fridge") to potentially reach separate demand pools with a single supply chain.

## Caveats / risks

- Very idea-sparking/speculative in nature — most items are reactions to unverified viral clips rather than confirmed case studies; only the rent-a-chicken ($17M/yr) and storm shelter ($4.5M/yr) figures are attributed to what the creator presents as real known businesses.
- Explicitly does not "condone" the informal dog-bike racing shown in the source clip, while still extracting the underlying business concept.
- The engine-block table and inner-tube-ride ideas carry no cited revenue data — presented purely as low-barrier niche ideas, not validated businesses.
- No discussion of regulatory/insurance issues despite these being physically risky activities (towing people behind a tractor, storm shelters) — the creator notes anticipating "insurance" comments but does not address the concern substantively.
