# This AI Finds Cheap Products You Can Sell for 5x More
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b4_DbNaCeGc | Published: 2026-04-02

- Business idea: import/sell a physical product using an AI sourcing agent (Accio) to go from raw idea to production-ready supplier in one sitting, no engineering/design/CAD skills needed.
- Worked example in the video: a "porch pirate box" / parcel lockbox (secure delivery box disguised as outdoor furniture) to address the growing "porch piracy" problem.

Process demonstrated:
1. Validate demand first with Google Trends (checked "porch pirate" search volume trending up over 22 years/5 years, then "porch pirate box" exploding over the last 9 months).
2. Describe the product in natural language to Accio (an AI sourcing agent covering 400M+ products and 1.5M+ suppliers across countries, not just China). If unsure of the prompt, have ChatGPT refine it first. Choose a "fast" model for quick results or a slower/more thorough model for deeper reasoning; Accio also uses image recognition to match products to a description.
3. Let the AI surface category/product names you didn't know existed (e.g., discovered these are called "parcel boxes"/"parcel lockers"), then re-check Google Trends with the correct terminology.
4. If no existing product looks right, generate a "tech pack" (construction details, specs, measurements, colors — the doc manufacturers need) from a single AI prompt, based on modifying an existing design (e.g., "make it more wood grain and natural"). No CAD/3D modeling skill required.
5. Price-check: compare Accio/supplier sourcing price against Amazon retail price for the same/similar item to calculate potential margin. Example found: a $27 wholesale/wooden delivery box vs. $143 Amazon retail price — roughly 75-80% gross margin.
6. Use "Amazon photo search" (a free third-party app) to reverse-image-search a supplier photo and find the exact matching product already listed on Amazon, to validate it's a real sellable item and see live pricing/sales volume.
7. Compare near-identical competing listings (screenshot both, ask an AI which one likely sold more and why, based on photos/price/color options alone) to learn what design/pricing choices drive more sales. One competitor had 400+ units sold/month, another 500+, at this price point.
8. Contact suppliers directly through Accio's "send inquiry" (auto-fills common sourcing questions); can enable auto-reply while supplier is in a different time zone (suppliers often 11-15 hours ahead of US).
9. Get a free or low-cost (~$100) sample from a supplier to shoot photos/video and validate demand before committing to a full order (video cites a full 100-unit order costing about $2,700) — supplier minimum order quantities in the example were around 100 units.
10. Three suggested sales channels: Facebook Marketplace (can list in ~5 minutes, get inquiries in 10-15 min), Amazon, or your own Shopify store driven by Google/Facebook/Instagram ads.
11. Can set standing sourcing preferences in Accio (e.g., "only show me China," "only distributors, not manufacturers") to refine results going forward.

Numbers cited:
- Estimated 75-80% gross margin between supplier cost and Amazon retail price for the parcel-box example.
- After accounting for shipping and Amazon fees, expect roughly 20-30% net profit margin.
- Sample cost: as low as free, or ~$100.
- Full first order of ~100 units: ~$2,700.

Tools/platforms named: Accio (AI sourcing agent, accio.com), Google Trends, Amazon (as sales channel and price benchmark), "Amazon photo search" reverse image search app, Facebook Marketplace, Shopify, ChatGPT (for prompt refinement), AliExpress/Alibaba (supplier sources Accio pulls from).

Caveats/risks:
- Anecdotal belief in demand isn't enough — verify with Google Trends before sourcing.
- Some suppliers set high minimum order quantities (e.g., 100 pieces) — not the only vendor, shop around.
- Don't go all-in on a full bulk order before validating demand with a sample.
- Sponsored/ad-driven competitor listings can be a signal of profitability (companies won't pay for ads on unprofitable products) but also signal more competition in that specific sub-niche.
