# A Business Anyone Can Start Today With Just $100
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R6-Es5rLfuA | Published: 2025-06-23

Chris Koerner solo deep-dive (not an interview) into mailbox installation/repair as a low-cost, low-competition local service business, with real case studies pulled from Reddit and Facebook, plus a scalable e-commerce variant.

## The idea
- Mailbox installation and repair as a local service business: fixing/replacing leaning, rusted, or damaged residential mailboxes (160 million delivery points/mailboxes exist in the US). Framed as a "hiding in plain sight" opportunity because it's unsexy and virtually absent from TikTok/social content compared to pressure washing, gutter cleaning, window washing, etc.
- Three variant business models: (1) local handyman/repair service, (2) custom/novelty mailbox maker (can become a nationwide e-commerce business, not geography-limited), (3) HOA-focused contractor (fewer, larger, recurring contracts).

## Numbers
- Startup cost: as low as $100 (concrete, post, mailbox, screws from Home Depot) plus $1-500 for basic tools (post hole digger).
- Standard mailbox install/repair pricing: $350-$550 per job (per Thumbtack/contractor-site research), taking 1-2 hours — nets roughly $100-200/hour after ~$100 in materials; ~90%+ gross margin.
- Brick/stone/masonry mailbox jobs: $800-$1,600, takes longer and requires more skill, but a motorized auger can complete a hole in ~45 minutes.
- Custom/novelty mailbox route: charge $600-$900 (vs. $200-$400 for standard), lower volume but higher profit per job; one Arizona-based maker sells contemporary custom mailboxes for $300-$2,300 shipped worldwide.
- E-commerce variant unit economics: source novelty mailboxes from AliExpress/Alibaba/DHGate/Temu for ~$150 each (+ ~$50/unit bulk shipping), resell for $600-$900 via a Shopify store + targeted Facebook ads (e.g., a Dallas Cowboys-branded mailbox ad targeted only at DFW-area homeowners, exploiting near-zero ad competition in this specific niche).
- HOA contractor path: average homeowner job cited at ~$500 vs. average HOA job at ~$5,000 (10x larger), even though acquisition is harder (relationship-based, longer sales cycle) — framed as worthwhile as long as HOA acquisition isn't more than ~10x harder than a homeowner lead.
- Real case study (Reddit, r/handyman): one poster fixed/straightened/painted 23 mailbox stands (replacing 2 stands holding 6 mailboxes) in about a week for $9,000 total — the entire job originated from a single Facebook group comment reply (not even his own post), which reached an HOA president who then called him directly.
- Real case study: "David" in Pennsylvania built a dog-shaped mailbox for himself during COVID, posted it in one Facebook group, got millions of views, and turned it into a full e-commerce business shipping dog-shaped mailboxes worldwide (geography-independent).
- Market-sizing (TAM) math walked through explicitly: mailboxes need replacement roughly every 10 years → ~16 million of the 160 million US mailboxes need replacing per year (~5% of the population, i.e., ~5% of a local population size approximates the annual addressable mailbox count for that area). Example: in a city of 100,000 people, that's ~5,000 addressable mailboxes/year.
  - Conservative case: capturing 5% of that local market/year = 250 jobs/year × $500 = $125,000 revenue/year.
  - Optimistic case: capturing 20% of that local market/year = 1,000 jobs/year × $500 = $500,000 revenue; minus ~20% materials cost = ~$400,000 net profit/year (roughly 2 mailboxes/working day).
- Google Keyword Planner data cited: "mailbox install" has high search competition; "mailbox repair" has identical search volume but low competition — signals most existing competitors focus on installs, leaving repair underserved. Top-of-page ad bids for these terms run only ~$0.90-$1.00, considered cheap for a high-ticket service.
- Nextdoor/Facebook ad cost example: ~$20-30 spend to reach 2,000-4,000 homeowners in a specifically targeted (older-home) neighborhood.
- Direct mail costs: postcards $0.30-$0.50 each; letters over $0.50 each; can be sent via USPS's Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) program.
- Industry growth cited: multi-billion-dollar industry, growing ~10%/year (source not detailed).

## Process / how to do it
1. **Do an "opportunity audit" first, this week**: walk or drive a target area, log addresses of mailboxes needing repair in a spreadsheet, and note the specific defect type (leaning, rusted, faded) to learn the market. Aim to find at least 20 opportunities within a 15-minute drive, and write down exact addresses — these become the first prospect list, not just research. Knock on doors immediately rather than "coming back later," to capitalize on motivation in the moment.
2. **Validate demand before buying tools**: Koerner explicitly says not to buy any tools until landing the first job; if 10 out of 10 people approached decline even a cheap offer (implying they'd rather DIY or already have a handyman), treat that as a signal to abandon the idea for that area rather than force it.
3. **Underprice the first few jobs deliberately** to build case studies, 5-star reviews, and momentum — e.g., if materials cost $100 and eventual target price is $400, charge $200 on early jobs (still ~$50/hour) purely to get portfolio photos/videos and traction, prioritizing momentum over per-job margin at the start.
4. **Skip formalities initially** — no LLC, no domain name, no branding investment before the first sale; just start driving neighborhoods, learning, and selling.
5. **Choose a target neighborhood using aging-housing data** (e.g., Zillow neighborhood age data) to find areas where many mailboxes are old/failing versus newer areas with few opportunities.
6. **Customer acquisition tactics, ranked by ROI**:
   - Neighborhood Facebook groups and Nextdoor.com are called the single highest-ROI channel, "not even close." Strategy: don't post overt sales pitches immediately — "lurk" and reply directly and quickly when someone else posts about a broken mailbox (the $9,000 case study came from a reply, not an original post). Spend 1-3 weeks first posting non-salesy, value-adding content (4-6 posts) to earn a Facebook "top contributor" badge, which improves algorithmic trust/visibility, before posting promotional content (before/after photos of your own work).
   - A third-party tool (Devi, mentioned by name) can monitor Facebook groups automatically and alert you when a post matches pre-set keywords (e.g., "mailbox").
   - Rocks-in-a-bag flyers thrown into driveways of targeted neighborhoods (same tactic described by his contact Daniel for pressure washing, with a reported double-digit conversion rate) — adapted here specifically to homes with visibly broken mailboxes.
   - Google Business Profile: described as a "non-negotiable," low-lift (~30 minutes to fully set up with photos, videos, FAQs, hours) source of ongoing free leads.
   - Timed direct mail: send postcards/letters specifically after major snowstorms or windstorms (weather that plausibly damages mailboxes) — the mailing costs the same regardless of timing, but response is expected to be much higher right after a damage-causing event. Include before/after photos in direct mail pieces.
   - Realtor partnerships: realtors constantly touch multiple homes and need reliable handymen; a mailbox often needs fixing right before a home sale/listing — a "sell to one to sell to many" channel (Koerner notes his tree-trimming business got almost all its first-year jobs from realtors).
   - Door hangers and flyers taped to the outside of mailboxes or trash cans work, but putting flyers INSIDE mailboxes is explicitly called out as illegal (a federal mail-tampering restriction) and must be avoided.
   - Bandit signs left at the job site (e.g., "This mailbox installed by [Business Name]") are cited as a low-cost, high-conversion tactic for visually obvious outdoor home services (a tree-trimming business contact reportedly gets 100% of jobs from bandit signs).
7. **Test many small marketing angles cheaply** — run ~10-20 different Nextdoor/Facebook ad angles targeted narrowly at aging neighborhoods (not broad-targeted) at ~$20-30 each to find the one or two angles that significantly outperform the rest.
8. **Scaling paths once the base business works**:
   - Upgrade tools (post hole digger → motorized auger) to increase throughput/profit per job.
   - Upsell existing customers to premium options (standard post → brick mailbox) or adjacent services (e.g., pressure washing) — but only pursue this if the local addressable mailbox market alone can't support a six/seven-figure business; otherwise stay focused.
   - Name the business generically (e.g., "John Services" rather than "John's Mailbox Services") to preserve future optionality for adding services, even if you never actually expand beyond mailboxes — Koerner cites his own earlier business, Phone Restore, losing iPad-repair customers early on because the name implied phone-only service.
   - Geographic expansion: add second/third crews and expand into nearby cities rather than staying single-city.
   - "Piggybacking" partnerships: pay handymen who don't want to do mailbox jobs a referral fee to send those leads your way; similarly partner with landscapers who see 20-30 homes/day and pay them a referral fee for spotting mailbox opportunities.
9. **Segment approach by target customer type**: (a) handyman/local repair route targets aging neighborhoods and individual homeowners; (b) custom/novelty mailbox route targets higher-income, style-conscious homeowners (Pottery Barn/HGTV demographic) and can scale nationally via e-commerce with Facebook ads and AI-generated image mockups showing a mailbox in front of a specific home; (c) HOA contractor route targets property managers/HOA boards directly via purchased HOA-president contact databases, cold outbound, and in-person attendance at HOA meetings, aiming for large, potentially recurring maintenance contracts.

## Tools/platforms/resources named
- Devi (ddevi.com) — third-party tool that monitors Facebook groups for keyword-matching posts and notifies the user (video's own affiliate link included in description).
- Nextdoor.com and Facebook Groups — primary organic and paid lead channels.
- Google Business Profile — free local SEO/lead tool.
- Google Keyword Planner / Google Trends — used to assess "mailbox install" vs. "mailbox repair" competition and search volume.
- Zillow — used to research neighborhood housing age to target aging-mailbox areas.
- Thumbtack (and similar "hire a contractor" sites) — used for pricing research/benchmarking.
- Outscraper — mentioned as the tool Koerner used to scrape how many companies in Texas specialize in mailbox repair (competitive research).
- Shopify — suggested storefront platform for the e-commerce/custom mailbox variant.
- AliExpress, Alibaba, DHGate, Temu — sourcing platforms for novelty/custom mailbox products to resell.
- Facebook Ads — for both local geotargeted lead gen and national e-commerce customer acquisition.
- USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) — direct mail distribution option.
- AI image generation tools — suggested for mocking up what a custom mailbox would look like in front of a specific customer's home, as a sales aid.

## Caveats / risks
- Putting flyers inside mailboxes is illegal (federal mail regulations) — must use taping to the outside, door hangers, or driveway placement instead.
- The attractive TAM math ($125K-$500K/year back-of-envelope) is explicitly flagged as "spreadsheet math" — Koerner cautions that real execution is harder: insurance is needed, customers will be upset at times, and redoing jobs for free will happen; "business is hard... just pick your problems."
- Validate local demand before investing — if door-to-door conversations reveal most homeowners are happy DIYing or already have a handyman, the advice is to either expand the sample size or abandon the idea for that market rather than force it.
- Custom/novelty mailbox and HOA-contractor approaches involve materially higher upfront costs (import inventory, longer relationship-building sales cycles) and are explicitly framed as trade-offs (lower volume/higher profit, or longer sales cycle/higher deal size) rather than strictly superior to the basic handyman approach.
- No hard data given on realistic conversion rates for cold HOA outbound or e-commerce ad campaigns — these are presented as promising but untested-by-the-video-itself approaches, unlike the two Reddit/Facebook case studies which are real, sourced accounts.
