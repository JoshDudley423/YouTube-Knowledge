# 10 at Once!? Watch me Break ChatGPT Operator
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oAQhg3K8xJI | Published: 2025-02-02

Live demo/stress-test of OpenAI's "Operator" agent (part of the $200/month ChatGPT Pro plan at the time) running up to 10 simultaneous browser-automation tasks. Not a dedicated business pitch, but demonstrates concrete automatable workflows relevant to sourcing/arbitrage-style side hustles.

## What Operator is / cost
- A ChatGPT feature (bundled in the $200/month "Pro" plan at time of filming) that operates a real browser autonomously — clicking, typing, navigating, filling forms — based on a natural-language prompt, and can run multiple independent instances in parallel tabs.
- Host ran 6, then attempted 10, simultaneous Operator tasks across different browser tabs at once.

## Concrete automatable tasks demonstrated (each a template for a data/sourcing workflow)
1. **Resale arbitrage sourcing**: "Find underpriced items on OfferUp and Craigslist to flip on Amazon or eBay." Operator searched listings live and began logging finds.
2. **Product-gap mining via reviews**: "Pull product review data from Amazon and scrape one-star complaints to identify gaps for a private-label product." (Same review-mining-for-product-opportunity concept referenced elsewhere in the channel's content.) Note: Operator initially couldn't view one-star reviews without being logged into an Amazon account — host had to manually sign in for it to proceed.
3. **Flight price tracking/arbitrage**: "Track Google Flights from a specified airport for the cheapest fares, any date in a given year, log to a Google Sheet." Operator searched multiple dates and reasoned (when asked) about *why* it chose certain dates — e.g., it explained it deliberately searched midweek dates because weekend fares are typically pricier, and searched months out because those dates are less likely to already be booked, thus revealing clearer arbitrage.
4. **Lead-list building**: "Extract an email list from Texas Chamber of Commerce directories and put them in a Google Sheet." Operator located directories not requiring login/form entry and pulled emails successfully.
5. **Dropshipping/product-sourcing research on Etsy**: "Analyze Etsy [for trending/profitable products]" (left deliberately vague). Operator found a product, then — when asked — performed a reverse image search (via Google Images) on AliExpress/Alibaba/Temu to locate the original overseas manufacturer/supplier and calculated an ~80% profit margin opportunity (found a matching product for about $25 wholesale against a higher Etsy retail price). This full pipeline (find trending product -> reverse image search -> find supplier -> estimate margin) is a ready-made dropshipping/private-label research workflow.
6. **Underpriced vehicle-flipping research**: Applied to Craigslist listings — asked Operator to compare listed vehicle prices against NADA/Kelly Blue Book values and calculate percent under/overvalued, logging results to a sheet (with mixed reliability — one calculation produced a nonsensical negative percentage).
7. **Short-term rental arbitrage research**: "Analyze Airbnb listings in a specific city (e.g., Minneapolis) for underpriced listings," with Operator asked to explain its own "method for finding arbitrage" — a generally useful prompting technique (ask the AI to narrate its reasoning to learn/refine the approach, and to reveal what signals it's using to flag "value").
8. **Marketplace deal-finding with constraints**: "Find an iPhone 15 on Facebook Marketplace listed today, significantly underpriced, but not stolen or broken." Facebook required active login/session and repeatedly paused the automation requiring the host's attention — flagged as the platform most resistant to unattended automation.
9. **Meta/self-referential task**: had Operator itself use ChatGPT to generate a full tactical business plan (market analysis, operations, marketing, financial projections, regulatory considerations, case studies) for launching a tree-trimming business in Dallas — used as a plan-generation shortcut, though accuracy against the host's real tree-trimming business experience wasn't deeply validated on camera.
10. **Social content research**: asked Operator to find and bookmark viral Instagram Reels (200K+ views) in the "business" niche from the host's own account; after some friction, it succeeded, and could describe what was actually happening inside a video (not just its transcript) — surfacing a bigger idea (see below).

## The most business-relevant emergent idea: training a personal virality predictor
- Once the host confirmed the AI could actually "watch" and describe a video's visual content (not just read a transcript), he outlined a workflow: feed all of your own past social videos (300+ Reels, via API) along with their engagement metrics, have the model learn correlations between content elements (hooks, background images, framing) and view counts, then have it score/predict how a *new*, not-yet-posted video would likely perform before publishing, and suggest specific improvements (e.g., "this hook works better," "this background image outperforms that one"). Framed as a major untapped use case for content-driven businesses/creators. (Credited to a friend, "Sam," for the underlying idea.)

## Operational observations/caveats about using Operator for this kind of work
- Running many parallel automated tabs is technically demanding to supervise — tasks can "glitch," misfire, log data into the wrong sheet, or require the human to intervene (solve CAPTCHAs it flags incorrectly, log into accounts, click non-CAPTCHA checkboxes it misidentifies).
- Facebook and Instagram both required active login sessions and repeatedly demanded human attention/interaction to keep running, unlike more open sites (Craigslist, OfferUp, Etsy, Google Flights, Chamber of Commerce directories), which ran largely unattended.
- Amazon required being logged in to view one-star reviews specifically (not needed for other browsing).
- Useful meta-technique: ask the agent to narrate *why* it's approaching a task the way it is (e.g., why it picked certain search dates) — reveals its assumptions and can be corrected/redirected, and teaches the user new ways to think about the underlying arbitrage problem.
- Speculative business idea floated by the host: hiring a virtual assistant (e.g., in the Philippines, cited at roughly $6/hour, framed as ~10x local minimum wage) to babysit/operate multiple parallel Operator-style agent sessions as a "switchboard operator" for running these sourcing/research workflows at scale.

## Caveat
- This is a live, unscripted product demo/experiment rather than a documented, revenue-verified business case — no actual profit/revenue numbers are reported from any of the sourcing workflows shown; the value here is the list of concrete automatable research/arbitrage workflows and prompting techniques, not a proven income claim.
