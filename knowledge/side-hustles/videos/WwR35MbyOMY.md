# This Little AI Device Will Print Millionaires
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WwR35MbyOMY | Published: 2025-04-09

Conversational episode (Chris + guest Nick) about business models built around AI wearable recording pendants (specifically the "Limitless" pendant) and AI implementation/automation agencies more broadly.

## Core business idea: AI implementation/automation agency for local businesses
- Thesis: two "mega trends" converging — the shift toward entrepreneurship and AI adoption — create a window (they argue especially for people under 30) to build agencies that implement AI for business owners who don't understand tools like Zapier, Make, ChatGPT vs. Claude, etc.
- Value proposition: business owners will pay well because you're a needed, scarce resource, and because AI keeps evolving, the relationship isn't one-and-done — it's an ongoing (recurring revenue) engagement, not a single project.
- Caveat raised directly in the conversation: no concrete existing "AI implementation agency" case study/pricing model to copy was identified — this is discussed as a strong theoretical opportunity, not a proven playbook with real numbers.

## Specific idea: sell/rent AI wearable pendants (e.g., Limitless) to physicians as a scribe replacement
- Origin story: guest noticed a friend's wife (an optometry office manager) manually hand-keying patient data into a spreadsheet; her husband (an optometrist who owns his practice) was already experimenting with AI tools (ChatGPT, Otter AI, Fathom for meeting summaries) and was deciding whether to hire virtual assistants or keep onshore staff to replace/support "medical scribes."
- Context: many physicians pay a human scribe to follow them and document patient visits so the doctor doesn't have to write notes — this lets a doctor who could see 10 patients/day instead see 15-20/day (more billable visits), justifying the scribe's cost.
- Proposed business model: buy a batch of Limitless pendants (~$100/unit cited) and give/lend them to physicians to wear during patient visits.
  1. Tier 1 ("get them hooked"): let the physician wear the pendant for 1-2 days for free/low-risk trial; the pendant records locally on-device by default (privacy-focused, not automatically cloud-synced), which is an easy pitch to a privacy-conscious doctor ("nothing leaves the device; I'll come collect and offload it"). At the end of the trial, deliver an AI-generated summary of their patient conversations — described as a likely "lightbulb moment" that converts most doctors immediately.
  2. Tier 2 (optimize): once bought in, give the physician a script/note card of specific questions to ask patients (name, email, phone) so automations (Zapier, Gumloop, Lindy, etc.) can pull that structured data from the recorded conversation into the practice's own patient records/software automatically.
  3. Monetization: pitch as a flat monthly fee, e.g., "$1,500/month and you won't need a scribe anymore, I'll manage all of this for you" — framed as directly replacing the cost of a human scribe, so it's an easy budget swap rather than a new expense.
  4. Compliance note explicitly raised: need to address HIPAA compliance with physicians as part of the pitch/setup.
- Adjacent vertical mentioned: assisted-living/memory-care facilities — give pendants to residents so families get a daily AI-generated summary (activities, meals, medication timing) as an accountability/upsell feature the facility could offer families for an extra charge.
- Adjacent vertical mentioned: relationship building / "foot in the door" lead-magnet — give a pendant as a free trial gift to a high-value target contact (e.g., an executive you want a relationship with) as a way to start a relationship, then later offer to help them set up automations/reminders once they're hooked on the device.

## Specific idea: AI pendants for sales team coaching/accountability (framed as an even bigger opportunity than the healthcare angle)
- Premise: "how do you make the most money? You help people make even more money" — sales coaching is high-leverage because it directly drives revenue.
- Applied to door-to-door sales teams (drawing on the host's own past pest-control sales experience): give every sales rep a pendant, have them wear it all day during customer interactions/pitches.
- At the end of the day, upload all reps' transcripts to ChatGPT and ask it to identify the top-performing rep's 5 best specific verbatim moments/techniques, then use those real examples to coach the rest of the team the next day (e.g., "John, why didn't you mention the neighbor's cockroach problem the way you did with Mary?").
- Framed as dramatically more scalable than a sales manager doing individual ride-alongs (which cost a full day per rep) — one manager could review a full team's conversations, summarized by AI, within a normal workday.
- Extended to retail: give pendants to a slow-performing store's staff for a couple days to capture real (not "best behavior") customer interactions, since employees won't feel watched by a manager standing over them; use the analysis to coach/improve conversion.
- Data point cited: found a correlation in one team between total words spoken per rep per day and their sales ranking (example figures used: John 3,400 words = top performer, Peter 2,100 words = 2nd, Mary 400 words = lowest) — suggested using ChatGPT to run this kind of word-count/sales regression analysis on uploaded spreadsheets to find what actually correlates with performance (e.g., route efficiency, time between houses) rather than "spray and pray" sales management.
- Monetization framing for a coaching/consulting business built on this: charge a flat fee or a percentage of the sales lift you help generate.
- Tangential idea: apply the same device+AI-analysis model to any profession that depends on face-to-face communication performance — cited examples: a marriage/couples therapist (charge patients an extra ~$50/hour for a pendant-enabled "AI analysis of how you talk to each other," split the upcharge with the therapist), or leadership tracking for large volunteer/field organizations (e.g., an example of comparing missionary baptism rates using recorded conversation analysis).
- Legal caveat raised: recording-consent laws vary by state (example given: in Texas, only one party to the conversation needs to know they're being recorded) — must be checked before deploying pendants to record others' conversations.
- Remote-work application: mail pendants to remote employees ($100 device vs. companies already spending $1,000+ on a laptop) as both an accountability tool and a way to track/improve performance in real time — referenced a stat that remote work costs about one lost day of productivity per 5-day week compared to in-office work, as the underlying rationale for wanting better remote productivity tracking.

## Tangential idea: pay-per-use "Deep Research" API service ("Dollar DR")
- Observation: ChatGPT's Deep Research feature can do very deep, detailed background/OSINT-style research (e.g., locating a specific business owner by name/city/property with minimal starting info) but normally requires a ~$200/month subscription tier that's overkill for someone who'd only use it once or twice.
- Proposed business: build a website (working name "Dollar DR" / dollardr) that lets users run one Deep-Research-style prompt for $1 using the host's own Pro-tier account/API access.
- Unit economics estimate: cost to fulfill one query estimated at $0.30-$0.50 in token usage, versus $1 charged — thin per-transaction margin, but framed as viable at volume, especially given "subscription fatigue" pushing consumer preference toward pay-per-use over monthly subscriptions.
- Rationale: most people (e.g., a real estate agent doing off-market outreach) only need a Deep Research query occasionally — nowhere near the ~200 queries/month needed to justify a $200/month plan.
- Caveat/status at time of recording: OpenAI's Deep Research API was not yet publicly available, so this specific business could not be launched immediately as described — host planned to wait for API access. Compared this pay-per-call resale model to how AI "wrapper" products generally monetize (buy API access wholesale, meter and resell it).
- Additional example use case for Deep Research as a service: a business-buyer ("searcher") looking for a wholesale souvenir distributor to acquire (attractive because such businesses rarely fail, have high margins, are B2B/recurring, and there are only ~150 of them in the US) used deep research to identify and find contact info for one within driving distance — cited as a strong applied example of the research-as-a-service value proposition, not itself a packaged product yet.
- Side note: the pendant's captured conversational history can be used to quickly build a personalized context profile ("who I am") to feed into AI tools for more tailored recommendations, reducing the effort of manually prompting AI with your full background/preferences each time.

## Caveats across the episode
- Most ideas discussed (AI implementation agency, pendant-based scribe replacement, sales coaching via recordings, Dollar DR) are speculative/untested by the hosts at time of recording — no revenue figures, client counts, or completed case studies are given for any of them.
- Consent/privacy/compliance issues (HIPAA for healthcare, one-party vs. two-party recording consent by state) are explicitly flagged as real obstacles that must be addressed, not afterthoughts.
- The Deep Research resale idea was blocked at time of recording by lack of API access — not yet executable.
