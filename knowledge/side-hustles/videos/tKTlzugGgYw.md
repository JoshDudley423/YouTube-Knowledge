# I Built an AI Agent in 5 Minutes (And Sold It Live)
Channel: The Koerner Office | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tKTlzugGgYw | Published: 2026-02-17

Chris Koerner does a live, unedited demo of selling AI voice agents to local home-service businesses via "ringless voicemail drops" (which he dubs "reverse cold calling"), then builds a working AI voice agent from scratch inside GoHighLevel.

## The business: selling AI voice agents to local businesses
- Pitch: businesses (especially trades/field-service businesses where the owner is out on jobs and can't answer the phone) are already paying **$500-$2,000/month** for an AI voice agent that answers inbound calls, captures info, and books/schedules — a real, currently-paid-for service (unlike many speculative AI use cases).
- Not a fit for every business type — explicitly called out as a poor fit for a business like a dentist's office (likely has front-desk staff already) but a strong fit for solo/small trade operators who take after-hours calls from a truck (tree trimming, HVAC, pool service, roofing, etc. all cited as targets).
- Community/education resource named: **Playmakers** (playmakersai.com) — a paid community teaching people to become AI consultants who implement AI for small businesses, charging $500-$5,000/month.

## Lead sourcing process
- Tool: **Outscraper** — scrape Google Maps business categories (typed search autocompletes into "approved" Google Maps categories, but manual/keyword search also works) by geography (can scope to a zip code, a city, or an entire state).
- Filter to only leads that have "with phone" data (skip paid enrichments/lead-gen packs you don't need) to avoid paying for unusable records.
- Rule of thumb: expect roughly **1/3 to 1/2** of scraped numbers to be usable (valid) numbers — so scrape ~3x the volume you actually want to end with (example: scraped 1,400 records to net ~500 usable/cleaned numbers, using 500 of his available Outscraper "credits" — cost was roughly $4 for that scrape).
- For phone-number type validation (cell vs. landline) beyond what Outscraper reports (only accurate for ~60% of numbers): upload the list into **Sly Broadcast**'s "manage list" feature (works even on a free/unpaid account) — it re-validates and tags each number as cell or landline for free, adding useful redundancy.
- Randomize lead order before outreach (simple `=RAND()` spreadsheet formula, then sort) to avoid geographic/alphabetical clustering skewing your sample — Outscraper results often come pre-sorted by city, which biases a "top N" outreach batch.
- Market-research method for picking a target niche/industry: use **Google Trends** to check whether a service category's search interest is rising (example: "pool cleaning" search volume climbing since COVID, on the thesis that pandemic-era pool installations are now aging into repair/service needs); optionally go deeper with the **Google Keyword Planner/tool** to compare search volume against ad-bidding competition/cost per keyword variant (e.g., "gutter cleaning" vs. "gutter repair" vs. "gutter replacement") to find "arbitrage" niches — high search demand but low advertiser competition/cheap keywords, suggesting local business owners in that niche aren't yet bidding for that traffic.

## Outreach mechanism: ringless voicemail drops ("reverse cold calling")
- Definition: upload a pre-recorded voice message plus a phone number list to a ringless-voicemail provider; the message is delivered directly to the recipient's voicemail without their phone ever ringing (distinct from a robocall, which does ring).
- Tool used: **Sly Broadcast** (chosen for low cost and usable interface; many competing providers exist). Sends roughly 50 messages/minute per their observed rate.
- Sly Broadcast automatically scrubs numbers against the Do Not Call registry — no manual DNC compliance needed on the sender's part.
- Message-crafting guidance: assume you have only ~2-10 seconds of a recipient's attention before they stop listening (roughly ~10% might listen to a full 10-second message) — keep it extremely short, personalize with whatever specific detail you have (industry/location), lead with a pain point, and end with a clear, low-friction call to action. Example script used: a ~18-second recording pointing out the recipient likely doesn't have an AI voice agent yet and inviting a callback; a shorter ~10-second voicemail-of-a-voicemail was used as his own Google Voice greeting once calls started coming back.
- Advises stacking this with other outreach channels (text, cold call, LinkedIn, email) since single-channel, single-touch campaigns generally underperform — a single ringless-voicemail-only campaign can still have positive ROI depending on the offer, but multi-touch, multi-medium outreach converts better. One live audience member's variant, described as effective: a triple-touch sequence of voice note → email → text message, in that order, yielding "a good response," particularly warmer than cold outreach.
- Live-tested response numbers (single ringless-voicemail batch of ~475-500 messages to Texas tree-trimming businesses): **56% delivery "success" rate** per the platform's own stats (177-282 successful sends observed at different points, out of ~500); Chris's own estimate/prediction for expected results generally: roughly **1-8% callback rate**, of which maybe **20-60%** of those callbacks will be from people who actually heard/understood the voicemail (the rest are low-intent people just returning a missed call, which is still valuable signal because business owners who reliably return missed calls value phone leads). He personally received on the order of a dozen callbacks within minutes of the campaign going out, and closed no formal sale live, but had one recipient express interest in a free build.
- Caveat/failure mode observed live: some voicemail deliveries the platform logged as "failed" (e.g., "unable to detect voicemail") nevertheless triggered automated missed-call-response texts from the recipient's own CRM/automation system (implying the call did register on their end despite the platform's failure flag) — so platform-reported failure rates may understate actual delivery.
- Platform-specific technical note discussed: sending via a personal **Google Voice** number (as used in the demo) does not require carrier/regulatory approval; if you additionally want an inbound **AI voice agent to actually answer the callback calls** (versus a human), you need to run that through a platform like **GoHighLevel** with a properly **A2P-registered ("HPU"/10DLC-approved)** number — Google Voice and Sly Broadcast numbers alone do not require that registration for outbound ringless drops, but a business-grade automated calling/texting number does.
- Related idea floated (untested): instead of manually taking the callback calls yourself, route the ringless-voicemail campaign's callback number directly to a pre-built AI voice agent as a live self-demo ("eat your own dog food") — caller hears the exact AI agent product being pitched when they call back, which doubles as the demo.

## Building the AI voice agent (step-by-step, done live in a few minutes)
- Platform: **GoHighLevel** (referral link in description gives a 30-day free trial instead of the standard 14-day trial). Navigate to AI Agents → Voice AI → Create Agent → build from scratch.
- Configuration fields: agent name, language, voice selection (ElevenLabs-style voice options), underlying LLM (GoHighLevel's suggested default, e.g., GPT-4o, is generally fine without overthinking it), timezone, and a scripted initial greeting line.
- Advanced settings worth understanding: max call time (defaults fine, calls rarely run long), idle-time-before-prompt, response speed (set to fastest), "interruption sensitivity" (how easily the AI thinks it's being talked over mid-sentence — relevant if there's background noise/crosstalk on the caller's end), LLM "temperature" (creativity/how far off-script it can go — left at default), voice playback speed (bumped to 1.2x to keep calls moving), and optional ambient background sound (e.g., a coffee-shop ambience) for a more natural feel.
- Knowledge base ("the brain" of the agent) creation: two options — (1) ask ChatGPT directly to draft a knowledge base document for your specific use case (prompt used: describe the business type and the typical questions/scheduling flow it needs to handle, ask ChatGPT to output something pasteable into GoHighLevel) and paste that in (accepts up to 25,000 characters, plus PDFs/files/tables); or (2) simply give GoHighLevel a URL to crawl (e.g., the business's existing website) and it auto-builds a knowledge base from that site's content/FAQs in under 30 seconds — no technical knowledge-base expertise required either way.
- Configure which caller details the agent should capture (name, address, phone, reason for call) and set up an email notification triggered when a call completes (with a transcript attached).
- Live test call demoed successfully: the agent answered as "Chris's Tree Trimming," handled a simulated storm-damage service request, correctly captured address and phone number, confirmed them back to the caller, and even truthfully disclosed "I'm an AI" when asked directly — described by Chris as sounding "a little robotic" but functionally solid enough that "if it solves a problem, people don't care."
- Fastest path/shortcut: GoHighLevel also has pre-built "Agent Templates" you can install with one click (already trained, no custom setup) rather than building from scratch.
- Distribution/resale note: a built agent (or agent template) can be listed and sold on GoHighLevel's app marketplace; you can either provision a new phone number inside GoHighLevel for the client or port in the client's existing business number.

## Caveats
- This was framed by the host as an unedited, no-plants live demo, but the video itself discloses that one incoming call played back on-air was a pre-recorded call from the Playmakers community, not a live organic response — worth noting when weighing how "live" the observed results actually were.
- No actual paid sale closed on-camera during the segment — engagement (callbacks, one expression of interest in a free trial build) is presented as proof of interest/response rate, not proof of completed monetization.
- Voice quality was self-assessed as "a little robotic," and picking a rushed default voice/name was called out as something to improve on for future builds.
